Hearts burning and eyes opened: from blindness and sorrow to revelation, in five stages of a seven-mile walk (Luke 24:13-35).

Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?

Five stages of the Emmaus road

Two disciples leave Jerusalem grieving. They walk about seven miles (“threescore furlongs”), and come back the same night with the news that the Lord is risen.

The unrecognized companionvv. 13-16

While they talked and reasoned, “Jesus himself drew near, and went with them.” But their eyes were holden.

  • Divine presence: Christ draws near when his people talk honestly together about him and about their struggles.
  • Human limitation: grief, doubt and natural reasoning can keep us from recognizing him, even when he is walking beside us.
The Master's questionsvv. 17-24

Jesus asks, “What manner of communications are these… as ye walk, and are sad?” and then, “What things?” He doesn't need the information. The questions bring their hearts into the open.

  • Purposeful dialogue: questions that expose what we really believe.
  • Broken expectations: “we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel.” They expected an earthly king, so the cross looked like defeat.
“O fools, and slow of heart”vv. 25-27

Jesus doesn't start with a sign. He starts with the Scriptures: “Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?” Beginning at Moses, he expounds in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself. See the next tab for the prophecies.

Revelation at the tablevv. 28-32

He made as though he would go further, and they urged him: “Abide with us.” At the table he took bread, blessed it, broke it and gave it, the same actions as the Last Supper, and their eyes were opened.

  • Hospitality: they invited him in, and he revealed himself there.
  • The Word, then the bread: the Scriptures opened on the road prepared them to know him at the table.
Witness to the bodyvv. 33-35

They rose “the same hour” and walked the seven miles back in the dark. They found the eleven saying, “The Lord is risen indeed,” and told how he was known of them in breaking of bread. A real encounter with Christ sends us back to his people.

What Jesus may have opened

Luke doesn't list the passages Jesus explained. These are prophecies the apostles later connected to him, following the same path: from Moses, through his birth and ministry, to the cross, the resurrection and the throne.

Isaac on MoriahA type

A father offers his only son on a mountain; the son carries the wood; God provides a ram in his place. Hebrews says Abraham received Isaac back “in a figure” from the dead.

The King reigns now and will reign visiblyPresent and future

Christ already reigns at the Father's right hand and has translated believers into his kingdom. We do not yet see all things put under him; that will come when he returns as King of kings and makes all things new.

Coming to the Lord's Table

A call to CommunionResponse
  • Lay down human reasoning, sorrow and earthly expectations.
  • Let Christ open the Scriptures to you until your heart burns.
  • Come to the Table with open eyes, and leave as a witness: the Lord is risen indeed.

For discussion

  1. What kept the two disciples from recognizing Jesus ? What keeps us from seeing him in our circumstances?
  2. “We trusted…” . What expectation of yours has God disappointed in order to give you something better?
  3. Jesus answered their grief with Scripture before he revealed himself . Why do you think he did it in that order?
  4. They had to invite him in . What does it look like to say “abide with us” today?
  5. After their eyes were opened, they went straight back to the others . Who needs to hear what Christ has shown you?
