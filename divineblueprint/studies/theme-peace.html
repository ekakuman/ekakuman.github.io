Peace

Overview

Peace (shalom) is more than the absence of conflict. It is wholeness, everything as it should be: between God and people, between people, within a person, and across creation. Sin broke all four. The blueprint's goal is to restore them through "the Prince of Peace" (Isaiah 9:6), who "made peace through the blood of his cross" (Colossians 1:20). Peace is a goal theme (group D): what the people are brought into. It is held with God now (Romans 5:1) and will fill the world in the end (Isaiah 9:7).

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. The LORD give thee peace — shalom as blessing; the covenant of peace
  2. The chastisement of our peace — peace bought by the suffering Servant
  3. Righteousness and peace have kissed — the Writings pray for and picture shalom
  4. He is our peace — peace with God, peace between peoples, the peace of God within
  5. Of the increase of His peace there shall be no end — a world at peace

Word study

Word Language / Strong's Meaning Key use

shalem Hebrew H8003 Whole, complete, perfect "Let your heart therefore be perfect with the LORD" (1 Kings 8:61)
shalam Hebrew H7999 To be complete; to make whole; to repay, restore "He shall surely make it good" (Exodus 22:6)

eirēnē Greek G1515 Peace; harmony; well-being "Peace I leave with you" (John 14:27)
eirēnopoieō Greek G1517 To make peace "Having made peace through the blood of his cross" (Colossians 1:20)
eirēnopoios Greek G1518 Peacemaker "Blessed are the peacemakers" (Matthew 5:9)
katallassō Greek G2644 To reconcile; to change enemies into friends "God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself" (2 Corinthians 5:19)
phroureō Greek G5432 To guard, garrison "The peace of God … shall keep [guard] your hearts" (Philippians 4:7)

Semantic note: Shalom shares its root with shalam, "to pay back, make whole." Peace is wholeness restored, often at a cost. Salem (Jerusalem), Melchizedek's city, means "peace" (Hebrews 7:2), and Solomon's name comes from the same root. Paul's phroureō is a military word: God's peace stands guard like a garrison around heart and mind.

Torah

The sacrifice of peace offering — Leviticus 3:1

1 And if his oblation be a sacrifice of peace offering, if he offer it of the herd; whether it be a male or female, he shall offer it without blemish before the LORD.

Meaning: The shelamim, the peace offering, was shared: part went to God, part to the priest, and part was eaten by the worshipper (7:15). It pictured fellowship restored at God's table, through blood. Christ is our peace offering (Ephesians 2:14).

Peace in the land — Leviticus 26:6

6 And I will give peace in the land, and ye shall lie down, and none shall make you afraid: and I will rid evil beasts out of the land, neither shall the sword go through your land.

Meaning: Covenant blessing includes safety and rest, the land as it should be. Ezekiel 34:25 repeats this almost word for word for the coming age.

And give thee peace — Numbers 6:24–26

24 The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: 25 The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: 26 The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.

Meaning: The priestly blessing builds to its final word, shalom. Peace is what results when God's face turns toward His people.

My covenant of peace — Numbers 25:11–13

11 Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, hath turned my wrath away from the children of Israel, while he was zealous for my sake among them, that I consumed not the children of Israel in my jealousy. 12 Wherefore say, Behold, I give unto him my covenant of peace: 13 And he shall have it, and his seed after him, even the covenant of an everlasting priesthood; because he was zealous for his God, and made an atonement for the children of Israel.

Meaning: Peace comes through atonement, when wrath is turned away. Peace and priesthood (theme 30) are bound together in one covenant.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Jehovah-shalom — Judges 6:22–24

22 And when Gideon perceived that he was an angel of the LORD, Gideon said, Alas, O Lord GOD! for because I have seen an angel of the LORD face to face. 23 And the LORD said unto him, Peace be unto thee; fear not: thou shalt not die. 24 Then Gideon built an altar there unto the LORD, and called it Jehovahshalom: unto this day it is yet in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

Meaning: Gideon expects to die for seeing God. Instead he hears "Peace be unto thee." God names Himself by the peace He gives. The risen Jesus greets His fearful disciples the same way (John 20:19).

The Prince of Peace — Isaiah 9:6–7

6 For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace. 7 Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom, to order it, and to establish it with judgment and with justice from henceforth even for ever. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will perform this.

Meaning: Peace is a Person, and His reign of peace keeps growing forever. It is built on justice (theme 28).

The chastisement of our peace — Isaiah 53:5

5 But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.

Meaning: The key text for how peace is made. The punishment that brings us peace fell on Him. Peace is costly, shalam in the sense of paid in full.

The covenant of My peace — Isaiah 54:10; Ezekiel 37:26

Isaiah 54:10 For the mountains shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed, saith the LORD that hath mercy on thee.

Ezekiel 37:26 Moreover I will make a covenant of peace with them; it shall be an everlasting covenant with them: and I will place them, and multiply them, and will set my sanctuary in the midst of them for evermore.

Meaning: More permanent than mountains. The covenant of peace ends with God's sanctuary among His people forever (theme 19).

Peace to him that is far off — Isaiah 57:19–21

19 I create the fruit of the lips; Peace, peace to him that is far off, and to him that is near, saith the LORD; and I will heal him. 20 But the wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. 21 There is no peace, saith my God, to the wicked.

Meaning: Peace offered to near (Israel) and far (the nations). Paul quotes this in Ephesians 2:17. Refusing it leaves the soul like a restless sea.

Peace, peace; when there is no peace — Jeremiah 6:14

14 They have healed also the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, Peace, peace; when there is no peace.

Meaning: False peace offers comfort without repentance. True shalom deals with the wound itself.

Seek the peace of the city — Jeremiah 29:7

7 And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.

Meaning: Even in exile, God's people work and pray for the shalom of the place they live.

This man shall be the peace — Micah 5:4–5

4 And he shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD, in the majesty of the name of the LORD his God; and they shall abide: for now shall he be great unto the ends of the earth. 5 And this man shall be the peace, when the Assyrian shall come into our land: and when he shall tread in our palaces, then shall we raise against him seven shepherds, and eight principal men.

Meaning: The ruler from Bethlehem (5:2) does not only bring peace; He is the peace. Paul echoes this: "he is our peace" (Ephesians 2:14).

Writings (Ketuvim)

I will both lay me down in peace — Psalm 4:8; 29:11

4:8 I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.

29:11 The LORD will give strength unto his people; the LORD will bless his people with peace.

Meaning: Peace as personal security: sleep in danger, because God alone keeps watch. After the storm psalm (29), peace is God's final gift.

Seek peace, and pursue it — Psalm 34:14; Proverbs 16:7

Psalm 34:14 Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.

Proverbs 16:7 When a man's ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him.

Meaning: Peace must be actively pursued. Peter quotes Psalm 34 in 1 Peter 3:11. God can turn even enemies toward peace.

He will speak peace — Psalm 85:8, 10

8 I will hear what God the LORD will speak: for he will speak peace unto his people, and to his saints: but let them not turn again to folly.

10 Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other.

Meaning: Peace comes as God's spoken word. It meets righteousness rather than replacing it (theme 28).

Great peace have they — Psalm 119:165

165 Great peace have they which love thy law: and nothing shall offend them.

Meaning: Inner stability comes from loving God's word. "Offend" means "cause to stumble."

Pray for the peace of Jerusalem — Psalm 122:6–8

6 Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: they shall prosper that love thee. 7 Peace be within thy walls, and prosperity within thy palaces. 8 For my brethren and companions' sakes, I will now say, Peace be within thee.

Meaning: Jerusalem (Yerushalayim, linked to shalom) should be the city of peace. The prayer looks forward to the new Jerusalem.

Fear not: peace be unto thee — Daniel 10:18–19

18 Then there came again and touched me one like the appearance of a man, and he strengthened me, 19 And said, O man greatly beloved, fear not: peace be unto thee, be strong, yea, be strong. And when he had spoken unto me, I was strengthened, and said, Let my lord speak; for thou hast strengthened me.

Meaning: Overwhelmed by the vision, Daniel receives peace and strength together. God's peace equips, not only calms.

New Testament

On earth peace — Luke 2:14

14 Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

Meaning: The Prince of Peace is born, and heaven announces the goal: glory to God and peace on earth.

My peace I give unto you — John 14:27; 16:33

14:27 Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.

16:33 These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.

Meaning: Christ's own peace, given on the night before the cross. It does not depend on circumstances: "in me … peace; in the world … tribulation."

Peace be unto you — John 20:19–21

19 Then the same day at evening, being the first day of the week, when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews, came Jesus and stood in the midst, and saith unto them, Peace be unto you. 20 And when he had so said, he shewed unto them his hands and his side. Then were the disciples glad, when they saw the Lord. 21 Then said Jesus to them again, Peace be unto you: as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you.

Meaning: The risen Lord's first word, followed by His wounds, which are the price of peace (Isaiah 53:5). Peace received becomes peace sent out.

Peace with God; made peace by the blood — Romans 5:1; Colossians 1:20

Romans 5:1 Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:

Colossians 1:20 And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.

Meaning: The first and deepest peace is with God. It reaches all the way to "all things" (theme 25).

He is our peace — Ephesians 2:14–17

14 For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us; 15 Having abolished in his flesh the enmity, even the law of commandments contained in ordinances; for to make in himself of twain one new man, so making peace; 16 And that he might reconcile both unto God in one body by the cross, having slain the enmity thereby: 17 And came and preached peace to you which were afar off, and to them that were nigh.

Meaning: Micah 5:5 and Isaiah 57:19 fulfilled. Peace between peoples flows from peace with God.

The ministry of reconciliation — 2 Corinthians 5:18–20

18 And all things are of God, who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation; 19 To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and hath committed unto us the word of reconciliation. 20 Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ's stead, be ye reconciled to God.

Meaning: God takes the initiative to make peace. Those who receive it become ambassadors of it.

The peace of God shall keep your hearts — Philippians 4:6–7; Colossians 3:15

Philippians 4:6 Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. 7 And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.

Colossians 3:15 And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which also ye are called in one body; and be ye thankful.

Meaning: Peace with God (Romans 5:1) becomes the peace of God guarding the heart. "Rule" means "act as umpire": peace settles decisions in the church.

Blessed are the peacemakers — Matthew 5:9; Romans 12:18

Matthew 5:9 Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.

Romans 12:18 If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.

Meaning: Making peace is a family likeness: children resemble the "God of peace."

The God of peace — Romans 16:20; Hebrews 13:20–21

Romans 16:20 And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen.

Hebrews 13:20 Now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, 21 Make you perfect in every good work to do his will, working in you that which is wellpleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ; to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

Meaning: Genesis 3:15 echoed: the God of peace crushes the serpent. Peace is God's final victory over everything that destroys shalom.

Principles

  1. Shalom is the goal of the blueprint. Everything made whole: with God, with others, within, and across creation (Colossians 1:20).
  2. Peace is costly. It was bought by the Servant's wounds (Isaiah 53:5).
  3. Peace is a Person before it is a feeling. Christ is our peace (Micah 5:5; Ephesians 2:14).
  4. Peace with God comes first. The peace of God follows peace with God (Romans 5:1; Philippians 4:7).
  5. False peace avoids the wound; true peace heals it. (Jeremiah 6:14)
  6. Those who receive peace make peace. Children of the God of peace become peacemakers and ambassadors (Matthew 5:9; 2 Corinthians 5:20).

For a teaching session: Read Isaiah 53:5, John 20:19–21 and Ephesians 2:14–17. Ask: what did peace cost, and who are the "far off" and "near" in our lives today who need to hear it?
