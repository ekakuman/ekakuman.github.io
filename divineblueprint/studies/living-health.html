The Divine Blueprint and Health

Overview

The body is not a shell the soul will one day discard. God formed it from the dust and breathed life into it (Genesis 2:7). The Son took a body (John 1:14) and rose in one (Luke 24:39). The Spirit makes it His temple (1 Corinthians 6:19). And God will raise it in glory (Philippians 3:21). So the Divine Blueprint includes our health, our weakness and our mortality. God cares for bodies now and will redeem them fully at the resurrection (Romans 8:23).

This study walks health and the body through the five groups of the blueprint:

  • A · Purpose: why God made us embodied
  • B · Provision: what God supplies in sickness and health
  • C · People: who God shapes through care of the body and through weakness
  • D · Goal: rest, refreshment and wholeness
  • E · Consummation: the resurrection body

A pastoral word. This study is not medical advice. Scripture honours physicians (Luke is "the beloved physician," Colossians 4:14) and practical remedies (1 Timothy 5:23). Seeking medical care, including for mental health, is fully consistent with faith.

Key passage — 1 Corinthians 6:13–14, 19–20

13 Meats for the belly, and the belly for meats: but God shall destroy both it and them. Now the body is not for fornication, but for the Lord; and the Lord for the body. 14 And God hath both raised up the Lord, and will also raise up us by his own power.

19 What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? 20 For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's.

Meaning:

  • v. 13 Some in Corinth said the body didn't matter. Paul answers: "the body is … for the Lord; and the Lord for the body." God is committed to our bodies.
  • v. 14 The proof is resurrection. God raised Jesus' body and will raise ours, so what we do with our bodies matters.
  • v. 19 The body is "the temple of the Holy Ghost" (theme 19, Dwelling; theme 18, Spirit). God's presence lives in it.
  • v. 20 "Bought with a price" (theme 10, Redemption): our bodies belong to the One who paid for them. Therefore "glorify God in your body."

The five lenses

A · Purpose — why God made us embodied

The breath of life — Genesis 2:7; 1:31

2:7 And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

1:31 And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.

Meaning: Humanity is dust and breath, body and spirit together (theme 16, Life). God called the embodied creation "very good."

Fearfully and wonderfully made — Psalm 139:13–14

13 For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. 14 I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.

Meaning: Each body is God's handiwork, shaped personally. This is the basis for honouring every body, whatever its ability or appearance (theme 15, Image).

The unseen side. "What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?" (1 Corinthians 6:19). The body is the dwelling place of an unseen Guest. Caring for it, using it in purity, and enduring its weakness are all done in His presence.

B · Provision — what God supplies

I am the LORD that healeth thee — Exodus 15:26; Psalm 103:2–3

Exodus 15:26 And said, If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, and wilt do that which is right in his sight, and wilt give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, I will put none of these diseases upon thee, which I have brought upon the Egyptians: for I am the LORD that healeth thee.

Psalm 103:2 Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: 3 Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases;

Meaning: YHWH Rophe, "the LORD who heals." Healing, whether through prayer, medicine or recovery, is His gift to be received with thanks.

He hath borne our griefs — Isaiah 53:4–5; Matthew 8:16–17

Isaiah 53:4 Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. 5 But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.

Matthew 8:16 When the even was come, they brought unto him many that were possessed with devils: and he cast out the spirits with his word, and healed all that were sick: 17 That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses.

Meaning: Christ's healing ministry was a foretaste of what the cross secures (themes 7 and 10). Full healing comes at the resurrection; foretastes come now.

Call for the elders — James 5:14–15

14 Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord: 15 And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him.

Meaning: God's provision includes the praying church. The sick are not to suffer alone.

C · People — who God shapes

A living sacrifice — Romans 12:1; 1 Timothy 4:8

Romans 12:1 I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.

1 Timothy 4:8 For bodily exercise profiteth little: but godliness is profitable unto all things, having promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come.

Meaning: The body is offered to God in worship (theme 17, Holiness). Bodily care has real but limited value; godliness has eternal value.

My strength is made perfect in weakness — 2 Corinthians 12:7–10

7 And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure. 8 For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. 9 And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. 10 Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong.

Meaning: Paul prayed three times for healing and received grace instead (theme 8). Weakness can become the place where Christ's power rests.

D · Goal — rest and wholeness

Arise and eat — 1 Kings 19:4–8

4 But he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness, and came and sat down under a juniper tree: and he requested for himself that he might die; and said, It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers. 5 And as he lay and slept under a juniper tree, behold, then an angel touched him, and said unto him, Arise and eat. 6 And he looked, and, behold, there was a cake baken on the coals, and a cruse of water at his head. And he did eat and drink, and laid him down again. 7 And the angel of the LORD came again the second time, and touched him, and said, Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee. 8 And he arose, and did eat and drink, and went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights unto Horeb the mount of God.

Meaning: Exhausted and despairing, Elijah receives sleep, food and gentle care before any rebuke or teaching. God tends the body as part of tending the soul (theme 22, Rest).

Come ye apart — Mark 6:31; 3 John 2

Mark 6:31 And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.

3 John 2 Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth.

Meaning: Jesus prescribes rest for tired disciples. John prays for bodily health alongside spiritual health: wholeness, shalom (theme 32).

E · Consummation — the resurrection body

Fashioned like unto His glorious body — Philippians 3:20–21

20 For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ: 21 Who shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorious body, according to the working whereby he is able even to subdue all things unto himself.

Meaning: "Vile" means "lowly" or humble, not wicked. Our weak bodies will become like Christ's risen body (theme 27).

The lame man shall leap — Isaiah 35:5–6; Revelation 21:4

Isaiah 35:5 Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped. 6 Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing: for in the wilderness shall waters break out, and streams in the desert.

Revelation 21:4 And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

Meaning: Every disability and illness has an end date. "No more pain" is part of the new creation (theme 26).

The inward man is renewed — 2 Corinthians 4:16–18

16 For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day. 17 For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; 18 While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal.

Meaning: Ageing and illness are real, and so is inner renewal. Present weakness is preparing "an eternal weight of glory."

Common struggles, and what the blueprint says

Struggle What the blueprint says Anchor text
Illness that isn't healed Paul prayed and received grace, not removal; he left Trophimus sick. Unhealed illness is not proof of weak faith. 2 Corinthians 12:7–10; 2 Timothy 4:20
Depression and despair God met Elijah's despair with sleep, food and gentle presence. The psalmists asked "Why art thou cast down, O my soul?" 1 Kings 19:4–8; Psalm 42:11
Body image and comparison Man looks on the outward appearance; the LORD looks on the heart. Each body is wonderfully made. 1 Samuel 16:7; Psalm 139:14; 1 Peter 3:3–4
Neglect or overindulgence The body is a temple, bought with a price; self-control is a fruit of the Spirit. 1 Corinthians 6:19–20; 9:27; Galatians 5:23; Proverbs 23:20–21
Ageing "Even to hoar hairs will I carry you." The inward man is renewed day by day. Isaiah 46:4; 2 Corinthians 4:16
Disability Disability is not punishment for sin; God's works can be shown in every life. John 9:2–3; Psalm 139:13–14
Caring for a sick family member Bear one another's burdens; God strengthens the weary carer too. Galatians 6:2; Isaiah 40:29–31
Fear of death Christ came to deliver those who through fear of death were in bondage. Hebrews 2:14–15; 1 Corinthians 15:55–57
Faith versus medicine Luke was a physician; Paul recommended a remedy. Medicine and prayer belong together. Colossians 4:14; 1 Timothy 5:23; James 5:14

Interpretive balance: Christians differ on what to expect about healing in this life. Some teach that healing is normally available through faith, citing Isaiah 53:5 and Christ's ministry. Others stress God's sovereign freedom, noting that the apostles were not always healed (2 Corinthians 12; 2 Timothy 4:20; Philippians 2:27). All agree that we should pray for the sick, use medical help gratefully, and rest our final hope in the resurrection body.

Pastoral note: Depression and anxiety are real conditions, not simply spiritual failure. Elijah's story shows God caring for a despairing servant's body and spirit together. Anyone struggling should reach out to a doctor, counsellor, pastor or trusted friend, and anyone thinking about ending their life should seek help right away from local emergency services or a crisis line.

Practices

For yourself

Practice Anchor text
Treat sleep as trust, not laziness; protect a regular bedtime. Psalm 127:2; 4:8
Offer your body to God each morning: "Lord, my hands, feet and mind are Yours today." Romans 12:1
Care for food, movement and rest as stewardship, without obsession. 1 Corinthians 6:19–20; 1 Timothy 4:8
In illness, ask the elders and friends to pray, and see a doctor. James 5:14; Colossians 4:14
When you feel low, tell someone; don't hide under the juniper tree alone. 1 Kings 19:4–5; Galatians 6:2

For family and friends

Practice Anchor text
Bring meals, visit and offer practical help to the sick and their carers. Matthew 25:36
Speak about bodies, including your children's, with respect and gratitude. Psalm 139:14

For the church

Practice Anchor text
Pray for the sick by name and offer anointing and prayer as James describes. James 5:14–15
Make worship and fellowship accessible to people with disabilities. Luke 14:13, 21
Speak openly and kindly about mental health. Psalm 42:11; 1 Kings 19
Support carers with respite and help. Galatians 6:2

Discussion questions

  1. What does 1 Corinthians 6:19–20 say about the idea that the body doesn't matter spiritually?
  2. How does Psalm 139:14 speak to body image and comparison?
  3. Paul prayed for healing and received grace instead (2 Corinthians 12:9). How do we hold faith and unanswered prayer together?
  4. What do you notice about how God cared for Elijah in 1 Kings 19:5–8? What does it teach about depression and exhaustion?
  5. How can we pray for healing while also using medicine, without feeling one cancels the other?
  6. How does the promise of a resurrection body (Philippians 3:21) shape how we face illness and ageing?
  7. How can our church care better for the sick, people with disabilities and carers?
  8. Which one practice from this study will you start this week?
