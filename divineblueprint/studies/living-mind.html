The Divine Blueprint and the Mind and Speech

Overview

What we think shapes what we say, and what we say shapes our lives and the lives of others. Jesus taught that "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh" (Matthew 12:34). In Scripture the "heart" includes the mind: thoughts, beliefs and desires.

The blueprint begins with God speaking: "And God said, Let there be light" (Genesis 1:3). The Son is "the Word" (John 1:1). Human beings, made in God's image, are the creatures who think and speak. Sin twisted both: the first sin began with a lie believed (Genesis 3:1–6). Redemption renews both: the mind is renewed (Romans 12:2) and the tongue is cleansed (Isaiah 6:7). At the end "every tongue" will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord (Philippians 2:11).

This study covers:

  • The mind — renewing how we think, guarding against lies, worry and impure thoughts.
  • Speech — truthfulness, kindness, and the power of words to build up or tear down, face to face and online.

Key passages — Romans 12:2 and James 3:2–10

Romans 12:2 — And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.

James 3:2–10 2 For in many things we offend all. If any man offend not in word, the same is a perfect man, and able also to bridle the whole body. 3 Behold, we put bits in the horses' mouths, that they may obey us; and we turn about their whole body. 4 Behold also the ships, which though they be so great, and are driven of fierce winds, yet are they turned about with a very small helm, whithersoever the governor listeth. 5 Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth! 6 And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity: so is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body, and setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell. 7 For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind: 8 But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison. 9 Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God. 10 Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.

Meaning

Romans 12:2 says the Christian life is not mainly changed by outside rules but by a renewed mind. The world presses us into its mould; God transforms from within. The result is that we "prove" — test and approve — God's will.

James uses three pictures: a bit, a rudder and a spark. Small things steer and set on fire large things. The tongue "can no man tame" (v. 8) — which means it needs God's grace, not just self-effort. Verse 9 links speech to the blueprint: we curse people "made after the similitude of God." Speaking evil of a person is an attack on God's image.

Notes

  • Anxious or intrusive thoughts are not always sin. Many believers struggle with anxiety, depression or obsessive thoughts. These are often illnesses, not failures of faith. Renewing the mind includes prayer and Scripture and may also include medical help and counselling.
  • Online speech counts. Posts, comments and messages are words. Ephesians 4:29 and James 3 apply to screens as much as conversations.
  • Truth and kindness together. Scripture calls for both "speaking the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15). Truth without love wounds; love without truth misleads.

Mind and speech through the five lenses

A · The Purpose — made in the image of a speaking God

Genesis 1:3 — And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

John 1:1, 14 — In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. ... And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

Meaning. God creates by speaking and reveals Himself through His Word, finally in His Son. People made in His image share, in a small way, the gift of thought and language. Our words were meant to reflect His: true, creative, life-giving, "full of grace and truth."

Theme links: The Image of God; The Son; Truth and Faithfulness.

The unseen side. "For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh ... casting down imaginations ... and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:3, 5). The mind is a battlefield in an unseen war. The first sin began with a lie whispered by the serpent (Genesis 3:1–5), and James says an unbridled tongue "is set on fire of hell" (James 3:6).

B · The Provision — cleansed lips and a new mind

Isaiah 6:5–7 — Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts. Then flew one of the seraphims unto me, having a live coal in his hand, which he had taken with the tongs from off the altar: and he laid it upon my mouth, and said, Lo, this hath touched thy lips; and thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged.

1 Corinthians 2:16 — For who hath known the mind of the Lord, that he may instruct him? but we have the mind of Christ.

2 Timothy 1:7 — For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.

Meaning. Isaiah saw God and confessed "unclean lips." The coal came from the altar — the place of sacrifice. Our speech is cleansed by atonement, not by trying harder. In Christ believers are given "the mind of Christ" and a "sound mind" by the Spirit.

Theme links: The Lamb; Holiness; The Holy Spirit; Grace.

C · The People — renewed thinking, gracious words

Philippians 4:6–8 — Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things.

2 Corinthians 10:5 — Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ;

Ephesians 4:29 — Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.

Meaning. Worry is met with prayer and thanksgiving, and God's peace stands guard over the mind. Believers choose what to dwell on (Philippians 4:8) and take wrong thoughts "captive" (2 Corinthians 10:5). Words are tested by one question: does this build up and give grace to the hearer?

Theme links: Peace; Sonship; Love.

D · The Goal — words that will be weighed

Matthew 12:34–37 — O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh. A good man out of the good treasure of the heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things. But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned.

Psalm 19:14 — Let the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, be acceptable in thy sight, O LORD, my strength, and my redeemer.

Meaning. Words reveal the heart, and they will be accounted for. That is sobering, but Psalm 19:14 turns it into prayer: words and meditation "acceptable" to the One who is our Redeemer. The goal is a heart full of good treasure, so that good words overflow.

Theme links: Righteousness and Justice; Final Judgment; Holiness.

E · The Consummation — every tongue confessing

Zephaniah 3:9 — For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the LORD, to serve him with one consent.

Philippians 2:10–11 — That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; and that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

1 Corinthians 13:12 — For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.

Meaning. Babel scattered language in pride (Genesis 11:7–9); God promises "a pure language" for worship. At the end every tongue will confess Christ, and every mind will know fully. Our present struggle with thoughts and words will give way to clear sight and pure praise.

Theme links: The Heading Up of All Things; Glorification; The Kingdom.

Common struggles

Struggle What it sounds like What Scripture says
Worry Replaying fears late at night. Philippians 4:6–7; Matthew 6:25–34; 1 Peter 5:7
Believing lies "I'm worthless." "God could never forgive this." John 8:32, 44; Romans 8:1; 1 John 1:9
Impure thoughts Imagination fed by what we watch. Psalm 101:3; Matthew 5:28; Philippians 4:8
Gossip "I'm only telling you so you can pray." Proverbs 11:13; 16:28; 26:20
Harsh or careless words Snapping at family; cutting jokes; angry posts. Proverbs 15:1; 18:21; Ephesians 4:29–31
Small lies Exaggerating, excusing, flattering. Ephesians 4:25; Colossians 3:9; Proverbs 12:22
Grumbling Constant complaint about church, work or family. Philippians 2:14–15; Numbers 11:1

Practices

For yourself

Practice How Scripture
Feed the mind Memorise one verse a week; read Scripture before news or social media. Psalm 119:11; Joshua 1:8
Pray worries When a worry comes, turn it into a request and a thanksgiving. Philippians 4:6
Name the lie, speak the truth Write down a lie you believe and a verse that answers it. 2 Corinthians 10:5; John 17:17
Guard your inputs Review what you watch, read and scroll. Cut what pollutes. Psalm 101:3; Proverbs 4:23
Pause before speaking Swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath. Wait before posting. James 1:19; Proverbs 29:20

For others

Practice How Scripture
Speak blessing Each day say one specific word of encouragement. Ephesians 4:29; 1 Thessalonians 5:11
Stop gossip Don't pass it on; ask "Have you spoken to them?" Proverbs 26:20; Matthew 18:15
Answer gently Lower your voice when conflict rises. Proverbs 15:1; Colossians 4:6
Make things right Apologise specifically for hurtful words. Matthew 5:23–24

For the church

Practice How Scripture
Teach how to think Help people test ideas against Scripture. Acts 17:11; 1 John 4:1
Set a speech culture Agree together not to speak about absent people in ways you would not say to them. James 4:11; Psalm 15:1–3
Care for the anxious Pray with them; connect them with good medical and counselling help. 1 Peter 5:7; Galatians 6:2

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening James 3:3–5 Ask: "What is a word someone said to you years ago that you still remember?" Words are small but powerful.
7 min A · God's purpose Genesis 1:3; John 1:1, 14; James 3:9 Made in the image of a speaking God; cursing people attacks His image.
8 min B · God's provision Isaiah 6:5–7; 1 Corinthians 2:16 Lips cleansed from the altar; the mind of Christ given.
10 min C · Renewed thinking, gracious words Romans 12:2; Philippians 4:6–8; Ephesians 4:29 Pray worries, choose what to dwell on, speak to build up.
5 min D and E · Words weighed, tongues confessing Matthew 12:34–37; Philippians 2:10–11 The mouth shows the heart; every tongue will confess Christ.
5 min Response and prayer Psalm 19:14 Pray the verse together. Each person names one speech habit to change.

Discussion questions

  1. How does being made in the image of a speaking God (Genesis 1:3; James 3:9) raise the value of our words?
  2. In what ways does the world press us into its mould (Romans 12:2)?
  3. Why did Isaiah's cleansing come from the altar (Isaiah 6:6–7)? What does that teach about changing our speech?
  4. Which item in Philippians 4:8 do you find hardest to think on? Why?
  5. Think of a lie you have believed about yourself or God. Which verse answers it (2 Corinthians 10:5)?
  6. How does Ephesians 4:29 apply to messages and social media?
  7. Jesus says words reveal the heart (Matthew 12:34). What do your words show?
  8. Which speech habit will you work on this week?

చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు

  1. మాటలాడే దేవుని స్వరూపంలో సృష్టించబడినందున (ఆదికాండము 1:3; యాకోబు 3:9) మన మాటల విలువ ఎలా పెరుగుతుంది?
  2. లోకం మనల్ని తన మాదిరిలోకి ఎలా నెట్టుతుంది (రోమీయులకు 12:2)?
  3. యెషయా పవిత్రత బలిపీఠం నుండి ఎందుకు వచ్చింది (యెషయా 6:6–7)? మాటలను మార్చుకోవడం గురించి ఇది ఏమి నేర్పుతుంది?
  4. ఫిలిప్పీయులకు 4:8లో ఏ విషయాన్ని ధ్యానించడం మీకు కష్టం? ఎందుకు?
  5. మీ గురించి లేదా దేవుని గురించి మీరు నమ్మిన ఒక అబద్ధం ఆలోచించండి. దానికి ఏ వచనం జవాబు ఇస్తుంది (2 కొరింథీయులకు 10:5)?
  6. ఎఫెసీయులకు 4:29 సందేశాలకు, సోషల్ మీడియాకు ఎలా వర్తిస్తుంది?
  7. మాటలు హృదయాన్ని చూపిస్తాయని యేసు చెప్పాడు (మత్తయి 12:34). మీ మాటలు ఏమి చూపిస్తాయి?
