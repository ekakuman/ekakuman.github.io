The Promised Seed

Overview and word study

The Seed is the promised Offspring through whom God's plan reaches the world. The promise was given the moment sin entered (Genesis 3:15), narrowed through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Judah and David, protected through every threat, and fulfilled "when the fulness of the time was come" (Galatians 4:4). The Old Testament can be read as the story of the Seed's line.

The word works in two ways at once: one Seed (Christ, Galatians 3:16) and many seed (all who belong to Him, Galatians 3:29). Christ is the Seed who, by dying like a grain of wheat, produces many seeds (John 12:24).

The Branch titles in the prophets each show a side of Christ: the King (Jeremiah 23:5), the Servant (Zechariah 3:8), the Man (Zechariah 6:12), and the Branch "of the LORD" (Isaiah 4:2) — matching the four Gospels' portraits.

Torah

Her seed shall bruise thy head — Genesis 3:14–15

14 And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field; upon thy belly shalt thou go, and dust shalt thou eat all the days of thy life: 15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.

Meaning: The first gospel. Unusually, the seed is the woman's, hinting at a virgin birth (Isaiah 7:14). "It" is a singular "he": one Seed will crush the serpent's head while suffering a wound. Romans 16:20 and Hebrews 2:14 show the victory.

In thy seed all nations — Genesis 22:16–18

16 And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son: 17 That in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand which is upon the sea shore; and thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies; 18 And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.

Meaning: Both senses in one promise: many seed like stars and sand, and a seed who "shall possess the gate of his enemies" ("his" is singular). Through this Seed blessing reaches "all the nations."

In Isaac shall thy seed be called — Genesis 21:1–2, 12

1 And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken. 2 For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.

12 And God said unto Abraham, Let it not be grievous in thy sight because of the lad, and because of thy bondwoman; in all that Sarah hath said unto thee, hearken unto her voice; for in Isaac shall thy seed be called.

Meaning: The Seed line comes through a miracle birth at God's "set time." God chooses the line; it is not the natural first-born (Romans 9:7–8).

The sceptre and the Star — Genesis 49:10; Numbers 24:17

Genesis 49:10 The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be.

Numbers 24:17 I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh: there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel, and shall smite the corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth.

Meaning: The line narrows to Judah, and a royal Seed is promised. "Shiloh" is often read "he to whom it belongs." Balaam, a foreign prophet, sees a Star — the magi followed a star to Him (Matthew 2:2).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

A virgin shall conceive — Isaiah 7:14

14 Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.

Meaning: The Seed of the woman (Genesis 3:15) comes through a virgin. His name: "God with us" (Matthew 1:23).

A rod out of the stem of Jesse — Isaiah 11:1–2, 10

1 And there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots: 2 And the spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD;

10 And in that day there shall be a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people; to it shall the Gentiles seek: and his rest shall be glorious.

Meaning: David's royal tree is cut down to a stump, yet a shoot (choter) grows from it. By verse 10 the Branch is also the "root" — both David's descendant and his source (Revelation 22:16). The Gentiles seek Him.

I will set up thy seed — 2 Samuel 7:12–13

12 And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom. 13 He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.

Meaning: The Seed becomes a King with an everlasting throne. Every later Branch prophecy builds on this.

The Branch of righteousness — Jeremiah 33:14–16

14 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will perform that good thing which I have promised unto the house of Israel and to the house of Judah. 15 In those days, and at that time, will I cause the Branch of righteousness to grow up unto David; and he shall execute judgment and righteousness in the land. 16 In those days shall Judah be saved, and Jerusalem shall dwell safely: and this is the name wherewith she shall be called, The LORD our righteousness.

Meaning: Spoken while Jerusalem was falling. The line seemed finished, yet God promises the Branch will "grow up." Notice: in verse 16 the city shares His name — the Seed's righteousness is given to His people.

He shall see His seed — Isaiah 53:10

10 Yet it pleased the LORD to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in his hand.

Meaning: The Seed dies without natural children, yet "shall see his seed" — a spiritual family born through His death and resurrection.

My words in thy seed's mouth — Isaiah 59:20–21

20 And the Redeemer shall come to Zion, and unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob, saith the LORD. 21 As for me, this is my covenant with them, saith the LORD; My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth, nor out of the mouth of thy seed, nor out of the mouth of thy seed's seed, saith the LORD, from henceforth and for ever.

Meaning: The Redeemer's Spirit and words are passed on to His seed and their seed — the spiritual family continues His mission generation after generation.

Writings (Ketuvim)

A son born to Naomi — Ruth 4:13, 17

13 So Boaz took Ruth, and she was his wife: and when he went in unto her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son.

17 And the women her neighbours gave it a name, saying, There is a son born to Naomi; and they called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David.

Meaning: The Seed line passes through a Moabite widow. "The LORD gave her conception" — God's hand again in the line. The book ends with David's name.

I will settle Him in Mine house for ever — 1 Chronicles 17:11–14

11 And it shall come to pass, when thy days be expired that thou must go to be with thy fathers, that I will raise up thy seed after thee, which shall be of thy sons; and I will establish his kingdom. 12 He shall build me an house, and I will stablish his throne for ever. 13 I will be his father, and he shall be my son: and I will not take my mercy away from him, as I took it from him that was before thee: 14 But I will settle him in mine house and in my kingdom for ever: and his throne shall be established for evermore.

Meaning: The Chronicler's version adds "mine house and … my kingdom" — the Seed rules God's own kingdom. Notice the line about sin (2 Samuel 7:14) is absent here; the Chronicler looks toward the sinless Son.

His seed shall endure for ever — Psalm 89:35–37

35 Once have I sworn by my holiness that I will not lie unto David. 36 His seed shall endure for ever, and his throne as the sun before me. 37 It shall be established for ever as the moon, and as a faithful witness in heaven. Selah.

Meaning: God swears by His holiness. The Seed's throne is as lasting as the sun and moon.

A seed shall serve Him — Psalm 22:27, 30–31

27 All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD: and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before thee.

30 A seed shall serve him; it shall be accounted to the Lord for a generation. 31 They shall come, and shall declare his righteousness unto a people that shall be born, that he hath done this.

Meaning: The psalm that begins "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" ends with a seed — a spiritual family from all nations, declaring "he hath done this" (Hebrew asah, close to "It is finished").

The seed preserved — Esther 4:14

14 For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father's house shall be destroyed: and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?

Meaning: Haman's decree threatened to destroy the whole people from whom the Seed would come. God preserved them through Esther. The Seed line survives every attack.

New Testament

The son of David, the son of Abraham — Matthew 1:1, 21–23

1 The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham.

21 And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins. 22 Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, 23 Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.

Meaning: The New Testament's first verse names Jesus as the Seed of both covenants: David (king) and Abraham (blessing to nations). The genealogy that follows includes Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and Bathsheba — God's grace running through the line.

Made of the seed of David — Romans 1:3–4

3 Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh; 4 And declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead:

Meaning: Two natures: David's Seed by birth, God's Son declared by resurrection.

To thy seed, which is Christ — Galatians 3:16, 29

16 Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.

29 And if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise.

Meaning: Paul presses the singular: the promise focuses on one Seed, Christ. Then everyone "in" Him becomes Abraham's seed too. One Seed, many seed.

Except a corn of wheat fall — John 12:23–24

23 And Jesus answered them, saying, The hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified. 24 Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit.

Meaning: The Seed multiplies by dying. This is how Isaiah 53:10's "he shall see his seed" comes true.

He took on Him the seed of Abraham — Hebrews 2:14–16

14 Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; 15 And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage. 16 For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham.

Meaning: Genesis 3:15 fulfilled: by His death the Seed destroys the devil's power.

The woman, the child and the dragon — Revelation 12:4–5, 17

4 And his tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven, and did cast them to the earth: and the dragon stood before the woman which was ready to be delivered, for to devour her child as soon as it was born. 5 And she brought forth a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron: and her child was caught up unto God, and to his throne.

17 And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ.

Meaning: The whole conflict of Genesis 3:15 in one vision. The dragon tries to devour the Seed and fails; the child is enthroned. The war continues against "the remnant of her seed" — believers.

Satan bruised under your feet — Romans 16:20

20 And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen.

Meaning: The head-crushing promised in Genesis 3:15 is shared with the many seed.

The root and the offspring — Revelation 22:16

16 I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the churches. I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.

Meaning: Both David's source (root) and descendant (offspring), and the Star of Numbers 24:17.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. One Seed, many seed "Thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies" and "as the stars" (Genesis 22:17) "He shall see his seed" (Isaiah 53:10) "A seed shall serve him" (Psalm 22:30) "Thy seed, which is Christ … ye are Abraham's seed" (Galatians 3:16, 29)
2. The Seed line is attacked and preserved Pharaoh's edict against Hebrew sons (Exodus 1:16) Athaliah "destroyed all the seed royal" — Joash hidden (2 Kings 11:1–2) Haman's decree overturned (Esther 4:14) Herod's massacre (Matthew 2:16); the dragon (Revelation 12:4)
3. Born by God's intervention "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?" (Genesis 18:14); Isaac at the set time (21:2) "A virgin shall conceive" (Isaiah 7:14) "The LORD gave her conception" (Ruth 4:13) "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee" (Luke 1:35)
4. The royal line "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah" (Genesis 49:10) "I will set up thy seed … establish his kingdom" (2 Samuel 7:12) "His seed shall endure for ever" (Psalm 89:36) "The seed of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3)
5. Victory over the serpent "It shall bruise thy head" (Genesis 3:15) "The LORD … shall punish leviathan … that crooked serpent" (Isaiah 27:1) "The dragon shalt thou trample under feet" (Psalm 91:13) "Bruise Satan under your feet" (Romans 16:20); "destroy him that had the power of death" (Hebrews 2:14)
6. The Seed multiplies by dying Isaac offered and received back (Genesis 22) "When thou shalt make his soul an offering … he shall see his seed" (Isaiah 53:10) "He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed" (Psalm 126:6) "Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die" (John 12:24)
7. Blessing to all nations "In thy seed shall all the nations … be blessed" (Genesis 22:18) "To it shall the Gentiles seek" (Isaiah 11:10) "All nations shall call him blessed" (Psalm 72:17) "That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles" (Galatians 3:14)

Principles

  1. The Seed was promised the moment sin entered. God's remedy came with the judgment (Genesis 3:15).
  2. God guards His line through history. No decree, massacre or exile could stop it (Esther 4:14; Revelation 12:5).
  3. The Seed is God's work, not human achievement. Barren wombs and a virgin birth show it (Genesis 21:1–2; Luke 1:35).
  4. One Seed creates many seed. Christ's death produces a family (John 12:24; Galatians 3:29).
  5. The Seed's victory is shared. Believers will see Satan crushed under their feet (Romans 16:20).
  6. The Seed blesses every nation. The promise to Abraham reaches the Gentiles (Galatians 3:14).
