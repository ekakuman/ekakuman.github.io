The Love of God

Overview

Love is the motive behind the entire blueprint. Before anything was created, the Father loved the Son: "thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world" (John 17:24). God's purpose (theme 1) and counsel (theme 2) flow from this love, and their goal is to extend it to a people: "that the love wherewith thou hast loved me may be in them" (John 17:26).

Four things set God's love apart in Scripture:

  • It is eternal. "I have loved thee with an everlasting love" (Jeremiah 31:3).
  • It is unearned. "Not because ye were more in number … but because the LORD loved you" (Deuteronomy 7:7–8); "while we were yet sinners" (Romans 5:8).
  • It is costly. It is measured by the gift of an only Son (Genesis 22:2; John 3:16).
  • It is unbreakable. Nothing "shall be able to separate us from the love of God" (Romans 8:39).

The New Testament sums it up in three words: "God is love" (1 John 4:8). Love is not only something God does; it is who He is. Passages below are given in full from the King James Version, where charity translates agapē.

1. Word study

Hebrew has several words that together describe God's love as chosen, loyal, tender and attached; Greek gives agapē its fullest meaning in the cross.

Two semantic links:

  1. The first "love" in the Bible (Genesis 22:2) is a father's love for an only son he is asked to offer. Scripture's first use of the word sets the pattern for John 3:16.
  2. Chashaq in Deuteronomy 7:7 ("set his love upon you") pictures love that attaches itself to its object by choice. The reason is given in the next verse: "because the LORD loved you." God's love is its own reason.

2. Torah

The Torah introduces God's love through a father and his only son, grounds Israel's election in love alone, commands love in return, and pictures the beloved resting on God's shoulders.

Thine only son, whom thou lovest — Genesis 22:1–2

1 And it came to pass after these things, that God did tempt Abraham, and said unto him, Abraham: and he said, Behold, here I am. 2 And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.

Meaning: The first occurrence of ahav in the Bible. Three phrases tighten the focus: "thy son," "thine only son" (yechidekha), "whom thou lovest." The love of a father for an only son, offered on a mountain, previews the love of God in John 3:16 and Romans 8:32 ("spared not his own Son").

Chosen because He loved you — Deuteronomy 7:6–9

6 For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God: the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself, above all people that are upon the face of the earth. 7 The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people: 8 But because the LORD loved you, and because he would keep the oath which he had sworn unto your fathers, hath the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand, and redeemed you out of the house of bondmen, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt. 9 Know therefore that the LORD thy God, he is God, the faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations;

Meaning: God's love has no cause outside itself. "Set his love" is chashaq, to attach oneself. Love leads to redemption (v. 8) and covenant faithfulness "to a thousand generations" (v. 9).

Owner of heaven, lover of a family — Deuteronomy 10:14–15

14 Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens is the LORD's thy God, the earth also, with all that therein is. 15 Only the LORD had a delight in thy fathers to love them, and he chose their seed after them, even you above all people, as it is this day.

Meaning: The contrast is deliberate: God owns everything (v. 14), yet "only" (raq) set His heart on a small family (v. 15). His love is a free choice made by the One who needs nothing.

Love with all thine heart — Deuteronomy 6:4–6

4 Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: 5 And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. 6 And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart:

Meaning: The Shema. Love for God is commanded in response to who He is — one LORD. Heart, soul and might mean the whole person. Jesus calls this "the first and great commandment" (Matthew 22:38).

Love thy neighbour — Leviticus 19:18

18 Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD.

Meaning: The second great commandment. "I am the LORD" grounds it in God's own character. Love for neighbour is defined negatively first: no revenge, no grudge.

The beloved dwells in safety — Deuteronomy 33:3, 12

3 Yea, he loved the people; all his saints are in thy hand: and they sat down at thy feet; every one shall receive of thy words.

12 And of Benjamin he said, The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety by him; and the Lord shall cover him all the day long, and he shall dwell between his shoulders.

Meaning: Moses' final blessing. Love means being held ("in thy hand"), taught ("at thy feet"), covered "all the day long," and carried "between his shoulders" — the way a shepherd carries a lamb (Luke 15:5).

3. Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets show God's love under strain: an everlasting love that draws, a father's love wounded by a wandering son, a husband's love for an unfaithful wife, a mother's love that cannot forget, and a love that sings over its beloved.

An everlasting love — Jeremiah 31:2–4

2 Thus saith the LORD, The people which were left of the sword found grace in the wilderness; even Israel, when I went to cause him to rest. 3 The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee. 4 Again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O virgin of Israel: thou shalt again be adorned with thy tabrets, and shalt go forth in the dances of them that make merry.

Meaning: "Everlasting love" is ahavat olam; "lovingkindness" is chesed. Eternal love (ahavah) expresses itself in covenant loyalty (chesed) that draws (mashak) people to God. Spoken to survivors of judgment, it promises rebuilding and joy.

Bands of love — Hosea 11:1–4, 8–9

1 When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt. 2 As they called them, so they went from them: they sacrificed unto Baalim, and burned incense to graven images. 3 I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms; but they knew not that I healed them. 4 I drew them with cords of a man, with bands of love: and I was to them as they that take off the yoke on their jaws, and I laid meat unto them.

8 How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? how shall I deliver thee, Israel? how shall I make thee as Admah? how shall I set thee as Zeboim? mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together. 9 I will not execute the fierceness of mine anger, I will not return to destroy Ephraim: for I am God, and not man; the Holy One in the midst of thee: and I will not come into the city.

Meaning: God as a father teaching a toddler to walk. "Cords of a man" means human, gentle ties, not ropes for cattle. Verse 8 shows God's inner struggle: "mine heart is turned within me." His love wins over His anger because "I am God, and not man" — divine love is not fickle like human love. Matthew 2:15 applies verse 1 to Jesus.

Love an adulteress — Hosea 3:1

1 Then said the LORD unto me, Go yet, love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel, who look to other gods, and love flagons of wine.

Meaning: Hosea's marriage becomes a living picture. God tells him to love an unfaithful wife "according to the love of the LORD." God's love pursues those who have betrayed Him.

Precious in My sight — Isaiah 43:1, 4

1 But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.

4 Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honourable, and I have loved thee: therefore will I give men for thee, and people for thy life.

Meaning: God's love declares worth: "precious" (yaqar, costly, valuable) and "honourable." Love pays a ransom ("I will give men for thee"), pointing ahead to the ransom of Christ (Mark 10:45).

Graven upon My palms — Isaiah 49:14–16

14 But Zion said, The LORD hath forsaken me, and my Lord hath forgotten me. 15 Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. 16 Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me.

Meaning: "Compassion" is racham, womb-love. God's love is stronger than a nursing mother's. "Graven" (chaqaq) means cut or engraved — a permanent mark, often linked by Christians to the nail-prints in Christ's hands (John 20:27).

He will rejoice over thee with singing — Zephaniah 3:17

17 The LORD thy God in the midst of thee is mighty; he will save, he will rejoice over thee with joy; he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing.

Meaning: God's love is joyful, not grudging. "Rest in his love" can be read "be quiet in his love" — a love too deep for words — and then it breaks into song. He is "in the midst," the dwelling theme (Exodus 25:8).

I have loved you — Malachi 1:2

2 I have loved you, saith the LORD. Yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us? Was not Esau Jacob's brother? saith the LORD: yet I loved Jacob,

Meaning: The last prophet opens with God's first word: "I have loved you." The people's question ("Wherein?") shows how love can be doubted. God answers by pointing to His free choice of Jacob — echoing Deuteronomy 7. Paul quotes it in Romans 9:13.

4. Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings give love its voice in poetry and prayer: a delight between Wisdom and God before creation, a love stronger than death, a lovingkindness better than life, and a love returned by the one who has been loved.

Daily His delight — Proverbs 8:17, 30–31

17 I love them that love me; and those that seek me early shall find me.

30 Then was I by him, as one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him; 31 Rejoicing in the habitable part of his earth; and my delights were with the sons of men.

Meaning: Before creation there was delight (sha'ashu'im, joyful play) between God and Wisdom — a picture the New Testament fulfils in the Father's love for the Son (John 17:24). And that delight turns toward "the sons of men." Love within God overflows to humanity.

Love strong as death — Song of Solomon 8:6–7

6 Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm: for love is strong as death; jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame. 7 Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it: if a man would give all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned.

Meaning: The Bible's great poem of love. "Seal" (chotam) marks ownership and belonging. "A most vehement flame" (shalhevetyah) ends in -yah, a short form of God's name — many read it as "a flame of the LORD." Love cannot be bought and cannot be put out. Christian readers have long seen in it Christ's love for His church (Ephesians 5:25–32).

His banner over me — Song of Solomon 2:3–4

3 As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow with great delight, and his fruit was sweet to my taste. 4 He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love.

Meaning: A banner (degel) marks identity and protection, like a flag over a tribe (Numbers 2:2). The beloved is covered by love, brought into feasting and rest.

Better than life — Psalm 63:1–3

1 O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee: my soul thirsteth for thee, my flesh longeth for thee in a dry and thirsty land, where no water is; 2 To see thy power and thy glory, so as I have seen thee in the sanctuary. 3 Because thy lovingkindness is better than life, my lips shall praise thee.

Meaning: Written in the wilderness of Judah. "Lovingkindness" is chesed. David values God's covenant love above life itself. Love from God produces longing for God.

I love the LORD — Psalm 18:1–2; 116:1–2

18:1 I will love thee, O LORD, my strength. 18:2 The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower.

116:1 I love the LORD, because he hath heard my voice and my supplications. 116:2 Because he hath inclined his ear unto me, therefore will I call upon him as long as I live.

Meaning: In 18:1, "love" is the rare racham — tender, inward love, usually used of God toward people, here returned by David. In 116:1, love answers God's attentiveness. Both show the pattern of 1 John 4:19: "We love him, because he first loved us."

Like as a father — Psalm 103:11–13

11 For as the heaven is high above the earth, so great is his mercy toward them that fear him. 12 As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us. 13 Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.

Meaning: God's love is measured by height (heaven above earth) and distance (east from west). "Pitieth" is racham: a father's tender compassion. Love removes sin rather than ignoring it.

5. New Testament

The New Testament reveals the source of God's love (the Father's love for the Son), its proof (the cross), its security (nothing can separate), its nature ("God is love"), and its fruit (love for God and one another).

My beloved Son — Mark 1:9–11

9 And it came to pass in those days, that Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and was baptized of John in Jordan. 10 And straightway coming up out of the water, he saw the heavens opened, and the Spirit like a dove descending upon him: 11 And there came a voice from heaven, saying, Thou art my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

Meaning: Father, Son and Spirit together at the Jordan. "Beloved" is agapētos, echoing Genesis 22:2 in the Greek Old Testament. "Well pleased" is eudokēsa, the same root as God's "good pleasure" in Ephesians 1:9. The love behind the blueprint is heard aloud.

Loved before the foundation — John 17:23–26

23 I in them, and thou in me, that they may be made perfect in one; and that the world may know that thou hast sent me, and hast loved them, as thou hast loved me. 24 Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me: for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world. 25 O righteous Father, the world hath not known thee: but I have known thee: and these have known that thou hast sent me. 26 And I have declared unto them thy name, and will declare it: that the love wherewith thou hast loved me may be in them, and I in them.

Meaning: The heart of the theme. Three astonishing statements: the Father loved the Son before creation (v. 24); He loves believers "as" He loves the Son (v. 23); and the goal is that this same love "may be in them" (v. 26). The blueprint is the eternal love of the Godhead shared with a people.

God so loved the world — John 3:14–17

14 And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: 15 That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life. 16 For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. 17 For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved.

Meaning: "So" (houtōs) means "in this way" — the manner of the love, not just its size. Love gave (edōken) the "only begotten" (monogenēs, one of a kind), fulfilling Genesis 22:2. Its scope is "the world"; its aim is saving, not condemning.

While we were yet sinners — Romans 5:5–8

5 And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us. 6 For when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly. 7 For scarcely for a righteous man will one die: yet peradventure for a good man some would even dare to die. 8 But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.

Meaning: God's love is proven (synistēsin, demonstrated) at the point of our worst. Three descriptions: "without strength," "ungodly," "sinners." Verse 5 adds that this love is "shed abroad" (ekkechytai, poured out) inside believers by the Spirit — not just shown to us but placed in us.

Nothing can separate — Romans 8:35–39

35 Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? 36 As it is written, For thy sake we are killed all the day long; we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter. 37 Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. 38 For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, 39 Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Meaning: Paul lists every possible threat — circumstances, death, spiritual powers, time, space — and none can separate. The love is located "in Christ Jesus," so it is as secure as He is. This is Song 8:7 ("many waters cannot quench love") in New Testament form.

The love that passeth knowledge — Ephesians 3:17–19

17 That Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith; that ye, being rooted and grounded in love, 18 May be able to comprehend with all saints what is the breadth, and length, and depth, and height; 19 And to know the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge, that ye might be filled with all the fulness of God.

Meaning: Love is the soil ("rooted") and foundation ("grounded") of the Christian life. Its four dimensions are known together "with all saints," not alone. Knowing this love leads to being "filled with all the fulness of God" — the goal of the blueprint.

God is love — 1 John 4:7–12, 16, 19

7 Beloved, let us love one another: for love is of God; and every one that loveth is born of God, and knoweth God. 8 He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love. 9 In this was manifested the love of God toward us, because that God sent his only begotten Son into the world, that we might live through him. 10 Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins. 11 Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another. 12 No man hath seen God at any time. If we love one another, God dwelleth in us, and his love is perfected in us.

16 And we have known and believed the love that God hath to us. God is love; and he that dwelleth in love dwelleth in God, and God in him.

19 We love him, because he first loved us.

Meaning: The fullest New Testament statement. Love is God's nature ("God is love"), shown in sending the Son (vv. 9–10), received as life ("that we might live"), and completed when believers love one another (v. 12). The invisible God becomes visible through a loving community.

What manner of love — 1 John 3:1–2

1 Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God: therefore the world knoweth us not, because it knew him not. 2 Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.

Meaning: "What manner" (potapēn) means "from what country" — a love foreign to this world. Its gift is sonship now and likeness to Christ later, linking love to themes 14 and 15.

The two great commandments — Matthew 22:37–40

37 Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. 38 This is the first and great commandment. 39 And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. 40 On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.

Meaning: Jesus joins Deuteronomy 6:5 and Leviticus 19:18. The whole Torah and Prophets "hang" on love — the canon's own summary of itself.

Charity never faileth — 1 Corinthians 13:4–8, 13

4 Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, 5 Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil; 6 Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth; 7 Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things. 8 Charity never faileth: but whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away.

13 And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.

Meaning: "Charity" is agapē. Fifteen descriptions, most of them verbs: love is something done. It outlasts every gift because it belongs to the age to come. Many teachers note that "Christ" can be read in place of "charity" throughout — He is love fully lived.

6. Patterns across the canon

Seven patterns recur in all four divisions.

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Love begins in God, not in its object "Because the LORD loved you" (Deuteronomy 7:8) "I have loved thee with an everlasting love" (Jeremiah 31:3); "I have loved you" (Malachi 1:2) Wisdom "daily his delight" before creation (Proverbs 8:30) "Thou lovedst me before the foundation" (John 17:24); "not that we loved God" (1 John 4:10)
2. A father's love for a beloved son is the measure "Thine only son … whom thou lovest" (Genesis 22:2) "When Israel was a child, then I loved him" (Hosea 11:1) "Thou art my Son" (Psalm 2:7); "like as a father" (Psalm 103:13) "My beloved Son" (Mark 1:11); "gave his only begotten Son" (John 3:16)
3. Love holds on to the unfaithful "Keepeth covenant and mercy … to a thousand generations" (Deuteronomy 7:9) "How shall I give thee up?" (Hosea 11:8); "love … an adulteress" (Hosea 3:1) "Many waters cannot quench love" (Song 8:7) "While we were yet sinners" (Romans 5:8); nothing can separate (Romans 8:39)
4. Love pays a ransom Redeemed "because the LORD loved you" (Deuteronomy 7:8) "I will give men for thee" (Isaiah 43:4) "Removed our transgressions" (Psalm 103:12) "Sent his Son to be the propitiation" (1 John 4:10)
5. Love rejoices over its beloved "The LORD will again rejoice over thee" (Deuteronomy 30:9) "He will joy over thee with singing" (Zephaniah 3:17) "His banner over me was love" (Song 2:4) The father ran, embraced and feasted (Luke 15:20–24)
6. Love keeps its beloved close "Dwell between his shoulders" (Deuteronomy 33:12) "Graven … upon the palms of my hands" (Isaiah 49:16) "Set me as a seal upon thine heart" (Song 8:6) "That the love … may be in them, and I in them" (John 17:26); "shed abroad in our hearts" (Romans 5:5)
7. Love received becomes love returned "Thou shalt love the LORD … thy neighbour" (Deuteronomy 6:5; Leviticus 19:18) "To love mercy" (Micah 6:8) "I love the LORD, because he hath heard" (Psalm 116:1) "We love him, because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19); the two commandments (Matthew 22:37–40)

7. Principles and practices

The patterns yield seven principles, each with a practice for personal life, the church and teaching.

  1. God's love is eternal and self-caused. It began within God before creation and has no reason outside Him (John 17:24; Deuteronomy 7:7–8).
  2. The cross is its measure. God's love is defined by the gift of His only Son, not by feelings or circumstances (John 3:16; Romans 5:8).
  3. It holds on through failure. God's love outlasts unfaithfulness and every threat (Hosea 11:8; Romans 8:38–39).
  4. It delights and rejoices. God does not merely tolerate His people; He sings over them (Zephaniah 3:17).
  5. It is placed within us by the Spirit. God's love is poured into believers' hearts, so the Father's love for the Son becomes our inner life (Romans 5:5; John 17:26).
  6. Love for God and neighbour is the response. The law and the prophets hang on these two (Matthew 22:40); we love because He first loved (1 John 4:19).
  7. A loving community makes the invisible God visible. "No man hath seen God … If we love one another, God dwelleth in us" (1 John 4:12).
Principle Practice Anchor text
1. Eternal, self-caused love Each morning, read John 17:23 aloud with your name, before any request. John 17:23–24
2. The cross its measure When you doubt God's love, look first at the cross, not at your circumstances. Keep Romans 5:8 where you see it daily. Romans 5:8
3. Holds through failure After sin, return quickly rather than hiding. Read Hosea 11 and Luke 15 as God's posture toward you. Hosea 11:8–9; Luke 15:20
4. Love that sings Spend time in worship that receives God's delight rather than performing for it. Meditate on Zephaniah 3:17. Zephaniah 3:17
5. Love within Ask the Spirit daily to pour God's love into your heart afresh, especially before difficult conversations. Romans 5:5
6. Love returned Weekly, pick one concrete act of love for God (worship, obedience) and one for a neighbour (service, forgiveness). Matthew 22:37–40
7. Love made visible Measure church life by 1 Corinthians 13: read it together and ask which phrase your group most needs to practise. 1 John 4:12; 1 Corinthians 13:4–7

For a teaching session: Read Genesis 22:1–2 and John 3:16 side by side. Ask what the first "love" in the Bible teaches about the greatest love in the Bible. Close with 1 John 4:19.
