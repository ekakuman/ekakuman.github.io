The Grace of God

Overview and word study

Grace is God's unearned favor toward the undeserving, and His power at work in them. It was "given us in Christ Jesus before the world began" (2 Timothy 1:9), so it is part of the blueprint, not a later fix. Grace saves (Ephesians 2:8), strengthens (2 Corinthians 12:9), teaches (Titus 2:11–12), and will be displayed "in the ages to come" (Ephesians 2:7).

Grace differs from mercy (theme 9): mercy withholds what we deserve; grace gives what we do not deserve.

The Hebrew chanan pictures a superior bending down to someone lower — grace is condescension in the best sense. The Greek charis and eucharistia share a root: grace received becomes thanksgiving returned.

Torah

Noah found grace — Genesis 6:5–8

5 And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. 6 And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart. 7 And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air; for it repenteth me that I have made them. 8 But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.

Meaning: The first use of "grace" (chen) in the Bible. The background is total corruption; then a single "But." Noah did not earn grace — he "found" it. Righteousness follows grace (6:9), not the other way round.

I know thee by name — Exodus 33:12–13, 17, 19

12 And Moses said unto the LORD, See, thou sayest unto me, Bring up this people: and thou hast not let me know whom thou wilt send with me. Yet thou hast said, I know thee by name, and thou hast also found grace in my sight. 13 Now therefore, I pray thee, if I have found grace in thy sight, shew me now thy way, that I may know thee, that I may find grace in thy sight: and consider that this nation is thy people.

17 And the LORD said unto Moses, I will do this thing also that thou hast spoken: for thou hast found grace in my sight, and I know thee by name.

19 And he said, I will make all my goodness pass before thee, and I will proclaim the name of the LORD before thee; and will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy.

Meaning: "Grace" appears five times in this short exchange, right after the golden calf. Grace is personal ("I know thee by name"), opens access ("shew me thy way"), and is sovereign ("to whom I will be gracious"). Paul quotes verse 19 in Romans 9:15.

Not for thy righteousness — Deuteronomy 9:5–6

5 Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee, and that he may perform the word which the LORD sware unto thy fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. 6 Understand therefore, that the LORD thy God giveth thee not this good land to possess it for thy righteousness; for thou art a stiffnecked people.

Meaning: The inheritance is a gift tied to God's oath, not to Israel's merit. The same principle governs salvation: "not of works" (Ephesians 2:9).

The LORD be gracious unto thee — Numbers 6:24–27

24 The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: 25 The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: 26 The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace. 27 And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them.

Meaning: The priestly blessing. Grace is pictured as God's face shining on His people. Paul's letters open the same way: "Grace be unto you, and peace" (Romans 1:7).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Waiting to be gracious — Isaiah 30:18

18 And therefore will the LORD wait, that he may be gracious unto you, and therefore will he be exalted, that he may have mercy upon you: for the LORD is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him.

Meaning: God waits (chakah) eagerly for the moment to show grace. Grace is not reluctant; God longs to give it.

Without money, without price — Isaiah 55:1–3

1 Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price. 2 Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good, and let your soul delight itself in fatness. 3 Incline your ear, and come unto me; hear, and your soul shall live; and I will make an everlasting covenant with you, even the sure mercies of David.

Meaning: Grace pictured as a free market: "buy … without money." The only qualification is thirst. Revelation 22:17 echoes it: "take the water of life freely."

A gracious God — Jonah 4:1–2

1 But it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was very angry. 2 And he prayed unto the LORD, and said, I pray thee, O LORD, was not this my saying, when I was yet in my country? Therefore I fled before unto Tarshish: for I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repentest thee of the evil.

Meaning: Jonah quotes Exodus 34:6 as a complaint. Grace reached Israel's enemies in Nineveh. The prophet knew God's grace was wider than his own heart.

Grace, grace unto it — Zechariah 4:6–7

6 Then he answered and spake unto me, saying, This is the word of the LORD unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts. 7 Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain: and he shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it.

Meaning: God's house is finished by the Spirit, not human strength, and the final stone is greeted with "Grace, grace." Grace begins and completes God's building (1 Corinthians 3:10).

The spirit of grace — Zechariah 12:10

10 And I will pour upon the house of David, and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the spirit of grace and of supplications: and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son, and shall be in bitterness for him, as one that is in bitterness for his firstborn.

Meaning: Grace produces repentance, not the reverse. Grace is poured out, and people look on the One they pierced. John 19:37 applies this to the crucifixion.

Writings (Ketuvim)

Why have I found grace? — Ruth 2:10–12

10 Then she fell on her face, and bowed herself to the ground, and said unto him, Why have I found grace in thine eyes, that thou shouldest take knowledge of me, seeing I am a stranger? 11 And Boaz answered and said unto her, It hath fully been shewed me, all that thou hast done unto thy mother in law since the death of thine husband: and how thou hast left thy father and thy mother, and the land of thy nativity, and art come unto a people which thou knewest not heretofore. 12 The LORD recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust.

Meaning: A foreign widow is astonished by grace. Boaz, her kinsman-redeemer, pictures Christ. She came "under whose wings" — the refuge of God's grace — and became an ancestor of David and Jesus (Matthew 1:5).

Grace and glory — Psalm 84:10–12

10 For a day in thy courts is better than a thousand. I had rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God, than to dwell in the tents of wickedness. 11 For the LORD God is a sun and shield: the LORD will give grace and glory: no good thing will he withhold from them that walk uprightly. 12 O LORD of hosts, blessed is the man that trusteth in thee.

Meaning: Grace and glory come together: grace now, glory later (Romans 5:2). God as "sun" gives light; as "shield" gives protection.

Grace unto the lowly — Proverbs 3:34

34 Surely he scorneth the scorners: but he giveth grace unto the lowly.

Meaning: Grace flows downhill to the humble. James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5 quote this verse: "God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble."

Grace poured into His lips — Psalm 45:2

2 Thou art fairer than the children of men: grace is poured into thy lips: therefore God hath blessed thee for ever.

Meaning: A royal wedding psalm applied to Christ (Hebrews 1:8–9). Grace is His speech. At Nazareth people "wondered at the gracious words which proceeded out of his mouth" (Luke 4:22).

A little space of grace — Ezra 9:8

8 And now for a little space grace hath been shewed from the LORD our God, to leave us a remnant to escape, and to give us a nail in his holy place, that our God may lighten our eyes, and give us a little reviving in our bondage.

Meaning: After exile, grace leaves a remnant, a secure place ("a nail") in God's house, and revival. Grace is God's way of rebuilding what sin destroyed.

New Testament

Grace and truth — John 1:14, 16–17

14 And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

16 And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace. 17 For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.

Meaning: Grace is a Person: Jesus is "full" of it. "Grace for grace" (charin anti charitos) means grace upon grace, wave after wave. Law came through Moses; grace came embodied.

Justified freely by His grace — Romans 3:23–24

23 For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; 24 Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus:

Meaning: "Freely" is dōrean, as a gift — the same word as "without a cause" in John 15:25. Grace is the source, redemption in Christ the means.

Grace much more abounded — Romans 5:20–21

20 Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound: 21 That as sin hath reigned unto death, even so might grace reign through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.

Meaning: "Much more abound" is hypereperisseusen, super-abounded. Grace is not merely equal to sin; it overflows it. Grace now "reigns" like a king.

By grace are ye saved — Ephesians 2:4–10

4 But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us, 5 Even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;) 6 And hath raised us up together, and made us sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus: 7 That in the ages to come he might shew the exceeding riches of his grace in his kindness toward us through Christ Jesus. 8 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: 9 Not of works, lest any man should boast. 10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

Meaning: The clearest summary. Mercy, love and grace act on the dead (vv. 4–5). Saved believers are God's eternal exhibit of grace (v. 7). Grace is received by faith and excludes boasting (vv. 8–9), yet produces good works "before ordained" (v. 10) — part of the blueprint.

By the grace of God I am what I am — 1 Corinthians 15:9–10

9 For I am the least of the apostles, that am not meet to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God. 10 But by the grace of God I am what I am: and his grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain; but I laboured more abundantly than they all: yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.

Meaning: Grace both forgives the worst past and empowers the hardest work. Grace makes people work more, not less — "yet not I, but the grace of God."

My grace is sufficient — 2 Corinthians 12:9–10

9 And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. 10 Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong.

Meaning: Grace as power. God's answer to the thorn was not removal but sufficiency. "Rest upon me" is episkēnōsē, to tabernacle upon — grace as God's presence dwelling over weakness.

He became poor — 2 Corinthians 8:9

9 For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.

Meaning: Grace defined by Christ's exchange: His riches for our poverty. Paul uses it to motivate generous giving.

The grace that teaches — Titus 2:11–14

11 For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, 12 Teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world; 13 Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ; 14 Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works.

Meaning: Grace is a teacher (paideuousa, training like a child). It saves, trains in godliness, and points to Christ's return. Grace never permits sin; it disciplines away from it.

The throne of grace — Hebrews 4:15–16

15 For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. 16 Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

Meaning: God's throne, once a place of terror (Isaiah 6:5), is now "the throne of grace" because of a sympathetic High Priest. Grace comes "in time of need" (eukairon, well-timed).

The last word of the Bible — Revelation 22:21

21 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

Meaning: The Bible's final sentence is a blessing of grace. The blueprint ends where it began: in God's free favor.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Grace found by the undeserving "But Noah found grace" amid total corruption (Genesis 6:8) Grace to Nineveh (Jonah 4:2) A foreign widow finds grace (Ruth 2:10) "Even when we were dead in sins" (Ephesians 2:5)
2. Grace, not merit "Not for thy righteousness" (Deuteronomy 9:6) "Without money and without price" (Isaiah 55:1) "He giveth grace unto the lowly" (Proverbs 3:34) "Not of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:9)
3. Grace as God's shining face "Make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious" (Numbers 6:25) "They shall look upon me" (Zechariah 12:10) "Cause thy face to shine; and we shall be saved" (Psalm 80:3) "Full of grace and truth … we beheld his glory" (John 1:14)
4. Grace greater than sin Golden calf followed by "thou hast found grace" (Exodus 33:17) "Though your sins be as scarlet" (Isaiah 1:18) "He hath not dealt with us after our sins" (Psalm 103:10) "Grace did much more abound" (Romans 5:20)
5. Grace builds God's house Tabernacle built after grace is renewed (Exodus 34–40) "Grace, grace unto it" (Zechariah 4:7) "A nail in his holy place" (Ezra 9:8) "According to the grace of God … as a wise masterbuilder" (1 Corinthians 3:10)
6. Grace strengthens the weak "As thy days, so shall thy strength be" (Deuteronomy 33:25) "He giveth power to the faint" (Isaiah 40:29) "The LORD will give grace and glory" (Psalm 84:11) "My grace is sufficient" (2 Corinthians 12:9)
7. Grace transforms the heart "The LORD … will circumcise thine heart" (Deuteronomy 30:6) "A new heart also will I give you" (Ezekiel 36:26) "Grant me thy law graciously" (Psalm 119:29) "Teaching us … we should live soberly" (Titus 2:12)

Principles

  1. Grace was given before the world began. It is God's original plan, not an emergency fix (2 Timothy 1:9).
  2. Grace is for the undeserving. Only the needy qualify (Isaiah 55:1; Ephesians 2:5).
  3. Grace is a Person. It came with Jesus and is received from His fulness (John 1:14–17).
  4. Grace overflows sin. No sin is greater than God's grace (Romans 5:20).
  5. Grace empowers. It is strength in weakness and energy for work (2 Corinthians 12:9; 1 Corinthians 15:10).
  6. Grace trains for godliness. It never licenses sin (Titus 2:11–12).
  7. Grace is displayed forever. Saved people are God's eternal exhibit of grace (Ephesians 2:7).

Given before time Thank God daily that His grace toward you was settled before you were born. 2 Timothy 1:9

For a teaching session: Compare Genesis 6:8 ("Noah found grace") with Ephesians 2:8–10. Ask what grace does before, during and after salvation.
