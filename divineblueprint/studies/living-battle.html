The Divine Blueprint and Spiritual Battle

Overview

The blueprint unfolds in a world at war. From the garden onward, an enemy opposes God's purpose: the serpent deceives (Genesis 3:1–5), Satan accuses (Job 1:9–11; Zechariah 3:1), tempts (Matthew 4:1–11) and "walketh about, seeking whom he may devour" (1 Peter 5:8). Scripture also names two other enemies: the world (1 John 2:15–16) and the flesh (Galatians 5:17).

But the battle is not between equals. The first gospel promise was that the woman's seed would crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15). The Son of God came "that he might destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8), and at the cross He "spoiled principalities and powers" (Colossians 2:15). Believers fight from victory, not for it.

This study looks at:

  • Who the enemy is — and what he can and cannot do.
  • What God has provided — Christ's victory and the armour of God.
  • How believers stand — in daily temptation, fear and spiritual opposition.

Key passage — Ephesians 6:10–18

10 Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might. 11 Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. 12 For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. 13 Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand. 14 Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness; 15 And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace; 16 Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked. 17 And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God: 18 Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints;

Meaning

The strength is "in the Lord" (v. 10), not in us. The armour is "of God" — He supplies it. Every piece is a gospel truth already covered in the blueprint: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and the Word. The main command is not "attack" but "stand" (vv. 11, 13, 14). Our real enemies are not people ("not against flesh and blood," v. 12) — so the battle is never a reason to hate or harm anyone. Prayer (v. 18) runs through the whole passage, and it is prayer "for all saints": no one fights alone.

Balance notes

  • Two opposite mistakes. Some ignore the devil entirely; others see him behind everything. Scripture takes him seriously but gives far more attention to Christ, to the flesh and to the world.
  • Not every problem is demonic. Illness, mental illness, family conflict and bad decisions have many causes. Believers should pray and also seek medical, psychological and practical help. Labelling an illness as a demon can cause great harm.
  • Fear of curses and black magic. In many communities people fear witchcraft, curses or the evil eye. A believer belongs to Christ, who has triumphed over every power (Colossians 2:15; 1 John 4:4). There is no need for charms, threads, amulets or rituals for protection (Deuteronomy 18:10–12; Acts 19:18–20).
  • Deliverance ministry. Jesus and the apostles cast out demons, and Christians today hold different views on how this applies. Any such ministry should be done with humility, under church leadership, with no force, harm, shaming or payment, and with care for medical needs.

Spiritual battle through the five lenses

A · The Purpose — the serpent's defeat promised

Genesis 3:15 — And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.

1 John 3:8 — He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.

Meaning. The battle was announced in the garden, along with its outcome: a bruised heel for the Seed, a crushed head for the serpent. The Son came "for this purpose." The devil's opposition is real, but it was never able to cancel God's plan.

Theme links: The Seed; The Purpose of God; The Son.

The unseen side. "Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them. ... And the LORD opened the eyes of the young man; and he saw: and, behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha" (2 Kings 6:16–17). The enemy is unseen, but so is the help. The servant's problem was not too many enemies but too little sight.

B · The Provision — victory won at the cross

Colossians 2:15 — And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

Hebrews 2:14–15 — Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; and deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.

Matthew 4:4 — But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.

Meaning. At the cross Christ disarmed the powers and publicly triumphed over them. By His death He broke the devil's grip of fear and death. In the wilderness He answered every temptation with Scripture — showing believers how to use "the sword of the Spirit."

Theme links: The Lamb; Redemption; The Kingdom.

C · The People — standing, resisting, drawing near

James 4:7–8 — Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.

1 Peter 5:8–9 — Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.

1 Corinthians 10:13 — There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.

Meaning. The order matters: submit to God first, then resist the devil. Watchfulness and sobriety are ordinary disciplines, not dramatic ones. Temptation is "common to man" — the believer is not uniquely targeted — and God always provides a way of escape. Fellow believers worldwide face the same battle.

Theme links: Holiness; Faith; The Holy Spirit; Sonship.

D · The Goal — more than conquerors

Romans 8:37–39 — Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

1 John 4:4 — Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.

Revelation 12:10–11 — And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night. And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

Meaning. "Principalities" and "powers" are named in Romans 8 — and none can separate a believer from God's love. The accuser is answered by the blood of the Lamb. Believers overcome not by special techniques but by Christ's blood, faithful testimony and willingness to suffer.

Theme links: Love; Election; Righteousness and Justice.

E · The Consummation — Satan crushed for ever

Romans 16:20 — And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen.

Revelation 20:10 — And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

Meaning. Genesis 3:15 comes full circle: "the God of peace" will bruise Satan under the feet of His people. The deceiver's end is certain and final. The battle has an end date, and the blueprint ends not in war but in peace.

Theme links: Final Judgment; Peace; The Heading Up of All Things; New Creation.

Practices

For yourself

Practice How Scripture
Put on the armour daily Pray through Ephesians 6:14–18 each morning. Ephesians 6:13–18
Store up the sword Memorise verses that answer your common temptations. Matthew 4:4–10; Psalm 119:11
Submit, then resist Confess sin quickly; then say no in Jesus' name. James 4:7–8; 1 John 1:9
Flee where needed Remove triggers; avoid places and screens that lead you down. 2 Timothy 2:22; Proverbs 4:14–15
Remove occult objects Get rid of charms, amulets and objects tied to other powers. Acts 19:18–20; Deuteronomy 18:10–12

For others

Practice How Scripture
Pray for others by name Keep a list; pray "for all saints." Ephesians 6:18; Luke 22:31–32
Restore gently Help those who fall without shame or harshness. Galatians 6:1
Calm the fearful Speak Christ's victory to those afraid of curses. 1 John 4:4; Romans 8:38–39

For the church

Practice How Scripture
Teach Christ's victory Keep the focus on the cross, not on the devil. Colossians 2:15; Hebrews 2:14–15
Pray together Hold regular prayer meetings for the church and the city. Acts 4:23–31; Acts 12:5
Safe pastoral care Handle oppression cases with leaders, medical advice, and no force or payment. 1 Corinthians 14:40; Matthew 10:8

5 min Opening Ephesians 6:10–12 Ask: "What comes to mind when you hear 'spiritual warfare'?" The battle is real, but it is "in the Lord."

8 min B · God's provision Colossians 2:15; Hebrews 2:14–15; Matthew 4:1–11 Victory won at the cross; Jesus answered temptation with Scripture.

Discussion questions

  1. How does Genesis 3:15 shape the way we see the whole Bible story?
  2. What did Christ do to the "principalities and powers" at the cross (Colossians 2:15)?
  3. Why is the main command in Ephesians 6 "stand" rather than "attack"?
  4. Which piece of armour do you most need right now? Why?
  5. Why does James say "submit to God" before "resist the devil" (James 4:7)?
  6. What are the dangers of ignoring the devil? Of seeing him everywhere?
  7. How should a believer respond to fear of curses or black magic (1 John 4:4)?
  8. Who will you pray for by name this week (Ephesians 6:18)?
