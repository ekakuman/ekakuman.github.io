Before the Foundation of the World

Overview

"Before the foundation of the world" is the starting point of the divine blueprint. Before anything was created, God had a purpose, a beloved Son, a Lamb, a people chosen in Him, a kingdom prepared, and a book of life. Creation and history are how that purpose is carried out.

The exact phrase pro katabolēs kosmou appears three times (John 17:24; Ephesians 1:4; 1 Peter 1:20). Its companion apo katabolēs kosmou, "from the foundation of the world," appears seven times. Related expressions — "before the world was," "before the world began," "from everlasting" — widen the theme to about 40 passages across the canon.

The study traces the theme through the Hebrew canon's three divisions and then the New Testament:

  • Torah — Genesis to Deuteronomy
  • Prophets (Nevi'im) — Joshua to Kings, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, the Twelve
  • Writings (Ketuvim) — Psalms, Proverbs, Job, Song, Ruth, Lamentations, Ecclesiastes, Esther, Daniel, Ezra–Nehemiah, Chronicles
  • New Testament

It then moves from patterns (what recurs) to principles (what the patterns teach) to practices (how the principles are lived). Passages are given in full from the King James Version (public domain), each followed by its semantic meaning. This structure can serve as the template for each later topic in the blueprint series.

1. Word study

The vocabulary separates God's eternal counsel (before time) from its execution in creation and history (from the foundation onward).

Two semantic points shape the whole study:

  1. Before vs. from. Pro passages describe relationships within God in eternity: love, glory, choosing, ordaining. Apo passages describe what God set in motion within history: a kingdom prepared, works finished, a book of life.
  2. A minority reading. A few teachers read katabolē as "overthrow" (tying it to Genesis 1:2 "without form, and void"). Most lexicons and translations take it as "foundation," which is the reading used here.

2. Torah

The Torah does not use the phrase, but it lays down every seed of the theme: a God who exists before "the beginning," a Redeemer promised before any sacrifice, a lamb God provides Himself, a people chosen in love, and a sanctuary built from a pattern shown in advance.

Before the beginning — Genesis 1:1–3

1 In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. 2 And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. 3 And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

Meaning: Bereshit ("in the beginning") marks the start of time; God is already there. Father (God), Spirit (moving), and Word ("God said") act together, which John 1:1–3 later makes explicit. The Hebrew reshit also means firstfruits; Colossians 1:18 calls Christ "the beginning."

The seed promised — Genesis 3:14–15

14 And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field; upon thy belly shalt thou go, and dust shalt thou eat all the days of thy life: 15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.

Meaning: The protoevangelium (first gospel). The remedy is announced in the same breath as the judgment, showing it was not improvised. God had already purposed the Seed (Galatians 3:16; 4:4).

God will provide Himself a lamb — Genesis 22:7–8, 13–14

7 And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering? 8 And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.

13 And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son. 14 And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovahjireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.

Meaning: "Provide" is ra'ah, "to see" — God sees ahead and so provides. The ram was already there before Abraham looked. This is the first lamb question in Scripture ("where is the lamb?"); John 1:29 answers it, and 1 Peter 1:20 says the Lamb was foreknown before the foundation.

The lamb set apart in advance — Exodus 12:3, 5–6

3 Speak ye unto all the congregation of Israel, saying, In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house: 5 Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year: ye shall take it out from the sheep, or from the goats: 6 And ye shall keep it up until the fourteenth day of the same month: and the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it in the evening.

Meaning: The lamb is chosen four days before it is slain, a small-scale picture of the Lamb chosen before the world and slain in time. "Without blemish" (tamim) is echoed in 1 Peter 1:19.

Chosen in love, not merit — Deuteronomy 7:6–8

6 For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God: the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself, above all people that are upon the face of the earth. 7 The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people: 8 But because the LORD loved you, and because he would keep the oath which he had sworn unto your fathers, hath the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand, and redeemed you out of the house of bondmen, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt.

Meaning: "Chosen" is bachar; "special people" is segullah, a personal treasure. The only reason given for the choice is love itself. Ephesians 1:4 follows the same logic: chosen "in love."

The pattern shown in the mount — Exodus 25:8–9, 40

8 And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them. 9 According to all that I shew thee, after the pattern of the tabernacle, and the pattern of all the instruments thereof, even so shall ye make it.

40 And look that thou make them after their pattern, which was shewed thee in the mount.

Meaning: Tabnit is literally a blueprint. God's goal is stated first: "that I may dwell among them." The design existed in heaven before it was built on earth; Hebrews 8:5 says the tabernacle was a copy and shadow of heavenly things. This is the Torah's clearest picture of a divine blueprint.

The eternal God — Deuteronomy 33:27

27 The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms: and he shall thrust out the enemy from before thee; and shall say, Destroy them.

Meaning: "Eternal" is qedem, the God of before. "Everlasting" is olam. The God who was before all things holds His people from beneath.

Other Torah references: Genesis 2:1–3 (works finished, rest); Exodus 32:32–33 (God's book); Numbers 23:19 (God does not change His word).

3. Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets make the theme explicit: God declares the end from the beginning, formed the earth with a purpose, knew His servants before their birth, and sent a Ruler whose origins are from everlasting.

Known from the foundations of the earth — Isaiah 40:21–22

21 Have ye not known? have ye not heard? hath it not been told you from the beginning? have ye not understood from the foundations of the earth? 22 It is he that sitteth upon the circle of the earth, and the inhabitants thereof are as grasshoppers; that stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a tent to dwell in:

Meaning: "Foundations" is mosedot, from yasad. Creation itself is a message about God, available from the start. The heavens are spread "as a tent to dwell in" — dwelling is the purpose, as in Exodus 25:8.

Formed to be inhabited — Isaiah 45:18

18 For thus saith the LORD that created the heavens; God himself that formed the earth and made it; he hath established it, he created it not in vain, he formed it to be inhabited: I am the LORD; and there is none else.

Meaning: "In vain" is tohu, the same word as "without form" in Genesis 1:2. God's design for the earth was never emptiness but a home for His people.

The end from the beginning — Isaiah 46:9–11

9 Remember the former things of old: for I am God, and there is none else; I am God, and there is none like me, 10 Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done, saying, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure: 11 Calling a ravenous bird from the east, the man that executeth my counsel from a far country: yea, I have spoken it, I will also bring it to pass; I have purposed it, I will also do it.

Meaning: The Old Testament's clearest statement of a blueprint. "End" (acharit) is declared from "the beginning" (reshit). "Counsel" (etsah) and "pleasure" (chephets, delight) parallel Ephesians 1:9–11, "good pleasure" and "counsel of his own will."

The Lamb and the pleasure of the LORD — Isaiah 53:7, 10

7 He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.

10 Yet it pleased the LORD to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in his hand.

Meaning: The suffering of the Lamb "pleased" (chaphets) the LORD — the same root as "my pleasure" in Isaiah 46:10. The cross was within God's purpose, not outside it (Acts 2:23). "He shall see his seed" points to the many sons of Romans 8:29.

Known before formed — Jeremiah 1:4–5

4 Then the word of the LORD came unto me, saying, 5 Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.

Meaning: Three verbs before birth: knew (yada, intimate knowing), sanctified (qadash, set apart), ordained (natan, gave, appointed). This is a personal, individual form of foreknowledge, matching proginōskō in Romans 8:29.

An everlasting love — Jeremiah 31:3

3 The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.

Meaning: "Everlasting" is olam: love that reaches back beyond memory. "Drawn" (mashak) shows that the eternal love results in a drawing in time (John 6:44).

Goings forth from everlasting — Micah 5:2

2 But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.

Meaning: The Ruler has two origins: Bethlehem in time, and "from of old" (qedem), "from everlasting" (yemei olam, days of eternity). The One born in Bethlehem existed before the foundation of the world (John 17:5).

Other Prophets references: Isaiah 14:24, 26–27 (purposed and it shall stand); Isaiah 41:4 (calling the generations from the beginning); Isaiah 48:12–13 (my hand laid the foundation); Isaiah 64:4 (eye hath not seen, quoted in 1 Corinthians 2:9); Malachi 3:16 (book of remembrance).

4. Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings give the theme its inner, worshipful side: Wisdom rejoicing with God before creation, every person's days written in God's book, a counsel that stands forever, and eternity set in the human heart.

Wisdom before the earth — Proverbs 8:22–31

22 The LORD possessed me in the beginning of his way, before his works of old. 23 I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning, or ever the earth was. 24 When there were no depths, I was brought forth; when there were no fountains abounding with water. 25 Before the mountains were settled, before the hills was I brought forth: 26 While as yet he had not made the earth, nor the fields, nor the highest part of the dust of the world. 27 When he prepared the heavens, I was there: when he set a compass upon the face of the depth: 28 When he established the clouds above: when he strengthened the fountains of the deep: 29 When he gave to the sea his decree, that the waters should not pass his commandment: when he appointed the foundations of the earth: 30 Then I was by him, as one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him; 31 Rejoicing in the habitable part of his earth; and my delights were with the sons of men.

Meaning: The fullest Old Testament scene of "before the foundation." "Set up" (nasak) means installed, as a king. "Brought up with him" (amon) can mean master craftsman or nursling — Wisdom is both architect and beloved child. Verse 30 ("daily his delight") anticipates John 17:24 ("thou lovedst me before the foundation"). Verse 31 reveals the blueprint's heart: delight in "the sons of men." The New Testament applies this Wisdom language to Christ (1 Corinthians 1:24, 30; Colossians 2:3).

Days written in God's book — Psalm 139:14–16

14 I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well. 15 My substance was not hid from thee, when I was made in secret, and curiously wrought in the lowest parts of the earth. 16 Thine eyes did see my substance, yet being unperfect; and in thy book all my members were written, which in continuance were fashioned, when as yet there was none of them.

Meaning: "Curiously wrought" (raqam) is embroidery, the word used for the tabernacle's woven work. "Substance, yet being unperfect" is golem, an unformed embryo. "Members" can also be read "days" (yamim, verse 16 "which in continuance were fashioned"): a person's life was written before it began.

The counsel that stands — Psalm 33:10–11

10 The LORD bringeth the counsel of the heathen to nought: he maketh the devices of the people of none effect. 11 The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever, the thoughts of his heart to all generations.

Meaning: Two counsels contrast: nations' plans fail; God's etsah stands le'olam. "Thoughts of his heart" shows the blueprint is not cold decree but the expression of God's heart.

From everlasting to everlasting — Psalm 90:1–2

1 Lord, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations. 2 Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God.

Meaning: Moses' psalm. Before the world, God; and that eternal God is His people's "dwelling place" (ma'on). The blueprint is mutual dwelling: God in His people, His people in God (John 14:23; 15:4).

Where wast thou? — Job 38:4–7

4 Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding. 5 Who hath laid the measures thereof, if thou knowest? or who hath stretched the line upon it? 6 Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened? or who laid the corner stone thereof; 7 When the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?

Meaning: Building language throughout: foundations, measures, line, cornerstone. The laying of the foundation was an occasion of worship by heavenly beings. Humility is the right response: the blueprint was drawn before we existed.

Eternity in the heart — Ecclesiastes 3:11, 14–15

11 He hath made every thing beautiful in his time: also he hath set the world in their heart, so that no man can find out the work that God maketh from the beginning to the end.

14 I know that, whatsoever God doeth, it shall be for ever: nothing can be put to it, nor any thing taken from it: and God doeth it, that men should fear before him. 15 That which hath been is now; and that which is to be hath already been; and God requireth that which is past.

Meaning: "The world" is ha'olam, eternity. God placed a sense of the eternal plan in human hearts, while the full plan stays beyond our discovery. Verse 15 sounds like Hebrews 4:3: what is to be has already been, in God's sight.

The kingdom given to the Son of man — Daniel 7:13–14

13 I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. 14 And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.

Meaning: Daniel is among the Writings in the Hebrew canon. The "Ancient of days" (atiq yomin) is the God before time. The kingdom given to the Son of man, and shared with "the saints" (7:27), is the kingdom "prepared from the foundation of the world" (Matthew 25:34).

Other Writings references: Psalm 69:28 (book of the living); Psalm 102:25–27; Proverbs 3:19–20; Proverbs 25:2 (glory of God to conceal); Daniel 12:1 (written in the book); 1 Chronicles 28:11–12, 19 (the temple pattern given in writing).

5. New Testament — "before the foundation"

Before creation, Scripture shows five realities in God: the Father's love and glory shared with the Son, a people chosen in Christ, a Lamb foreordained, grace given in Christ, and a hidden wisdom ordained for our glory.

Glory and love before the world — John 17:5, 22–24

5 And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was.

22 And the glory which thou gavest me I have given them; that they may be one, even as we are one: 23 I in them, and thou in me, that they may be made perfect in one; and that the world may know that thou hast sent me, and hast loved them, as thou hast loved me. 24 Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me: for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world.

Meaning: The foundation beneath the foundation: love within the Godhead. Before anything existed, the Father loved (ēgapēsas) the Son and shared glory (doxa) with Him. The blueprint is that this love and glory be extended to believers: "loved them, as thou hast loved me" (v. 23) and "be with me where I am" (v. 24).

Chosen in Him — Ephesians 1:3–6, 9–11

3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ: 4 According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love: 5 Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will, 6 To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.

9 Having made known unto us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he hath purposed in himself: 10 That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him: 11 In whom also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestinated according to the purpose of him who worketh all things after the counsel of his own will:

Meaning: The New Testament's master blueprint text. The choice is:

  • In Him — election is located in Christ, not apart from Him.
  • For a goal — "holy and without blame before him": character fit for His presence.
  • In love — the motive, matching Deuteronomy 7:8.
  • Unto sonship — "adoption of children" (huiothesia, placing as a son).
  • To His glory — "the praise of the glory of his grace."

Verse 10's "gather together in one" (anakephalaiōsasthai, to sum up under one head) states the final purpose: all things headed up in Christ. "Dispensation" (oikonomia) is household management — God's plan for running His house. Interpretive note: Reformed readers see individual, unconditional election here; Arminian readers see election of a people in Christ, with individuals included through faith. Both agree it is in Christ and by grace.

The Lamb foreordained — 1 Peter 1:18–21

18 Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; 19 But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot: 20 Who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you, 21 Who by him do believe in God, that raised him up from the dead, and gave him glory; that your faith and hope might be in God.

Meaning: "Foreordained" is proegnōsmenou, foreknown. Redemption was not God's response to an unexpected fall; the Lamb was in view before the world. Note the two-stage pattern: foreknown before, "manifest" (phanerōthentos) in the last times.

Grace given before time — 2 Timothy 1:9–10

9 Who hath saved us, and called us with an holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, which was given us in Christ Jesus before the world began, 10 But is now made manifest by the appearing of our Saviour Jesus Christ, who hath abolished death, and hath brought life and immortality to light through the gospel:

Meaning: Grace was "given" (aorist, a completed act) in Christ "before times eternal" (pro chronōn aiōniōn). Salvation rests on God's purpose (prothesis), not our works. Same pattern: given before, manifest now.

Life promised before time — Titus 1:1–3

1 Paul, a servant of God, and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of God's elect, and the acknowledging of the truth which is after godliness; 2 In hope of eternal life, which God, that cannot lie, promised before the world began; 3 But hath in due times manifested his word through preaching, which is committed unto me according to the commandment of God our Saviour;

Meaning: Before any human existed to receive it, God promised eternal life — a promise within the Godhead, Father to Son. "Cannot lie" (apseudēs) guarantees the blueprint. This links directly to the earlier study, The Life of God.

Hidden wisdom ordained for our glory — 1 Corinthians 2:7–9

7 But we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, even the hidden wisdom, which God ordained before the world unto our glory: 8 Which none of the princes of this world knew: for had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. 9 But as it is written, Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared for them that love him.

Meaning: "Ordained" is proōrisen, predestined. "Before the world" is pro tōn aiōnōn, before the ages. The goal is "our glory" — the glory of John 17:22. Verse 9 quotes Isaiah 64:4, linking the Prophets to the blueprint.

The golden chain — Romans 8:28–30

28 And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose. 29 For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. 30 Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified.

Meaning: Five links run from eternity to eternity: foreknew → predestinated → called → justified → glorified. The goal is "conformed to the image" (symmorphous tēs eikonos) of the Son — Genesis 1:26's image restored and completed — and a family, with Christ as "firstborn among many brethren." "Glorified" is in the past tense: in God's sight, it is already done.

The eternal purpose and the mystery — Ephesians 3:9–11; Romans 16:25–26; Colossians 1:15–17

Ephesians 3:9 And to make all men see what is the fellowship of the mystery, which from the beginning of the world hath been hid in God, who created all things by Jesus Christ: 10 To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly places might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God, 11 According to the eternal purpose which he purposed in Christ Jesus our Lord:

Romans 16:25 Now to him that is of power to stablish you according to my gospel, and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began, 26 But now is made manifest, and by the scriptures of the prophets, according to the commandment of the everlasting God, made known to all nations for the obedience of faith:

Colossians 1:15 Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature: 16 For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: 17 And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.

Meaning: "Eternal purpose" is prothesin tōn aiōnōn, the purpose of the ages. The church is the means of displaying God's "manifold" (polypoikilos, many-colored) wisdom. Colossians gives the blueprint's three prepositions: all things created by Him (agent), for Him (goal), and held together in Him ("consist," synestēken).

6. New Testament — "from the foundation"

The seven apo katabolēs passages show what God built into the world from its first day: hidden truths, a prepared kingdom, a finished rest, the sacrifice in view, and a book of life — along with the accountability that runs through history.

Things kept secret — Matthew 13:34–35

34 All these things spake Jesus unto the multitude in parables; and without a parable spake he not unto them: 35 That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world.

Meaning: Quoting Psalm 78:2 (the Writings). "Kept secret" is kekrymmena, hidden. Jesus' parables of the kingdom reveal what was built into God's plan from the start.

The kingdom prepared — Matthew 25:31–34

31 When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory: 32 And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as a shepherd divideth his sheep from the goats: 33 And he shall set the sheep on his right hand, but the goats on the left. 34 Then shall the King say unto them on his right hand, Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world:

Meaning: "Prepared" (hētoimasmenēn, perfect participle) means made ready and remaining ready. The inheritance existed before the heirs. Compare verse 41: the fire was "prepared for the devil and his angels," not for people — God's design for humanity was the kingdom.

The works finished — Hebrews 4:3–4

3 For we which have believed do enter into rest, as he said, As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest: although the works were finished from the foundation of the world. 4 For he spake in a certain place of the seventh day on this wise, And God did rest the seventh day from all his works.

Meaning: God's rest began after creation (Genesis 2:2) and has stayed open ever since. Faith does not create the rest; it enters a rest already prepared. The Sabbath is a pattern of the blueprint: work completed, then enjoyed together.

Once, at the end of the ages — Hebrews 9:24–26

24 For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us: 25 Nor yet that he should offer himself often, as the high priest entereth into the holy place every year with blood of others; 26 For then must he often have suffered since the foundation of the world: but now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.

Meaning: "Figures" (antitypa) are copies of the true heavenly sanctuary, the original from Exodus 25:40. Christ's single sacrifice (hapax, once for all) is sufficient for every generation since the foundation of the world.

The Lamb slain and the book of life — Revelation 13:8; 17:8

13:8 And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.

17:8 The beast that thou sawest was, and is not; and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit, and go into perdition: and they that dwell on the earth shall wonder, whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, when they behold the beast that was, and is not, and yet is.

Meaning: The book of life belongs to the Lamb. In 13:8 the Greek allows two readings: "the Lamb slain from the foundation" (KJV) or "names not written from the foundation" (ESV, NASB, based on 17:8). Both are true elsewhere: the Lamb was foreknown before the foundation (1 Peter 1:20), and names are written in His book. The book traces back to Exodus 32:32 and Daniel 12:1.

Accountability since the foundation — Luke 11:49–51

49 Therefore also said the wisdom of God, I will send them prophets and apostles, and some of them they shall slay and persecute: 50 That the blood of all the prophets, which was shed from the foundation of the world, may be required of this generation; 51 From the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias, which perished between the altar and the temple: verily I say unto you, It shall be required of this generation.

Meaning: The other side of the blueprint: opposition to God's messengers has run from Abel (first book of the Hebrew canon) to Zechariah (2 Chronicles 24, the last book of the Hebrew canon). Jesus' phrase spans the whole Tanakh — Torah through Writings.

7. Patterns across the canon

Eight patterns run through all four divisions. Each one is seeded in the Torah, announced in the Prophets, made personal in the Writings, and fulfilled in the New Testament.

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Purpose comes before building Pattern shown first, then tabernacle built (Exodus 25:9, 40) End declared from the beginning (Isaiah 46:10) Counsel stands forever (Psalm 33:11); temple pattern in writing (1 Chronicles 28:19) Eternal purpose in Christ (Ephesians 1:11; 3:11); copy of the heavenly (Hebrews 8:5)
2. The Son / Wisdom with God before creation God speaks and the Spirit moves (Genesis 1:1–3) Goings forth from everlasting (Micah 5:2) Wisdom daily His delight (Proverbs 8:22–31) Glory and love before the world (John 17:5, 24); before all things (Colossians 1:17)
3. The Lamb provided before the need God will provide Himself a lamb (Genesis 22:8); lamb set apart early (Exodus 12:3–6) Lamb to the slaughter; it pleased the LORD (Isaiah 53:7, 10) Sacrifice and offering not desired; "in the volume of the book it is written of me" (Psalm 40:6–7) Foreordained before the foundation (1 Peter 1:20); slain from the foundation (Revelation 13:8)
4. Chosen in love, not merit Chosen because the LORD loved you (Deuteronomy 7:6–8) Known before formed (Jeremiah 1:5); everlasting love (Jeremiah 31:3) Days written before they came (Psalm 139:16) Chosen in Him, in love (Ephesians 1:4–5); not according to works (2 Timothy 1:9)
5. Hidden, then revealed Seed promised in a riddle (Genesis 3:15) Eye hath not seen (Isaiah 64:4) Glory of God to conceal (Proverbs 25:2); eternity in the heart (Ecclesiastes 3:11) Mystery kept secret, now manifest (Romans 16:25–26; 1 Corinthians 2:7; Matthew 13:35)
6. Finished work, open rest God rested the seventh day (Genesis 2:1–3) My counsel shall stand (Isaiah 46:10) Nothing can be added or taken (Ecclesiastes 3:14–15) Works finished from the foundation; enter by faith (Hebrews 4:3)
7. A kingdom and a dwelling prepared Dominion given to man (Genesis 1:26–28); "that I may dwell among them" (Exodus 25:8) Formed to be inhabited (Isaiah 45:18) Kingdom given to the Son of man and saints (Daniel 7:14, 27); "our dwelling place" (Psalm 90:1) Kingdom prepared from the foundation (Matthew 25:34)
8. Names in God's book "Blot me … out of thy book" (Exodus 32:32–33) Book of remembrance (Malachi 3:16) Book of the living (Psalm 69:28); found written in the book (Daniel 12:1) Lamb's book of life (Revelation 13:8; 17:8; 21:27)

A ninth pattern runs through every line of the table: before → manifest. What is purposed in eternity is revealed in time (1 Peter 1:20; 2 Timothy 1:9–10; Titus 1:2–3; Romans 16:25–26). This gives the blueprint its shape: eternal counsel, historical execution, eternal consummation.

8. Principles

The patterns yield nine principles. Each one states what is true about God's blueprint and why it matters.

  1. Love is the foundation beneath the foundation. Before God made anything, the Father loved the Son (John 17:24). Every other part of the blueprint comes from that love and aims to share it (John 17:23, 26).
  2. Purpose comes before creation and governs history. God does not react; He carries out a counsel already settled (Isaiah 46:10; Ephesians 1:11). History is the working out of a plan, not a series of accidents.
  3. Christ is the center, agent, and goal. All things were made by Him, for Him, and are summed up in Him (Colossians 1:16–17; Ephesians 1:10). Any reading of the blueprint that is not Christ-centered misreads it.
  4. Redemption was not plan B. The Lamb was foreknown before the foundation (1 Peter 1:20). The fall did not surprise God, and the cross was within His purpose (Isaiah 53:10; Acts 2:23).
  5. Election is in Christ, by grace, for a purpose. God's choosing is located "in him," motivated by love, and aimed at holiness, sonship, and His glory (Ephesians 1:4–6; Deuteronomy 7:7–8). It is never based on merit (2 Timothy 1:9).
  6. The goal is image, family, and dwelling. God is reproducing His Son's image in many brothers and sisters (Romans 8:29) and making a home where He and His people dwell together (Exodus 25:8; Revelation 21:3).
  7. What God purposed is finished in His sight. The works were finished from the foundation (Hebrews 4:3); the glorified are spoken of in the past tense (Romans 8:30). Believers enter and receive rather than achieve.
  8. Revelation is progressive. The blueprint was hidden in God, seeded in the Torah, announced by the Prophets, sung in the Writings, and revealed in Christ and the church (Romans 16:25–26; Ephesians 3:9–10).
  9. Building must follow the pattern. What is built for God must match what God showed (Exodus 25:40; Hebrews 8:5; 1 Corinthians 3:10–11). Human ideas, however sincere, do not replace the pattern.

A balancing note: Scripture holds God's eternal purpose together with real human responsibility (Luke 11:50; Revelation 22:17). Christian traditions weigh these differently, but each affirms both.

9. Practices

Each principle becomes a concrete practice for personal life, the church, and teaching.

Principle Practice Anchor text
1. Love is the foundation Begin prayer by receiving the Father's love before asking for anything. Pray John 17:23 in the first person: "You have loved me as You loved Jesus." John 17:23–26
2. Purpose before creation Weekly, review life events against God's purpose: "How is this serving conformity to Christ?" Keep a journal of providences. Romans 8:28–29
3. Christ the center Read each Bible passage asking, "Where is Christ in this?" Test teaching and plans by whether they exalt Him. Colossians 1:16–18; Luke 24:27
4. Redemption not plan B In seasons of failure or crisis, rehearse that the Lamb was provided before the need. Practice thanksgiving for the cross at communion. 1 Peter 1:18–21; Genesis 22:14
5. Chosen in Christ Read Ephesians 1:3–14 aloud daily for a week, personalizing "us." Reject merit-based identity and performance anxiety. Ephesians 1:3–6
6. Image, family, dwelling Choose one Christlike trait to pursue each month. Practice hospitality and family life in the church as the blueprint in miniature. Romans 8:29; Ephesians 2:19–22
7. Finished in His sight Keep a weekly Sabbath rest as a confession that God's work is finished. Pray from a finished work, not toward one. Hebrews 4:3, 9–11
8. Progressive revelation Trace one theme each month through Torah, Prophets, Writings, and New Testament using this document's structure. Romans 16:25–26; Luke 24:44
9. Build by the pattern Before starting a ministry, program, or teaching series, write down the Scripture pattern it follows. Review it with others. Exodus 25:40; 1 Corinthians 3:10–11

For a teaching series: Each principle can be a session — the reading from the four divisions, the pattern, the principle, then one practice assigned for the week.
