The Counsel and Will of God

Overview

God's counsel is the deliberate, wise decision-making by which He carries out His eternal purpose; His will is what He wants done. God "worketh all things after the counsel of his own will" (Ephesians 1:11). If the eternal purpose (theme 1) is what God intends, His counsel and will are how He decides and directs it.

Scripture uses these words in two ways that must be held together:

  • God's sovereign counsel — what He has determined will happen. It cannot be overturned: "My counsel shall stand" (Isaiah 46:10); Christ was "delivered by the determinate counsel … of God" (Acts 2:23).
  • God's revealed will — what He commands and desires of people. It can be resisted, to our own loss: the Pharisees "rejected the counsel of God against themselves" (Luke 7:30).

Theologians call these the decretive and preceptive will; traditions weigh them differently, but all affirm both.

Three further threads run through the study: God takes no counsel from anyone (Isaiah 40:13–14); He shares His counsel with His friends (Genesis 18:17; John 15:15); and Christ is both the Wonderful Counsellor (Isaiah 9:6) and the One who perfectly did the Father's will (John 6:38). Passages are given in full from the King James Version.

1. Word study

Hebrew and Greek distinguish counsel (a settled plan), will (a desire), pleasure (delight), and secret counsel (an inner circle God invites people into).

Two semantic insights carry the study:

  1. Boulē and thelēma. Boulē tends toward God's fixed decision; thelēma toward His desire. Acts 4:28 uses boulē for what could not fail; Matthew 7:21 uses thelēma for what people must do.
  2. Sod. The word pictures a circle of close friends sitting together. To "stand in the counsel (sod) of the LORD" (Jeremiah 23:18) means being admitted to His confidence — the Old Testament form of John 15:15.

2. Torah

The Torah shows God's counsel as personal and steady: He reveals His plans to a friend, steers events behind the scenes, does not change His word, keeps some things secret while revealing what we must obey, and answers the prayer "shew me thy way."

Counsel shared with a friend — Genesis 18:17–19

17 And the LORD said, Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do; 18 Seeing that Abraham shall surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him? 19 For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.

Meaning: God deliberates aloud and decides to include Abraham in His plans for Sodom. Abraham is called God's "friend" (2 Chronicles 20:7; James 2:23). The reason given is the purpose: Abraham will teach his household "the way of the LORD." Sharing counsel serves the larger plan.

It was not you, but God — Genesis 45:5–8

5 Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life. 6 For these two years hath the famine been in the land: and yet there are five years, in the which there shall neither be earing nor harvest. 7 And God sent me before you to preserve you a posterity in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance. 8 So now it was not you that sent me hither, but God: and he hath made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.

Meaning: Three times Joseph says God "sent" (shalach) him. The brothers acted freely and wrongly, yet God's counsel was the deeper cause. "Preserve you a posterity" ties the event to the Seed promise (Genesis 3:15; 12:3).

Shew me thy way — Exodus 33:12–14

12 And Moses said unto the LORD, See, thou sayest unto me, Bring up this people: and thou hast not let me know whom thou wilt send with me. Yet thou hast said, I know thee by name, and thou hast also found grace in my sight. 13 Now therefore, I pray thee, if I have found grace in thy sight, shew me now thy way, that I may know thee, that I may find grace in thy sight: and consider that this nation is thy people. 14 And he said, My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.

Meaning: Moses asks to know God's "way" (derek) — His manner of working — so that he may know God Himself. Psalm 103:7 later contrasts the two: "He made known his ways unto Moses, his acts unto the children of Israel." Knowing God's counsel is relational, and His answer is His presence.

God does not lie or change — Numbers 23:19–20

19 God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good? 20 Behold, I have received commandment to bless: and he hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it.

Meaning: Spoken by Balaam, a hired opponent, who could not curse those God had blessed. "Repent" (nacham) here means change His mind about a settled decision. God's word and His deed match; His counsel cannot be reversed by any outside power.

Secret things and revealed things — Deuteronomy 29:29

29 The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.

Meaning: The key verse for handling God's will. Some of His counsel is hidden (nistarot); some is revealed (niglot). The hidden part is His business; the revealed part is ours, and it is given to be done, not just known.

Refusing to go up — Deuteronomy 1:26, 32

26 Notwithstanding ye would not go up, but rebelled against the commandment of the LORD your God:

32 Yet in this thing ye did not believe the LORD your God,

Meaning: The other side. Israel refused God's revealed will at Kadesh and lost a generation. God's purpose went on through their children, but that generation forfeited its part. Unbelief is the root (v. 32).

3. Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets show God taking counsel from no one, frustrating human plots, sending a Messiah who embodies counsel, and admitting His prophets into His secret council.

Not a man, that He should repent — 1 Samuel 15:29

29 And also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent: for he is not a man, that he should repent.

Meaning: Repeats Numbers 23:19 in the Former Prophets. Saul's rejection would stand. God's settled counsel is as reliable as His character, "the Strength (netsach, eternal one) of Israel."

Take counsel together — Isaiah 8:9–10

9 Associate yourselves, O ye people, and ye shall be broken in pieces; and give ear, all ye of far countries: gird yourselves, and ye shall be broken in pieces; gird yourselves, and ye shall be broken in pieces. 10 Take counsel together, and it shall come to nought; speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us.

Meaning: Human alliances and counsels against God's people fail. The reason is a name: "God is with us" — Immanu El, the same name given in Isaiah 7:14 and fulfilled in Matthew 1:23.

The Wonderful Counsellor — Isaiah 9:6–7

6 For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace. 7 Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom, to order it, and to establish it with judgment and with justice from henceforth even for ever. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will perform this.

Meaning: "Wonderful, Counsellor" (Pele Yo'ets) can be read as one title: a Counsellor whose counsel is beyond human understanding. The Messiah does not merely receive God's counsel; He gives it. "The zeal of the LORD … will perform this" — the plan is guaranteed.

The spirit of counsel — Isaiah 11:1–2

1 And there shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots: 2 And the spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD;

Meaning: "Counsel" (etsah) is paired with "might" (gevurah): wise planning joined with power to carry it out. The Spirit rests on the Branch so that God's counsel is perfectly expressed in a human life.

Wonderful in counsel — Isaiah 28:24–29

24 Doth the plowman plow all day to sow? doth he open and break the clods of his ground? 25 When he hath made plain the face thereof, doth he not cast abroad the fitches, and scatter the cummin, and cast in the principal wheat and the appointed barley and the rie in their place? 26 For his God doth instruct him to discretion, and doth teach him.

29 This also cometh forth from the LORD of hosts, which is wonderful in counsel, and excellent in working.

Meaning: A farming parable. A wise farmer does each task in its season and in the right measure. God's counsel is like that: timed, proportioned and purposeful, even when His people feel only the plowing.

Who hath been His counsellor? — Isaiah 40:12–14

12 Who hath measured the waters in the hollow of his hand, and meted out heaven with the span, and comprehended the dust of the earth in a measure, and weighed the mountains in scales, and the hills in a balance? 13 Who hath directed the Spirit of the LORD, or being his counsellor hath taught him? 14 With whom took he counsel, and who instructed him, and taught him in the path of judgment, and taught him knowledge, and shewed to him the way of understanding?

Meaning: God's counsel is underived. No one advised Him at creation. Paul quotes verse 13 in Romans 11:34 at the climax of his discussion of God's plan: "For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor?"

Standing in His counsel — Jeremiah 23:18, 21–22

18 For who hath stood in the counsel of the LORD, and hath perceived and heard his word? who hath marked his word, and heard it?

21 I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran: I have not spoken to them, yet they prophesied. 22 But if they had stood in my counsel, and had caused my people to hear my words, then they should have turned them from their evil way, and from the evil of their doings.

Meaning: "Counsel" here is sod, God's intimate council. True prophets have "stood" in it and heard; false prophets speak their own minds. The test of standing in God's counsel is fruit: people turned from evil.

His secret revealed — Amos 3:7

7 Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.

Meaning: Sod again. God's pattern is to disclose His plans before He acts. In the New Testament this extends to all believers (John 15:15; Ephesians 1:9).

4. Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings bring God's counsel into prayer and daily life: His secret is shared with those who fear Him, His will is a delight to do, His counsel guides step by step, and no human counsel can succeed against Him.

The secret of the LORD — Psalm 25:12–14

12 What man is he that feareth the LORD? him shall he teach in the way that he shall choose. 13 His soul shall dwell at ease; and his seed shall inherit the earth. 14 The secret of the LORD is with them that fear him; and he will shew them his covenant.

Meaning: "Secret" is sod, the intimate council. Access is not by rank but by reverence: "them that fear him." The content shared is His covenant — His committed plan.

I will guide thee with mine eye — Psalm 32:8–9

8 I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye. 9 Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.

Meaning: "Guide" is ya'ats, to counsel. God offers personal counsel, close enough to guide with a look. The warning: do not be the mule that must be forced. God prefers understanding cooperation.

I delight to do thy will — Psalm 40:6–8

6 Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire; mine ears hast thou opened: burnt offering and sin offering hast thou not required. 7 Then said I, Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me, 8 I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.

Meaning: "Will" is ratson, what pleases God. Hebrews 10:5–10 applies these words to Christ entering the world. He is the perfect doer of God's will, and the will itself is written "within my heart" — the new covenant pattern (Jeremiah 31:33).

Guided by Thy counsel — Psalm 73:23–26

23 Nevertheless I am continually with thee: thou hast holden me by my right hand. 24 Thou shalt guide me with thy counsel, and afterward receive me to glory. 25 Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee. 26 My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever.

Meaning: Written after Asaph nearly lost faith over the prosperity of the wicked. God's counsel (etsah) guides through confusion now, and "afterward" leads to glory. Counsel and companionship go together: "I am continually with thee."

Teach me to do Thy will — Psalm 143:8, 10

8 Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning; for in thee do I trust: cause me to know the way wherein I should walk; for I lift up my soul unto thee.

10 Teach me to do thy will; for thou art my God: thy spirit is good; lead me into the land of uprightness.

Meaning: A model prayer. David asks not only to know God's will but to be taught to do it, and he relies on God's good Spirit to lead.

No counsel against the LORD — Proverbs 19:21; 21:30–31

19:21 There are many devices in a man's heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.

21:30 There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the LORD. 21:31 The horse is prepared against the day of battle: but safety is of the LORD.

Meaning: Human "devices" (machashavot) are many; God's counsel is one, and it stands. Preparation is wise (the horse is made ready), but the outcome belongs to God.

Rejecting counsel — Proverbs 1:24–25, 30–31

24 Because I have called, and ye refused; I have stretched out my hand, and no man regarded; 25 But ye have set at nought all my counsel, and would none of my reproof:

30 They would none of my counsel: they despised all my reproof. 31 Therefore shall they eat of the fruit of their own way, and be filled with their own devices.

Meaning: Wisdom speaking. God's revealed counsel can be refused, and the result is to "eat of the fruit of their own way." The loss falls on the one who refuses.

He changeth times and seasons — Daniel 2:20–22

20 Daniel answered and said, Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever: for wisdom and might are his: 21 And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding: 22 He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the darkness, and the light dwelleth with him.

Meaning: Daniel's praise after God revealed the king's dream. Wisdom (counsel) and might (power to execute) belong together, as in Isaiah 11:2. God governs history and reveals His secrets to those who seek Him.

5. New Testament

In the New Testament, God's counsel reaches its center at the cross, Jesus models perfect submission to the Father's will, and believers are brought into the counsel as friends and called to prove God's will in daily life.

Thy will be done — Matthew 6:9–10

9 After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. 10 Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.

Meaning: The first requests of the Lord's Prayer align the one praying with God's purpose: His name, His kingdom, His will (thelēma). Heaven is the pattern; earth is where it is to be carried out — the tabernacle principle (Exodus 25:40) applied to all of life.

Not my will — Matthew 26:39, 42

39 And he went a little further, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.

42 He went away again the second time, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if this cup may not pass away from me, except I drink it, thy will be done.

Meaning: The deepest scene of God's will in Scripture. Jesus' human will is real and honestly expressed, yet freely surrendered. The second prayer quotes the Lord's Prayer word for word. The eternal counsel is accomplished through a human "yes."

The will of Him that sent me — John 6:38–40

38 For I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me. 39 And this is the Father's will which hath sent me, that of all which he hath given me I should lose nothing, but should raise it up again at the last day. 40 And this is the will of him that sent me, that every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, may have everlasting life: and I will raise him up at the last day.

Meaning: Jesus defines the Father's will in two parts: keep all given to Him (security) and give life to all who believe (salvation). The will of God here is life-giving and inclusive of "every one" who believes.

Friends, not servants — John 15:14–15

14 Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you. 15 Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you.

Meaning: The New Testament fulfilment of sod. What Abraham (Genesis 18:17) and the prophets (Amos 3:7) received, every disciple now receives: access to the Father's counsel through the Son.

The determinate counsel — Acts 2:22–24

22 Ye men of Israel, hear these words; Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved of God among you by miracles and wonders and signs, which God did by him in the midst of you, as ye yourselves also know: 23 Him, being delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, ye have taken, and by wicked hands have crucified and slain: 24 Whom God hath raised up, having loosed the pains of death: because it was not possible that he should be holden of it.

Meaning: "Determinate" is hōrismenē, bounded and fixed. Peter holds both truths in one sentence: the cross was God's set counsel, and those who crucified Jesus were guilty ("wicked hands"). God's counsel does not excuse human sin; it overrules it.

Whatsoever Thy counsel determined — Acts 4:24, 27–28

24 And when they heard that, they lifted up their voice to God with one accord, and said, Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all that in them is:

27 For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus, whom thou hast anointed, both Herod, and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles, and the people of Israel, were gathered together, 28 For to do whatsoever thy hand and thy counsel determined before to be done.

Meaning: The church prays Psalm 2 under persecution. The world's most powerful people "took counsel together" (Psalm 2:2), yet only accomplished what God's boulē had "determined before" (proōrisen). Confidence in God's counsel produced bold prayer, not passivity (Acts 4:29–31).

Rejected against themselves — Luke 7:29–30

29 And all the people that heard him, and the publicans, justified God, being baptized with the baptism of John. 30 But the Pharisees and lawyers rejected the counsel of God against themselves, being not baptized of him.

Meaning: The same word boulē, but here God's offered counsel for these people. It could be refused, and the loss was theirs ("against themselves"). This is the New Testament counterpart to Proverbs 1:25.

All the counsel of God — Acts 20:26–27

26 Wherefore I take you to record this day, that I am pure from the blood of all men. 27 For I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God.

Meaning: Paul's ministry standard: the whole plan of God, not selected parts. Teaching the full counsel is part of faithfulness.

Proving the will of God — Romans 12:1–2

1 I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service. 2 And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.

Meaning: "Prove" (dokimazō) means test and approve by experience. God's will is discovered by a surrendered life and a renewed mind, not by a formula. It is described three ways: good, acceptable, perfect.

The immutability of His counsel — Hebrews 6:17–19

17 Wherein God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath: 18 That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us: 19 Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast, and which entereth into that within the veil;

Meaning: God added an oath to His promise to Abraham so that believers would be certain His counsel (boulē) cannot change. Unchangeable counsel becomes an anchor for the soul.

Filled with the knowledge of His will — Colossians 1:9–10

9 For this cause we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to desire that ye might be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding; 10 That ye might walk worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God;

Meaning: Paul's prayer for believers. Knowing God's will leads to a worthy walk, good works, and growing knowledge of God Himself.

The will of God for you — 1 Thessalonians 4:3; 5:16–18; 1 John 2:17

1 Thessalonians 4:3 For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication:

1 Thessalonians 5:16 Rejoice evermore. 17 Pray without ceasing. 18 In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.

1 John 2:17 And the world passeth away, and the lust thereof: but he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever.

Meaning: Much of God's will is plainly stated: holiness, joy, prayer, thanksgiving. Doing it connects a passing life to what lasts forever.

6. Patterns across the canon

Seven patterns recur in all four divisions.

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. God's counsel is underived and unchanging "God is not a man, that he should lie" (Numbers 23:19) "Who … being his counsellor hath taught him?" (Isaiah 40:13); "will not lie nor repent" (1 Samuel 15:29) "The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever" (Psalm 33:11) "The immutability of his counsel" (Hebrews 6:17); "who hath been his counsellor?" (Romans 11:34)
2. God's counsel overrules human counsel "It was not you … but God" (Genesis 45:8) "Take counsel together, and it shall come to nought" (Isaiah 8:10) "No … counsel against the LORD" (Proverbs 21:30) Rulers did "whatsoever thy … counsel determined" (Acts 4:28)
3. God shares His counsel with friends "Shall I hide from Abraham?" (Genesis 18:17) "He revealeth his secret unto … the prophets" (Amos 3:7); "stood in my counsel" (Jeremiah 23:22) "The secret of the LORD is with them that fear him" (Psalm 25:14) "I have called you friends" (John 15:15)
4. Secret things and revealed things "The secret things belong unto the LORD" (Deuteronomy 29:29) "He revealeth his secret" (Amos 3:7) "He revealeth the deep and secret things" (Daniel 2:22) "The mystery of his will" made known (Ephesians 1:9)
5. Counsel embodied in the Messiah "With him will I speak mouth to mouth" — Moses as mediator (Numbers 12:8) "Wonderful, Counsellor" (Isaiah 9:6); "the spirit of counsel" (Isaiah 11:2) "I delight to do thy will" (Psalm 40:8) "Not as I will, but as thou wilt" (Matthew 26:39); "not to do mine own will" (John 6:38)
6. Personal guidance for those who ask "Shew me now thy way" (Exodus 33:13) "This is the way, walk ye in it" (Isaiah 30:21) "I will guide thee with mine eye" (Psalm 32:8); "guide me with thy counsel" (Psalm 73:24) "Prove what is that good … will of God" (Romans 12:2); "filled with the knowledge of his will" (Colossians 1:9)
7. Revealed counsel can be refused, to one's own loss "Ye would not go up" (Deuteronomy 1:26) False prophets did not stand in the counsel (Jeremiah 23:21–22) "Ye have set at nought all my counsel" (Proverbs 1:25) "Rejected the counsel of God against themselves" (Luke 7:30)

Patterns 1–2 describe God's sovereign counsel; patterns 6–7 describe His revealed will. Patterns 3–5 bridge them: God brings people into His counsel, supremely through Christ.

7. Principles

The patterns yield eight principles about God's counsel and will.

  1. God's counsel is complete in Himself. No one advises or corrects Him (Isaiah 40:13–14). His plans are wise beyond our understanding — "wonderful in counsel" (Isaiah 28:29).
  2. His sovereign counsel cannot be overturned. Human opposition, even the crucifixion, ends up serving what He determined (Acts 2:23; 4:28). This gives believers an anchor (Hebrews 6:19).
  3. God's counsel never excuses human sin. Joseph's brothers and Jesus' crucifiers were responsible for their acts (Genesis 50:20; Acts 2:23). Sovereignty and responsibility stand side by side.
  4. His revealed will can be resisted, at our own cost. "Rejected the counsel of God against themselves" (Luke 7:30). Refusal does not stop God's plan; it forfeits our share in it.
  5. The secret belongs to God; the revealed is for obedience. Do not try to read hidden counsel; do what is plainly revealed (Deuteronomy 29:29).
  6. God invites His friends into His counsel. Access comes through reverence and relationship (Psalm 25:14) and is now given to every disciple through Christ (John 15:15).
  7. Christ is both Counsellor and perfect doer of God's will. He gives counsel (Isaiah 9:6) and submitted His own will to the Father's (Matthew 26:39). He is the model and the means.
  8. Much of God's will is already plain and good. Sanctification, thanksgiving, prayer and a renewed mind (1 Thessalonians 4:3; 5:18; Romans 12:2). Doing it leads to what lasts (1 John 2:17).

A balancing note: traditions differ on how God's sovereign decree relates to human choice — Reformed, Arminian, Lutheran and others frame it differently. This study keeps to what the texts affirm together.

8. Practices

Principle Practice Anchor text
1. Counsel complete in God Before big decisions, read Isaiah 40:12–31 and list what it says about God's wisdom before listing your options. Isaiah 40:13–14
2. Sovereign counsel stands Under pressure or opposition, pray Acts 4:24–30: start with God as Creator, name the threat, ask for boldness. Acts 4:27–31
3. Responsibility remains When you wrong someone, own it fully without hiding behind "God's plan." When wronged, trust God's plan without excusing the wrong. Genesis 50:19–21
4. Revealed will can be refused Weekly, ask: "Is there anything God has plainly shown me that I am putting off?" Act on one item. Luke 7:30; Deuteronomy 1:26
5. Obey what is revealed When unsure of God's specific will, first do the plain things: holiness, prayer, thanksgiving, love. Clarity often follows obedience. Deuteronomy 29:29
6. Invited into His counsel Spend unhurried time in Scripture and prayer as a friend listening, not only a servant receiving orders. Keep notes of what you sense. Psalm 25:14; John 15:15
7. Christ's surrender Pray Gethsemane's words in your hardest choice: state honestly what you want, then add "not as I will, but as thou wilt." Matthew 26:39
8. Prove God's will Practice Romans 12:1–2: offer your body, limit worldly shaping, renew your mind daily with Scripture, then test decisions by what is good, acceptable and perfect. Romans 12:1–2

For a teaching session: Contrast Acts 4:28 with Luke 7:30 — the same word boulē used for counsel that cannot fail and counsel that was refused. Ask the group what each teaches, then close by praying Matthew 6:10 together.
