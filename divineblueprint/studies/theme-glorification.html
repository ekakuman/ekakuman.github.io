Glorification

Overview

Glorification is the last link in the golden chain of Romans 8:29–30: "whom he justified, them he also glorified." Paul uses the past tense because the outcome is already certain in God's purpose. It is the completion of everything the Blueprint set out to do. Humanity, made in God's image and crowned with glory (Psalm 8:5), fell short of that glory (Romans 3:23). In Christ, believers are restored to it and raised beyond it: conformed to the image of the Son, given resurrection bodies like His glorious body, sharing His glory forever. The "Lord of glory" (1 Corinthians 2:8) brings "many sons unto glory" (Hebrews 2:10).

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. Crowned, then fallen — made for glory; Moses' shining face as a fading foretaste
  2. Hope of resurrection — "thy dead men shall live"; "they that be wise shall shine"
  3. Glory beheld in Christ — the Transfiguration, and the Son's prayer to share His glory
  4. Being changed from glory to glory — the Spirit's present work of transformation
  5. Glorified together — the resurrection body; "we shall be like him"

Semantic note: Kabod first means "weight." Paul's "eternal weight of glory" (2 Corinthians 4:17) is a deliberate Hebrew wordplay. The same verb, metamorphoō, describes Jesus' Transfiguration and the believer's present change. What was seen on the mountain is what the Spirit is doing in us now.

Torah

In the image of God — Genesis 1:26–27

26 And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth. 27 So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.

Meaning: Glorification is the image (theme 15) brought to completion. What began in Eden finishes in being "conformed to the image of his Son" (Romans 8:29).

God took him — Genesis 5:24

24 And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him.

Meaning: In the middle of a chapter where every life ends "and he died," Enoch is taken alive. It is an early sign that death is not God's final word (Hebrews 11:5).

Shew me Thy glory — Exodus 33:18–20

18 And he said, I beseech thee, shew me thy glory. 19 And he said, I will make all my goodness pass before thee, and I will proclaim the name of the LORD before thee; and will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy. 20 And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.

Meaning: Moses longs to see God's glory but cannot see His face and live. In glory, God's servants "shall see his face" (Revelation 22:4). Glorification makes us able to bear it.

The skin of his face shone — Exodus 34:29–30

29 And it came to pass, when Moses came down from mount Sinai with the two tables of testimony in Moses' hand, when he came down from the mount, that Moses wist not that the skin of his face shone while he talked with him. 30 And when Aaron and all the children of Israel saw Moses, behold, the skin of his face shone; and they were afraid to come nigh him.

Meaning: Time spent with God left Moses shining. Paul contrasts this fading glory with the lasting, increasing glory of the new covenant (2 Corinthians 3:7–18).

The LORD make His face shine upon thee — Numbers 6:24–26

24 The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: 25 The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: 26 The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.

Meaning: The priestly blessing is a prayer for God's glory-light to rest on His people. Glorification is that blessing fulfilled forever.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Elijah went up — 2 Kings 2:11

11 And it came to pass, as they still went on, and talked, that, behold, there appeared a chariot of fire, and horses of fire, and parted them both asunder; and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.

Meaning: A second person taken without death. Elijah appears again in glory with Moses at the Transfiguration (Matthew 17:3).

Thy dead men shall live — Isaiah 25:8; 26:19

25:8 He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the LORD hath spoken it.

26:19 Thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust: for thy dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth shall cast out the dead.

Meaning: Bodily resurrection is clearly promised. Paul quotes 25:8 at the climax of his resurrection chapter (1 Corinthians 15:54).

O death, I will be thy plagues — Hosea 13:14

14 I will ransom them from the power of the grave; I will redeem them from death: O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave, I will be thy destruction: repentance shall be hid from mine eyes.

Meaning: God ransoms His people from the grave itself. Paul's "O death, where is thy sting?" (1 Corinthians 15:55) draws from this.

Arise, shine; thy God thy glory — Isaiah 60:1–3, 19

1 Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the LORD is risen upon thee. 2 For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the LORD shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee. 3 And the Gentiles shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising.

19 The sun shall be no more thy light by day; neither for brightness shall the moon give light unto thee: but the LORD shall be unto thee an everlasting light, and thy God thy glory.

Meaning: God's glory rises upon His people and is "seen upon" them. In the end God Himself is their glory, which Revelation 21:23 fulfils.

The Sun of righteousness — Malachi 4:2

2 But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings; and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall.

Meaning: The last prophetic book ends with a rising Sun bringing healing and joyful freedom. This is a dawn picture of resurrection glory.

Writings (Ketuvim)

Crowned with glory and honour — Psalm 8:4–5

4 What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him? 5 For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour.

Meaning: Humanity's original dignity. Hebrews 2:6–10 applies it to Jesus, who is "crowned with glory" so that He might bring "many sons unto glory."

In my flesh shall I see God — Job 19:25–27

25 For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth: 26 And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God: 27 Whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another; though my reins be consumed within me.

Meaning: In deep suffering Job confesses a living Redeemer and a bodily vision of God after death. Glorification includes the body.

Satisfied with Thy likeness — Psalm 16:10–11; 17:15

16:10 For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption. 11 Thou wilt shew me the path of life: in thy presence is fulness of joy; at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore.

17:15 As for me, I will behold thy face in righteousness: I shall be satisfied, when I awake, with thy likeness.

Meaning: Christ's resurrection fulfils Psalm 16 (Acts 2:27–31). Psalm 17:15 joins seeing God's face with waking into His likeness, which 1 John 3:2 echoes.

Afterward receive me to glory — Psalm 73:24

24 Thou shalt guide me with thy counsel, and afterward receive me to glory.

Meaning: God's counsel (theme 2) guides the journey, and glory is where it ends.

Shining more and more — Proverbs 4:18

18 But the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day.

Meaning: A Wisdom picture of progressive glory, from dawn to full day (2 Corinthians 3:18).

They that be wise shall shine — Daniel 12:2–3

2 And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt. 3 And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament; and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.

Meaning: The clearest Old Testament promise of resurrection and radiance. Jesus echoes it: "Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun" (Matthew 13:43).

New Testament

He was transfigured — Matthew 17:1–3

1 And after six days Jesus taketh Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart, 2 And was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light. 3 And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.

Meaning: A preview of glory. Moses, whose face shone, and Elijah, who was taken up, stand with Christ in His glory, and the Torah and the Prophets meet in Him.

That they may behold My glory — John 17:22, 24

22 And the glory which thou gavest me I have given them; that they may be one, even as we are one:

24 Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me: for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world.

Meaning: Glorification flows from the love the Father had for the Son "before the foundation of the world." The series began there, and this is its goal.

Whom He justified, them He also glorified — Romans 8:17–18, 29–30

17 And if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; if so be that we suffer with him, that we may be also glorified together. 18 For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.

29 For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. 30 Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified.

Meaning: The golden chain runs from foreknowledge to glory, with no link missing. "Glorified" is past tense: in God's purpose, it is already done.

From glory to glory — 2 Corinthians 3:18; 4:16–18

3:18 But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.

4:16 For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day. 17 For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; 18 While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal.

Meaning: Unlike Moses' veiled and fading glory, believers are being transformed now, by beholding. Suffering produces glory instead of preventing it.

Raised in glory — 1 Corinthians 15:42–44, 51–54

42 So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption: 43 It is sown in dishonour; it is raised in glory: it is sown in weakness; it is raised in power: 44 It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body. There is a natural body, and there is a spiritual body.

51 Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, 52 In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed. 53 For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality. 54 So when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory.

Meaning: A seed sown and raised. The "spiritual body" is a real body fully empowered by the Spirit, not an immaterial one. Isaiah 25:8 is fulfilled.

Fashioned like unto His glorious body — Philippians 3:20–21; Colossians 3:4

Philippians 3:20 For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ: 21 Who shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorious body, according to the working whereby he is able even to subdue all things unto himself.

Colossians 3:4 When Christ, who is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory.

Meaning: The pattern for our glorified body is Christ's own risen body. The power that does it is the same power that subdues all things (theme 25).

We shall be like Him — 1 John 3:2–3

2 Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is. 3 And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure.

Meaning: Seeing Him transforms us into His likeness (Psalm 17:15). This hope makes us pure now.

They shall see His face — Revelation 22:4–5

4 And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads. 5 And there shall be no night there; and they need no candle, neither light of the sun; for the Lord God giveth them light: and they shall reign for ever and ever.

Meaning: Moses' request (Exodus 33:18) is granted to all God's servants. The priestly blessing (Numbers 6:25) is fulfilled. The Divine Blueprint ends in face-to-face glory.

Interpretive balance: Christians describe the result differently. Eastern Orthodox teaching speaks of theosis, "partakers of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4). Western traditions speak of glorification and the beatific vision. All mainstream traditions agree that the redeemed share God's glory as creatures. They are perfected and filled with God, never becoming God in essence.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Made for glory "In the image of God" (Genesis 1:27) "Thy God thy glory" (Isaiah 60:19) "Crowned him with glory and honour" (Psalm 8:5) "Bringing many sons unto glory" (Hebrews 2:10)
2. Death is not the last word "God took him" (Genesis 5:24) Elijah taken up (2 Kings 2:11); "thy dead men shall live" (Isaiah 26:19) "In my flesh shall I see God" (Job 19:26) "Death is swallowed up in victory" (1 Corinthians 15:54)
3. Beholding transforms Moses' face shone after speaking with God (Exodus 34:29) "Arise, shine; for thy light is come" (Isaiah 60:1) "Satisfied … with thy likeness" (Psalm 17:15) "Beholding … changed into the same image" (2 Corinthians 3:18)
4. Glory grows "His face shine upon thee" (Numbers 6:25) "The Sun of righteousness arise" (Malachi 4:2) "Shineth more and more unto the perfect day" (Proverbs 4:18) "From glory to glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18)
5. Suffering before glory Joseph, the pit, then the throne (Genesis 41:41) The Servant marred, then exalted (Isaiah 52:13–14) Job's affliction, then the vision (Job 19:25–27) "Light affliction … eternal weight of glory" (2 Corinthians 4:17; Romans 8:18)
6. Shining like the stars "So shall thy seed be" (Genesis 15:5) "The brightness of thy rising" (Isaiah 60:3) "Shine as the brightness of the firmament" (Daniel 12:3) "The righteous shine forth as the sun" (Matthew 13:43)
7. Seeing God's face "Thou canst not see my face" (Exodus 33:20) "Mine eyes have seen the King" (Isaiah 6:5) "I will behold thy face in righteousness" (Psalm 17:15) "They shall see his face" (Revelation 22:4); "we shall see him as he is" (1 John 3:2)

Principles

  1. Glorification completes God's purpose. The chain from foreknowledge to glory cannot break (Romans 8:29–30).
  2. We are glorified in union with Christ. His risen body is the pattern for ours (Philippians 3:21; Colossians 3:4).
  3. Glory includes the body. Resurrection, not escape from the body, is the Christian hope (1 Corinthians 15:42–44; Job 19:26).
  4. Glory has already begun. The Spirit transforms us now, by beholding Christ (2 Corinthians 3:18).
  5. Suffering is the road to glory. Present affliction produces lasting weight (Romans 8:17–18; 2 Corinthians 4:17).
  6. The goal is seeing God. To see His face is the highest joy and the final transformation (1 John 3:2; Revelation 22:4).
  7. Hope purifies. Those who expect to be like Him start becoming like Him now (1 John 3:3).

For a teaching session: Read Exodus 34:29–35, Matthew 17:1–3 and 2 Corinthians 3:18 together. Trace the line from Moses' face to Christ's face to ours. Ask: what are we beholding, and what is it making us into?

Series close: the Blueprint complete

The series began with the life of God and His purpose "before the foundation of the world." It ends with that purpose fulfilled. The Father's love for the Son "before the foundation of the world" (John 17:24) overflows into a people chosen in Him (Ephesians 1:4), redeemed by the Lamb (1 Peter 1:19–20), written in His book (Revelation 13:8), made heirs of His kingdom (Matthew 25:34), gathered under one Head (Ephesians 1:10), placed in a new creation (Revelation 21:5), and brought into His glory (Romans 8:30). "For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen." (Romans 11:36)
