The Knowledge of God

Introduction: the prayer of Ephesians 1:17

The knowledge of God is the personal, growing knowing of God that He Himself gives by revelation; it is the goal of His plan and the heart of eternal life (John 17:3).

"That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him: the eyes of your understanding being enlightened; that ye may know what is the hope of his calling, and what the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints, and what is the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe." (Eph 1:17–19)

The words. Paul uses epignōsis, a full, precise, experienced knowledge, not gnōsis alone. Behind it stands the Hebrew yada, the word for close covenant relationship: "Adam knew Eve his wife" (Gen 4:1). To know God in Scripture is to be joined to Him, not only to hold facts about Him.

Its place in the blueprint. Ephesians 1:3–14 lays out the plan made "before the foundation of the world" (1:4): the mystery of His will, to gather all things in one in Christ (1:9–10). Then 1:15–23 prays that believers would grasp that plan. God planned before the foundation; He executes the plan in history; and He reveals it so His people can know Him through it.

Torah: God makes Himself known by what He does

In the Torah, God takes the first step: He acts in history so that people will know Him.

  • God knows first. "For I know him [Abraham], that he will command his children" (Gen 18:19). His knowing is His choosing.
  • Knowing through redemption. Pharaoh says "I know not the LORD" (Ex 5:2). God answers with the plagues and the Exodus, "and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God" (Ex 6:7; also 7:5; 10:2; 29:46).
  • Moses' prayer. "Shew me now thy way, that I may know thee" (Ex 33:13). God answers "I know thee by name" (33:17), then proclaims His name and character: merciful, gracious, longsuffering, abundant in goodness and truth (34:6–7).
  • Remember to know. "Unto thee it was shewed, that thou mightest know that the LORD he is God" (Deut 4:35, 39). What God reveals belongs "unto us and to our children for ever" (Deut 29:29).
  • The warning. After Joshua "there arose another generation… which knew not the LORD" (Judg 2:10).

Prophets: losing the knowledge, and the promise of it

The prophets name the lack of the knowledge of God as the root of Israel's ruin, and promise a covenant in which all will know Him.

  • No knowledge, no life. "There is no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God in the land… My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (Hos 4:1, 6). Eli's sons "knew not the LORD" (1 Sam 2:12). Samuel "did not yet know the LORD" until "the LORD revealed himself to Samuel… by the word of the LORD" (1 Sam 3:7, 21).
  • More than ritual. "I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings" (Hos 6:6). "Then shall we know, if we follow on to know the LORD" (Hos 6:3).
  • The only true boast. "Let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me" (Jer 9:23–24). Paul quotes it twice (1 Cor 1:31; 2 Cor 10:17).
  • Knowing shows in justice. Of Josiah: "He judged the cause of the poor and needy… was not this to know me? saith the LORD" (Jer 22:16).
  • The new covenant. "They shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest" (Jer 31:34; quoted in Heb 8:11).
  • The Messiah's Spirit. "The spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding… the spirit of knowledge" (Isa 11:2). Paul's prayer for "the spirit of wisdom and revelation" echoes this: what rests on the Messiah is given to those in Him. "By his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many" (Isa 53:11).
  • The goal. "The earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea" (Isa 11:9; Hab 2:14). In Ezekiel, "ye shall know that I am the LORD" runs through the whole book as God's purpose in judgment and restoration.
  • Strength. "The people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits" (Dan 11:32).

Writings: seek it, and God gives it

The Writings teach that the knowledge of God is sought like treasure, yet received as a gift.

  • Where it starts. "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge" (Prov 1:7). "The knowledge of the holy is understanding" (Prov 9:10).
  • Seek and receive. "If thou seekest her as silver… then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God. For the LORD giveth wisdom" (Prov 2:4–6). This is why Paul prays for it.
  • Knowing leads to trust. "They that know thy name will put their trust in thee" (Ps 9:10). "Be still, and know that I am God" (Ps 46:10).
  • Known by Him. "O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me" (Ps 139:1).
  • From hearsay to sight. "I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee" (Job 42:5).
  • Knowing and serving. David to Solomon: "Know thou the God of thy father, and serve him with a perfect heart" (1 Chr 28:9).

Gospels: the Son reveals the Father

In the Gospels the promised knowledge arrives in a Person: to know Jesus is to know the Father, and that knowing is eternal life.

  • Revelation through the Son. "Neither knoweth any man the Father, save the Son, and he to whomsoever the Son will reveal him" (Matt 11:27).
  • Seen in Him. "He that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9).
  • Eternal life defined. "This is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent" (John 17:3).
  • Mutual knowing. "I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine" (John 10:14). The tragic reverse: "I never knew you" (Matt 7:23).

Paul's thinking: what he meant by "the knowledge of Him"

For Paul, the knowledge of God is God revealing Himself in the face of Christ, by the Spirit, to the heart. It grows across a lifetime until we know as we are known.

# Paul's idea Key texts
1 Source: a gift of the Spirit, not human achievement Eph 1:17; 1 Cor 2:9–12; Eph 3:3–5
2 Organ: the eyes of the heart, not intellect alone Eph 1:18; 1 Cor 8:1
3 Content: His purpose, His people, His power Eph 1:18–23
4 Center: God's glory in the face of Jesus Christ 2 Cor 4:6; Col 2:2–3
5 Foundation: we are known by God first Gal 4:9; 1 Cor 8:3
6 Paradox: knowing a love that surpasses knowledge Eph 3:17–19
7 Fruit: a worthy walk and a renewed image Col 1:9–10; Col 3:10
8 Growth: corporate maturity, now in part, then face to face Eph 4:13; 1 Cor 13:12
9 Paul's own story: from zeal without knowledge to "that I may know him" Rom 10:2; Acts 9:5; Phil 3:8–10

1. It is given, not achieved. Paul prays to "the Father of glory" to give it (Eph 1:17). "The Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God… that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God" (1 Cor 2:10–12). The mystery "was made known unto me by revelation" (Eph 3:3).

2. It reaches the heart. "The eyes of your understanding [heart] being enlightened" (Eph 1:18). Without love, knowledge "puffeth up" (1 Cor 8:1). The Gentiles walk "having the understanding darkened, being alienated from the life of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their heart" (Eph 4:18).

3. Its content is the blueprint itself. The prayer asks for three things:

  1. His purpose: "the hope of his calling", what He called us for before the foundation of the world (1:4).
  2. His people: "the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints", the church as God's treasured possession (1:11, 14).
  3. His power: "the exceeding greatness of his power", the same power that raised Christ, seated Him far above all, and made Him head over all things to the church (1:19–23).

4. Its center is Christ. "God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Cor 4:6). It echoes the light of Genesis 1:3 and Moses' shining face (Ex 34:29–35; 2 Cor 3). In Christ "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3).

5. It rests on being known. "Now, after that ye have known God, or rather are known of God" (Gal 4:9). "If any man love God, the same is known of him" (1 Cor 8:3). Our knowing answers His prior knowing, as in Gen 18:19 and Ex 33:17.

6. It is a paradox. Paul prays that we may "know the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge, that ye might be filled with all the fulness of God" (Eph 3:19). The goal is not to master God but to be filled by Him.

7. It changes how we live. "Filled with the knowledge of his will… that ye might walk worthy of the Lord… increasing in the knowledge of God" (Col 1:9–10). The new man "is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him" (Col 3:10), the Genesis 1:27 image restored. The opposite is Romans 1: "when they knew God, they glorified him not… they did not like to retain God in their knowledge" (1:21, 28).

8. It grows, together and toward the end. Paul prays for believers who already have faith and love (Eph 1:15). The church grows "till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man" (Eph 4:13). "Now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known" (1 Cor 13:12).

9. Paul lived it. He once had "a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge" (Rom 10:2). On the Damascus road his knowing began with a question: "Who art thou, Lord?" (Acts 9:5). Later he counted all things loss "for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord… that I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings" (Phil 3:8–10). He ends with confidence: "I know whom I have believed" (2 Tim 1:12).

Patterns, principles, practices

Pattern: from revelation to a full earth

  1. God knows first: He knows and chooses His people (Gen 18:19; Eph 1:4).
  2. God reveals: He makes Himself known by His acts and His word (Ex 6:7; 1 Sam 3:21).
  3. People respond: knowing Him leads to trust, obedience and justice (Ps 9:10; Jer 22:16).
  4. People fall away: a generation that "knew not the LORD" is destroyed for lack of knowledge (Judg 2:10; Hos 4:6).
  5. God promises: under a new covenant "they shall all know me" (Jer 31:34).
  6. Christ fulfils: the Son reveals the Father, and the Spirit opens the heart (Matt 11:27; Eph 1:17).
  7. The end: we know as we are known, and the earth is full of the knowledge of the LORD (1 Cor 13:12; Isa 11:9).

Principles

  • God always starts it: we know Him because He first knew us and revealed Himself.
  • It is personal, not only factual. It is covenant relationship (yada), centered on Christ.
  • True knowledge always shows in life: trust, obedience, love, justice. Knowing about God without glorifying Him is the knowledge Romans 1 condemns.
  • It is given and sought at the same time: God gives it, and we seek it as treasure (Prov 2).
  • It is eternal life now and its fullness later (John 17:3; 1 Cor 13:12).

Practices

  • Pray Ephesians 1:17–19 and 3:14–19 by name for family, the church and those you teach.
  • Read Scripture asking "What does this show me about who God is?", not only "What should I do?"
  • Remember and retell what God has done, as Israel was told to (Deut 4:9).
  • Measure growth by Colossians 1:9–10: is knowledge producing a walk worthy of the Lord?
  • Join knowing to doing: one act of justice or mercy this week (Jer 22:16).

Key verses and discussion questions

Memory verses: Ephesians 1:17–18 · John 17:3 · Jeremiah 9:23–24 · 2 Corinthians 4:6 · Philippians 3:10

  1. Why does Paul pray for this knowledge instead of simply teaching it?
  2. What is the difference between knowing about God and knowing God? Use Job 42:5.
  3. Which of the three (His purpose, His people, His power) do you most need your eyes opened to now?
  4. How does being known by God (Gal 4:9) change the way you pursue knowing Him?
  5. Jeremiah 22:16 links knowing God with caring for the poor. What does that say about our knowledge?
  6. How does this theme tie to eternal life (John 17:3) and to God's plan made before the foundation of the world (Eph 1:4)?
