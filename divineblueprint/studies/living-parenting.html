The Divine Blueprint and Parenting

Overview

God's plan moves through generations. He chose Abraham "that he will command his children and his household after him" (Genesis 18:19), and His mercy is "unto children's children" (Psalm 103:17). Parenting is one of the main ways the blueprint is handed on: faith told, shown and lived from one generation to the next. God is also the model parent: "Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him" (Psalm 103:13). Christian parents raise children as God's trust, on God's behalf, in the pattern of God's own fatherhood (theme 14, Sonship).

This study walks parenting and family through the five groups of the blueprint:

  • A · Purpose: why God gives children and family
  • B · Provision: what God supplies to parents
  • C · People: who God shapes, in children and in parents
  • D · Goal: what family life is heading toward
  • E · Consummation: how the end shapes parenting now

For every reader. This study is for parents, grandparents, step-parents, foster and adoptive parents, godparents, teachers and church family, and for adult children caring for ageing parents. Those without children are not outside this theme: God gives "a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters" (Isaiah 56:5).

Key passages

Teach them diligently — Deuteronomy 6:4–9

4 Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: 5 And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. 6 And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: 7 And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. 8 And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. 9 And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates.

Meaning:

  • vv. 4–5 come first: a parent's own love for God. Faith passed on begins as faith possessed.
  • v. 6 "in thine heart": the word must be in the parent before it can be taught to the child.
  • v. 7 "teach them diligently" (shanan, "to sharpen, impress") happens in four ordinary settings: sitting at home, walking, lying down and rising. Faith is taught in daily life, not only in formal lessons.
  • vv. 8–9 hands, eyes, doorposts, gates: faith shapes what we do, how we see, our home and our public life.

Bring them up in the nurture of the Lord — Ephesians 6:1–4

1 Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. 2 Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise; 3 That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth. 4 And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.

Meaning:

  • vv. 1–3 speak to children directly, so they are full members of the church. Obedience is "in the Lord," and honouring parents comes with a promise (Exodus 20:12).
  • v. 4 is the father's charge. Negatively: don't provoke, exasperate or crush. Positively: "nurture" (paideia, training and discipline) and "admonition" (nouthesia, instruction and correction in words), both "of the Lord." Parents train children in the Lord's ways and by the Lord's pattern. Colossians 3:21 adds the reason: "lest they be discouraged."

The five lenses

A · Purpose — why God gives children and family

He will command his children — Genesis 18:19

19 For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.

Meaning: Abraham's calling (theme 13, Election) includes his household. God's promise to bless the nations runs through parents teaching "the way of the LORD" and "justice and judgment" (theme 28).

The generation to come — Psalm 78:4–7

4 We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. 5 For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: 6 That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: 7 That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:

Meaning: Four generations in three verses: fathers, children, "the children which should be born," and their children. The aim is "that they might set their hope in God."

The unseen side. "For this cause I bow my knees unto the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, of whom the whole family in heaven and earth is named" (Ephesians 3:14–15). Earthly families take their name from an unseen Father. Parents are a visible picture of His fatherhood to their children, and Jesus warns that little ones are precious in heaven's sight (Matthew 18:10).

B · Provision — what God supplies to parents

The promise is unto your children — Acts 2:38–39

38 Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. 39 For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the LORD our God shall call.

Meaning: The covenant promise (theme 11) is extended to children. Parents can raise children inside God's promises, not outside them.

Like as a father pitieth his children — Psalm 103:13, 17

13 Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.

17 But the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that fear him, and his righteousness unto children's children;

Meaning: Parents learn compassion from God's compassion for them. His mercy (theme 9) reaches across generations.

Gently lead those that are with young — Isaiah 40:11; James 1:5

Isaiah 40:11 He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young.

James 1:5 If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.

Meaning: The Shepherd is especially gentle with those caring for little ones. Wisdom for parenting is promised to all who ask, without reproach.

C · People — who God shapes, in children and parents

From a child thou hast known the holy scriptures — 2 Timothy 1:5; 3:14–15

1:5 When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also.

3:14 But continue thou in the things which thou hast learned and hast been assured of, knowing of whom thou hast learned them; 15 And that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus.

Meaning: "Unfeigned" faith, real and unpretended, was seen in a grandmother and mother, then taken up by the son (theme 31, Faith). Children learn from "whom" as much as from what.

Train up a child — Proverbs 22:6

6 Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.

Meaning: "Train up" (chanak) is the word for dedicating a house or temple: setting something apart for God. "In the way he should go" can also mean according to the child's own bent, so wise parents know each child.

Jesus increased in wisdom — Luke 2:51–52

51 And he went down with them, and came to Nazareth, and was subject unto them: but his mother kept all these sayings in her heart. 52 And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.

Meaning: The Son of God grew up in a family and was "subject" to His parents. Growth is whole-person: wisdom (mind), stature (body), favour with God (spirit) and with man (relationships).

D · Goal — what family life is heading toward

Children are an heritage — Psalm 127:3–5

3 Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward. 4 As arrows are in the hand of a mighty man; so are children of the youth. 5 Happy is the man that hath his quiver full of them: they shall not be ashamed, but they shall speak with the enemies in the gate.

Meaning: Children are God's inheritance entrusted to us (theme 23), not our possession. Arrows are aimed and released: parenting is meant to send children out.

His father saw him, and ran — Luke 15:20–24

20 And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him. 21 And the son said unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in thy sight, and am no more worthy to be called thy son. 22 But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet: 23 And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it; and let us eat, and be merry: 24 For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry.

Meaning: The goal of a home is a place a wandering child can always come back to. This is the Father's heart for all of us (theme 14, Sonship).

E · Consummation — how the end shapes parenting now

He shall turn the heart of the fathers — Malachi 4:5–6

5 Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD: 6 And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.

Meaning: The Old Testament ends with family reconciliation as a sign of the coming day (theme 33). Luke 1:17 applies this to John the Baptist preparing for Christ.

All thy children shall be taught of the LORD — Isaiah 54:13

13 And all thy children shall be taught of the LORD; and great shall be the peace of thy children.

Meaning: The final hope for children is not our teaching but God's. Jesus quotes it in John 6:45.

No greater joy — 3 John 4

4 I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth.

Meaning: A spiritual father's deepest joy, and the goal every parent can pray toward.

Common struggles, and what the blueprint says

Struggle What the blueprint says Anchor text
Anger and harshness Don't provoke children or crush their spirit. God is slow to anger with us. Ephesians 6:4; Colossians 3:21; Psalm 103:8, 13
A wandering or prodigal child Keep the door open and keep watching the road; the Father runs to meet returning children. Proverbs describe wisdom, not guarantees. Luke 15:20; Proverbs 22:6
Guilt over past parenting failures No condemnation in Christ. God redeems what parents get wrong and is the child's Father too. Romans 8:1; Joel 2:25
Busyness, screens and distraction Faith is taught "when thou sittest … walkest … liest down … risest up": in ordinary time together. Deuteronomy 6:7
Favouritism It wounded Jacob's family for a generation. Each child is a distinct gift. Genesis 37:3–4
Single parenting God is "a father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows"; the church is family. Psalm 68:5–6; James 1:27
Childlessness or infertility God gives "a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters"; spiritual parenting is real parenting. Isaiah 56:4–5; Psalm 113:9; 1 Corinthians 4:15
Caring for ageing parents Honouring parents continues into adulthood; providing for family is an act of faith. Exodus 20:12; 1 Timothy 5:4, 8
Adult children making different choices Release them to God, keep loving them, and pray as Job did for his children. Job 1:5; Isaiah 54:13

Interpretive balance: Christians differ on methods of discipline. Proverbs' "rod" (13:24; 22:15) is read by some literally as measured physical correction and by others as a picture of firm, loving discipline in general. All agree that discipline must be loving, self-controlled and aimed at the child's good, never abusive (Hebrews 12:10–11; Ephesians 6:4). Proverbs 22:6 is a general principle of wisdom, not an unbreakable promise; children make their own choices before God (Ezekiel 18:20).

Practices

For yourself

Practice Anchor text
Tend your own walk with God first; let children see you pray and read. Deuteronomy 6:5–6
When you're angry, pause before correcting; correct to restore, not to vent. Ephesians 6:4; James 1:19–20
Pray for each child by name, with one specific request, every day. Job 1:5
Ask God daily for wisdom for the particular child in front of you. James 1:5

For the family

Practice Anchor text
A short family time most days: a verse, a question, a prayer. Deuteronomy 6:7
Tell your children the story of God's faithfulness in your own life. Psalm 78:4
Use mealtimes and car rides to talk about God naturally. Deuteronomy 6:7
Bless your children aloud (for example with Numbers 6:24–26). Numbers 6:23–27
Ask forgiveness from your children when you get it wrong. Colossians 3:13

For the church

Practice Anchor text
Welcome children as full members, not as distractions. Mark 10:14
Connect older believers with younger families as spiritual grandparents. Titus 2:3–5; Psalm 71:18
Support single parents and foster or adoptive families practically. James 1:27
Honour childless members and spiritual parents. Isaiah 56:5; 1 Corinthians 4:15

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening Psalm 78:4–6 Ask: "Who passed faith on to you?" Faith travels through generations.
8 min A · God's purpose Genesis 18:19; Deuteronomy 6:4–7 Parents are part of God's plan to bless the nations; faith is taught in ordinary life.
7 min B · God's provision Acts 2:39; Psalm 103:13; James 1:5 God's promise reaches children; He is the model Father and gives wisdom freely.
10 min C · Who God is shaping Ephesians 6:1–4; 2 Timothy 1:5; Luke 2:52 Children honour; parents nurture without provoking; real faith is caught as well as taught.
5 min D and E · Where it is heading Luke 15:20; Malachi 4:6; 3 John 4 A home a child can return to; hearts turned toward one another; children walking in truth.
5 min Response and prayer Numbers 6:24–26 Each person chooses one family practice. Pray for children by name, and for those without children.

Discussion questions

  1. Who passed faith on to you, and how? What made it real?
  2. Deuteronomy 6:7 names four everyday settings for teaching faith. Which comes most naturally in your home, and which is missing?
  3. What does it look like to "provoke" a child (Ephesians 6:4)? What does the opposite look like?
  4. What can parents learn from how God treats His children in Psalm 103:13?
  5. Timothy's faith came through his grandmother and mother (2 Timothy 1:5). How can grandparents and church family share in passing faith on?
  6. How does the father in Luke 15 shape how we respond to a wandering child?
  7. How can the church honour and include people without children, and single parents?
  8. Which one practice from this study will you start this week?

చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు

  1. మీకు విశ్వాసాన్ని ఎవరు అందించారు, ఎలా? అది నిజమని మీకు ఎలా అనిపించింది?
  2. ద్వితీయోపదేశకాండము 6:7 నాలుగు సాధారణ సందర్భాలను చెపుతుంది. మీ ఇంటిలో ఏది సహజంగా జరుగుతుంది, ఏది లేదు?
  3. పిల్లలకు "కోపం రేపడం" (ఎఫెసీయులకు 6:4) అంటే ఏమిటి? దానికి వ్యతిరేకంగా ఉండడం ఎలా కనిపిస్తుంది?
  4. కీర్తనలు 103:13లో దేవుడు తన పిల్లలతో ప్రవర్తించే విధానం నుండి తల్లిదండ్రులు ఏమి నేర్చుకోవచ్చు?
  5. తిమోతి విశ్వాసం అతని అవ్వ మరియు తల్లి ద్వారా వచ్చింది (2 తిమోతికి 1:5). విశ్వాసాన్ని అందించడంలో తాతమ్మలు, సంఘ కుటుంబం ఎలా పాలుపంచుకోగలదు?
  6. లూకా 15లోని తండ్రి దారి తప్పిన పిల్లవానికి మనం ఎలా స్పందించాలో ఎలా నేర్పిస్తాడు?

  8. ఈ అధ్యయనం నుండి ఈ వారం మీరు ఏ ఒక్క అభ్యాసాన్ని మొదలుపెడతారు?
