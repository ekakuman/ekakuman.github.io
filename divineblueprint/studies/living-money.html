The Divine Blueprint and Money

Overview

Jesus spoke about money more than about almost any other everyday subject, because it so quickly becomes a rival god: "Ye cannot serve God and mammon" (Matthew 6:24). The Divine Blueprint puts money in its place. Everything belongs to God ("The earth is the LORD's, and the fulness thereof," Psalm 24:1). Christ "became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich" (2 Corinthians 8:9). God's people are stewards, not owners. Money is meant to serve the kingdom and care for neighbours, and its real value is measured against eternity: "where your treasure is, there will your heart be also" (Matthew 6:21).

This study walks money and possessions through the five groups of the blueprint:

  • A · Purpose: whose money it is, and what it shows of Him
  • B · Provision: what God supplies
  • C · People: who God makes us through how we handle it
  • D · Goal: what money is for
  • E · Consummation: how the end changes how we use it now

Key passage — 1 Timothy 6:6–10, 17–19

6 But godliness with contentment is great gain. 7 For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out. 8 And having food and raiment let us be therewith content. 9 But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition. 10 For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.

17 Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy; 18 That they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate; 19 Laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life.

Meaning:

  • v. 6 redefines gain: godliness plus contentment. This answers the false teachers who were "supposing that gain is godliness" (6:5).
  • vv. 7–8 We arrive with nothing and leave with nothing, so contentment with basic needs is wisdom, not resignation.
  • vv. 9–10 The danger is not money itself but the desire to be rich and the love of money. Both lead to "many sorrows."
  • v. 17 speaks to those who already have wealth (most readers, by world standards). Don't be proud or trust in it; trust God, "who giveth us richly all things to enjoy." Enjoyment is allowed; idolatry is not.
  • v. 18 Be "rich in good works": generous and sharing ("willing to communicate" means ready to share).
  • v. 19 Generosity is "laying up in store" for the age to come: treasure in heaven.

Interpretive balance: Christians differ on tithing. Some hold the tenth (Malachi 3:10) as the continuing standard. Others see the New Testament calling for generous, proportional, cheerful giving without a fixed percentage (1 Corinthians 16:2; 2 Corinthians 9:7), often with the tithe as a helpful starting point. All agree that giving should be regular, generous, joyful and directed by love for God and neighbour.

The five lenses

A · Purpose — whose money it is

The earth is the LORD's — Psalm 24:1; Haggai 2:8

Psalm 24:1 The earth is the LORD's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein.

Haggai 2:8 The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the LORD of hosts.

Meaning: God owns everything, including people. We hold possessions as stewards on His behalf (theme 1, the Eternal Purpose: all things are "for him").

Of thine own have we given thee — 1 Chronicles 29:11–12, 14

11 Thine, O LORD, is the greatness, and the power, and the glory, and the victory, and the majesty: for all that is in the heaven and in the earth is thine; thine is the kingdom, O LORD, and thou art exalted as head above all. 12 Both riches and honour come of thee, and thou reignest over all; and in thine hand is power and might; and in thine hand it is to make great, and to give strength unto all.

14 But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly after this sort? for all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee.

Meaning: David's prayer after Israel gave generously for the temple. Giving is returning to God what was His all along, and it is an act of worship that gives Him the glory (theme 4).

The unseen side. "But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven ... For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also" (Matthew 6:20–21). "Ye cannot serve God and mammon" (6:24). Jesus names money as an unseen rival master competing for worship. Every financial decision quietly answers which master we serve, and treasure stored in the unseen is the only kind that lasts.

B · Provision — what God supplies

He became poor — 2 Corinthians 8:9

9 For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.

Meaning: The gospel is the root of generosity. Paul doesn't command the Corinthians to give; he reminds them of grace (theme 8).

My God shall supply all your need — Philippians 4:19; Matthew 6:31–32

Philippians 4:19 But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.

Matthew 6:31 Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? 32 (For after all these things do the Gentiles seek:) for your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things.

Meaning: God provides for needs (not every want) out of His riches. Philippians 4:19 was written to a church that had given generously to Paul (4:15–18).

Prove Me now herewith — Malachi 3:10

10 Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.

Meaning: God invites His people to trust Him with their giving. The blessing is His to define, not a formula for getting rich (see the balance note on tithing above).

C · People — who God makes us through it

Faithful in that which is least — Luke 16:10–11

10 He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much. 11 If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?

Meaning: Money is a training ground for faithfulness (theme 29). How we handle small amounts shows what we'd do with greater trust, including "the true riches."

I have learned … to be content — Philippians 4:11–13

11 Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. 12 I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. 13 I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.

Meaning: Contentment is "learned," in plenty and in want alike. Verse 13 is about contentment in every financial situation through Christ's strength.

God loveth a cheerful giver — 2 Corinthians 9:6–7

6 But this I say, He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully. 7 Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.

Meaning: Giving shapes the heart: planned ("purposeth"), willing and joyful. God loves the giver more than the gift.

D · Goal — what money is for

Seek ye first the kingdom — Matthew 6:33

33 But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.

Meaning: Money serves the kingdom (theme 20), not the other way round. Priorities decide where the money goes.

Open thine hand wide — Deuteronomy 15:7–8, 11

7 If there be among you a poor man of one of thy brethren within any of thy gates in thy land which the LORD thy God giveth thee, thou shalt not harden thine heart, nor shut thine hand from thy poor brother: 8 But thou shalt open thine hand wide unto him, and shalt surely lend him sufficient for his need, in that which he wanteth.

11 For the poor shall never cease out of the land: therefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land.

Meaning: Money's goal includes justice and mercy for the poor (themes 9 and 28). The heart and the hand must both be open.

Neither poverty nor riches — Proverbs 30:8–9; Acts 4:34

Proverbs 30:8 Remove far from me vanity and lies: give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me: 9 Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the LORD? or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain.

Acts 4:34 Neither was there any among them that lacked: for as many as were possessors of lands or houses sold them, and brought the prices of the things that were sold,

Meaning: The goal is enough: enough for faithfulness, and enough shared so that "neither was there any among them that lacked." That is shalom in material form (theme 32).

E · Consummation — how the end shapes money now

Treasures in heaven — Matthew 6:19–21

19 Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: 20 But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: 21 For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

Meaning: Earthly wealth decays; heavenly treasure lasts (theme 23, Inheritance). The heart follows the treasure.

This night thy soul shall be required — Luke 12:15, 20–21

15 And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.

20 But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided? 21 So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God.

Meaning: The rich fool planned for everything except death and judgment (theme 33). Being "rich toward God" is the only lasting wealth.

Naked shall I return — Job 1:21

21 And said, Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.

Meaning: Job's worship after losing everything. Possessions are on loan, so they can be released with trust.

Common struggles, and what the blueprint says

Struggle What the blueprint says Anchor text
Anxiety about money The Father knows what you need; seek the kingdom first. Matthew 6:25–33; Philippians 4:6–7
Debt The borrower becomes servant to the lender. Aim to owe nothing but love, and repay honestly. Proverbs 22:7; Romans 13:8; Psalm 37:21
Greed, comparison and "more" Life is not in the abundance of possessions. Contentment rests on God's presence. Luke 12:15; Hebrews 13:5; Ecclesiastes 5:10
The "prosperity gospel" Godliness is not a means of gain; Christ became poor. Blessing is not measured in wealth. 1 Timothy 6:5–6; 2 Corinthians 8:9; James 2:5
Stinginess or fear of giving God loves a cheerful giver and is able to make all grace abound. 2 Corinthians 9:6–8
Dishonesty: taxes, invoices, shortcuts "A false balance is abomination to the LORD." Render to all their due. Proverbs 11:1; Romans 13:7; Luke 16:10
Poverty and hardship God has chosen the poor rich in faith; the church is called to share. James 2:5; Psalm 37:25; Acts 4:34
Money conflict in marriage Two walk together only if agreed; decide together, seeking the kingdom first. Amos 3:3; Ecclesiastes 4:9
Sudden loss The LORD gave and the LORD has taken away; rejoice in God when the fields fail. Job 1:21; Habakkuk 3:17–18

Practices

For yourself

Practice Anchor text
Give first, as soon as income arrives, before other spending. Proverbs 3:9
Write a simple budget and pray over it: what does it show you treasure? Matthew 6:21; Luke 14:28
Practise contentment: for a month, delay a non-essential purchase and give thanks instead. Philippians 4:11; Hebrews 13:5
Be exact in small things: receipts, taxes, change. Luke 16:10; Romans 13:7
If you are in debt, make a plan to repay it and seek wise counsel. Proverbs 22:7; 15:22

For the household

Practice Anchor text
Make money decisions together; review giving together each year. Amos 3:3
Teach children to give, save and spend, in that order. Proverbs 22:6
Keep a "generosity line" in the budget for needs that come up. Deuteronomy 15:11
Pray 1 Chronicles 29:14 together when you give. 1 Chronicles 29:14

For the church

Practice Anchor text
Care for members in financial need, discreetly and practically. Acts 4:34; Galatians 6:10
Teach on money honestly, without manipulation or prosperity promises. 1 Timothy 6:5–10
Handle church funds with transparent accountability. 2 Corinthians 8:20–21
Celebrate generosity as worship, not as fundraising. 2 Corinthians 9:12–15

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening Matthew 6:21, 24 Ask: "What does your bank statement say you treasure?" Money competes for the heart.
8 min A · Whose it is Psalm 24:1; 1 Chronicles 29:11–14 God owns everything; we are stewards; giving is returning what is His.
7 min B · God's provision 2 Corinthians 8:9; Philippians 4:19 Christ became poor for us; God supplies our needs.
10 min C · Who God makes us 1 Timothy 6:6–10; Luke 16:10; Philippians 4:11–13 Contentment, faithfulness in little, freedom from the love of money.
5 min D and E · What it is for Deuteronomy 15:11; Matthew 6:19–21; Luke 12:20–21 Kingdom first, open hands to the poor, treasure in heaven.
5 min Response and prayer 1 Timothy 6:17–19 Each person chooses one money practice. Pray 1 Chronicles 29:14 together.

Discussion questions

  1. If someone read your spending for a month, what would they say you treasure (Matthew 6:21)?
  2. How does "of thine own have we given thee" (1 Chronicles 29:14) change how you feel about giving?
  3. Paul says contentment is "learned" (Philippians 4:11). What has taught you contentment, or what is teaching you now?
  4. Where is the line between enjoying God's gifts (1 Timothy 6:17) and loving money (6:10)?
  5. What does "faithful in that which is least" (Luke 16:10) look like with money this week?
  6. Deuteronomy 15 says to open your hand and your heart. Who in your community needs both?
  7. What would it mean for you to be "rich toward God" (Luke 12:21)?
  8. Which one practice from this study will you start this week?

చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు

  1. ఒక నెల మీ ఖర్చులు చూసినవారు మీ ధనం (మీ హృదయం) ఎక్కడ ఉందని చెప్తారు (మత్తయి 6:21)?
  2. "నీ సొత్తులో నుండి నీకు ఇచ్చితిమి" (1 దినవృత్తాంతములు 29:14) అనే మాట ఇవ్వడం గురించి మీ భావనను ఎలా మారుస్తుంది?
  3. సంతృప్తి "నేర్చుకున్నది" అని పౌలు చెప్పాడు (ఫిలిప్పీయులకు 4:11). మీకు సంతృప్తిని ఏమి నేర్పింది, లేదా ఇప్పుడు ఏమి నేర్పిస్తోంది?
  4. దేవుని వరాలను ఆనందించడానికి (1 తిమోతికి 6:17) ధనాపేక్షకు (6:10) మధ్య గీత ఎక్కడ ఉంది?
  5. "అతి స్వల్పమైనదానిలో నమ్మకంగా ఉండడం" (లూకా 16:10) ఈ వారం డబ్బు విషయంలో ఎలా కనిపిస్తుంది?
  6. ద్వితీయోపదేశకాండము 15 చేయిని, హృదయాన్ని రెండింటినీ తెరవమని చెప్తుంది. మీ సమాజంలో రెండూ అవసరమైనవారు ఎవరు?
  7. "దేవుని యెడల ధనవంతుడు" (లూకా 12:21) అవ్వడం అంటే మీకు ఏమిటి?
  8. ఈ అధ్యయనం నుండి ఈ వారం మీరు ఏ ఒక్క అభ్యాసాన్ని మొదలుపెడతారు?
