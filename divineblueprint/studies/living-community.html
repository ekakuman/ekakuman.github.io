The Divine Blueprint and Community and Citizenship

Overview

Believers live in two places at once. Their "conversation" — citizenship — "is in heaven" (Philippians 3:20), yet they live in real towns, under real governments, beside neighbours of every faith and none. How should the blueprint shape life in the wider community?

From the start God's plan reached beyond one family: "in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed" (Genesis 12:3). Israel in exile was told to "seek the peace of the city" (Jeremiah 29:7). Jesus called His followers salt and light (Matthew 5:13–16). The apostles taught respect for rulers and prayer for them, while making clear that God's command comes first (Romans 13:1–7; Acts 5:29).

This study covers:

  • Neighbours and the city — seeking the good of the place where God has put us.
  • Government and law — respect, taxes, prayer, and the limits of human authority.
  • Justice and mercy — care for the poor, the stranger and the weak.

Key passages — Jeremiah 29:4–7 and Romans 13:1–7

Jeremiah 29:4–7 4 Thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, unto all that are carried away captives, whom I have caused to be carried away from Jerusalem unto Babylon; 5 Build ye houses, and dwell in them; and plant gardens, and eat the fruit of them; 6 Take ye wives, and beget sons and daughters; and take wives for your sons, and give your daughters to husbands, that they may bear sons and daughters; that ye may be increased there, and not diminished. 7 And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.

Romans 13:1–7 1 Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God. 2 Whosoever therefore resisteth the power, resisteth the ordinance of God: and they that resist shall receive to themselves damnation. 3 For rulers are not a terror to good works, but to the evil. Wilt thou then not be afraid of the power? do that which is good, and thou shalt have praise of the same: 4 For he is the minister of God to thee for good. But if thou do that which is evil, be afraid; for he beareth not the sword in vain: for he is the minister of God, a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil. 5 Wherefore ye must needs be subject, not only for wrath, but also for conscience sake. 6 For for this cause pay ye tribute also: for they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing. 7 Render therefore to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due; custom to whom custom; fear to whom fear; honour to whom honour.

Meaning

Jeremiah wrote to exiles in Babylon — a pagan empire that had destroyed their temple. God told them neither to withdraw nor to rebel, but to settle, build, and seek the city's peace (Hebrew shalom: wholeness, welfare). Their own good was bound up with their neighbours' good.

Paul wrote Romans 13 under Nero's empire. Government is "ordained of God" for a purpose: to restrain evil and reward good. Christians pay taxes and give honour "for conscience sake," not just from fear. The passage describes what authority is for; it does not say every ruler fulfils that purpose.

Balance notes

  • Two sides held together. Romans 13 calls for respect and submission. Acts 4:19–20 and 5:29 show that when rulers command what God forbids, or forbid what God commands, "we ought to obey God rather than men." Daniel, his friends and the apostles disobeyed respectfully and accepted the consequences.
  • No party owns the blueprint. Faithful Christians differ on parties and policies. This study does not tell anyone how to vote. It sets out principles — justice, mercy, truth, care for the weak — that believers apply with conscience.
  • Minority and persecuted believers. Many Christians live where their faith is restricted. Jeremiah 29, 1 Peter 2 and Hebrews 13:3 speak directly to them: do good, pray for rulers, endure patiently, and trust God's justice.
  • The church is not the state. The kingdom of Christ "is not of this world" (John 18:36). Believers serve the common good without trying to make the gospel advance by force.

Community and citizenship through the five lenses

A · The Purpose — blessing for all the families of the earth

Genesis 12:2–3 — And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing: and I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

Acts 17:26–27 — And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation; that they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him, though he be not far from every one of us:

Meaning. God's purpose was never for His people alone. Abraham was blessed to be a blessing. All nations come from "one blood," and God has placed people in their times and places so they might seek Him. Where you live is not an accident.

Theme links: The Purpose of God; The Seed; Election; Covenant.

The unseen side. "The most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will" (Daniel 4:17). Behind earthly nations stand unseen powers (Daniel 10:13, 20; Ephesians 6:12), and above them all the Most High. Praying for rulers (1 Timothy 2:1–2) is therefore no formality: it is engagement in the unseen realm that governs the seen.

B · The Provision — a King above every ruler

Daniel 2:21 — And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding:

Matthew 22:21 — They say unto him, Caesar's. Then saith he unto them, Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's; and unto God the things that are God's.

Philippians 3:20 — For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ:

Meaning. God raises and removes rulers. Jesus gave Caesar his due — the coin bore Caesar's image — but the person bears God's image and belongs to God. Believers have a heavenly citizenship and a King who is coming. That frees them from both fear of rulers and worship of rulers.

Theme links: The Kingdom; The Son; The Image of God.

C · The People — salt, light and good neighbours

Matthew 5:13–16 — Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted? it is thenceforth good for nothing, but to be cast out, and to be trodden under foot of men. Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid. Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth light unto all that are in the house. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.

1 Timothy 2:1–2 — I exhort therefore, that, first of all, supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; for kings, and for all that are in authority; that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty.

1 Peter 2:12, 17 — Having your conversation honest among the Gentiles: that, whereas they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they shall behold, glorify God in the day of visitation. ... Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king.

Meaning. Salt preserves and flavours; light shows the way. Believers are to be visible in good works so that God, not they, is praised. They pray for rulers "first of all" and "honour all men" — including neighbours of other faiths and opponents.

Theme links: Priesthood; Love; Holiness.

D · The Goal — justice, mercy and the good of all

Micah 6:8 — He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?

Leviticus 19:33–34 — And if a stranger sojourn with thee in your land, ye shall not vex him. But the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God.

Galatians 6:10 — As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith.

Meaning. God's people aim at justice and mercy in every sphere. The stranger and the vulnerable get special care because God's people were once strangers. Doing good is "unto all men" — not only fellow believers.

Theme links: Righteousness and Justice; Mercy; Peace.

E · The Consummation — the kingdoms become Christ's

Revelation 11:15 — And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.

Revelation 21:24–26 — And the nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it. And the gates of it shall not be shut at all by day: for there shall be no night there. And they shall bring the glory and honour of the nations into it.

Hebrews 13:14 — For here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come.

Meaning. No earthly nation is the final kingdom. One day all kingdoms will be Christ's, and the glory of the nations will be brought into the holy city. That hope keeps believers from despair when governments fail and from idolatry when they succeed.

Theme links: The Kingdom; The Heading Up of All Things; New Creation; Final Judgment.

Common struggles

Struggle What it sounds like What Scripture says
Withdrawal "The world is evil; we should keep to ourselves." Jeremiah 29:7; Matthew 5:14–16; John 17:15
Political idolatry Hoping in a party or leader as if they were the saviour. Psalm 146:3–5; Philippians 3:20
Contempt for rulers or opponents Mocking leaders; treating the other side as enemies. 1 Peter 2:17; Titus 3:1–2; Jude 8–9
Fear under hostile authorities "What if they come after us?" Matthew 10:28; 1 Peter 3:14–16; Hebrews 13:6
Cutting corners on the law Avoiding tax, bribes, ignoring regulations. Romans 13:6–7; Proverbs 17:23; Luke 3:12–14
Ignoring the poor and the stranger "That's someone else's job." Leviticus 19:33–34; Proverbs 31:8–9; James 2:15–16

Practices

For yourself

Practice How Scripture
Pray for leaders by name Local, state and national leaders, whatever their party. 1 Timothy 2:1–2
Be honest with the law Pay taxes fully; refuse bribes; keep regulations. Romans 13:7; Proverbs 11:1
Know your neighbours Learn names; offer help; share meals. Luke 10:29–37; Jeremiah 29:7
Engage with conscience Vote, speak and serve by principle, not party loyalty. Micah 6:8; Proverbs 31:8–9

For others

Practice How Scripture
Welcome the stranger Help migrants, students and newcomers settle. Leviticus 19:34; Matthew 25:35
Speak up for the voiceless Support the poor, widows, orphans and the disabled. Proverbs 31:8–9; James 1:27
Serve the community Volunteer in schools, hospitals, disaster relief. Galatians 6:10; Titus 3:8

For the church

Practice How Scripture
Pray publicly for the nation Include rulers and the city in every gathering. 1 Timothy 2:1–2; Jeremiah 29:7
Serve the neighbourhood Run something for the whole community, not only members. Matthew 5:16; Galatians 6:10
Stay above party lines Teach principles, not party slogans; welcome members of different views. Romans 14:19; Ephesians 4:3
Stand with the persecuted Pray and give for believers who suffer for their faith. Hebrews 13:3; 1 Corinthians 12:26

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening Jeremiah 29:7 Ask: "If our church disappeared tomorrow, would our neighbourhood notice?" God told exiles to seek their city's peace.
7 min A · God's purpose Genesis 12:2–3; Acts 17:26–27 Blessed to be a blessing; God placed us where we live.
8 min B · God's provision Daniel 2:21; Matthew 22:21; Philippians 3:20 A King above every ruler; give Caesar his due and God His.
10 min C · Salt, light, good neighbours Romans 13:1–7; 1 Timothy 2:1–2; Acts 5:29 Respect, taxes and prayer — and obey God first when they conflict.
5 min D and E · Justice now, the kingdom to come Micah 6:8; Revelation 11:15 Do justly, love mercy; all kingdoms will become Christ's.
5 min Response and prayer 1 Timothy 2:1–2 Pray for leaders by name. Each person chooses one way to serve the neighbourhood.

Discussion questions

  1. Why do you think God told the exiles to seek Babylon's peace instead of withdrawing (Jeremiah 29:7)?
  2. How does Genesis 12:3 shape the way we see our neighbours of other faiths?
  3. What belongs to "Caesar" and what belongs to God (Matthew 22:21)?
  4. Paul wrote Romans 13 under Nero. What does that teach about respecting imperfect rulers?
  5. When should a Christian say "we ought to obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29)? How should they do it?
  6. How can a church stay united when members hold different political views (Ephesians 4:3)?
  7. Who are the "strangers" in our community today (Leviticus 19:34)?
  8. What one thing could you do this month for the good of your town?

  1. బంధీలు వేరుగా ఉండకుండా బబులు సమాధానం కోరాలని దేవుడు ఎందుకు చెప్పాడు (యిర్మీయా 29:7)?

  3. "కైసరువి" ఏమిటి, దేవునివి ఏమిటి (మత్తయి 22:21)?
  4. పౌలు నీరో పాలనలో రోమీయులకు 13 వ్రాశాడు. అసంపూర్ణ అధికారులను గౌరవించడం గురించి ఇది ఏమి నేర్పుతుంది?
