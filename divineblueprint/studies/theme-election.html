Election and Foreknowledge

Overview and word study

Election is God choosing a people for Himself, in Christ, by grace, for a purpose. Foreknowledge is His prior, personal knowing of them. Both are rooted in eternity: "he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world" (Ephesians 1:4); "elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father" (1 Peter 1:2).

Across Scripture, God's choices consistently surprise: the younger over the older, the small over the great, the shepherd boy over his brothers, "the foolish things of the world" (1 Corinthians 1:27). Election is always for something — to draw near, to be holy, to bear fruit, to bless the nations.

Interpretive note: Christians differ on how election works. Reformed readers hold that God unconditionally chose specific individuals for salvation. Arminian and Wesleyan readers hold that God chose Christ and all who are in Him, with individuals included through foreseen faith. Others stress corporate election of a people. All agree that election is by grace, in Christ, and never a reason for pride or presumption. This study presents the texts and lets them speak.

The Hebrew yada explains "foreknow": in Amos 3:2, "you only have I known" does not mean God was unaware of other nations but that He had a unique relationship with Israel.

Torah

For I know him — Genesis 18:18–19

18 Seeing that Abraham shall surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him? 19 For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.

Meaning: "I know him" is yedativ, often translated "I have chosen him." Election here has a purpose: blessing the nations and teaching a household in God's ways.

The elder shall serve the younger — Genesis 25:22–23

22 And the children struggled together within her; and she said, If it be so, why am I thus? And she went to enquire of the LORD. 23 And the LORD said unto her, Two nations are in thy womb, and two manner of people shall be separated from thy bowels; and the one people shall be stronger than the other people; and the elder shall serve the younger.

Meaning: God's choice is announced before birth, reversing the normal order. Paul uses it in Romans 9:11–12 to show election rests "not of works, but of him that calleth."

A peculiar people unto Himself — Deuteronomy 14:1–2

1 Ye are the children of the LORD your God: ye shall not cut yourselves, nor make any baldness between your eyes for the dead. 2 For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God, and the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth.

Meaning: Election is "unto himself" — for relationship. It leads to holiness: because they are chosen, they must live differently.

Him whom He hath chosen will He cause to come near — Numbers 16:5

5 And he spake unto Korah and unto all his company, saying, Even to morrow the LORD will shew who are his, and who is holy; and will cause him to come near unto him: even him whom he hath chosen will he cause to come near unto him.

Meaning: Election means access: God brings His chosen near. "The Lord knoweth them that are his" (2 Timothy 2:19) quotes this verse.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

The LORD looketh on the heart — 1 Samuel 16:7, 11–12

7 But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.

11 And Samuel said unto Jesse, Are here all thy children? And he said, There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep. And Samuel said unto Jesse, Send and fetch him: for we will not sit down till he come hither. 12 And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the LORD said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he.

Meaning: God's choice ignores outward criteria. The youngest, left in the field, is the one. "This is he."

Jacob whom I have chosen — Isaiah 41:8–10

8 But thou, Israel, art my servant, Jacob whom I have chosen, the seed of Abraham my friend. 9 Thou whom I have taken from the ends of the earth, and called thee from the chief men thereof, and said unto thee, Thou art my servant; I have chosen thee, and not cast thee away. 10 Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.

Meaning: Election is the ground of comfort: "I have chosen thee, and not cast thee away … fear thou not." Chosen people are upheld people.

Mine elect — Isaiah 42:1

1 Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him: he shall bring forth judgment to the Gentiles.

Meaning: The supreme Chosen One is the Servant — Christ (Matthew 12:18; Luke 9:35, "my chosen Son" in many manuscripts). Believers are chosen "in him" (Ephesians 1:4).

Before I formed thee — Jeremiah 1:4–5

4 Then the word of the LORD came unto me, saying, 5 Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.

Meaning: Individual foreknowledge for a calling: known, set apart, appointed — all before birth.

You only have I known — Amos 3:1–2

1 Hear this word that the LORD hath spoken against you, O children of Israel, against the whole family which I brought up from the land of Egypt, saying, 2 You only have I known of all the families of the earth: therefore I will punish you for all your iniquities.

Meaning: Election brings responsibility, not immunity. Being known by God raises accountability.

Writings (Ketuvim)

Blessed is the man whom Thou choosest — Psalm 65:4

4 Blessed is the man whom thou choosest, and causest to approach unto thee, that he may dwell in thy courts: we shall be satisfied with the goodness of thy house, even of thy holy temple.

Meaning: Election → approach → dwelling → satisfaction. The purpose of being chosen is nearness to God.

The people He hath chosen — Psalm 33:12

12 Blessed is the nation whose God is the LORD; and the people whom he hath chosen for his own inheritance.

Meaning: God's chosen people are His "inheritance" — what He treasures (Ephesians 1:18).

He chose David — Psalm 78:67–72

67 Moreover he refused the tabernacle of Joseph, and chose not the tribe of Ephraim: 68 But chose the tribe of Judah, the mount Zion which he loved. 69 And he built his sanctuary like high palaces, like the earth which he hath established for ever. 70 He chose David also his servant, and took him from the sheepfolds: 71 From following the ewes great with young he brought him to feed Jacob his people, and Israel his inheritance. 72 So he fed them according to the integrity of his heart; and guided them by the skilfulness of his hands.

Meaning: Election narrows to a tribe, a mountain, a sanctuary and a shepherd-king. David is chosen from the sheepfolds to shepherd God's people — chosen to serve.

Who didst choose Abram — Nehemiah 9:7–8

7 Thou art the LORD the God, who didst choose Abram, and broughtest him forth out of Ur of the Chaldees, and gavest him the name of Abraham; 8 And foundest his heart faithful before thee, and madest a covenant with him to give the land … to give it, I say, to his seed, and hast performed thy words; for thou art righteous:

Meaning: Israel's confession after exile begins with God's choice. Election leads to a new name, a covenant and a faithful heart. (Verse 8 is shortened at the ellipsis.)

New Testament

I have chosen you — John 15:16

16 Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain: that whatsoever ye shall ask of the Father in my name, he may give it you.

Meaning: Christ takes the initiative. Election is for fruit that lasts and for prayer that is answered.

All that the Father giveth Me — John 6:37, 44

37 All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.

44 No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him: and I will raise him up at the last day.

Meaning: Both sides held together: the Father gives and draws; whoever comes is never rejected. Election is never a barrier to any who come.

Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? — Romans 8:29–33

29 For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. 30 Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified. 31 What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us? 32 He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things? 33 Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? It is God that justifieth.

Meaning: Election is presented as assurance, not anxiety. Foreknown → predestined → called → justified → glorified, and the conclusion is confidence: "If God be for us."

God hath chosen the foolish things — 1 Corinthians 1:26–29

26 For ye see your calling, brethren, how that not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called: 27 But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty; 28 And base things of the world, and things which are despised, hath God chosen, yea, and things which are not, to bring to nought things that are: 29 That no flesh should glory in his presence.

Meaning: Three times "God hath chosen." His choices overturn human status so that no one can boast.

Chosen to salvation through sanctification — 2 Thessalonians 2:13–14

13 But we are bound to give thanks alway to God for you, brethren beloved of the Lord, because God hath from the beginning chosen you to salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth: 14 Whereunto he called you by our gospel, to the obtaining of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Meaning: Election works through means: the Spirit's sanctifying, belief of the truth, and the preaching of the gospel. Its end is glory.

Elect according to foreknowledge — 1 Peter 1:1–2

1 Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia, 2 Elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ: Grace unto you, and peace, be multiplied.

Meaning: The whole Trinity in election: the Father foreknows, the Spirit sanctifies, the Son's blood cleanses. Written to scattered, suffering believers as comfort.

A chosen generation — 1 Peter 2:4, 9–10

4 To whom coming, as unto a living stone, disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious,

9 But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light: 10 Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.

Meaning: Christ is the chosen Stone; those who come to Him share His chosenness. Exodus 19:6 is applied to the church. Purpose: "shew forth the praises."

Make your calling and election sure — 2 Peter 1:10–11; Colossians 3:12

2 Peter 1:10 Wherefore the rather, brethren, give diligence to make your calling and election sure: for if ye do these things, ye shall never fall: 11 For so an entrance shall be ministered unto you abundantly into the everlasting kingdom of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

Colossians 3:12 Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering;

Meaning: Election calls for diligence and character. The elect are to look like what they are: holy, beloved, merciful.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Chosen by grace, not merit "Not because ye were more in number" (Deuteronomy 7:7) "The LORD looketh on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7) David "from the sheepfolds" (Psalm 78:70) "God hath chosen the foolish things" (1 Corinthians 1:27)
2. Chosen in the Chosen One "In thy seed" (Genesis 22:18) "Mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth" (Isaiah 42:1) "I have made a covenant with my chosen" (Psalm 89:3) "Chosen us in him" (Ephesians 1:4); "chosen of God, and precious" (1 Peter 2:4)
3. Chosen for a purpose "Be a blessing" (Genesis 12:2); "a kingdom of priests" (Exodus 19:6) "I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations" (Jeremiah 1:5) "For such a time as this" (Esther 4:14) "That ye should go and bring forth fruit" (John 15:16); "shew forth the praises" (1 Peter 2:9)
4. Chosen to draw near "Will he cause to come near unto him" (Numbers 16:5) "I will cause him to draw near" (Jeremiah 30:21) "Causest to approach unto thee" (Psalm 65:4) "Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out" (John 6:37)
5. Election brings responsibility "An holy people" (Deuteronomy 14:2) "You only have I known … therefore I will punish" (Amos 3:2) "Foundest his heart faithful" (Nehemiah 9:8) "Make your calling and election sure" (2 Peter 1:10); "put on therefore" (Colossians 3:12)
6. The younger and weaker chosen "The elder shall serve the younger" (Genesis 25:23); Ephraim over Manasseh (48:14) "There remaineth yet the youngest" (1 Samuel 16:11) "Took him from the sheepfolds" (Psalm 78:70) "Not many mighty, not many noble" (1 Corinthians 1:26)
7. God's choice is secure "I will not … break my covenant" (Leviticus 26:44) "I have chosen thee, and not cast thee away" (Isaiah 41:9) "The LORD will not cast off his people" (Psalm 94:14) "Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect?" (Romans 8:33)

Principles

  1. Election is in Christ. He is the Chosen One; believers are chosen in Him (Isaiah 42:1; Ephesians 1:4).
  2. Election is by grace. God's choices overturn human merit and status (1 Corinthians 1:26–29).
  3. Election is for nearness. God chooses people to bring them close (Psalm 65:4; Numbers 16:5).
  4. Election is for mission. Chosen to bless, bear fruit and declare His praises (John 15:16; 1 Peter 2:9).
  5. Election produces assurance, not anxiety. "If God be for us" (Romans 8:31).
  6. Election calls for holiness and diligence. The chosen are to look like Christ (Colossians 3:12; 2 Peter 1:10).
  7. Election never closes the door to those who come. "Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out" (John 6:37).

For a teaching session: Read 1 Samuel 16:6–13 and 1 Corinthians 1:26–31. Ask what God looks for and why.
