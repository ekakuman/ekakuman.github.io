God's Dwelling Place

Overview and word study

God's desire to dwell with His people is the thread that runs from Eden to the New Jerusalem. He walked with Adam in a garden, met Jacob at the "gate of heaven," filled the tabernacle and temple, "tabernacled" among us in Jesus, now dwells in the church by the Spirit, and will finally announce: "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:3).

The dwelling moves through five stages: garden → tabernacle → temple → Christ's body → the church → the city. Each stage is larger and more personal than the one before.

God has always been the one who builds the house. When David offered to build God a house, God replied that He would build David a house (2 Samuel 7:5, 11).

Torah

Walking in the garden — Genesis 3:8

8 And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden.

Meaning: Eden was the first sanctuary: God walked there with people. "Walking" (mithallekh) is the same verb used of God in the tabernacle (Leviticus 26:12).

The house of God, the gate of heaven — Genesis 28:16–17

16 And Jacob awaked out of his sleep, and he said, Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not. 17 And he was afraid, and said, How dreadful is this place! this is none other but the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.

Meaning: Jacob names the place Beth-el, "house of God." A ladder joins heaven and earth. Jesus claims to be that ladder (John 1:51).

That I may dwell among them — Exodus 25:8–9; 29:45–46

25:8 And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them. 25:9 According to all that I shew thee, after the pattern of the tabernacle, and the pattern of all the instruments thereof, even so shall ye make it.

29:45 And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. 29:46 And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them: I am the LORD their God.

Meaning: The stated purpose of the exodus: "that I may dwell among them." The sanctuary is built to a heavenly pattern.

I will walk among you — Leviticus 26:11–12

11 And I will set my tabernacle among you: and my soul shall not abhor you. 12 And I will walk among you, and will be your God, and ye shall be my people.

Meaning: Eden's walking returns. Paul quotes this for the church: "I will dwell in them, and walk in them" (2 Corinthians 6:16).

The place for Thee to dwell in — Exodus 15:17

17 Thou shalt bring them in, and plant them in the mountain of thine inheritance, in the place, O LORD, which thou hast made for thee to dwell in, in the Sanctuary, O Lord, which thy hands have established.

Meaning: Redemption's goal in the first song of Scripture: God's people planted in God's dwelling, built by God's hands.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Shalt thou build Me an house? — 2 Samuel 7:5–6, 11, 13

5 Go and tell my servant David, Thus saith the LORD, Shalt thou build me an house for me to dwell in? 6 Whereas I have not dwelt in any house since the time that I brought up the children of Israel out of Egypt, even to this day, but have walked in a tent and in a tabernacle.

11 … Also the LORD telleth thee that he will make thee an house.

13 He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.

Meaning: God reverses David's offer: God builds David a house (dynasty), and David's Seed builds God's house. Ultimately Christ builds His church (Matthew 16:18). (Verse 11 shortened.)

Will God indeed dwell on the earth? — 1 Kings 8:27–30

27 But will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded? 28 Yet have thou respect unto the prayer of thy servant, and to his supplication, O LORD my God, to hearken unto the cry and to the prayer, which thy servant prayeth before thee to day: 29 That thine eyes may be open toward this house night and day, even toward the place of which thou hast said, My name shall be there: that thou mayest hearken unto the prayer which thy servant shall make toward this place. 30 And hearken thou to the supplication of thy servant, and of thy people Israel, when they shall pray toward this place: and hear thou in heaven thy dwelling place: and when thou hearest, forgive.

Meaning: Solomon holds two truths: no building can contain God, yet God chooses to put His name there. The temple is a place of prayer and forgiveness.

Heaven is My throne — Isaiah 66:1–2

1 Thus saith the LORD, The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool: where is the house that ye build unto me? and where is the place of my rest? 2 For all those things hath mine hand made, and all those things have been, saith the LORD: but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word.

Meaning: God's real dwelling is not stone but a humble, contrite person. Stephen quotes this (Acts 7:48–50).

I will dwell in the midst of thee — Zechariah 2:10–11

10 Sing and rejoice, O daughter of Zion: for, lo, I come, and I will dwell in the midst of thee, saith the LORD. 11 And many nations shall be joined to the LORD in that day, and shall be my people: and I will dwell in the midst of thee, and thou shalt know that the LORD of hosts hath sent me unto thee.

Meaning: God comes to dwell, and "many nations" join His people. The dwelling widens to include the world.

The LORD is there — Ezekiel 43:7; 48:35

43:7 And he said unto me, Son of man, the place of my throne, and the place of the soles of my feet, where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever, and my holy name, shall the house of Israel no more defile, neither they, nor their kings, by their whoredom, nor by the carcases of their kings in their high places.

48:35 It was round about eighteen thousand measures: and the name of the city from that day shall be, The LORD is there.

Meaning: Ezekiel's book ends with the city's new name: YHWH Shammah. The whole story of Scripture is summed up in those words.

Writings (Ketuvim)

How amiable are Thy tabernacles — Psalm 84:1–4

1 How amiable are thy tabernacles, O LORD of hosts! 2 My soul longeth, yea, even fainteth for the courts of the LORD: my heart and my flesh crieth out for the living God. 3 Yea, the sparrow hath found an house, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her young, even thine altars, O LORD of hosts, my King, and my God. 4 Blessed are they that dwell in thy house: they will be still praising thee. Selah.

Meaning: God's dwelling is lovely and longed for. Even small birds find a home at His altars. The human heart was made for God's house.

This is My rest for ever — Psalm 132:13–14

13 For the LORD hath chosen Zion; he hath desired it for his habitation. 14 This is my rest for ever: here will I dwell; for I have desired it.

Meaning: Twice God "desired" (ivvah) His dwelling. It is His rest and His home by choice.

One thing have I desired — Psalm 27:4

4 One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to enquire in his temple.

Meaning: God desires to dwell with us; David desires to dwell with God. The two desires meet.

I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever — Psalm 23:6

6 Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.

Meaning: The Shepherd psalm ends at home. Life's journey leads to God's house forever.

Lord, Thou hast been our dwelling place — Psalm 90:1

1 Lord, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations.

Meaning: A surprising reversal: God is not only dwelling with us; He is our dwelling place. Mutual indwelling (John 15:4).

New Testament

The Word tabernacled among us — John 1:14, 51

14 And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

51 And he saith unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man.

Meaning: "Dwelt" is eskēnōsen, tabernacled. Jesus is God's tent among people, and Jacob's ladder — the true "gate of heaven."

The temple of His body — John 2:19–21

19 Jesus answered and said unto them, Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. 20 Then said the Jews, Forty and six years was this temple in building, and wilt thou rear it up in three days? 21 But he spake of the temple of his body.

Meaning: The building points to the Person. His body is the true temple, destroyed and raised.

We will make our abode — John 14:2–3, 23

2 In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. 3 And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.

23 Jesus answered and said unto him, If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him.

Meaning: Two dwellings: the Father's house prepared for believers (v. 2), and the Father and Son making their "abode" (monēn, same word as "mansions") in believers now (v. 23).

Ye are the temple of God — 1 Corinthians 3:16–17; 6:19–20

3:16 Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you? 3:17 If any man defile the temple of God, him shall God destroy; for the temple of God is holy, which temple ye are.

6:19 What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? 6:20 For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's.

Meaning: The church together (3:16, "ye" plural) and each believer's body (6:19) are God's sanctuary (naos).

An habitation of God through the Spirit — Ephesians 2:19–22

19 Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God; 20 And are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone; 21 In whom all the building fitly framed together groweth unto an holy temple in the Lord: 22 In whom ye also are builded together for an habitation of God through the Spirit.

Meaning: Jew and Gentile built into one growing temple, with Christ as cornerstone. "Habitation" (katoikētērion) is a permanent home.

Lively stones — 1 Peter 2:4–5

4 To whom coming, as unto a living stone, disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious, 5 Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ.

Meaning: Believers are both the stones and the priests of God's house.

The tabernacle of God is with men — Revelation 21:3, 22

3 And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.

22 And I saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it.

Meaning: The whole city becomes God's dwelling. No separate temple is needed because God and the Lamb fill everything. The blueprint's goal is reached.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. God walks with His people "Walking in the garden" (Genesis 3:8); "I will walk among you" (Leviticus 26:12) "I come, and I will dwell in the midst of thee" (Zechariah 2:10) "Thou art with me" (Psalm 23:4) "I will dwell in them, and walk in them" (2 Corinthians 6:16)
2. Built by God's pattern "After the pattern … shewed thee in the mount" (Exodus 25:9, 40) "Shew them the form of the house" (Ezekiel 43:10–11) "The pattern of all that he had by the spirit" (1 Chronicles 28:12) "The pattern shewed to thee in the mount" (Hebrews 8:5)
3. Glory fills the dwelling "The glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle" (Exodus 40:34) "The glory of the LORD filled the house" (Ezekiel 43:5) Glory fills Solomon's temple (2 Chronicles 7:1) Wind and fire fill the house at Pentecost (Acts 2:2–3)
4. Heaven cannot contain Him; He chooses the humble "In the place, O LORD, which thou hast made" (Exodus 15:17) "Heaven … cannot contain thee" (1 Kings 8:27); "to this man will I look" (Isaiah 66:2) "Who humbleth himself to behold" (Psalm 113:6) "The most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands" (Acts 7:48); "we will … make our abode with him" (John 14:23)
5. From tent to temple to people The tabernacle (Exodus 40) Solomon's temple (1 Kings 8) "The LORD hath chosen Zion" (Psalm 132:13) His body (John 2:21); the church (Ephesians 2:22); the city (Revelation 21:3)
6. The gate of heaven Beth-el, "the gate of heaven" (Genesis 28:17) "Open ye the gates, that the righteous nation … may enter" (Isaiah 26:2) "Lift up your heads, O ye gates" (Psalm 24:7) "Angels … ascending and descending upon the Son of man" (John 1:51)
7. A dwelling forever "The Sanctuary … which thy hands have established" (Exodus 15:17) "My tabernacle also shall be with them"; "The LORD is there" (Ezekiel 37:27; 48:35) "This is my rest for ever: here will I dwell" (Psalm 132:14) "The tabernacle of God is with men" (Revelation 21:3)

Principles

  1. God's goal has always been to live with His people. It is the purpose of the exodus, the covenant and the cross (Exodus 29:46; Revelation 21:3).
  2. God builds His own house. People cooperate, but He sets the pattern and does the building (2 Samuel 7:11; Matthew 16:18).
  3. Christ is the true temple. God's dwelling is a Person before it is a place (John 1:14; 2:21).
  4. The church is God's house now. Built together of living stones by the Spirit (Ephesians 2:22; 1 Peter 2:5).
  5. God dwells with the humble. No building contains Him; a contrite heart welcomes Him (Isaiah 66:2).
  6. The end is mutual indwelling. God in us, we in God, forever (Psalm 90:1; John 14:23; Revelation 21:3).
Principle Practice Anchor text
Living with God Practise awareness of God's presence at set times each day. Psalm 27:4; John 14:23

Christ the temple Come to God through Christ in prayer, not through places or rituals alone. John 2:21; 14:6
The church as God's house Commit to gathered worship; treat your congregation as God's dwelling. Ephesians 2:19–22

Your body a temple Honour God in how you treat your body. 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

For a teaching session: Trace God's dwelling through the five stages: Genesis 3:8, Exodus 40:34, 1 Kings 8:27, John 1:14, Ephesians 2:22, Revelation 21:3.
