The Divine Blueprint and Singleness

Overview

The Lord Jesus lived His whole earthly life unmarried, and so did Paul. Scripture never treats singleness as second best or a waiting room. It is a calling and a gift, in a season or for a lifetime, through which a believer can "attend upon the Lord without distraction" (1 Corinthians 7:35). Because every believer belongs to the bride of Christ (theme 21), single people are not outside the blueprint's central picture; they are inside it. Singleness points directly to the final reality: in the resurrection "they neither marry, nor are given in marriage" (Matthew 22:30).

This study walks singleness through the five groups of the blueprint:

  • A · Purpose: identity and completeness in Christ
  • B · Provision: what God supplies to single believers
  • C · People: who God shapes through singleness
  • D · Goal: belonging, family and friendship in God's household
  • E · Consummation: how the end gives singleness meaning now

For every reader. This study is for the never-married, the widowed, the divorced, and those waiting and hoping for marriage, and for married believers and churches who want to honour and include them.

Key passage — 1 Corinthians 7:7–8, 32–35

7 For I would that all men were even as I myself. But every man hath his proper gift of God, one after this manner, and another after that. 8 I say therefore to the unmarried and widows, It is good for them if they abide even as I.

32 But I would have you without carefulness. He that is unmarried careth for the things that belong to the Lord, how he may please the Lord: 33 But he that is married careth for the things that are of the world, how he may please his wife. 34 There is difference also between a wife and a virgin. The unmarried woman careth for the things of the Lord, that she may be holy both in body and in spirit: but she that is married careth for the things of the world, how she may please her husband. 35 And this I speak for your own profit; not that I may cast a snare upon you, but for that which is comely, and that ye may attend upon the Lord without distraction.

Meaning:

  • v. 7 Both marriage and singleness are a "proper gift of God" (charisma, a grace-gift). Neither is the higher spiritual state.
  • v. 8 Paul calls singleness "good," using the same word God used of creation.
  • vv. 32–34 Married life rightly involves care for a spouse; singleness can offer more undivided time and attention for "the things of the Lord."
  • v. 35 Paul's aim is freedom, "not that I may cast a snare upon you": an undistracted devotion to the Lord, whatever one's state.

Interpretive balance: Christians differ on the "gift" of singleness. Some see it as a special calling with a particular grace for lifelong celibacy. Others see it simply as the state a person is in, which God gives grace to live faithfully in, whether for a season or for life. All agree that singleness is honourable, that the desire for marriage is good (Proverbs 18:22), and that single believers are full members of God's family.

The five lenses

A · Purpose — complete in Christ

Ye are complete in him — Colossians 2:9–10

9 For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. 10 And ye are complete in him, which is the head of all principality and power:

Meaning: Completeness comes from union with Christ, not from a spouse. Every believer is "complete in him" (theme 5, the Son).

For the kingdom of heaven's sake — Matthew 19:11–12

11 But he said unto them, All men cannot receive this saying, save they to whom it is given. 12 For there be some eunuchs, which were so born from their mother's womb: and there are some eunuchs, which were made eunuchs of men: and there be eunuchs, which have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven's sake. He that is able to receive it, let him receive it.

Meaning: Jesus names three paths to singleness: by birth, by circumstance, and by choice for the kingdom. All can serve the kingdom's purposes (theme 20). Jesus Himself lived this way.

The unseen side. "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven" (Matthew 22:30). The single believer's life already points to the unseen age to come, when union with Christ fills every longing. Undivided devotion to the Lord (1 Corinthians 7:32–35) is a visible sign of that invisible reality.

B · Provision — what God supplies

A place and a name better than of sons — Isaiah 56:3–5

3 Neither let the son of the stranger, that hath joined himself to the LORD, speak, saying, The LORD hath utterly separated me from his people: neither let the eunuch say, Behold, I am a dry tree. 4 For thus saith the LORD unto the eunuchs that keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that please me, and take hold of my covenant; 5 Even unto them will I give in mine house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters: I will give them an everlasting name, that shall not be cut off.

Meaning: In a culture where a name lived on through children, God promises the childless an "everlasting name" in His house. Their legacy is secured by God's covenant (theme 11), not by descendants.

An hundredfold now in this time — Mark 10:29–30

29 And Jesus answered and said, Verily I say unto you, There is no man that hath left house, or brethren, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my sake, and the gospel's, 30 But he shall receive an hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life.

Meaning: Jesus promises a family "now in this time": brothers, sisters, mothers and children in the church. God's provision for single people comes through His people.

C · People — who God shapes through singleness

Holy both in body and in spirit — 1 Thessalonians 4:3–4

3 For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication: 4 That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour;

Meaning: Holiness (theme 17) includes the body. Single believers honour God through chastity, which is not denial for its own sake but devotion to Him.

She served God … night and day — Luke 2:36–38

36 And there was one Anna, a prophetess, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Aser: she was of a great age, and had lived with an husband seven years from her virginity; 37 And she was a widow of about fourscore and four years, which departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day. 38 And she coming in that instant gave thanks likewise unto the Lord, and spake of him to all them that looked for redemption in Jerusalem.

Meaning: Anna, widowed for decades, became a woman of prayer and one of the first to proclaim the infant Christ. Undistracted devotion bears fruit.

D · Goal — belonging in God's household

God setteth the solitary in families — Psalm 68:5–6

5 A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation. 6 God setteth the solitary in families: he bringeth out those which are bound with chains: but the rebellious dwell in a dry land.

Meaning: God's goal for the lonely is family. His "holy habitation" (theme 19, Dwelling) is a home for the solitary.

Thy people shall be my people — Ruth 1:16–17

16 And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God: 17 Where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the LORD do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me.

Meaning: Two widows bound by covenant friendship. Deep, committed belonging is possible outside marriage.

Knit with the soul of David — 1 Samuel 18:1, 3

1 And it came to pass, when he had made an end of speaking unto Saul, that the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.

3 Then Jonathan and David made a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul.

Meaning: Scripture honours deep friendship. Single believers need, and can give, friendships of this depth.

E · Consummation — how the end gives singleness meaning

The time is short — 1 Corinthians 7:29, 31

29 But this I say, brethren, the time is short: it remaineth, that both they that have wives be as though they had none;

31 And they that use this world, as not abusing it: for the fashion of this world passeth away.

Meaning: Every earthly state, married or single, is temporary. "The fashion of this world passeth away."

Neither marry, nor are given in marriage — Matthew 22:30; Revelation 19:9

Matthew 22:30 For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven.

Revelation 19:9 And he saith unto me, Write, Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb. And he saith unto me, These are the true sayings of God.

Meaning: Single believers live now as a sign of the age to come. Everyone is invited to the one marriage that lasts: the marriage of the Lamb.

Common struggles, and what the blueprint says

Struggle What the blueprint says Anchor text
Loneliness God sets the solitary in families and is present to the desolate. Loneliness is real, and so is His nearness. Psalm 68:6; 25:16; Hebrews 13:5
Feeling second-class in church God promises "a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters." Jesus and Paul were single. Isaiah 56:3–5; 1 Corinthians 7:7–8
Longing for marriage that hasn't come The desire is good and can be brought honestly to God. "Hope deferred" is acknowledged in Scripture. Proverbs 13:12; 18:22; Psalm 37:4
Sexual temptation God makes a way of escape; holiness in body and spirit is His will and His work in us. 1 Corinthians 10:13; 1 Thessalonians 4:3–4
Pressure from family or community to marry Seek God's approval, not men's; honour parents while following God's leading. Galatians 1:10; Exodus 20:12
Widowhood God "relieveth the fatherless and widow"; the church is to honour and care for widows. Psalm 146:9; 1 Timothy 5:3, 5
After divorce God is near the brokenhearted, and there is no condemnation in Christ; the church is family. Psalm 34:18; Romans 8:1
Anxiety about the future (who will care for me?) The Father knows our needs; Jesus provided for His mother through His church family. Matthew 6:34; John 19:26–27

Practices

For yourself

Practice Anchor text
Use some undivided time for a regular rhythm of prayer and service. 1 Corinthians 7:35; Luke 2:37
Bring your desires and loneliness honestly to God in prayer; lament is allowed. Psalm 25:16; 62:8
Invest in one or two deep, covenant-like friendships. 1 Samuel 18:1; Ruth 1:16
Set clear boundaries for purity, with someone to be accountable to. 1 Thessalonians 4:3–4; James 5:16
Practise hospitality; singleness can be a gift for opening your home. Romans 12:13

For friends and family

Practice Anchor text
Stop treating singleness as a problem to fix; ask about life and calling, not only marriage. 1 Corinthians 7:7
Invite single friends into family life: meals, holidays, birthdays. Psalm 68:6; Mark 10:30

For the church

Practice Anchor text
Use single members' gifts in leadership and ministry. 1 Corinthians 7:32–35
Make teaching and events inclusive, not only couple-centred. Isaiah 56:5
Care for widows practically and regularly. James 1:27; 1 Timothy 5:3
Plan for older single members: visits, transport, care. John 19:26–27

5 min Response and prayer Psalm 68:5–6 Singles choose one personal practice; families and the church choose one way to include.

Discussion questions

  1. What messages, spoken or unspoken, does our culture or church give about singleness?
  2. How does "ye are complete in him" (Colossians 2:10) speak to the idea that marriage completes a person?
  3. What does Isaiah 56:5 promise those without children? Why would that matter so much?
  4. How can the church be the "hundredfold" family Jesus promised (Mark 10:30)?
  5. What can we learn from Anna (Luke 2:36–38) about using a season of singleness or widowhood?
  6. Ruth and Naomi, and David and Jonathan, show deep friendship. How can we build friendships like that?
  7. How does Matthew 22:30 change the way we see both marriage and singleness?
  8. Which one practice from this study will you start this week?
