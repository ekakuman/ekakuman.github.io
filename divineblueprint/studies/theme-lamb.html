The Lamb of God

Overview and word study

The Lamb is God's provision for the blueprint, foreknown "before the foundation of the world" (1 Peter 1:20) and enthroned at its end (Revelation 22:1). Abraham's question, "where is the lamb?" (Genesis 22:7), runs through the Old Testament until John the Baptist answers it: "Behold the Lamb of God" (John 1:29). The book of Revelation names Christ "the Lamb" 28 times.

The striking contrast in Revelation: arnion is a small, gentle word, yet this Lamb holds the scroll of history, receives worship, and makes war (Revelation 5:6–13; 17:14). Strength through apparent weakness is the Lamb's signature.

Torah

The Torah builds the Lamb's portrait step by step: an accepted offering, a lamb God provides in place of a son, a lamb whose blood turns judgment aside, and lambs offered every morning and evening.

The firstlings of the flock — Genesis 4:3–5

3 And in process of time it came to pass, that Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD. 4 And Abel, he also brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof. And the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering: 5 But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell.

Meaning: The first offering from the flock is accepted. Hebrews 11:4 says Abel offered "by faith" a more excellent sacrifice, and Hebrews 12:24 says Christ's blood "speaketh better things than that of Abel."

God will provide Himself a lamb — Genesis 22:7–8, 13–14

7 And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering? 8 And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.

13 And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son. 14 And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovahjireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.

Meaning: The Bible's first "lamb" (seh) is a question. A ram is provided "in the stead of" Isaac — substitution. "God will provide himself" can be read "God will provide Himself as the lamb." Moriah is the region where Solomon's temple stood (2 Chronicles 3:1), near where Jesus was crucified.

The Passover lamb — Exodus 12:3, 5–7, 12–13

3 Speak ye unto all the congregation of Israel, saying, In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house:

5 Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year: ye shall take it out from the sheep, or from the goats: 6 And ye shall keep it up until the fourteenth day of the same month: and the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it in the evening. 7 And they shall take of the blood, and strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post of the houses, wherein they shall eat it.

12 For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD. 13 And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.

Meaning: A lamb for each house, without blemish, set apart in advance, killed, its blood applied, its flesh eaten. Safety depended on the blood, not on the household's merit. John notes that none of Jesus' bones were broken "that the scripture should be fulfilled" (John 19:36; Exodus 12:46).

Lambs morning and evening — Exodus 29:38–39, 42–43

38 Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar; two lambs of the first year day by day continually. 39 The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even:

42 This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD: where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee. 43 And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory.

Meaning: Every day began and ended with a lamb. The purpose is fellowship: "where I will meet you" and glory filling the place. The Lamb makes God's dwelling with people possible.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets turn the lamb from an animal into a Person: a silent, suffering Servant who bears others' sins.

As a lamb to the slaughter — Isaiah 53:4–7, 10–11

4 Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. 5 But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. 6 All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. 7 He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.

10 Yet it pleased the LORD to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in his hand. 11 He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied: by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities.

Meaning: The heart of the Lamb theme in the Old Testament. We are the straying sheep; He is the Lamb on whom our iniquity is "laid" (hipgia, made to meet). "An offering for sin" is asham, the guilt offering. He dies, yet "shall prolong his days" — resurrection. Philip preached Jesus from verse 7 (Acts 8:32–35).

A lamb brought to the slaughter — Jeremiah 11:19

19 But I was like a lamb or an ox that is brought to the slaughter; and I knew not that they had devised devices against me, saying, Let us destroy the tree with the fruit thereof, and let us cut him off from the land of the living, that his name may be no more remembered.

Meaning: Jeremiah's own suffering at the hands of his townsmen foreshadows the innocent One plotted against and "cut off from the land of the living" (compare Isaiah 53:8).

Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings show the Lamb from the inside: the willing heart that replaces animal sacrifice, the Messiah "cut off, but not for himself," and the Shepherd who will one day be revealed as the Lamb.

Lo, I come — Psalm 40:6–8

6 Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire; mine ears hast thou opened: burnt offering and sin offering hast thou not required. 7 Then said I, Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me, 8 I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.

Meaning: Animal sacrifices could not satisfy God's desire. Hebrews 10:5–10 reads these as Christ's words on entering the world: "a body hast thou prepared me." The true Lamb comes willingly, not dragged to the altar.

Messiah cut off, but not for himself — Daniel 9:26

26 And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the end thereof shall be with a flood, and unto the end of the war desolations are determined.

Meaning: "Cut off" (karat) is the language of death and covenant-making. "Not for himself" — He dies for others. The city and sanctuary are destroyed afterward, fulfilled in AD 70.

The Shepherd who is the Lamb — Psalm 23:1–3

1 The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. 2 He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters. 3 He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake.

Meaning: A striking reversal in Revelation 7:17: "the Lamb which is in the midst of the throne shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters." The Lamb becomes the Shepherd. Because He was led to the slaughter, He can lead His people to the waters.

New Testament

The New Testament names Jesus as the Lamb, sets His death at Passover, roots it in God's eternal plan, and shows the Lamb enthroned, worshipped, shepherding, marrying, and lighting the new creation.

Behold the Lamb of God — John 1:29, 35–36

29 The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

35 Again the next day after John stood, and two of his disciples; 36 And looking upon Jesus as he walked, he saith, Behold the Lamb of God!

Meaning: The answer to Isaac's question. "Of God" means provided by God (Genesis 22:8). He "taketh away" (airōn, lifts and carries off) the sin of "the world" — wider than one household (Exodus 12) or one nation.

Not a bone broken — John 19:14, 33, 36

14 And it was the preparation of the passover, and about the sixth hour: and he saith unto the Jews, Behold your King!

33 But when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs:

36 For these things were done, that the scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken.

Meaning: John times Jesus' death to the preparation of Passover, when lambs were being slain, and applies the Passover rule (Exodus 12:46) to Him.

Christ our passover — 1 Corinthians 5:7–8

7 Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: 8 Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

Meaning: Because the Lamb is already sacrificed, believers "keep the feast" — the Christian life is a continuing Passover celebration, marked by sincerity and truth instead of malice.

A lamb foreordained — 1 Peter 1:18–20

18 Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; 19 But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot: 20 Who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you,

Meaning: The Lamb is the bridge between eternity and history: foreknown before the world, manifested in time. "Without blemish and without spot" — amōmou kai aspilou — inwardly and outwardly pure.

Worthy is the Lamb — Revelation 5:6, 9–12

6 And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth.

9 And they sung a new song, saying, Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation; 10 And hast made us unto our God kings and priests: and we shall reign on the earth. 11 And I beheld, and I heard the voice of many angels round about the throne and the beasts and the elders: and the number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands; 12 Saying with a loud voice, Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing.

Meaning: John is told to look for a Lion (5:5) and sees a Lamb. Standing (alive), yet bearing the marks of slaughter. Seven horns (full power) and seven eyes (full knowledge). He alone opens the scroll of history. His blood redeems people from every nation and makes them "kings and priests" — Exodus 19:6 fulfilled.

The Lamb shall feed them — Revelation 7:14, 17

14 And I said unto him, Sir, thou knowest. And he said to me, These are they which came out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.

17 For the Lamb which is in the midst of the throne shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters: and God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes.

Meaning: A paradox: robes made white in red blood. The Lamb becomes the Shepherd (Psalm 23), leading to living water and wiping away tears.

The marriage of the Lamb — Revelation 19:7–9

7 Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready. 8 And to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints. 9 And he saith unto me, Write, Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb. And he saith unto me, These are the true sayings of God.

Meaning: The Lamb's story ends in a wedding. The Passover meal becomes a marriage supper — sacrifice leads to union (theme 21).

The throne of God and of the Lamb — Revelation 22:1, 3

1 And he shewed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb.

3 And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:

Meaning: In eternity the Lamb shares the throne. The river of life flows from the place of sacrifice-turned-sovereignty. The curse of Genesis 3 is gone.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. God provides the Lamb "God will provide himself a lamb" (Genesis 22:8) "The LORD hath laid on him" (Isaiah 53:6) "A body hast thou prepared me" — Psalm 40:6 in Hebrews 10:5 "The Lamb of God" (John 1:29); "foreordained" (1 Peter 1:20)
2. Substitution — one for many The ram "in the stead of his son" (Genesis 22:13) "Wounded for our transgressions" (Isaiah 53:5) "Cut off, but not for himself" (Daniel 9:26) "Christ our passover is sacrificed for us" (1 Corinthians 5:7)
3. Without blemish "Your lamb shall be without blemish" (Exodus 12:5) "He had done no violence, neither was any deceit" (Isaiah 53:9) "I delight to do thy will" (Psalm 40:8) "Without blemish and without spot" (1 Peter 1:19)
4. Blood that turns judgment aside "When I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13) "With his stripes we are healed" (Isaiah 53:5) — "Redeemed us to God by thy blood" (Revelation 5:9)
5. Silent and willing Isaac goes "both of them together" (Genesis 22:8) "He openeth not his mouth" (Isaiah 53:7) "Lo, I come" (Psalm 40:7) "Brought as a sheep to the slaughter" — Philip to the eunuch (Acts 8:32)
6. The Lamb opens the way to meeting God "Where I will meet you" (Exodus 29:42) "He shall see his seed" (Isaiah 53:10) "The LORD is my shepherd" (Psalm 23:1) "The Lamb … shall lead them unto living fountains" (Revelation 7:17)
7. Slain Lamb, reigning Lamb Passover leads to exodus and freedom (Exodus 12:31) "He shall prolong his days" (Isaiah 53:10) — "Worthy is the Lamb that was slain" (Revelation 5:12); "the throne of God and of the Lamb" (Revelation 22:1)

Principles

  1. The Lamb was God's plan, not His reaction. Foreknown before the foundation, provided by God Himself (1 Peter 1:20; Genesis 22:8).
  2. Salvation is by substitution. One innocent dies for the guilty (Isaiah 53:5–6).
  3. Safety is in the blood, not in our worthiness. "When I see the blood" (Exodus 12:13).
  4. The Lamb is personally applied. A lamb "for an house," its blood applied, its flesh eaten — faith receives Him personally (Exodus 12:3–8; John 6:54).
  5. The slain Lamb is the reigning Lamb. Weakness and power meet in Him (Revelation 5:6).
  6. The Lamb leads to fellowship, feasting and marriage. Sacrifice opens the way to God's presence and union (Exodus 29:42; Revelation 19:9).

For a teaching session: Trace the question "Where is the lamb?" (Genesis 22:7) through Exodus 12, Isaiah 53, John 1:29 and Revelation 5:6.
