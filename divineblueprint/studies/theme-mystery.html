The Mystery of God

Overview

In Scripture, a mystery is not a puzzle no one can solve. It is a part of God's plan that was hidden in Him and is now revealed at His chosen time. Paul calls it "the mystery which hath been hid from ages and from generations, but now is made manifest to his saints" (Colossians 1:26).

The mystery is the content of the blueprint. Its core is stated three ways:

  • All things headed up in Christ — "the mystery of his will … that … he might gather together in one all things in Christ" (Ephesians 1:9–10).
  • Jew and Gentile in one body — "that the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body" (Ephesians 3:6).
  • Christ living in His people — "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (Colossians 1:27).

The Old Testament contains the seeds — one flesh in marriage, a promised Seed, blessing for all nations — but not the full picture. The prophets "enquired and searched diligently" (1 Peter 1:10). The Spirit has now revealed it to apostles and prophets (Ephesians 3:5), and it will be "finished" at the end (Revelation 10:7). Passages below are given in full from the King James Version.

2. Torah

The Torah separates hidden from revealed things and plants seeds that Paul later calls mysteries: one flesh in marriage, a promised Seed, and blessing to all families.

Secret things and revealed things — Deuteronomy 29:29

29 The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.

Meaning: The foundational distinction. What God keeps hidden (nistarot) is His; what He reveals (niglot) is given to be lived out across generations. A biblical mystery moves from the first category to the second at God's time.

One flesh — Genesis 2:21–24

21 And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof; 22 And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man. 23 And Adam said, This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man. 24 Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.

Meaning: Paul quotes verse 24 and says, "This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church" (Ephesians 5:31–32). The first marriage was a hidden picture: a bride taken from the side of a sleeping man, foreshadowing the church born from the pierced side of Christ (John 19:34).

The Seed in a riddle — Genesis 3:15

15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.

Meaning: A promise in veiled form. Who is the Seed? How will a heel-wound crush a head? Only the cross makes it plain (Galatians 4:4; Hebrews 2:14).

All families blessed — Genesis 12:3; 22:18

12:3 And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

22:18 And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.

Meaning: The inclusion of Gentiles was promised from the start, but how they would share fully as "fellowheirs, and of the same body" (Ephesians 3:6) was the hidden part. Paul says Scripture "preached before the gospel unto Abraham" (Galatians 3:8).

Mouth to mouth, not dark speeches — Numbers 12:6–8

6 And he said, Hear now my words: If there be a prophet among you, I the LORD will make myself known unto him in a vision, and will speak unto him in a dream. 7 My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house. 8 With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches; and the similitude of the LORD shall he behold: wherefore then were ye not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?

Meaning: God reveals in degrees: visions and riddles (chidot) to some, plain speech to Moses. In Christ, revelation is plainest of all: "he that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9).

3. Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets show God revealing His secret before He acts, hiding Himself even while He saves, preparing what no eye has seen, and announcing new things kept until their time.

His secret revealed to the prophets — Amos 3:6–8

6 Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid? shall there be evil in a city, and the LORD hath not done it? 7 Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets. 8 The lion hath roared, who will not fear? the Lord GOD hath spoken, who can but prophesy?

Meaning: "Secret" is sod. God's habit is disclosure before action. Once He speaks, His servants must pass it on (v. 8) — revelation creates responsibility.

A God that hideth Himself — Isaiah 45:15–17

15 Verily thou art a God that hideth thyself, O God of Israel, the Saviour. 16 They shall be ashamed, and also confounded, all of them: they shall go to confusion together that are makers of idols. 17 But Israel shall be saved in the LORD with an everlasting salvation: ye shall not be ashamed nor confounded world without end.

Meaning: God "hides" (mistatter) even as He saves. His ways are not obvious, yet His salvation is everlasting. Hiddenness is part of how He works, not a sign of absence.

Hidden things, now shewed — Isaiah 48:6–7

6 Thou hast heard, see all this; and will not ye declare it? I have shewed thee new things from this time, even hidden things, and thou didst not know them. 7 They are created now, and not from the beginning; even before the day when thou heardest them not; lest thou shouldest say, Behold, I knew them.

Meaning: "Hidden things" is netsurot, things kept guarded. God reveals new things at their time so that no one can claim to have figured them out. Revelation keeps people humble.

Eye hath not seen — Isaiah 64:4

4 For since the beginning of the world men have not heard, nor perceived by the ear, neither hath the eye seen, O God, beside thee, what he hath prepared for him that waiteth for him.

Meaning: What God has prepared is beyond human sight and hearing. Paul quotes this verse in 1 Corinthians 2:9, then adds: "But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit" (2:10). The hidden becomes known by the Spirit.

Great and mighty things — Jeremiah 33:2–3

2 Thus saith the LORD the maker thereof, the LORD that formed it, to establish it; the LORD is his name; 3 Call unto me, and I will answer thee, and shew thee great and mighty things, which thou knowest not.

Meaning: "Mighty" is betsurot, fortified or inaccessible things. They are opened in answer to prayer. Mystery is an invitation to call on God.

4. Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings introduce the word raz (secret) that Paul later builds on, celebrate God's right to conceal, speak of "dark sayings of old," and seal some revelation until the time of the end.

A God in heaven that revealeth secrets — Daniel 2:18–19, 27–28, 44, 47

18 That they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret; that Daniel and his fellows should not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon. 19 Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

27 Daniel answered in the presence of the king, and said, The secret which the king hath demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, shew unto the king; 28 But there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days. Thy dream, and the visions of thy head upon thy bed, are these;

44 And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever.

47 The king answered unto Daniel, and said, Of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret.

Meaning: The secret (raz) comes through prayer (v. 18), not human expertise (v. 27). Its content is the kingdom of God that outlasts every empire (v. 44) — the same kingdom Jesus calls a "mystery" in Matthew 13:11.

Dark sayings of old — Psalm 78:1–4

1 Give ear, O my people, to my law: incline your ears to the words of my mouth. 2 I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old: 3 Which we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us. 4 We will not hide them from the children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done.

Meaning: "Dark sayings" are chidot, riddles. Israel's history carries hidden lessons. Matthew 13:35 says Jesus' parables fulfil verse 2, uttering "things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world." Verse 4 adds the duty: do not hide them from the next generation.

The glory of God to conceal — Proverbs 25:2

2 It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter.

Meaning: Concealing is God's glory; searching is the dignity of royalty. Believers, a "royal priesthood" (1 Peter 2:9), are invited to search out what God has hidden for them to find.

Can you find out God? — Job 11:7–8

7 Canst thou by searching find out God? canst thou find out the Almighty unto perfection? 8 It is as high as heaven; what canst thou do? deeper than hell; what canst thou know?

Meaning: Human searching alone cannot reach God's depths. Only His self-revelation can — which is why Paul says the Spirit "searcheth … the deep things of God" (1 Corinthians 2:10).

Sealed till the time of the end — Daniel 12:4, 8–9

4 But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased.

8 And I heard, but I understood not: then said I, O my Lord, what shall be the end of these things? 9 And he said, Go thy way, Daniel: for the words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end.

Meaning: Even a prophet did not understand everything he received. Some revelation is timed. Revelation 5 shows the Lamb alone worthy to open the sealed book, and Revelation 22:10 says "Seal not" — the time has come.

5. New Testament

The New Testament opens the mystery: given to disciples, kept secret for ages and now made known, revealed by the Spirit, centered on Christ, embracing Jew and Gentile in one body, pictured in marriage, lived as "Christ in you," and finished at the end.

The mysteries of the kingdom — Matthew 13:10–11, 34–35

10 And the disciples came, and said unto him, Why speakest thou unto them in parables? 11 He answered and said unto them, Because it is given unto you to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it is not given.

34 All these things spake Jesus unto the multitude in parables; and without a parable spake he not unto them: 35 That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things which have been kept secret from the foundation of the world.

Meaning: Understanding is "given," not earned. Parables reveal to the willing and conceal from the hardened. The kingdom of Daniel 2:44 comes first in hidden form — seed, leaven, treasure in a field.

Kept secret, now made manifest — Romans 16:25–27

25 Now to him that is of power to stablish you according to my gospel, and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began, 26 But now is made manifest, and by the scriptures of the prophets, according to the commandment of the everlasting God, made known to all nations for the obedience of faith: 27 To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen.

Meaning: Romans closes with the mystery: silent (sesigēmenou) through the ages, now revealed through "the scriptures of the prophets" — the Old Testament read in light of Christ. Its goal is "the obedience of faith" among "all nations," and it ends in worship.

Revealed by the Spirit — 1 Corinthians 2:7–10

7 But we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, even the hidden wisdom, which God ordained before the world unto our glory: 8 Which none of the princes of this world knew: for had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. 9 But as it is written, Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared for them that love him. 10 But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit: for the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God.

Meaning: The cross was part of the hidden wisdom — had the powers understood it, they would not have crucified Christ, because the crucifixion defeated them (Colossians 2:15). The Spirit, who searches God's depths, is the One who reveals.

Gentiles fellow heirs — Ephesians 3:3–6, 8–10

3 How that by revelation he made known unto me the mystery; (as I wrote afore in few words, 4 Whereby, when ye read, ye may understand my knowledge in the mystery of Christ) 5 Which in other ages was not made known unto the sons of men, as it is now revealed unto his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit; 6 That the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body, and partakers of his promise in Christ by the gospel:

8 Unto me, who am less than the least of all saints, is this grace given, that I should preach among the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ; 9 And to make all men see what is the fellowship of the mystery, which from the beginning of the world hath been hid in God, who created all things by Jesus Christ: 10 To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly places might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God,

Meaning: Three "together" words in verse 6 (synklēronoma, syssōma, symmetocha): joint-heirs, joint-body, joint-sharers. Gentile blessing was foretold (Genesis 12:3), but full equality in one body was the hidden part. The church displays this mystery to heavenly powers.

Christ and the church — Ephesians 5:30–32

30 For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones. 31 For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh. 32 This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.

Meaning: Genesis 2:24 had a hidden meaning all along. Human marriage is a living picture of Christ's union with His people — linking this theme to theme 21, the bride.

Christ in you — Colossians 1:25–28

25 Whereof I am made a minister, according to the dispensation of God which is given to me for you, to fulfil the word of God; 26 Even the mystery which hath been hid from ages and from generations, but now is made manifest to his saints: 27 To whom God would make known what is the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles; which is Christ in you, the hope of glory: 28 Whom we preach, warning every man, and teaching every man in all wisdom; that we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus:

Meaning: The mystery in four words: "Christ in you, the hope of glory." Not only Christ for us or with us, but within. "To fulfil the word of God" (v. 25) means to complete its message. The aim is every person "perfect" (teleion, mature) in Christ.

Great is the mystery of godliness — 1 Timothy 3:16

16 And without controversy great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory.

Meaning: Probably an early hymn. Six lines trace Christ's story from incarnation to ascension. The mystery of godliness is a Person.

We shall all be changed — 1 Corinthians 15:51–52

51 Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, 52 In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.

Meaning: A future mystery now announced: the bodily transformation of believers at Christ's return.

What the prophets searched — 1 Peter 1:10–12

10 Of which salvation the prophets have enquired and searched diligently, who prophesied of the grace that should come unto you: 11 Searching what, or what manner of time the Spirit of Christ which was in them did signify, when it testified beforehand the sufferings of Christ, and the glory that should follow. 12 Unto whom it was revealed, that not unto themselves, but unto us they did minister the things, which are now reported unto you by them that have preached the gospel unto you with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven; which things the angels desire to look into.

Meaning: The prophets knew they held something they did not fully understand. "The Spirit of Christ" was speaking through them. Even angels "desire to look into" (parakypsai, stoop to peer into) what believers now know.

The mystery finished — Revelation 10:7

7 But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished, as he hath declared to his servants the prophets.

Meaning: "Finished" is etelesthē, completed. The same root as Jesus' cry "It is finished" (John 19:30). What God declared to the prophets (Amos 3:7) reaches its completion at the last trumpet (Revelation 11:15).

7. Principles and practices

  1. Mystery means revealed secret, not unknowable puzzle. What was hidden is now made known to God's people (Colossians 1:26).
  2. God reveals on His timetable. Some things were sealed for ages; some are still future (Daniel 12:9; 1 Corinthians 15:51).
  3. Revelation comes by the Spirit, not by cleverness. Human searching alone cannot find out God (Job 11:7); the Spirit reveals His depths (1 Corinthians 2:10).
  4. The Old Testament holds the seeds. Read it expecting to find Christ and the church in early form (Luke 24:27; 1 Peter 1:10–12).
  5. The mystery is Christ, and Christ in us. Its core is a Person living in His people (Colossians 1:27).
  6. The church is the mystery on display. Jew and Gentile in one body show God's wisdom to heavenly powers (Ephesians 3:6, 10).
  7. Revealed truth brings both responsibility and worship. We must pass it on (Psalm 78:4; Amos 3:8) and bow before what remains beyond us (Romans 11:33; Deuteronomy 29:29).
Principle Practice Anchor text
1. Revealed secret When reading Paul's letters, mark every use of "mystery" and write what is revealed in each. Ephesians 3:3–6
2. God's timetable Hold unanswered questions loosely; write them down and leave them with God rather than forcing answers. Deuteronomy 29:29; Daniel 12:8–9
3. By the Spirit Before studying Scripture, pray Psalm 119:18: "Open thou mine eyes." 1 Corinthians 2:10–12
4. Seeds in the Old Testament Read one Old Testament passage each week asking, "How does this point to Christ and His church?" Luke 24:27; 1 Peter 1:10–12
5. Christ in you Begin each day by affirming Colossians 1:27 and asking Christ to live through you that day. Colossians 1:27; Galatians 2:20
6. One body Build at least one real friendship across ethnic or cultural lines in your church. Ephesians 3:6; 2:14
7. Responsibility and worship Share one thing God has shown you with the next generation each month; close study times in praise (Romans 11:33–36). Psalm 78:4; Romans 11:33

For a teaching session: Read Daniel 2:27–28 and Ephesians 3:3–6. Ask: what does a "mystery" mean in each passage, how was it revealed, and what is its content? Close by reading Colossians 1:27 together.
