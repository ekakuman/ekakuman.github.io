All Things Headed Up in Christ

Overview

Ephesians 1:9–10 states the whole Divine Blueprint in one sentence. God made known "the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he hath purposed in himself: That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him." Every earlier theme — purpose, counsel, the Lamb, the covenant, the kingdom, the bride — comes together here. What sin divided (God and man, heaven and earth, Jew and Gentile, man and creation), God reunites under one Head.

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. Unity broken — Eden's harmony lost; Babel's false unity scattered
  2. A gathering promised — to Shiloh "shall the gathering of the people be"
  3. One Shepherd, one stick, one Lord — the prophets foresee a reunited people
  4. Christ the Head — all things created by Him, held together in Him, reconciled through Him
  5. God all in all — every knee bows; the Son delivers the kingdom to the Father

Semantic note: Anakephalaioō was used for summing up an argument or adding a column of figures under one total. God's plan is to bring all things to their total, their "bottom line," in Christ. Oikonomia pictures God as the master of a household, carrying out His plan in stages until the "fulness of times" (Galatians 4:4).

Torah

A false unity scattered — Genesis 11:1, 4, 9

1 And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.

4 And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.

9 Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.

Meaning: Humanity tried to unite heaven and earth around its own name, and God scattered them. True unity can come only around God's Name, in God's Head. Pentecost begins the reversal (Acts 2:5–11).

Heaven and earth joined — Genesis 28:12–14

12 And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it. 13 And, behold, the LORD stood above it, and said, I am the LORD God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac: the land whereon thou liest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed; 14 And thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south: and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.

Meaning: Where Babel's tower failed, God provides His own ladder. Jesus says He is that ladder (John 1:51). Through the Seed, "all the families of the earth" are gathered into blessing.

Unto Him shall the gathering be — Genesis 49:10

10 The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be.

Meaning: The coming Ruler from Judah is the gathering point for the peoples, both Israel and the nations.

The LORD is one — Deuteronomy 6:4–5

4 Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: 5 And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.

Meaning: The unity of all things rests on the oneness of God. Because He is one, His creation is meant to be one under Him (1 Corinthians 8:6).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

One head — Hosea 1:10–11

10 Yet the number of the children of Israel shall be as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered; and it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people, there it shall be said unto them, Ye are the sons of the living God. 11 Then shall the children of Judah and the children of Israel be gathered together, and appoint themselves one head, and they shall come up out of the land: for great shall be the day of Jezreel.

Meaning: The divided kingdom is gathered under "one head." Paul applies verse 10 to Gentiles too (Romans 9:25–26). The headship that Ephesians 1:10 describes is already foreshadowed here.

An ensign gathering the nations — Isaiah 11:10, 12

10 And in that day there shall be a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people; to it shall the Gentiles seek: and his rest shall be glorious.

12 And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth.

Meaning: The Messiah is the banner around which both scattered Israel and the nations gather (John 12:32).

Every knee shall bow — Isaiah 45:22–23

22 Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth: for I am God, and there is none else. 23 I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and shall not return, That unto me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear.

Meaning: Paul applies this oath about the LORD to Jesus (Philippians 2:10–11). The final unity is universal acknowledgment of Him.

One nation, one king, one shepherd — Ezekiel 37:21–22, 24

21 And say unto them, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I will take the children of Israel from among the heathen, whither they be gone, and will gather them on every side, and bring them into their own land: 22 And I will make them one nation in the land upon the mountains of Israel; and one king shall be king to them all: and they shall be no more two nations, neither shall they be divided into two kingdoms any more at all:

24 And David my servant shall be king over them; and they all shall have one shepherd: they shall also walk in my judgments, and observe my statutes, and do them.

Meaning: The two sticks become one in God's hand (37:19). Jesus: "there shall be one fold, and one shepherd" (John 10:16).

A pure language — Zephaniah 3:9

9 For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the LORD, to serve him with one consent.

Meaning: Babel reversed. The scattered peoples are brought back to one worship, "with one consent" (literally "one shoulder").

One LORD, and His name one — Zechariah 14:9

9 And the LORD shall be king over all the earth: in that day shall there be one LORD, and his name one.

Meaning: Deuteronomy 6:4 becomes the confession of the whole earth.

Writings (Ketuvim)

Wisdom at the Creator's side — Proverbs 8:27–31

27 When he prepared the heavens, I was there: when he set a compass upon the face of the depth: 28 When he established the clouds above: when he strengthened the fountains of the deep: 29 When he gave to the sea his decree, that the waters should not pass his commandment: when he appointed the foundations of the earth: 30 Then I was by him, as one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him; 31 Rejoicing in the habitable part of his earth; and my delights were with the sons of men.

Meaning: Wisdom stands beside God at creation, delighting in the world and in humanity. The New Testament reveals Christ as "the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24), by whom and for whom all things were made (Colossians 1:16). The One through whom creation began is the One in whom it is summed up.

All things under His feet — Psalm 8:4–6; 110:1

8:4 What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him? 5 For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour. 6 Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet:

110:1 The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.

Meaning: Paul joins these two psalms in Ephesians 1:20–22 and 1 Corinthians 15:25–27. What was given to Adam and lost is fulfilled in the exalted Christ.

All the ends of the world shall turn — Psalm 22:27–28

27 All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD: and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before thee. 28 For the kingdom is the LORD's: and he is the governor among the nations.

Meaning: The psalm of the cross (22:1, 16–18) ends with every nation gathered in worship. The gathering comes through suffering.

Ointment upon the head — Psalm 133

1 Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity! 2 It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron's beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments; 3 As the dew of Hermon, and as the dew that descended upon the mountains of Zion: for there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore.

Meaning: Unity flows down from the anointed head to the whole body. This is the very image of Christ the Head and His Church (Ephesians 4:15–16).

All people, nations and languages — Daniel 7:14

14 And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.

Meaning: Babel's scattered "languages" are united in serving the Son of man.

New Testament

Gather together in one the children of God — John 11:51–52; 10:16

11:51 And this spake he not of himself: but being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation; 52 And not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad.

10:16 And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd.

Meaning: The cross is the means of gathering. Ezekiel's "one shepherd" is fulfilled.

The mystery: all things in Christ — Ephesians 1:9–10, 20–23

9 Having made known unto us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he hath purposed in himself: 10 That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him:

20 Which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead, and set him at his own right hand in the heavenly places, 21 Far above all principality, and power, and might, and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in that which is to come: 22 And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church, 23 Which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all.

Meaning: The anchor text of this theme. The plan was "purposed in himself" (theme 1). The exalted Christ is already Head over all things for the Church. The Church, His body, is where His fulness is displayed now.

By Him, for Him, in Him — Colossians 1:15–20

15 Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature: 16 For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: 17 And he is before all things, and by him all things consist. 18 And he is the head of the body, the church: who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all things he might have the preeminence. 19 For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell; 20 And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.

Meaning: The full arc: Christ is the agent of creation, its goal ("for him"), its sustainer ("consist"), its Head and its Reconciler. The beginning and the end of the Blueprint meet in one Person.

One new man — Ephesians 2:13–16

13 But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ. 14 For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us; 15 Having abolished in his flesh the enmity, even the law of commandments contained in ordinances; for to make in himself of twain one new man, so making peace; 16 And that he might reconcile both unto God in one body by the cross, having slain the enmity thereby:

Meaning: The Jew–Gentile divide, the deepest human division in Scripture, is healed in one body. The Church is a preview of the gathering of all things.

Every knee should bow — Philippians 2:9–11

9 Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: 10 That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; 11 And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Meaning: Isaiah 45:23 fulfilled in Jesus. Babel sought a name for itself; God gives Jesus "the name which is above every name."

That God may be all in all — 1 Corinthians 15:27–28

27 For he hath put all things under his feet. But when he saith all things are put under him, it is manifest that he is excepted, which did put all things under him. 28 And when all things shall be subdued unto him, then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all.

Meaning: The headship of Christ serves the final goal: "that God may be all in all."

Every creature … unto the Lamb — Revelation 5:13

13 And every creature which is in heaven, and on the earth, and under the earth, and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, heard I saying, Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever.

Meaning: Heaven, earth and sea, every realm named in Ephesians 1:10 and Colossians 1:20, join in one song.

Interpretive balance: "Reconcile all things" (Colossians 1:20) and "every knee" (Philippians 2:10) are read in different ways. Most Christian traditions understand them as universal acknowledgment of Christ's lordship and the restoration of the created order, with judgment still real for the unrepentant (Revelation 20:15). A minority (Christian universalists) take them to mean the eventual salvation of all. Both readings affirm that Christ will be openly supreme over everything.

Principles

  1. The goal of God's plan is unity in Christ. All things are summed up in Him (Ephesians 1:10).
  2. Christ is Creator, Sustainer and Goal. All things are by Him, through Him and for Him (Colossians 1:16–17).
  3. Unity comes through the cross, not human effort. Babel failed; the blood of the cross makes peace (Colossians 1:20; Ephesians 2:13–16).
  4. Christ is already Head. His exaltation is a present reality, not only a future hope (Ephesians 1:20–22).
  5. The Church previews the gathering. In one body God shows what He will do with all things (Ephesians 2:15; 3:10).
  6. Christ's headship serves God's glory. The final goal is "that God may be all in all" (1 Corinthians 15:28).

For a teaching session: Read Genesis 11:1–9, Zephaniah 3:9 and Ephesians 1:9–10 together. Ask: how does God's way of uniting people differ from humanity's way, and what does that mean for our churches?
