Righteousness and Justice

Overview

Righteousness and justice are the foundation of God's throne: "Justice and judgment are the habitation of thy throne" (Psalm 89:14). They explain why the blueprint needed a Lamb. A holy and just God cannot simply overlook sin, yet His purpose is to have a righteous people. The gospel resolves this: God set forth Christ "to declare … his righteousness: that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus" (Romans 3:26). The righteousness God requires, He provides.

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. The Judge of all the earth — God's own character is the standard of right
  2. Righteousness counted — Abraham believed, "and he counted it to him for righteousness"
  3. The LORD our Righteousness — the prophets promise a righteous King and a righteousness from God
  4. Just and Justifier — at the cross God's justice and mercy meet
  5. Wherein dwelleth righteousness — a world finally set right

Semantic note: In Hebrew, tsedeq (righteousness) and mishpat (justice) often appear as a pair (Genesis 18:19; Psalm 89:14; Isaiah 9:7; Amos 5:24). Together they mean that what is right is actually done: a right standard and a right outcome. Righteousness is also relational, meaning faithfulness to covenant obligations. When Paul speaks of "the righteousness of God," he means both God's own faithfulness and the right standing He gives. Hilastērion is the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the mercy seat over the ark (Exodus 25:17, LXX). There God's justice and mercy met over sprinkled blood.

Torah

Counted for righteousness — Genesis 15:6

6 And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

Meaning: The first use of tsĕdaqah about a person. Righteousness is credited, not earned. Paul builds Romans 4 and Galatians 3 on this verse.

Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right? — Genesis 18:19, 25

19 For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.

25 That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked: and that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee: Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?

Meaning: Abraham's calling includes doing "justice and judgment" (tsĕdaqah and mishpat). His appeal rests on God's own character: the Judge always does right.

By no means clear the guilty — Exodus 34:6–7

6 And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, 7 Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty; visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, and upon the children's children, unto the third and to the fourth generation.

Meaning: God's self-description holds both sides together: He forgives sin, and He will not clear the guilty. How can both be true? The answer waits for the cross (Romans 3:26).

Just and right is He — Deuteronomy 32:4; 10:17–18

32:4 He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he.

10:17 For the LORD your God is God of gods, and Lord of lords, a great God, a mighty, and a terrible, which regardeth not persons, nor taketh reward: 18 He doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger, in giving him food and raiment.

Meaning: God's justice is impartial ("regardeth not persons") and protective of the vulnerable. Justice includes care for the weak, not only punishment.

That which is altogether just — Deuteronomy 16:20

20 That which is altogether just shalt thou follow, that thou mayest live, and inherit the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.

Meaning: Literally "justice, justice shalt thou pursue." Justice is tied to life and inheritance (theme 23).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Seek judgment, relieve the oppressed — Isaiah 1:17

17 Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.

Meaning: Worship without justice is rejected (1:11–15). Righteousness must take shape in how the weak are treated.

A just God and a Saviour — Isaiah 45:21

21 Tell ye, and bring them near; yea, let them take counsel together: who hath declared this from ancient time? who hath told it from that time? have not I the LORD? and there is no God else beside me; a just God and a Saviour; there is none beside me.

Meaning: The tension of Exodus 34:7, held in one title: just and Saviour. God does not set aside justice in order to save.

My righteous Servant shall justify many — Isaiah 53:11

11 He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied: by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities.

Meaning: The resolution. The righteous Servant justifies (tsadaq) the many by bearing their iniquities. Justice is satisfied by substitution, the Lamb of theme 7.

THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS — Jeremiah 23:5–6

5 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth. 6 In his days Judah shall be saved, and Israel shall dwell safely: and this is his name whereby he shall be called, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS.

Meaning: The Messiah both does justice and is His people's righteousness (YHWH Tsidqenu). Fulfilled in 1 Corinthians 1:30: Christ "is made unto us … righteousness."

Let judgment run down as waters — Amos 5:24; Micah 6:8

Amos 5:24 But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.

Micah 6:8 He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?

Meaning: Justice is meant to flow like a river through a society, not to trickle. Micah joins justice, mercy and humility; Jesus names the same trio in Matthew 23:23.

The just shall live by his faith — Habakkuk 2:4

4 Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith.

Meaning: The proud are set against the righteous person who lives by trusting God. This text is quoted three times in the New Testament (Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11; Hebrews 10:38) and links this theme to Faith (theme 31).

Writings (Ketuvim)

The habitation of Thy throne — Psalm 89:14

14 Justice and judgment are the habitation of thy throne: mercy and truth shall go before thy face.

Meaning: Righteousness and justice are the foundation God's rule stands on. Mercy and truth go out ahead of Him. The theme belongs to the Purpose group because it describes who God is.

How should man be just with God? — Job 9:2; Psalm 143:2

Job 9:2 I know it is so of a truth: but how should man be just with God?

Psalm 143:2 And enter not into judgment with thy servant: for in thy sight shall no man living be justified.

Meaning: The human problem stated plainly. No one is righteous enough to stand in God's court on their own record. Paul quotes Psalm 143:2 in Romans 3:20 and Galatians 2:16.

The LORD imputeth not iniquity — Psalm 32:1–2

1 Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered. 2 Blessed is the man unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile.

Meaning: David describes the blessing of sin not counted against him. Paul pairs this with Genesis 15:6 as his two witnesses (Romans 4:6–8).

Righteousness and peace have kissed — Psalm 85:10–11

10 Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other. 11 Truth shall spring out of the earth; and righteousness shall look down from heaven.

Meaning: A picture of reconciliation. Qualities that seem to pull apart meet and embrace. Christians see it fulfilled at the cross, where justice and mercy meet.

Give the king Thy judgments — Psalm 72:1–2, 4

1 Give the king thy judgments, O God, and thy righteousness unto the king's son. 2 He shall judge thy people with righteousness, and thy poor with judgment.

4 He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor.

Meaning: The ideal King's righteousness shows itself in defending the poor. This is the pattern for Messiah's reign (Isaiah 11:4).

More acceptable than sacrifice — Proverbs 21:3

3 To do justice and judgment is more acceptable to the LORD than sacrifice.

Meaning: Wisdom joins the prophets: ritual cannot replace righteous living (1 Samuel 15:22; Hosea 6:6).

To bring in everlasting righteousness — Daniel 9:18, 24

18 …for we do not present our supplications before thee for our righteousnesses, but for thy great mercies.

24 Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy.

Meaning: Daniel pleads on the basis of God's mercy, not Israel's righteousness. God then reveals a timetable that ends with sin dealt with and "everlasting righteousness" brought in.

New Testament

Hunger and thirst after righteousness — Matthew 5:6, 20

6 Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.

20 For I say unto you, That except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no case enter into the kingdom of heaven.

Meaning: The righteousness the kingdom requires goes beyond outward religion. It must be received by those who know they lack it, and it must be lived from the heart.

The weightier matters — Matthew 23:23

23 Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.

Meaning: Jesus echoes Micah 6:8. Justice is one of the "weightier matters."

The righteousness of God revealed — Romans 1:16–17

16 For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek. 17 For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith.

Meaning: The theme of Romans. The gospel reveals God's righteousness, which is received by faith (Habakkuk 2:4).

Just, and the Justifier — Romans 3:21–26

21 But now the righteousness of God without the law is manifested, being witnessed by the law and the prophets; 22 Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference: 23 For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; 24 Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: 25 Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God; 26 To declare, I say, at this time his righteousness: that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus.

Meaning: The key passage. Exodus 34:7's tension is resolved: God forgives and stays just, because Christ is the propitiation, the mercy seat sprinkled with blood. "Set forth" (protithemi) belongs to the same word family as prothesis, "purpose" (Ephesians 1:9, 11; 3:11). The cross was the plan made public.

Made the righteousness of God in Him — 2 Corinthians 5:21; Philippians 3:9; 1 Corinthians 1:30

2 Corinthians 5:21 For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.

Philippians 3:9 And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith:

1 Corinthians 1:30 But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:

Meaning: The great exchange: our sin on Him, His righteousness to us. Jeremiah 23:6, "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS," is fulfilled.

Faithful and just to forgive — 1 John 1:9

9 If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

Meaning: Forgiveness is not God bending justice; because of the cross, forgiving is the just thing for Him to do.

In righteousness He doth judge — Revelation 19:11; 2 Peter 3:13

Revelation 19:11 And I saw heaven opened, and behold a white horse; and he that sat upon him was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge and make war.

2 Peter 3:13 Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.

Meaning: The returning King judges in righteousness, and the new creation is the place where righteousness is finally "at home."

Interpretive balance: Christians differ on emphasis. Reformation traditions stress imputed righteousness: Christ's righteousness credited to the believer (Romans 4:6; Philippians 3:9). Catholic and Orthodox traditions stress righteousness imparted, a real inner transformation. Recent scholarship (the "New Perspective" on Paul) highlights God's covenant faithfulness and the inclusion of Gentiles. Scripture also holds together personal righteousness before God and practical justice toward others (Micah 6:8; James 2:14–17). Most traditions affirm both.

Principles

  1. Righteousness and justice belong to God's nature. They are the foundation of His throne, not rules outside Him (Psalm 89:14; Deuteronomy 32:4).
  2. The blueprint required a righteous solution. God would not save by setting justice aside (Exodus 34:7; Isaiah 45:21).
  3. The cross makes God both just and Justifier. Justice is satisfied in Christ, our propitiation (Romans 3:25–26).
  4. Righteousness is a gift received by faith. From Abraham onward (Genesis 15:6; Romans 4:3–5).
  5. Received righteousness produces practised justice. Those made right with God do right toward others (Micah 6:8; Matthew 23:23).
  6. The end of the plan is a righteous world. The King judges in righteousness and the new creation is its home (2 Peter 3:13).

For a teaching session: Read Exodus 34:6–7, Isaiah 53:11 and Romans 3:21–26 together. Ask: how can God forgive the guilty and still "by no means clear the guilty"? Let the group discover the answer in Romans 3:26.
