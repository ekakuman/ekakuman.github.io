The Priesthood

Overview

The Lamb answers what is offered; the priesthood answers who brings people to God. A priest stands between a holy God and a sinful people. He offers sacrifice, carries their names before God, intercedes, and comes out to bless. The blueprint includes a Priest who was appointed by God's oath, not by human descent: "Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek" (Psalm 110:4). Hebrews shows Christ fulfilling every part of the Aaronic pattern and surpassing it. The plan ends with all God's people as "a kingdom of priests" serving Him face to face.

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. Melchizedek — a priest-king who blesses Abraham, without recorded beginning or end
  2. Aaron and the tabernacle — the priestly pattern: sacrifice, names on the heart, the Day of Atonement, blessing
  3. A priest upon His throne — the prophets and psalms foresee one Person as both King and Priest
  4. Our great High Priest — Christ offers Himself once for all and ever lives to intercede
  5. A royal priesthood — the people of God made priests, serving Him forever

Semantic note: Kohen most likely comes from a root meaning "to stand," the one who stands before God on others' behalf. Hebrews never calls Jesus a Levitical priest. He is from Judah (7:14). His priesthood is "after the order of Melchizedek": appointed by oath (7:21), based on "an endless life" (7:16), and therefore permanent. Mesitēs (mediator) is also covenant language: Christ is "the mediator of a better covenant" (Hebrews 8:6), which links this theme to theme 11.

Torah

Priest of the most high God — Genesis 14:18–20

18 And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God. 19 And he blessed him, and said, Blessed be Abram of the most high God, possessor of heaven and earth: 20 And blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all.

Meaning: The first priest in Scripture is a king of Salem ("peace") whose name means "king of righteousness." He has no recorded genealogy and blesses Abraham with bread and wine. Hebrews 7:1–3 sees him as a picture of the Son, "made like unto the Son of God; abideth a priest continually."

For glory and for beauty; HOLINESS TO THE LORD — Exodus 28:1–2, 36–38

1 And take thou unto thee Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him, from among the children of Israel, that he may minister unto me in the priest's office, even Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron's sons. 2 And thou shalt make holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty.

36 And thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD. 37 And thou shalt put it on a blue lace, that it may be upon the mitre; upon the forefront of the mitre it shall be. 38 And it shall be upon Aaron's forehead, that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things, which the children of Israel shall hallow in all their holy gifts; and it shall be always upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD.

Meaning: The priest carries the people's names on his shoulders and heart (28:12, 29; theme 24). He bears even the imperfection of their worship so that "they may be accepted." Christ's priesthood makes our imperfect offerings acceptable (1 Peter 2:5).

Within the vail, once a year — Leviticus 16:15, 30

15 Then shall he kill the goat of the sin offering, that is for the people, and bring his blood within the vail, and do with that blood as he did with the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat:

30 For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD.

Meaning: The Day of Atonement is the high point of Aaron's ministry: blood carried behind the veil to the mercy seat (theme 28). Hebrews 9:11–12 shows Christ entering "by his own blood … once into the holy place."

Between the dead and the living — Numbers 16:47–48

47 And Aaron took as Moses commanded, and ran into the midst of the congregation; and, behold, the plague was begun among the people: and he put on incense, and made an atonement for the people. 48 And he stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed.

Meaning: A living picture of mediation. The priest runs into the plague and stands in the gap, and judgment stops.

They shall put My name upon them — Numbers 6:23, 27

23 Speak unto Aaron and unto his sons, saying, On this wise ye shall bless the children of Israel, saying unto them,

27 And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them.

Meaning: The priest's last act is to bless (6:24–26). The risen Christ ascends "while he blessed them" (Luke 24:50–51).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

A faithful priest — 1 Samuel 2:35

35 And I will raise me up a faithful priest, that shall do according to that which is in mine heart and in my mind: and I will build him a sure house; and he shall walk before mine anointed for ever.

Meaning: Spoken against the corrupt house of Eli. God promises a priest after His own heart. Hebrews 2:17 calls Christ "a merciful and faithful high priest."

He made intercession for the transgressors — Isaiah 53:12

12 Therefore will I divide him a portion with the great, and he shall divide the spoil with the strong; because he hath poured out his soul unto death: and he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.

Meaning: The Servant is both offering and Priest: He "bare the sin" and "made intercession." Fulfilled on the cross: "Father, forgive them" (Luke 23:34).

Clothed with change of raiment — Zechariah 3:3–5, 8

3 Now Joshua was clothed with filthy garments, and stood before the angel. 4 And he answered and spake unto those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment. 5 And I said, Let them set a fair mitre upon his head. So they set a fair mitre upon his head, and clothed him with garments. And the angel of the LORD stood by.

8 Hear now, O Joshua the high priest, thou, and thy fellows that sit before thee: for they are men wondered at: for, behold, I will bring forth my servant the BRANCH.

Meaning: The high priest himself needed cleansing. God removes his filth and clothes him again, a sign pointing to "the BRANCH" who will be the perfect Priest.

A priest upon His throne — Zechariah 6:12–13

12 And speak unto him, saying, Thus speaketh the LORD of hosts, saying, Behold the man whose name is The BRANCH; and he shall grow up out of his place, and he shall build the temple of the LORD: 13 Even he shall build the temple of the LORD; and he shall bear the glory, and shall sit and rule upon his throne; and he shall be a priest upon his throne: and the counsel of peace shall be between them both.

Meaning: In Israel, king and priest were kept separate (2 Chronicles 26:18). Here one Man is both, like Melchizedek. "The counsel of peace" between the two offices points to theme 32.

I will take of them for priests — Isaiah 66:21

21 And I will also take of them for priests and for Levites, saith the LORD.

Meaning: "Them" refers to the gathered nations (66:19–20). Gentiles will serve as priests, which anticipates 1 Peter 2:9 and Revelation 5:9–10.

Writings (Ketuvim)

No daysman betwixt us — Job 9:32–33; 16:19–21

9:32 For he is not a man, as I am, that I should answer him, and we should come together in judgment. 33 Neither is there any daysman betwixt us, that might lay his hand upon us both.

16:19 Also now, behold, my witness is in heaven, and my record is on high. 20 My friends scorn me: but mine eye poureth out tears unto God. 21 O that one might plead for a man with God, as a man pleadeth for his neighbour!

Meaning: Job longs for a "daysman," an umpire who could lay a hand on both God and man. Then he senses a Witness in heaven who pleads for him. Both longings are answered in "one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Timothy 2:5).

A priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek — Psalm 110:1, 4

1 The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.

4 The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.

Meaning: The most quoted Old Testament text in the New Testament. David's Lord is enthroned King (v. 1) and sworn Priest (v. 4). The oath makes this priesthood permanent and unchangeable (Hebrews 7:20–21, 28).

Stood before Him in the breach — Psalm 106:23

23 Therefore he said that he would destroy them, had not Moses his chosen stood before him in the breach, to turn away his wrath, lest he should destroy them.

Meaning: Intercession pictured as standing in a broken wall. God seeks such a person (Ezekiel 22:30) and finds Him fully in Christ.

Let Thy priests be clothed with righteousness — Psalm 132:9, 16

9 Let thy priests be clothed with righteousness; and let thy saints shout for joy.

16 I will also clothe her priests with salvation: and her saints shall shout aloud for joy.

Meaning: The prayer (v. 9) is answered beyond what was asked (v. 16). The priests' true garments are righteousness and salvation, given by God (Isaiah 61:10; Revelation 19:8).

It appertaineth not unto thee, Uzziah — 2 Chronicles 26:16, 18

16 But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction: for he transgressed against the LORD his God, and went into the temple of the LORD to burn incense upon the altar of incense.

18 And they withstood Uzziah the king, and said unto him, It appertaineth not unto thee, Uzziah, to burn incense unto the LORD, but to the priests the sons of Aaron, that are consecrated to burn incense: go out of the sanctuary; for thou hast trespassed; neither shall it be for thine honour from the LORD God.

Meaning: No king of Israel could seize the priesthood. This makes the promise of a King-Priest (Psalm 110:4; Zechariah 6:13) striking: only God could appoint such a person.

About the time of the evening oblation — Daniel 9:20–21

20 And whiles I was speaking, and praying, and confessing my sin and the sin of my people Israel, and presenting my supplication before the LORD my God for the holy mountain of my God; 21 Yea, whiles I was speaking in prayer, even the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning, being caused to fly swiftly, touched me about the time of the evening oblation.

Meaning: With no temple standing, Daniel still keeps the hour of the evening sacrifice as a time of intercessory prayer. Prayer stands in for the altar (Psalm 141:2), and the answer comes at that hour.

New Testament

I pray for them — John 17:9, 20

9 I pray for them: I pray not for the world, but for them which thou hast given me; for they are thine.

20 Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word;

Meaning: Jesus' "high-priestly prayer." He carries His people's names before the Father, including every later believer.

A great high priest — Hebrews 4:14–16

14 Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession. 15 For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. 16 Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

Meaning: Our Priest is both exalted ("passed into the heavens") and sympathetic ("touched with the feeling"). The result is bold access.

He ever liveth to make intercession — Hebrews 7:23–27; Romans 8:34

Hebrews 7:23 And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death: 24 But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood. 25 Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them. 26 For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens; 27 Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.

Romans 8:34 Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us.

Meaning: The key passage. Aaron's priests died; Christ lives forever. They offered for themselves; He was sinless. They offered daily; He offered once, and the offering was Himself. Priest and sacrifice are one.

By His own blood, once — Hebrews 9:11–12; 10:11–14

9:11 But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building; 12 Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.

10:11 And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: 12 But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;

14 For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

Meaning: Leviticus 16 fulfilled. Levitical priests stood because their work was never finished; Christ "sat down" because His is.

A new and living way — Hebrews 10:19–22

19 Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, 20 By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh; 21 And having an high priest over the house of God; 22 Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.

Meaning: The veil that kept everyone out except the high priest once a year is opened (Matthew 27:51). Now every believer may "draw near."

One mediator — 1 Timothy 2:5

5 For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;

Meaning: Job's "daysman" found. He can lay His hand on both because He is both God and "the man."

A royal priesthood — 1 Peter 2:5, 9

5 Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ.

9 But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:

Meaning: Exodus 19:6 fulfilled in the Church. Believers offer "spiritual sacrifices" (praise, good works, their own bodies; Hebrews 13:15–16; Romans 12:1), made acceptable "by Jesus Christ."

Kings and priests unto God — Revelation 1:5–6; 20:6

1:5 …Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, 6 And hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father; to him be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Amen.

20:6 Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.

Meaning: The Melchizedek pattern, priest and king together, is shared with all the redeemed.

Interpretive balance: Protestant traditions emphasise "the priesthood of all believers" (1 Peter 2:9) and Christ as the only mediator (1 Timothy 2:5). Catholic and Orthodox traditions also affirm the common priesthood of the baptised, alongside an ordained ministerial priesthood that serves Christ's one priesthood without adding to it. All agree Christ's sacrifice was "once for all" (Hebrews 10:10) and that He is the great High Priest.

Principles

  1. The blueprint includes a Mediator. A holy God and sinful people need one who stands between (Job 9:33; 1 Timothy 2:5).
  2. Christ's priesthood was sworn by oath. It is appointed by God, permanent and unchangeable (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7:21, 24).
  3. Christ is both Priest and sacrifice. "He offered up himself," once for all (Hebrews 7:27; 9:12).
  4. Christ ever lives to intercede. His priestly work continues now (Hebrews 7:25; Romans 8:34).
  5. The way into God's presence is open. The veil is torn; all believers may draw near (Hebrews 10:19–22).
  6. God's people are made priests. They offer spiritual sacrifices and intercede for others (1 Peter 2:5, 9).

For a teaching session: Read Leviticus 16:15, 30 and Hebrews 9:11–12; 10:11–14 side by side. Compare the priests who "stand daily" with Christ who "sat down." Ask what this means for assurance and prayer.
