The Everlasting Covenant

Overview and word study

A covenant is God binding Himself by solemn promise to carry out His purpose with a people. The covenants of Scripture — with Noah, Abraham, Israel at Sinai, David, and the new covenant — are stages of one plan, which Hebrews calls "the everlasting covenant" sealed by Christ's blood (Hebrews 13:20). It reaches back before time: eternal life was "promised before the world began" (Titus 1:2), a commitment within the Godhead.

Every covenant shares the same heart, the covenant formula: "I will be their God, and they shall be my people" (Jeremiah 31:33). It ends fulfilled: "God himself shall be with them, and be their God" (Revelation 21:3).

The Greek diathēkē also means a last will (Hebrews 9:16–17). A will takes effect at a death — the new covenant came into force when Christ died.

Torah

The bow in the cloud — Genesis 9:12–13, 16

12 And God said, This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: 13 I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.

16 And the bow shall be in the cloud; and I will look upon it, that I may remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is upon the earth.

Meaning: The first "everlasting covenant." It is one-sided: God promises and God remembers. The bow (qeshet, a war-bow) is hung up, pointed away from earth — a picture of peace.

God alone passes between the pieces — Genesis 15:6, 17–18

6 And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

17 And it came to pass, that, when the sun went down, and it was dark, behold a smoking furnace, and a burning lamp that passed between those pieces. 18 In the same day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying, Unto thy seed have I given this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates:

Meaning: In ancient covenants both parties walked between cut animals, calling a curse on themselves if they broke it (Jeremiah 34:18). Here only God passes through, while Abram sleeps. God takes the whole obligation. Faith, not works, is how Abram receives it (v. 6; Romans 4:3).

To be a God unto thee — Genesis 17:7

7 And I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee.

Meaning: The covenant formula appears: "to be a God unto thee." It extends to "thy seed" — fulfilled in Christ and all who are His (Galatians 3:16, 29).

The blood of the covenant — Exodus 24:7–8

7 And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient. 8 And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.

Meaning: The Sinai covenant is sealed with blood. Israel promises obedience and soon breaks it (Exodus 32). Jesus echoes Moses' words at the Last Supper: "this is my blood of the new testament" (Matthew 26:28).

I will not break my covenant — Leviticus 26:44–45

44 And yet for all that, when they be in the land of their enemies, I will not cast them away, neither will I abhor them, to destroy them utterly, and to break my covenant with them: for I am the LORD their God. 45 But I will for their sakes remember the covenant of their ancestors, whom I brought forth out of the land of Egypt in the sight of the heathen, that I might be their God: I am the LORD.

Meaning: Even in exile for covenant-breaking, God will not break His side. His faithfulness outlasts His people's failure.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Ordered in all things, and sure — 2 Samuel 23:5

5 Although my house be not so with God; yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure: for this is all my salvation, and all my desire, although he make it not to grow.

Meaning: David's last words. His own house was flawed, but the covenant was "ordered" (arukah, arranged in detail) and "sure" (shemurah, guarded). He rests his salvation on God's covenant, not his record.

A covenant of the people — Isaiah 42:6–7

6 I the LORD have called thee in righteousness, and will hold thine hand, and will keep thee, and give thee for a covenant of the people, for a light of the Gentiles; 7 To open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison, and them that sit in darkness out of the prison house.

Meaning: The Servant does not only make a covenant; He is the covenant. In Christ, God's promises are embodied in a Person (2 Corinthians 1:20).

The new covenant — Jeremiah 31:31–34

31 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah: 32 Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt; which my covenant they brake, although I was an husband unto them, saith the LORD: 33 But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people. 34 And they shall teach no more every man his neighbour, and every man his brother, saying, Know the LORD: for they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them, saith the LORD: for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more.

Meaning: The longest Old Testament passage quoted in the New (Hebrews 8:8–12). Four promises: law written within, relationship ("their God … my people"), personal knowledge of God for all, and full forgiveness. The old covenant failed through the people; the new succeeds through God's inner work.

I will not turn away — Jeremiah 32:40

40 And I will make an everlasting covenant with them, that I will not turn away from them, to do them good; but I will put my fear in their hearts, that they shall not depart from me.

Meaning: Both sides secured by God: He will not turn away from them, and He will keep them from turning away from Him.

A covenant of peace — Ezekiel 37:26–27

26 Moreover I will make a covenant of peace with them; it shall be an everlasting covenant with them: and I will place them, and multiply them, and will set my sanctuary in the midst of them for evermore. 27 My tabernacle also shall be with them: yea, I will be their God, and they shall be my people.

Meaning: The covenant's goal is God's dwelling among His people forever — quoted in 2 Corinthians 6:16 and fulfilled in Revelation 21:3.

Writings (Ketuvim)

My covenant will I not break — Psalm 89:3–4, 28–34

3 I have made a covenant with my chosen, I have sworn unto David my servant, 4 Thy seed will I establish for ever, and build up thy throne to all generations. Selah.

28 My mercy will I keep for him for evermore, and my covenant shall stand fast with him. 29 His seed also will I make to endure for ever, and his throne as the days of heaven. 30 If his children forsake my law, and walk not in my judgments; 31 If they break my statutes, and keep not my commandments; 32 Then will I visit their transgression with the rod, and their iniquity with stripes. 33 Nevertheless my lovingkindness will I not utterly take from him, nor suffer my faithfulness to fail. 34 My covenant will I not break, nor alter the thing that is gone out of my lips.

Meaning: The Davidic covenant in song. Sin brings discipline ("the rod"), but not cancellation. "Nevertheless" is the key word: God's chesed and faithfulness remain.

He hath remembered His covenant for ever — Psalm 105:8–10

8 He hath remembered his covenant for ever, the word which he commanded to a thousand generations. 9 Which covenant he made with Abraham, and his oath unto Isaac; 10 And confirmed the same unto Jacob for a law, and to Israel for an everlasting covenant;

Meaning: One covenant passed from Abraham to Isaac to Jacob to Israel — the continuity of God's plan across generations. "Remembered" means acted on, not merely recalled.

A covenant by sacrifice — Psalm 50:5

5 Gather my saints together unto me; those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice.

Meaning: God's people are defined as those in covenant "by sacrifice" — the sacrificial basis of every covenant.

He will shew them His covenant — Psalm 25:10, 14

10 All the paths of the LORD are mercy and truth unto such as keep his covenant and his testimonies.

14 The secret of the LORD is with them that fear him; and he will shew them his covenant.

Meaning: The covenant is not only a legal arrangement but an intimate disclosure — God "shews" it to those close to Him.

New Testament

The new testament in My blood — Luke 22:19–20

19 And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me. 20 Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.

Meaning: Jesus announces Jeremiah 31's new covenant at the Passover table and seals it with His blood "for you." Every communion service re-proclaims the covenant.

Confirmed before of God in Christ — Galatians 3:15–17

15 Brethren, I speak after the manner of men; Though it be but a man's covenant, yet if it be confirmed, no man disannulleth, or addeth thereto. 16 Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ. 17 And this I say, that the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul, that it should make the promise of none effect.

Meaning: The covenant of promise with Abraham was "confirmed before of God in Christ." The law came later and could not cancel it. Promise comes first and governs everything after.

Surety and Mediator of a better covenant — Hebrews 7:22; 8:6, 13

7:22 By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament.

8:6 But now hath he obtained a more excellent ministry, by how much also he is the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises.

8:13 In that he saith, A new covenant, he hath made the first old. Now that which decayeth and waxeth old is ready to vanish away.

Meaning: Jesus is both Mediator (bringing the parties together) and Surety (guaranteeing it will be kept). The new covenant rests on "better promises" — God's "I will" rather than Israel's "we will" (Exodus 24:7).

Ministers of the new testament — 2 Corinthians 3:5–6

5 Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to think any thing as of ourselves; but our sufficiency is of God; 6 Who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.

Meaning: The new covenant is administered by the Spirit, writing God's law on hearts (Jeremiah 31:33). Its ministry gives life.

Made nigh by the blood — Ephesians 2:12–13

12 That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world: 13 But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.

Meaning: Gentiles were outside "the covenants of promise." The blood of Christ brings them in — the covenant reaching all nations as promised to Abraham.

The blood of the everlasting covenant — Hebrews 13:20–21

20 Now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, 21 Make you perfect in every good work to do his will, working in you that which is wellpleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ; to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen.

Meaning: The phrase that names this theme. All the covenants converge into one "everlasting covenant" sealed by Christ's blood and proven by His resurrection. Its effect: God "working in you" what pleases Him.

God Himself with them — Revelation 21:3

3 And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.

Meaning: The covenant formula fulfilled at last. Every "I will be their God" from Genesis 17 to Ezekiel 37 comes true in the new creation.

Patterns, principles and practices

"I will be their God, and they my people" "To be a God unto thee" (Genesis 17:7); "I will take you to me for a people" (Exodus 6:7) "Will be their God, and they shall be my people" (Jeremiah 31:33; Ezekiel 37:27) "We are his people" (Psalm 100:3) "They shall be his people" (Revelation 21:3); 2 Corinthians 6:16

7. The covenant reaches the nations "In thee shall all families … be blessed" (Genesis 12:3) "The sons of the stranger … taketh hold of my covenant" (Isaiah 56:6) "All the ends of the world shall … turn" (Psalm 22:27) "Strangers from the covenants … made nigh by the blood" (Ephesians 2:12–13)

Principles

  1. The covenant comes from God's initiative and oath. It rests on His faithfulness, not ours (Genesis 15:17; Hebrews 6:17).
  2. Every covenant is sealed with blood. Relationship with a holy God requires sacrifice (Exodus 24:8; Hebrews 9:22).
  3. The goal is relationship and presence. "I will be their God" ends in "God himself shall be with them" (Revelation 21:3).
  4. The new covenant changes the heart. Law within, sins forgotten, God known personally (Jeremiah 31:33–34).
  5. Human failure cannot cancel God's covenant. Discipline, yes; abandonment, no (Psalm 89:30–34).
  6. Christ is the covenant in person. Mediator, Surety and sacrifice (Isaiah 42:6; Hebrews 7:22; 8:6).
  7. The covenant includes the nations. Gentiles are brought near by Christ's blood (Ephesians 2:13).

For a teaching session: Trace the covenant formula from Genesis 17:7 through Exodus 6:7, Jeremiah 31:33, Ezekiel 37:27 and Revelation 21:3.
