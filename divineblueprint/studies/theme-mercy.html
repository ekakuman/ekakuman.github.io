The Mercy of God

Overview and word study

Mercy is God's compassion and covenant loyalty toward the guilty and the suffering. Mercy withholds the judgment we deserve; grace (theme 8) gives the favor we do not deserve. The blueprint planned for mercy from the start: "vessels of mercy, which he had afore prepared unto glory" (Romans 9:23), and "the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting" (Psalm 103:17).

Mercy has a physical center in the Old Testament — the mercy seat, where blood was sprinkled and God met His people. In the New Testament, Christ Himself is that mercy seat (Romans 3:25).

The tax collector's prayer, "God be merciful to me" (Luke 18:13), uses hilasthēti — literally "be propitiated toward me." He was asking God to treat him on the basis of the mercy seat.

Torah

The LORD being merciful — Genesis 19:15–16

15 And when the morning arose, then the angels hastened Lot, saying, Arise, take thy wife, and thy two daughters, which are here; lest thou be consumed in the iniquity of the city. 16 And while he lingered, the men laid hold upon his hand, and upon the hand of his wife, and upon the hand of his two daughters; the LORD being merciful unto him: and they brought him forth, and set him without the city.

Meaning: Lot "lingered," yet mercy (chemlah, sparing pity) took him by the hand. Mercy rescues even the hesitant.

Merciful and gracious — Exodus 34:5–7

5 And the LORD descended in the cloud, and stood with him there, and proclaimed the name of the LORD. 6 And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, 7 Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty; visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, and upon the children's children, unto the third and to the fourth generation.

Meaning: God's self-description begins with mercy (rachum). Mercy is kept "for thousands"; judgment reaches three or four generations — mercy outweighs wrath by far. Yet He "will by no means clear the guilty": mercy must meet justice somewhere. The mercy seat and the cross are where they meet.

The mercy seat — Exodus 25:17, 21–22

17 And thou shalt make a mercy seat of pure gold: two cubits and a half shall be the length thereof, and a cubit and a half the breadth thereof.

21 And thou shalt put the mercy seat above upon the ark; and in the ark thou shalt put the testimony that I shall give thee. 22 And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony, of all things which I will give thee in commandment unto the children of Israel.

Meaning: The broken law lay inside the ark; the mercy seat covered it. God's meeting place with His people was above the covering — mercy sits on top of the law.

Blood on the mercy seat — Leviticus 16:14–15

14 And he shall take of the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it with his finger upon the mercy seat eastward; and before the mercy seat shall he sprinkle of the blood with his finger seven times. 15 Then shall he kill the goat of the sin offering, that is for the people, and bring his blood within the vail, and do with that blood as he did with the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat:

Meaning: On the Day of Atonement, blood was sprinkled on the mercy seat. Mercy is not cheap; it is grounded in a substitute's life (Leviticus 17:11).

According to the greatness of Thy mercy — Numbers 14:19–20

19 Pardon, I beseech thee, the iniquity of this people according unto the greatness of thy mercy, and as thou hast forgiven this people, from Egypt even until now. 20 And the LORD said, I have pardoned according to thy word:

Meaning: Moses pleads God's own character. Mercy is measured by "the greatness" of God's chesed, not by the size of the sin.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Fall into the hand of the LORD — 2 Samuel 24:14

14 And David said unto Gad, I am in a great strait: let us fall now into the hand of the LORD; for his mercies are great: and let me not fall into the hand of man.

Meaning: Even under God's judgment, David trusts God's mercy more than human kindness.

He delighteth in mercy — Micah 7:18–20

18 Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity, and passeth by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage? he retaineth not his anger for ever, because he delighteth in mercy. 19 He will turn again, he will have compassion upon us; he will subdue our iniquities; and thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea. 20 Thou wilt perform the truth to Jacob, and the mercy to Abraham, which thou hast sworn unto our fathers from the days of old.

Meaning: A wordplay on Micah's name ("Who is like the LORD?"). God "delighteth" (chaphets) in mercy — it is His pleasure, not a reluctant exception. Sins are "subdued" and drowned in the sea, like Pharaoh's army.

Everlasting kindness — Isaiah 54:7–10

7 For a small moment have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee. 8 In a little wrath I hid my face from thee for a moment; but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee, saith the LORD thy Redeemer. 9 For this is as the waters of Noah unto me: for as I have sworn that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth; so have I sworn that I would not be wroth with thee, nor rebuke thee. 10 For the mountains shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed, saith the LORD that hath mercy on thee.

Meaning: Wrath is "a small moment"; mercy is "everlasting." God backs His mercy with an oath, like the rainbow after the flood. Mountains are less stable than His chesed.

He will abundantly pardon — Isaiah 55:7

7 Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.

Meaning: Mercy waits at the end of every road home. "Abundantly" (yarbeh) means God multiplies pardon.

I desired mercy — Hosea 6:6

6 For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.

Meaning: God wants chesed in His people, not ritual alone. Jesus quotes it twice (Matthew 9:13; 12:7) to rebuke merciless religion.

In wrath remember mercy — Habakkuk 3:2

2 O LORD, I have heard thy speech, and was afraid: O LORD, revive thy work in the midst of the years, in the midst of the years make known; in wrath remember mercy.

Meaning: A prayer for revival under judgment. Mercy is the believer's appeal even when wrath is deserved.

Writings (Ketuvim)

Have mercy upon me — Psalm 51:1–2

1 Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness: according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out my transgressions. 2 Wash me throughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.

Meaning: David's prayer after his sin with Bathsheba. He appeals to three things in God: grace ("have mercy," chonneni), loyal love (chesed), and tender compassion (rachamim). He brings nothing of his own.

From everlasting to everlasting — Psalm 103:8–13, 17

8 The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy. 9 He will not always chide: neither will he keep his anger for ever. 10 He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities. 11 For as the heaven is high above the earth, so great is his mercy toward them that fear him. 12 As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us. 13 Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.

17 But the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that fear him, and his righteousness unto children's children;

Meaning: Exodus 34:6 sung back to God. Mercy is measured by heaven's height, the distance from east to west, and a father's tenderness. Verse 17 places mercy in the blueprint: "from everlasting to everlasting."

His mercy endureth for ever — Psalm 136:1–3, 23–26

1 O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever. 2 O give thanks unto the God of gods: for his mercy endureth for ever. 3 O give thanks to the Lord of lords: for his mercy endureth for ever.

23 Who remembered us in our low estate: for his mercy endureth for ever: 24 And hath redeemed us from our enemies: for his mercy endureth for ever. 25 Who giveth food to all flesh: for his mercy endureth for ever. 26 O give thanks unto the God of heaven: for his mercy endureth for ever.

Meaning: A refrain 26 times. The psalm retells creation, exodus, wilderness and conquest, and every event is explained by chesed. History is mercy in action.

New every morning — Lamentations 3:21–23, 31–32

21 This I recall to my mind, therefore have I hope. 22 It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. 23 They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

31 For the Lord will not cast off for ever: 32 But though he cause grief, yet will he have compassion according to the multitude of his mercies.

Meaning: Written amid Jerusalem's ruins. Hope comes from remembering mercy. Mercy is renewed daily — yesterday's supply does not run out, and today's is fresh.

A God ready to pardon — Nehemiah 9:17, 31

17 And refused to obey, neither were mindful of thy wonders that thou didst among them; but hardened their necks, and in their rebellion appointed a captain to return to their bondage: but thou art a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and forsookest them not.

31 Nevertheless for thy great mercies' sake thou didst not utterly consume them, nor forsake them; for thou art a gracious and merciful God.

Meaning: Israel's history of rebellion is answered again and again by mercy. "Ready to pardon" is eloah selichot, a God of forgivenesses.

New Testament

The tender mercy of our God — Luke 1:50, 54–55, 78–79

50 And his mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation.

54 He hath holpen his servant Israel, in remembrance of his mercy; 55 As he spake to our fathers, to Abraham, and to his seed for ever.

78 Through the tender mercy of our God; whereby the dayspring from on high hath visited us, 79 To give light to them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace.

Meaning: Mary and Zechariah both describe Christ's coming as God "remembering" His mercy promised to Abraham. Jesus is God's mercy arriving like sunrise.

God be merciful to me a sinner — Luke 18:13–14

13 And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner. 14 I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.

Meaning: "Be merciful" is hilasthēti — be propitiated, as at the mercy seat. The humble sinner who asks for mercy goes home justified.

Christ our mercy seat — Romans 3:24–26

24 Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: 25 Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God; 26 To declare, I say, at this time his righteousness: that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus.

Meaning: "Propitiation" is hilastērion, the mercy seat. The hidden lid of the ark is now "set forth" publicly in Christ. At the cross God is both "just" and "the justifier" — mercy and justice meet.

Mercy on whom I will — Romans 9:15–16, 23; 11:32

9:15 For he saith to Moses, I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I will have compassion. 9:16 So then it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy.

9:23 And that he might make known the riches of his glory on the vessels of mercy, which he had afore prepared unto glory,

11:32 For God hath concluded them all in unbelief, that he might have mercy upon all.

Meaning: Mercy is God's free decision, not earned by effort. "Afore prepared" places mercy in the blueprint. Romans 11:32 shows its scope: Jew and Gentile alike shut up to need so that mercy reaches all.

Rich in mercy — Ephesians 2:4–5; Titus 3:5

Ephesians 2:4 But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us, 5 Even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;)

Titus 3:5 Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;

Meaning: "But God" turns death to life. God is "rich" (plousios) in mercy. Salvation is "according to his mercy," not our works.

Be ye merciful — Luke 6:36; Matthew 5:7; James 2:13

Luke 6:36 Be ye therefore merciful, as your Father also is merciful.

Matthew 5:7 Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.

James 2:13 For he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy; and mercy rejoiceth against judgment.

Meaning: Mercy received must become mercy shown. "Mercy rejoiceth against judgment" — mercy triumphs over judgment.

The throne of grace — Hebrews 4:16

16 Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

Meaning: The mercy seat has become a throne open to all believers. Mercy first, then grace to help.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Mercy is first in God's name "Merciful and gracious" (Exodus 34:6) "A gracious God, and merciful" (Jonah 4:2) "Ready to pardon, gracious and merciful" (Nehemiah 9:17); Psalm 103:8 "The Father of mercies" (2 Corinthians 1:3)
2. The mercy seat — meeting place "There I will meet with thee … from above the mercy seat" (Exodus 25:22) "The LORD of hosts, which dwelleth between the cherubims" (1 Samuel 4:4) "Thou that dwellest between the cherubims, shine forth" (Psalm 80:1) "Set forth to be a propitiation" (Romans 3:25); "the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16)
3. Mercy outlasts wrath "Keeping mercy for thousands" vs. three or four generations (Exodus 34:7) "For a small moment … with everlasting kindness" (Isaiah 54:7–8) "His anger endureth but a moment" (Psalm 30:5) "Mercy rejoiceth against judgment" (James 2:13)
4. Mercy is everlasting and new daily "Keepeth covenant and mercy … to a thousand generations" (Deuteronomy 7:9) "My kindness shall not depart from thee" (Isaiah 54:10) "His mercy endureth for ever" (Psalm 136); "new every morning" (Lamentations 3:23) "From generation to generation" (Luke 1:50)
5. Mercy is God's free choice "I will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy" (Exodus 33:19) "He will abundantly pardon" (Isaiah 55:7) "For thy great mercies' sake" (Nehemiah 9:31) "Not of him that willeth … but of God that sheweth mercy" (Romans 9:16)
6. Mercy reaches the nations Mercy to Lot outside the covenant line (Genesis 19:16) "Should not I spare Nineveh?" (Jonah 4:11) "O praise the LORD, all ye nations … his merciful kindness" (Psalm 117:1–2) "That he might have mercy upon all" (Romans 11:32)
7. Mercy received becomes mercy shown "Love ye therefore the stranger" (Deuteronomy 10:19) "I desired mercy, and not sacrifice" (Hosea 6:6); "to love mercy" (Micah 6:8) "He that hath mercy on the poor, happy is he" (Proverbs 14:21) "Be ye therefore merciful" (Luke 6:36); "Go, and do thou likewise" (Luke 10:37)

Principles

  1. Mercy is part of God's eternal character and plan. "From everlasting to everlasting" (Psalm 103:17; Romans 9:23).
  2. Mercy is costly. It rests on blood sprinkled on the mercy seat — fulfilled in Christ (Leviticus 16:14; Romans 3:25).
  3. Mercy and justice meet at the cross. God remains just while justifying the guilty (Romans 3:26).
  4. Mercy is God's delight, not His reluctance. "He delighteth in mercy" (Micah 7:18).
  5. Mercy is renewed daily and never runs out. "New every morning" (Lamentations 3:23).
  6. Mercy is received by humble asking. "God be merciful to me a sinner" (Luke 18:13).
  7. Mercy received must be shown. "Blessed are the merciful" (Matthew 5:7).

For a teaching session: Follow the mercy seat from Exodus 25:22 to Leviticus 16:14, Luke 18:13, Romans 3:25 and Hebrews 4:16.
