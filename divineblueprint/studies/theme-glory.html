The Glory of God

Overview

God's glory is His worth made visible: the weight of who He is, shining out in light, presence and character. It is the goal of the blueprint. The Son shared it "before the world was" (John 17:5); creation declares it (Psalm 19:1); the tabernacle and temple were filled with it (Exodus 40:34; 2 Chronicles 7:1); and the plan ends with a city "having the glory of God" (Revelation 21:11).

The theme follows a clear storyline:

  1. Shared in eternity — between the Father and the Son (John 17:5).
  2. Displayed in creation and given to man — "crowned him with glory and honour" (Psalm 8:5).
  3. Lost through sin — "all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).
  4. Dwelling among Israel — in the cloud, the tabernacle and the temple, then departing (Ezekiel 10:18).
  5. Revealed in Christ — "we beheld his glory" (John 1:14), above all at the cross (John 12:23).
  6. Transforming believers — "from glory to glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18).
  7. Filling all things — "the earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD" (Habakkuk 2:14).

Glory is closely tied to love (theme 3): Jesus prays that believers will be "with me where I am; that they may behold my glory … for thou lovedst me" (John 17:24). Passages below are given in full from the King James Version.

1. Word study

Hebrew pictures glory as weight; Greek pictures it as radiance and reputation. Together they describe the substance of God made visible.

Two semantic insights:

  1. Glory as weight. Kavod means God's reality is heavy, substantial and real; idols are "vanity" (hevel, vapour). To "give glory" is to treat God as weighty.
  2. Glory and the tent. John 1:14 joins skēnoō (tabernacled) and doxa (glory) in one verse — the tabernacle scene of Exodus 40:34 fulfilled in the person of Jesus.

2. Torah

In the Torah, God's glory comes down: as fire on Sinai, as His goodness passing before Moses, as light on Moses' face, and as the cloud filling the tabernacle. It also withdraws when sin is tolerated.

Glory on the mountain — Exodus 24:15–17

15 And Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered the mount. 16 And the glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days: and the seventh day he called unto Moses out of the midst of the cloud. 17 And the sight of the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Israel.

Meaning: "Abode" is shakan — God's glory dwells. From the people's distance it looked like "devouring fire"; from within the cloud, Moses heard God's voice. Six days of waiting, then the call on the seventh echoes the creation week.

Shew me thy glory — Exodus 33:18–23; 34:5–7

33:18 And he said, I beseech thee, shew me thy glory. 19 And he said, I will make all my goodness pass before thee, and I will proclaim the name of the LORD before thee; and will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy. 20 And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live. 21 And the LORD said, Behold, there is a place by me, and thou shalt stand upon a rock: 22 And it shall come to pass, while my glory passeth by, that I will put thee in a clift of the rock, and will cover thee with my hand while I pass by: 23 And I will take away mine hand, and thou shalt see my back parts: but my face shall not be seen.

34:5 And the LORD descended in the cloud, and stood with him there, and proclaimed the name of the LORD. 6 And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, 7 Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty; visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, and upon the children's children, unto the third and to the fourth generation.

Meaning: The key Torah text. Moses asks for glory; God answers with "goodness" and His "name" — His glory is His character. The proclamation (34:6–7) lists mercy, grace, patience, goodness, truth and justice. John 1:14 echoes it: Christ is "full of grace and truth." The "clift of the rock" has long been read by Christians as a picture of shelter in Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4).

The shining face — Exodus 34:29–30, 35

29 And it came to pass, when Moses came down from mount Sinai with the two tables of testimony in Moses' hand, when he came down from the mount, that Moses wist not that the skin of his face shone while he talked with him. 30 And when Aaron and all the children of Israel saw Moses, behold, the skin of his face shone; and they were afraid to come nigh him.

35 And the children of Israel saw the face of Moses, that the skin of Moses' face shone: and Moses put the vail upon his face again, until he went in to speak with him.

Meaning: "Shone" is qaran, to send out rays. Time in God's presence left a visible mark, and Moses "wist not" — he did not know. Paul uses this scene in 2 Corinthians 3:7–18: Moses' glory faded and was veiled, but believers behold the Lord's glory with "open face" and are being changed.

The glory fills the tabernacle — Exodus 40:33–35

33 And he reared up the court round about the tabernacle and the altar, and set up the hanging of the court gate. So Moses finished the work. 34 Then a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle. 35 And Moses was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode upon it, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.

Meaning: The climax of Exodus. The work was finished according to the pattern ("so Moses finished the work"), and then glory filled it. The building existed for the glory; the glory made it a home. Even Moses could not enter — the glory was too weighty.

I will not go up in the midst — Exodus 33:3–4

3 Unto a land flowing with milk and honey: for I will not go up in the midst of thee; for thou art a stiffnecked people: lest I consume thee in the way. 4 And when the people heard these evil tidings, they mourned: and no man did put on him his ornaments.

Meaning: After the golden calf, God offers the land without His presence. The people rightly mourn: blessings without God's glory are "evil tidings." Moses' intercession (33:15) — "If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence" — leads to the request "shew me thy glory."

The earth filled — Numbers 14:21

21 But as truly as I live, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD.

Meaning: God swears by His own life at the moment of Israel's worst rebellion. The glory once confined to a tent is destined to fill the whole earth.

3. Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets trace glory departing and returning: lost with the ark, seen enthroned by Isaiah, guarded jealously against idols, leaving the temple in Ezekiel's vision and coming back, and promised greater in a latter house.

Ichabod — 1 Samuel 4:21–22

21 And she named the child Ichabod, saying, The glory is departed from Israel: because the ark of God was taken, and because of her father in law and her husband. 22 And she said, The glory is departed from Israel: for the ark of God is taken.

Meaning: I-kavod means "no glory" or "where is the glory?" Israel had treated the ark as a lucky charm. When God's presence is used rather than honoured, the glory departs (galah, goes into exile).

Isaiah sees the King — Isaiah 6:1–5

1 In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up, and his train filled the temple. 2 Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly. 3 And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory. 4 And the posts of the door moved at the voice of him that cried, and the house was filled with smoke. 5 Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts.

Meaning: Holiness is God's inner perfection; glory is that holiness shining out to fill "the whole earth." Isaiah's first response is self-awareness of sin. John 12:41 says Isaiah "saw his glory, and spake of him" — the glory of Christ.

All flesh shall see it — Isaiah 40:3–5

3 The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God. 4 Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low: and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain: 5 And the glory of the LORD shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see it together: for the mouth of the LORD hath spoken it.

Meaning: The way is prepared so that glory can be revealed to "all flesh." The Gospels apply verse 3 to John the Baptist; the glory revealed is Jesus (John 1:14).

My glory will I not give to another — Isaiah 42:8

8 I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images.

Meaning: God guards His glory from idols. Yet Jesus says, "the glory which thou gavest me I have given them" (John 17:22). The difference: idols compete with God; His people share His glory in union with Him.

The glory departs and returns — Ezekiel 10:18–19; 43:2, 4–5

10:18 Then the glory of the LORD departed from off the threshold of the house, and stood over the cherubims. 10:19 And the cherubims lifted up their wings, and mounted up from the earth in my sight: when they went out, the wheels also were beside them, and every one stood at the door of the east gate of the LORD'S house; and the glory of the God of Israel was over them above.

43:2 And, behold, the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east: and his voice was like a noise of many waters: and the earth shined with his glory. 43:4 And the glory of the LORD came into the house by the way of the gate whose prospect is toward the east. 43:5 So the spirit took me up, and brought me into the inner court; and, behold, the glory of the LORD filled the house.

Meaning: The glory leaves slowly, in stages, as if reluctant — from the threshold, to the east gate, to the mountain (11:23). Then it returns by the same eastern way and fills the house again. Judgment is real, but God's purpose is to dwell with His people.

Arise, shine — Isaiah 60:1–3, 19

1 Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the LORD is risen upon thee. 2 For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the LORD shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee. 3 And the Gentiles shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising.

19 The sun shall be no more thy light by day; neither for brightness shall the moon give light unto thee: but the LORD shall be unto thee an everlasting light, and thy God thy glory.

Meaning: Glory is no longer only in a building; it rises "upon thee" — upon the people. The nations are drawn by it. Verse 19 is quoted almost directly in Revelation 21:23.

The glory of the latter house — Haggai 2:7, 9

7 And I will shake all nations, and the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the LORD of hosts.

9 The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the LORD of hosts: and in this place will I give peace, saith the LORD of hosts.

Meaning: Spoken over a small rebuilt temple that disappointed the elders. The greater glory came when Jesus walked in that temple — and, in the New Testament, in the church as God's house (Ephesians 2:21–22).

4. Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings turn glory into worship: creation preaches it, man was crowned with it, the King of glory enters His gates, the temple is filled with it, and the prayer is that the whole earth will be filled.

The heavens declare — Psalm 19:1–4

1 The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. 2 Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge. 3 There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard. 4 Their line is gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world. In them hath he set a tabernacle for the sun,

Meaning: Creation is a non-stop sermon about God's kavod, heard in every language. Romans 10:18 quotes verse 4 of the gospel going to all the world. Even the sky has "a tabernacle" — creation itself is shaped like a dwelling.

Crowned with glory — Psalm 8:1, 3–6

1 O LORD our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth! who hast set thy glory above the heavens.

3 When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars, which thou hast ordained; 4 What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him? 5 For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour. 6 Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet:

Meaning: God's glory is "above the heavens," yet He crowns small humanity with glory (kavod) and honour (hadar). This is Genesis 1:26–28 in song. Hebrews 2:6–9 shows it fulfilled in Jesus, "crowned with glory and honour" for all who follow.

The King of glory — Psalm 24:7–10

7 Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in. 8 Who is this King of glory? The LORD strong and mighty, the LORD mighty in battle. 9 Lift up your heads, O ye gates; even lift them up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in. 10 Who is this King of glory? The LORD of hosts, he is the King of glory. Selah.

Meaning: Probably sung when the ark entered Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6). The glory is personal — a King who enters. Christians have long read it of Christ's ascension into heaven and His entry into believing hearts.

Glory fills Solomon's temple — 2 Chronicles 7:1–3

1 Now when Solomon had made an end of praying, the fire came down from heaven, and consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices; and the glory of the LORD filled the house. 2 And the priests could not enter into the house of the LORD, because the glory of the LORD had filled the LORD'S house. 3 And when all the children of Israel saw how the fire came down, and the glory of the LORD upon the house, they bowed themselves with their faces to the ground upon the pavement, and worshipped, and praised the LORD, saying, For he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever.

Meaning: The pattern of Exodus 40 repeats: a finished house, prayer, sacrifice, then glory. The people's response joins glory with mercy: "For he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever."

Not unto us — Psalm 115:1–4

1 Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, and for thy truth's sake. 2 Wherefore should the heathen say, Where is now their God? 3 But our God is in the heavens: he hath done whatsoever he hath pleased. 4 Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men's hands.

Meaning: The right posture toward glory: it belongs to God's name. Verse 3 links glory to God's sovereign pleasure (theme 1 and 2), and verse 4 contrasts it with lifeless idols.

Let the whole earth be filled — Psalm 72:18–19

18 Blessed be the LORD God, the God of Israel, who only doeth wondrous things. 19 And blessed be his glorious name for ever: and let the whole earth be filled with his glory; Amen, and Amen.

Meaning: The closing prayer of Book Two of the Psalms. The Messianic King's reign (Psalm 72:1–17) leads to the earth filled with glory — the prayer behind Numbers 14:21 and Habakkuk 2:14.

They changed their glory — Psalm 106:19–20

19 They made a calf in Horeb, and worshipped the molten image. 20 Thus they changed their glory into the similitude of an ox that eateth grass.

Meaning: God was Israel's glory, and they traded Him for a statue of a grass-eating ox. Romans 1:23 uses the same language for all humanity.

5. New Testament

In the New Testament, glory takes flesh in Jesus, is revealed supremely through His death and resurrection, is given to believers who are being transformed into it, and will fill the new creation.

We beheld His glory — John 1:14, 18

14 And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

18 No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.

Meaning: Every word recalls Exodus 33–34. "Dwelt" is eskēnōsen, tabernacled. "Full of grace and truth" matches "abundant in goodness and truth" (Exodus 34:6). Moses could see only God's back; the Son, "in the bosom of the Father," has made Him fully known.

He manifested forth His glory — John 2:11

11 This beginning of miracles did Jesus in Cana of Galilee, and manifested forth his glory; and his disciples believed on him.

Meaning: The first sign was turning water into wine at a wedding — glory shown as joy and abundance, and producing faith.

Glory through death — John 12:23–24, 27–28, 41

23 And Jesus answered them, saying, The hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified. 24 Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit.

27 Now is my soul troubled; and what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour: but for this cause came I unto this hour. 28 Father, glorify thy name. Then came there a voice from heaven, saying, I have both glorified it, and will glorify it again.

41 These things said Esaias, when he saw his glory, and spake of him.

Meaning: The surprise of John's Gospel: the "hour" of glory is the cross. Like a seed, glory multiplies through death. Verse 41 identifies the glory Isaiah saw (Isaiah 6) as Christ's.

The glory I had with Thee — John 17:1, 4–5, 22, 24

1 These words spake Jesus, and lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee:

4 I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do. 5 And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was.

22 And the glory which thou gavest me I have given them; that they may be one, even as we are one:

24 Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory, which thou hast given me: for thou lovedst me before the foundation of the world.

Meaning: The whole arc in one prayer: glory shared before the world (v. 5), revealed by finishing the work (v. 4), given to believers to make them one (v. 22), and enjoyed forever with Christ (v. 24).

Short of the glory — Romans 3:23; 8:18

3:23 For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;

8:18 For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.

Meaning: "Come short" (hysterountai) is present tense: humanity keeps falling short of the glory it was made for (Psalm 8:5). The gospel restores it: glory "shall be revealed in us," outweighing all present suffering.

From glory to glory — 2 Corinthians 3:18; 4:6–7

3:18 But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.

4:6 For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ. 4:7 But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us.

Meaning: Moses' veiled, fading glory (Exodus 34) is contrasted with unveiled, increasing glory. Beholding leads to becoming: "changed" is metamorphoumetha, present tense — an ongoing transformation by the Spirit. The glory is seen "in the face of Jesus Christ," and carried in fragile "earthen vessels."

The brightness of His glory — Hebrews 1:1–3

1 God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, 2 Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds; 3 Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high;

Meaning: "Brightness" (apaugasma) is radiance streaming from a source, as light from the sun. The Son is not a reflection of glory but its outshining. He creates, sustains, purges sin and reigns.

Do all to the glory of God — 1 Corinthians 10:31

31 Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.

Meaning: Glory reaches the ordinary. Eating and drinking can display God's worth.

The city having the glory of God — Revelation 21:10–11, 22–23

10 And he carried me away in the spirit to a great and high mountain, and shewed me that great city, the holy Jerusalem, descending out of heaven from God, 11 Having the glory of God: and her light was like unto a stone most precious, even like a jasper stone, clear as crystal;

22 And I saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it. 23 And the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it: for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof.

Meaning: The consummation. No temple is needed because God's presence fills everything. Isaiah 60:19 is fulfilled. The Lamb is the lamp — the glory shines through the One who was slain.

6. Patterns across the canon

Seven patterns recur in all four divisions.

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Glory is God's character made visible Glory shown as "goodness" and "name" (Exodus 33:19; 34:6) "Holy, holy, holy … full of his glory" (Isaiah 6:3) "The King of glory … strong and mighty" (Psalm 24:8) "Full of grace and truth" (John 1:14); "brightness of his glory" (Hebrews 1:3)
2. Glory fills a finished dwelling "So Moses finished the work … the glory … filled" (Exodus 40:33–34) "The glory of the LORD filled the house" (Ezekiel 43:5); "the latter house" (Haggai 2:9) Glory fills Solomon's temple (2 Chronicles 7:1) "Dwelt among us" (John 1:14); the city "having the glory of God" (Revelation 21:11)
3. Glory withdraws at sin and returns in mercy "I will not go up in the midst of thee" (Exodus 33:3) Ichabod (1 Samuel 4:21); glory departs and returns (Ezekiel 10:18; 43:2) "They changed their glory into … an ox" (Psalm 106:20) "Come short of the glory" (Romans 3:23); glory "revealed in us" (Romans 8:18)
4. Beholding glory transforms Moses' face shone (Exodus 34:29) "Woe is me … mine eyes have seen the King" (Isaiah 6:5) "I shall be satisfied … with thy likeness" (Psalm 17:15) "Beholding … are changed … from glory to glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18)
5. Glory guarded from idols, shared with God's people "Thou shalt have no other gods before me" (Exodus 20:3) "My glory will I not give to another" (Isaiah 42:8); "his glory shall be seen upon thee" (Isaiah 60:2) "Not unto us" (Psalm 115:1); "crowned him with glory" (Psalm 8:5) "The glory which thou gavest me I have given them" (John 17:22)
6. Glory comes through suffering "Tell my father of all my glory in Egypt" — Joseph after the pit and prison (Genesis 45:13) The Servant marred, then "exalted" (Isaiah 52:13–14) "Thou … hast crowned him" after being made lower (Psalm 8:5) "Except a corn of wheat … die" (John 12:24); sufferings and glory (Romans 8:18)
7. The whole earth will be filled "All the earth shall be filled with the glory" (Numbers 14:21) "As the waters cover the sea" (Habakkuk 2:14); "all flesh shall see it" (Isaiah 40:5) "Let the whole earth be filled with his glory" (Psalm 72:19) "The glory of God did lighten it" (Revelation 21:23)

7. Principles and practices

The patterns yield seven principles, each with a practice.

  1. Glory is God's character on display. When God shows His glory, He shows His goodness, mercy and truth (Exodus 34:6; John 1:14).
  2. God's glory seeks a dwelling. From tabernacle to temple to Christ to the church to the New Jerusalem, glory fills what is built His way (Exodus 40:34; Ephesians 2:21–22).
  3. Sin trades glory for less. Humanity exchanged God's glory for images and falls short of it (Romans 1:23; 3:23). Restoration comes only through Christ.
  4. We become what we behold. Steady attention to the glory of Christ transforms believers by the Spirit (2 Corinthians 3:18).
  5. Glory belongs to God, yet He shares it. We give Him all glory (Psalm 115:1), and He gives His people a share in union with Christ (John 17:22).
  6. The path to glory runs through the cross. Jesus was glorified through death; believers share glory after suffering (John 12:24; Romans 8:17–18).
  7. The goal is a world full of God's glory. Ordinary life can display it now (1 Corinthians 10:31); the new creation will be filled with it (Revelation 21:23).
Principle Practice Anchor text
1. Character on display Memorise Exodus 34:6–7. When you think of God's glory, think of these qualities. Exodus 34:6–7; John 1:14
2. Glory seeks a dwelling Treat gathered worship as a meeting place with God, not only a meeting of people. Prepare your heart beforehand. Exodus 40:34; 2 Chronicles 7:1–3
3. Sin trades glory Name your "golden calf" — what you look to for worth instead of God — and confess it. Psalm 106:20; Romans 3:23
4. Becoming what we behold Spend daily time looking at Christ in the Gospels before reading anything else. 2 Corinthians 3:18; 4:6
5. Glory given back End each day with Psalm 115:1: "Not unto us … but unto thy name give glory." Psalm 115:1
6. Glory through suffering In hardship, read John 12:24 and Romans 8:18 and ask what may be growing from what is dying. John 12:24; Romans 8:18
7. Glory in the ordinary Pick one routine task each day — eating, work, driving — and do it consciously "to the glory of God." 1 Corinthians 10:31

For a teaching session: Read Exodus 33:18–23 and John 1:14–18 side by side. Ask what Moses saw, what the disciples saw, and what believers see now (2 Corinthians 3:18).
