The Book of Life

Overview

Scripture speaks of God keeping books: a register of the living, a record of deeds, a book of remembrance, and above all "the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world" (Revelation 13:8). This theme shows the personal side of the eternal blueprint. God's plan is not abstract; it is written with names. Jesus told His disciples to rejoice "because your names are written in heaven" (Luke 10:20).

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. Moses' plea — "Blot me … out of thy book"
  2. The register of Zion — names written among the living, Gentiles included
  3. The book of remembrance and Daniel's book — God remembers; the written are delivered
  4. Names written in heaven — Jesus and Paul speak of the book
  5. The Lamb's book of life — opened at the judgment; only the written enter the city

Semantic note: Ancient cities kept citizen registers. To be "written" meant belonging, rights and protection; to be "blotted out" meant losing citizenship or dying. Scripture uses this civic picture of heavenly citizenship (Philippians 3:20). In the Old Testament, "the book of the living" can mean simply physical life (Psalm 69:28). In the New Testament it clearly means eternal life.

Torah

Blot me out of Thy book — Exodus 32:31–33

31 And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, Oh, this people have sinned a great sin, and have made them gods of gold. 32 Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin—; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written. 33 And the LORD said unto Moses, Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book.

Meaning: The first mention of God's book. Moses offers himself in place of the people, a shadow of Christ, who was in fact cut off for others (Isaiah 53:8). Paul echoes this in Romans 9:3. Only the sinless Mediator can bear another's sin.

Names borne on the shoulders and heart — Exodus 28:9–12, 29

9 And thou shalt take two onyx stones, and grave on them the names of the children of Israel: 10 Six of their names on one stone, and the other six names of the rest on the other stone, according to their birth. 11 With the work of an engraver in stone, like the engravings of a signet, shalt thou engrave the two stones with the names of the children of Israel: thou shalt make them to be set in ouches of gold. 12 And thou shalt put the two stones upon the shoulders of the ephod for stones of memorial unto the children of Israel: and Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD upon his two shoulders for a memorial.

29 And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in unto the holy place, for a memorial before the LORD continually.

Meaning: Names engraved in stone are carried on the high priest's shoulders (strength) and heart (love) into God's presence. Christ, our High Priest, bears His people's names before the Father (Hebrews 7:25).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Written among the living — Isaiah 4:2–3

2 In that day shall the branch of the LORD be beautiful and glorious, and the fruit of the earth shall be excellent and comely for them that are escaped of Israel. 3 And it shall come to pass, that he that is left in Zion, and he that remaineth in Jerusalem, shall be called holy, even every one that is written among the living in Jerusalem:

Meaning: The remnant who survive judgment are "called holy" and "written among the living" in the day of the Branch, the Messiah.

Graven upon the palms — Isaiah 49:15–16

15 Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. 16 Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me.

Meaning: God's "book" is His own hands. The writing is permanent. Christians have long connected this to the nail-scarred hands of the risen Christ (John 20:27).

Written in the earth — Jeremiah 17:13; Ezekiel 13:9

Jeremiah 17:13 O LORD, the hope of Israel, all that forsake thee shall be ashamed, and they that depart from me shall be written in the earth, because they have forsaken the LORD, the fountain of living waters.

Ezekiel 13:9 And mine hand shall be upon the prophets that see vanity, and that divine lies: they shall not be in the assembly of my people, neither shall they be written in the writing of the house of Israel, neither shall they enter into the land of Israel; and ye shall know that I am the Lord GOD.

Meaning: The opposite of being written in heaven: names written in dust that fades, or left out of the register of God's people. Forsaking "the fountain of living waters" is forsaking life itself.

A book of remembrance — Malachi 3:16–17

16 Then they that feared the LORD spake often one to another: and the LORD hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD, and that thought upon his name. 17 And they shall be mine, saith the LORD of hosts, in that day when I make up my jewels; and I will spare them, as a man spareth his own son that serveth him.

Meaning: Even ordinary conversations among those who fear God are recorded. God treasures them as "jewels" (segullah, His special possession, Exodus 19:5).

Writings (Ketuvim)

In Thy book all my members were written — Psalm 139:16

16 Thine eyes did see my substance, yet being unperfect; and in thy book all my members were written, which in continuance were fashioned, when as yet there was none of them.

Meaning: Before a person exists, God's book holds their days and their making. The plan is personal and comes before birth (Jeremiah 1:5).

Are they not in Thy book? — Psalm 56:8

8 Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?

Meaning: God records not only names but sorrows. No tear is lost (Revelation 21:4).

The book of the living — Psalm 69:28

28 Let them be blotted out of the book of the living, and not be written with the righteous.

Meaning: A psalm quoted of Christ's sufferings (John 2:17; Romans 15:3). To be "written with the righteous" is set against being blotted out.

This man was born there — Psalm 87:4–6

4 I will make mention of Rahab and Babylon to them that know me: behold Philistia, and Tyre, with Ethiopia; this man was born there. 5 And of Zion it shall be said, This and that man was born in her: and the highest himself shall establish her. 6 The LORD shall count, when he writeth up the people, that this man was born there. Selah.

Meaning: Former enemies, Egypt ("Rahab") and Babylon among them, are enrolled as native citizens of Zion. This is new birth into the city of God (Galatians 4:26; John 3:3).

It was found written — Esther 6:1–3

1 On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king. 2 And it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's chamberlains, the keepers of the door, who sought to lay hand on the king Ahasuerus. 3 And the king said, What honour and dignity hath been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king's servants that ministered unto him, There is nothing done for him.

Meaning: An earthly picture of providence. A forgotten deed, recorded in a book, is remembered at exactly the right moment. God's books are never forgotten (Hebrews 6:10).

The books were opened; found written in the book — Daniel 7:9–10; 12:1–2

7:9 I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire. 10 A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him: thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened.

12:1 And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people: and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time: and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book. 2 And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.

Meaning: Daniel brings the two kinds of book together. "The books" record deeds for judgment; "the book" records names for deliverance. This leads directly into Revelation 20.

New Testament

Rejoice, your names are written in heaven — Luke 10:17–20

17 And the seventy returned again with joy, saying, Lord, even the devils are subject unto us through thy name. 18 And he said unto them, I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven. 19 Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy: and nothing shall by any means hurt you. 20 Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.

Meaning: Jesus ranks belonging above power. The deepest joy is not what we do for God but that He has written our names.

Whose names are in the book of life — Philippians 4:3; 3:20

4:3 And I intreat thee also, true yokefellow, help those women which laboured with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and with other my fellowlabourers, whose names are in the book of life.

3:20 For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ:

Meaning: Paul applies the book to ordinary co-workers, some of whom were in conflict (4:2). "Conversation" (politeuma) means citizenship: heaven holds the register.

The church of the firstborn, enrolled in heaven — Hebrews 12:22–23

22 But ye are come unto mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to an innumerable company of angels, 23 To the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven, and to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of just men made perfect,

Meaning: Psalm 87 fulfilled. Believers have already come to the heavenly Zion and are enrolled there.

I will not blot out his name — Revelation 3:5

5 He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.

Meaning: Christ's promise to the overcomer: white garments, a name kept, and a name confessed before the Father (Matthew 10:32).

The book of life of the Lamb slain — Revelation 13:8; 17:8

13:8 And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.

17:8 …and they that dwell on the earth shall wonder, whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, when they behold the beast that was, and is not, and yet is.

Meaning: The book belongs to the Lamb, so the names are written because of His sacrifice. In 13:8, "from the foundation of the world" can attach to "slain" (KJV order) or to "written" (as in 17:8). Either way the book belongs to the eternal blueprint (1 Peter 1:19–20; Ephesians 1:4).

Another book was opened — Revelation 20:11–15

11 And I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was found no place for them. 12 And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works. 13 And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works. 14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. 15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

Meaning: Daniel 7 and 12 fulfilled. The books of works show every person's need; only the book of life decides deliverance. Salvation rests on the Lamb's register, not on our record.

Only those written in the Lamb's book — Revelation 21:27

27 And there shall in no wise enter into it any thing that defileth, neither whatsoever worketh abomination, or maketh a lie: but they which are written in the Lamb's book of life.

Meaning: The citizen list of the new Jerusalem. The register of Isaiah 4:3 and Psalm 87 is complete.

Interpretive balance: Christians read the "blotting out" texts (Exodus 32:33; Revelation 3:5) differently. Reformed readers see the names written "from the foundation of the world" as secure, so Revelation 3:5 is a promise that it will never happen. Arminian and Wesleyan readers take the warning to mean a real possibility of being removed through persistent unbelief. Both agree the believer's safety lies in Christ and that the overcomer is assured: "I will not blot out his name."

Patterns, principles and practices

1. God keeps a written book "Thy book which thou hast written" (Exodus 32:32) "A book of remembrance was written" (Malachi 3:16) "In thy book all my members were written" (Psalm 139:16) "The book of life" (Philippians 4:3)
2. Names carried before God Names engraved on the high priest's shoulders and heart (Exodus 28:12, 29) "Graven thee upon the palms of my hands" (Isaiah 49:16) "This man was born there" (Psalm 87:6) "Your names are written in heaven" (Luke 10:20)
3. Written versus blotted out "Him will I blot out of my book" (Exodus 32:33) "Written in the earth" (Jeremiah 17:13) "Blotted out of the book of the living" (Psalm 69:28) "I will not blot out his name" (Revelation 3:5)
4. A mediator stands for the written Moses offers himself (Exodus 32:32) "He was cut off out of the land of the living" (Isaiah 53:8) Michael "standeth for the children of thy people" (Daniel 12:1) "The Lamb slain" owns the book (Revelation 13:8)
5. Enemies enrolled as citizens The "mixed multitude" joins Israel (Exodus 12:38) Strangers "as born in the country" (Ezekiel 47:22) Rahab, Babylon, Philistia "born there" (Psalm 87:4) "The church of the firstborn … written in heaven" (Hebrews 12:23)
6. Books of deeds and the book of life The deed of Amalek recorded (Exodus 17:14) God "hearkened, and heard" (Malachi 3:16) "The books were opened" / "found written in the book" (Daniel 7:10; 12:1) "The books … and another book … the book of life" (Revelation 20:12)
7. Only the written enter The census of the congregation by name (Numbers 1:2) "Every one that is written among the living" (Isaiah 4:3) "Thy people shall be delivered" (Daniel 12:1) "They which are written in the Lamb's book of life" (Revelation 21:27)

Principles

  1. God's plan is personal. He knows and writes names, not just numbers (Psalm 139:16; Luke 10:20).
  2. The book belongs to the Lamb. Names are written because of His sacrifice, planned from the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8).
  3. Belonging matters more than performance. Rejoice that your name is written, more than in what you achieve (Luke 10:20).
  4. Deeds are recorded, but deliverance rests on the book of life. Judgment exposes; grace saves (Revelation 20:12, 15).
  5. God remembers what others forget. Faithful words, tears and deeds are recorded (Malachi 3:16; Psalm 56:8; Hebrews 6:10).
  6. The register is open to outsiders. Former enemies become citizens of Zion (Psalm 87:4–6).
  7. The overcomer's name is secure in Christ. He confesses it before the Father (Revelation 3:5).

For a teaching session: Read Exodus 32:31–33, Daniel 12:1 and Revelation 20:11–15. Ask: what was Moses willing to give up, and what did Christ actually give up so our names could be written?
