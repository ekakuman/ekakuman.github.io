The Character of God: Justice, Righteousness, Mercy and More

Introduction / పరిచయం

God's justice, righteousness, mercy, steadfast love, grace, holiness, truth, patience, goodness and wrath are not separate parts of God. Each one is the whole God, seen from a different side. Scripture names many of them together in one breath (Ex 34:6–7; Ps 89:14; Jer 9:24). This study traces them together so that none is lost or set against another.

How this fits the Divine Blueprint. God's character is the foundation the plan stands on. Before the foundation of the world He purposed to show all of these attributes at once. In history He showed them one by one, through law, prophets, judgment and deliverance. At the cross He showed them all together. At the end He will reveal them fully before all creation.

Method. Each attribute is traced through the Torah, the Prophets, the Writings and the New Testament. From the four streams we draw patterns, extract principles and develop practices.

Key question for the whole study: How can God be perfectly just and perfectly merciful at the same time? The answer is the Lamb (Rom 3:26).

Verses are quoted from the KJV. Telugu terms follow the BSI Telugu Bible (పరిశుద్ధ గ్రంథము).

Before the Foundation / జగత్తు పునాది వేయబడక మునుపే

These attributes were not invented to manage a fallen world. God was just, holy, true and loving in eternity, within the fellowship of Father, Son and Spirit (John 17:24). The plan made before the foundation was shaped to display every one of them.

God's own self-description (the master text). When Moses asked to see God's glory, God proclaimed His name:

"The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty." (Ex 34:6–7)

In one sentence God names mercy, grace, longsuffering, goodness, truth, steadfast love and forgiveness. He also names justice: He "will by no means clear the guilty." The rest of the Bible quotes or echoes this text again and again (Num 14:18; Neh 9:17; Ps 86:15; 103:8; 145:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2).

The tension built into the name. How can God both forgive iniquity and not clear the guilty? The Old Testament raises the question and the New Testament answers it.

The answer was planned before the question was asked.

  • The Lamb was "foreordained before the foundation of the world" (1 Pet 1:19–20; Rev 13:8).
  • Grace was "given us in Christ Jesus before the world began" (2 Tim 1:9).
  • We were chosen in Him "that we should be holy and without blame before him in love" (Eph 1:4).

Blueprint principle: God never had to choose between His justice and His mercy. From eternity He planned a way to satisfy both completely.

1. Justice and Fairness / న్యాయము

God judges rightly, gives each person their due, and shows no partiality (Deut 10:17; Rom 2:11). "Justice and judgment are the habitation of thy throne" (Ps 89:14).

Torah — justice established

  • Gen 18:25: "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" Abraham appeals to God's own fairness, and God spares the city for ten righteous.
  • Gen 3; 6–9: God judges sin, yet mercy comes with it: the promise of 3:15, garments of skin, and the ark.
  • Ex 21:23–25: "Eye for eye" set a limit on revenge, so the penalty fit the wrong.
  • Lev 19:15; Deut 1:17: Judges must favor neither the poor nor the mighty. "The judgment is God's."
  • Deut 10:17–18: God "regardeth not persons" and "doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger."
  • Deut 24:16: Each person is put to death for his own sin.
  • Num 35: Cities of refuge protected the innocent from unjust vengeance.

Prophets — justice demanded and promised

  • Isa 1:17; Amos 5:24; Mic 6:8: "Seek judgment, relieve the oppressed" and "let judgment run down as waters."
  • Ezek 18:25–29: Israel says God's way "is not equal." God answers that it is their ways that are unequal.
  • Hab 1:13; 2:4: Habakkuk asks why God tolerates the wicked. The answer: "the just shall live by his faith."
  • Isa 30:18: "The LORD is a God of judgment: blessed are all they that wait for him."
  • Isa 42:1–4; Jer 23:5: The Servant and the righteous Branch will bring justice to the nations.

Writings — justice questioned and trusted

  • Job: The deepest challenge to God's fairness. God answers by showing Himself, not by explaining (Job 38–42).
  • Ps 73: The psalmist envies the prospering wicked "until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end" (v. 17).
  • Ps 9:7–8; 96:13: He judges the world in righteousness and the people with equity.
  • Prov 11:1; 17:15: A false balance is abomination. Justifying the wicked and condemning the just are both abomination.
  • Eccl 3:16–17; 12:14: God shall judge the righteous and the wicked, and every secret thing.

New Testament — justice fulfilled

  • Rom 3:25–26: At the cross God shows He is "just, and the justifier" of the one who believes. This is the keystone verse.
  • Matt 20:1–16: The vineyard workers. Grace goes beyond fairness but never breaks it: "Friend, I do thee no wrong."
  • Luke 18:1–8: If an unjust judge gives justice, how much more will God avenge His elect.
  • John 5:22; Acts 17:31: All judgment is committed to the Son, and the day is already appointed.
  • Rom 2:6–11; Col 3:25: He renders to every man according to his deeds, with no respect of persons.
  • Rev 15:3; 16:7: "Just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints."

Thread: God's justice begins in His throne and ends at His throne. In between, it falls on a Substitute, so that sinners can be forgiven justly.

2. Righteousness / నీతి

Righteousness is who God is; justice is what His righteousness does. God is the standard of right, and He acts in keeping with Himself. Scripture moves from God's own righteousness, to righteousness counted to believers, to righteousness lived out by them.

Torah — righteousness counted

  • Gen 6:9: Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and he walked with God.
  • Gen 15:6: "He believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness." This is the first mention of righteousness by faith.
  • Deut 6:25: For Israel, righteousness meant observing all the commandments, a standard no one could fully keep.
  • Deut 32:4: "A God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he."

Prophets — righteousness promised

  • Isa 45:21: "A just God and a Saviour; there is none beside me." His righteousness and His saving go together.
  • Isa 46:13; 51:5–6: "I bring near my righteousness." God's righteousness comes as salvation.
  • Isa 64:6: "All our righteousnesses are as filthy rags."
  • Isa 61:10: "He hath covered me with the robe of righteousness." It is a garment given, not earned.
  • Jer 23:6: The coming King is named "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS" (YHWH Tsidkenu).
  • Dan 9:7, 18: "O Lord, righteousness belongeth unto thee… we do not present our supplications for our righteousnesses, but for thy great mercies."

Writings — righteousness delighted in

  • Ps 11:7: "The righteous LORD loveth righteousness."
  • Ps 23:3: He leads me in paths of righteousness for His name's sake.
  • Ps 32:1–2: Blessed is the man to whom the LORD imputes not iniquity (quoted in Rom 4:7–8).
  • Ps 143:2: "In thy sight shall no man living be justified."
  • Prov 14:34: "Righteousness exalteth a nation."

New Testament — righteousness given and lived

  • Matt 5:6, 20; 6:33: Hunger for righteousness. It must exceed that of the Pharisees. Seek first His kingdom and His righteousness.
  • Rom 1:17; 3:21–22: The righteousness of God is revealed apart from the law, through faith in Jesus Christ, to all who believe.
  • Rom 4:3–5: Abraham's faith was counted for righteousness. God justifies the ungodly.
  • 2 Cor 5:21: "He hath made him to be sin for us… that we might be made the righteousness of God in him." This is the great exchange.
  • Phil 3:9: Not my own righteousness, but that which is through the faith of Christ.
  • 1 John 2:1, 29; 3:7: Jesus Christ the righteous is our advocate. Everyone who does righteousness is born of Him.
  • Rev 19:8: The bride's fine linen is "the righteousness of saints."

Thread: Righteousness is first God's, then credited to us in Christ (imputed), then worked out in us by the Spirit (imparted), and finally worn by the bride.

3. Mercy and Compassion / కనికరము

Mercy is God holding back the punishment we deserve and moving toward us in our misery. The Hebrew racham comes from the word for womb: a mother's tender feeling for her child (Isa 49:15).

Torah — mercy at the mercy seat

  • Gen 19:16: "The LORD being merciful unto him," the angels pulled Lot out of Sodom.
  • Ex 2:23–25; 3:7: "I have surely seen the affliction of my people… I know their sorrows."
  • Ex 25:17–22: The mercy seat (kapporet, కరుణాసనము) covered the law inside the ark. There God met His people, through sprinkled blood (Lev 16:14).
  • Num 14:18–20: Moses pleads Ex 34:6, and God answers, "I have pardoned according to thy word."
  • Deut 4:31: "The LORD thy God is a merciful God; he will not forsake thee."

Prophets — mercy in the middle of judgment

  • Isa 54:7–8: "For a small moment have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee."
  • Isa 55:7: Let the wicked return, "and he will have mercy upon him… for he will abundantly pardon."
  • Hos 11:8–9: "How shall I give thee up, Ephraim?… my repentings are kindled together."
  • Jonah 4:2, 11: Jonah resents Ex 34:6 being applied to Nineveh. God pities the city of 120,000.
  • Mic 7:18: "Who is a God like unto thee… he delighteth in mercy."
  • Hab 3:2: "In wrath remember mercy."

Writings — mercy sung

  • Ps 51:1: "Have mercy upon me, O God… according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies."
  • Ps 103:8–14: He has not dealt with us after our sins. "Like as a father pitieth his children."
  • Lam 3:22–23: "It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed… they are new every morning."
  • Prov 28:13: Whoever confesses and forsakes his sins shall have mercy.

New Testament — mercy in person

  • Matt 9:13, 36: "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice." Jesus was moved with compassion (splanchnizomai) on the crowds.
  • Luke 1:50, 78: Mary and Zechariah sing of the "tender mercy of our God" in the coming Messiah.
  • Luke 15:20: The father saw his son afar off, "had compassion, and ran."
  • Luke 18:13: "God be merciful to me a sinner." The Greek word hilasthēti is linked to the mercy seat.
  • Rom 9:15–16; Titus 3:5: Salvation rests on God who shows mercy, not on our works of righteousness.
  • Eph 2:4: "God, who is rich in mercy."
  • Heb 4:16: "Come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy."
  • Jas 2:13: "Mercy rejoiceth against judgment."

Thread: Mercy has always met people at a place covered with blood: the mercy seat in the Torah, the cross in the New Testament (Rom 3:25, where hilastērion means "mercy seat").

4. Steadfast Love / కృప (chesed)

Chesed is love bound by covenant: loyal, faithful, and stubborn. Mercy pities the miserable; chesed keeps its promise even when the other side breaks theirs. The KJV renders it "mercy," "lovingkindness" or "kindness." The BSI renders it కృప.

Torah — chesed sworn

  • Gen 24:27: Abraham's servant blesses God, who "hath not left destitute my master of his mercy (chesed) and his truth."
  • Ex 20:6: He shows chesed "unto thousands of them that love me."
  • Ex 34:6–7: "Abundant in goodness (chesed)… keeping mercy (chesed) for thousands."
  • Deut 7:9: "The faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy… to a thousand generations."

Prophets — chesed outlasting unfaithfulness

  • Isa 54:10: "The mountains shall depart… but my kindness shall not depart from thee."
  • Jer 31:3: "I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee."
  • Hos 2:19; 6:6: "I will betroth thee unto me… in lovingkindness." "I desired mercy (chesed), and not sacrifice."
  • Mic 6:8: "To love mercy (chesed)."

Writings — chesed celebrated

  • Ruth 2:20; 3:10: Boaz, the kinsman-redeemer, acts out God's chesed. This is a picture of Christ.
  • Ps 136: All 26 verses end "for his mercy (chesed) endureth for ever" (ఆయన కృప నిరంతరముండును).
  • Ps 63:3: "Thy lovingkindness is better than life."
  • Ps 89:1–4, 33–34: God's chesed to David will not be taken away, even when his sons sin.
  • Lam 3:22: "It is of the LORD's mercies (chesed) that we are not consumed."

New Testament — chesed fulfilled

  • Luke 1:72: God has come "to perform the mercy promised to our fathers, and to remember his holy covenant."
  • John 13:1: "Having loved his own… he loved them unto the end."
  • Rom 8:35–39: Nothing shall separate us from the love of God in Christ.
  • 2 Tim 2:13: "If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself."

Thread: Chesed explains why God did not abandon Israel or us. His love is not a feeling that fades but a covenant He has sworn by Himself (Heb 6:13–18).

5. Grace / కృప (charis)

Mercy is not getting what we deserve; grace is getting what we do not deserve. Grace is favor given freely to people who have earned the opposite.

Torah — grace found

  • Gen 6:8: "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD." This is the first use of the word, and it comes before Noah is called just (6:9).
  • Ex 33:12–17: Moses: "If I have found grace in thy sight, shew me now thy way."
  • Num 6:24–26: "The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee."
  • Deut 7:7–8: God chose Israel not because they were many, "but because the LORD loved you."

Prophets — grace announced

  • Isa 55:1: "Buy wine and milk without money and without price."
  • Ezek 16:1–14: God finds the abandoned baby in the field and says, "Live." Pure grace.
  • Zech 4:7: The capstone is brought forth "with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it."
  • Zech 12:10: "I will pour upon the house of David… the spirit of grace."

Writings — grace received

  • Ps 84:11: "The LORD will give grace and glory: no good thing will he withhold."
  • Prov 3:34: "He giveth grace unto the lowly" (quoted in Jas 4:6; 1 Pet 5:5).
  • Ruth 2:10: "Why have I found grace in thine eyes… seeing I am a stranger?" Ruth the Moabitess is a picture of undeserved welcome. (Mephibosheth at David's table, 2 Sam 9, is another, from the Former Prophets.)

New Testament — grace in full

  • John 1:14, 16–17: The Word was "full of grace and truth… and of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace."
  • Rom 3:24: "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus."
  • Rom 5:20–21: "Where sin abounded, grace did much more abound."
  • Eph 2:8–9: "By grace are ye saved through faith… not of works."
  • 2 Cor 12:9: "My grace is sufficient for thee."
  • Titus 2:11–12: Grace not only saves but teaches us to deny ungodliness and live righteously.
  • Rev 22:21: The Bible ends with grace.

Thread: Grace is not God lowering His standard. Grace is God meeting His own standard for us in Christ, then giving the result away freely.

6. Holiness / పరిశుద్ధత

Holiness is the attribute that makes all the others weighty. It is the only attribute called out three times in a row: "Holy, holy, holy" (Isa 6:3; Rev 4:8). Because God is holy, His justice cannot ignore sin; because He is holy, His love is pure and not sentimental.

Torah — holiness approached

  • Ex 3:5: "Put off thy shoes… the place whereon thou standest is holy ground."
  • Ex 15:11: "Who is like thee, glorious in holiness?"
  • Ex 19:12–24: Boundaries around Sinai: holiness is dangerous to the unclean.
  • Ex 28:36: The high priest's gold plate read "HOLINESS TO THE LORD."
  • Lev 10:1–3: Nadab and Abihu: "I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me."
  • Lev 11:44; 19:2: "Ye shall be holy; for I the LORD your God am holy."

Prophets — holiness revealed

  • Isa 6:1–7: Isaiah sees the Holy One and cries, "Woe is me!" A live coal from the altar purges his sin.
  • Isa 57:15: The high and holy One dwells "with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit."
  • Ezek 36:22–27: God acts "for mine holy name's sake" and gives a new heart and His Spirit.
  • Hab 1:13: "Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil."

Writings — holiness worshipped

  • Ps 24:3–4: "Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD?… He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart."
  • Ps 99:3, 5, 9: "He is holy" is the refrain.
  • Prov 9:10: "The knowledge of the holy is understanding."

New Testament — holiness shared

  • Luke 1:35: "That holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."
  • Heb 7:26: Our high priest is "holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners."
  • Heb 10:10, 14: We are sanctified once for all through the offering of His body.
  • Heb 12:10, 14: He disciplines us "that we might be partakers of his holiness."
  • 1 Pet 1:15–16: "Be ye holy in all manner of conversation."
  • Rev 21:2, 27: The holy city, where nothing that defiles shall enter.

Thread: Holiness first shuts sinners out (Sinai, the veil), then in Christ brings them in (the veil torn, Matt 27:51; Heb 10:19–20), and finally makes them holy like Him.

7. Truth and Faithfulness / సత్యము, నమ్మకత్వము

Truth and faithfulness come from the same Hebrew root (aman, from which we get "Amen"). God is true in what He says and faithful in what He does. Every other attribute is only as reliable as this one.

Torah — the God who keeps His word

  • Gen 15:17–18; 22:16: God binds Himself by covenant and swears by Himself.
  • Num 23:19: "God is not a man, that he should lie… hath he said, and shall he not do it?"
  • Deut 7:9; 32:4: "The faithful God… a God of truth."

Prophets — faithful when Israel was not

  • Josh 21:45; 23:14: "There failed not ought of any good thing which the LORD had spoken."
  • Isa 25:1: "Thy counsels of old are faithfulness and truth."
  • Isa 55:10–11: My word "shall not return unto me void."
  • Jer 33:20–21: If you could break the covenant of day and night, then His covenant with David could be broken.

Writings — faithfulness leaned on

  • Ps 36:5: "Thy faithfulness reacheth unto the clouds."
  • Ps 89:33–34: "My covenant will I not break, nor alter the thing that is gone out of my lips."
  • Ps 119:89–90: "For ever, O LORD, thy word is settled in heaven. Thy faithfulness is unto all generations."
  • Lam 3:23: "Great is thy faithfulness."

New Testament — truth embodied

  • John 1:17; 14:6: "Grace and truth came by Jesus Christ." "I am the way, the truth, and the life."
  • 2 Cor 1:20: "All the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen."
  • 1 Cor 10:13: "God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able."
  • 1 Thess 5:24: "Faithful is he that calleth you, who also will do it."
  • 1 John 1:9: "He is faithful and just to forgive us." His faithfulness to the covenant and His justice toward Christ's payment together guarantee forgiveness.
  • Rev 19:11: The rider on the white horse "was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge."

Thread: Because God is true, His warnings of judgment are as certain as His promises of mercy. Both stand on the same word.

8. Longsuffering and Goodness / దీర్ఘశాంతము, మంచితనము

Longsuffering is God's justice on a long fuse: judgment is certain, but He waits to give room for repentance. Goodness is His generous kindness to all He made, including the undeserving (Matt 5:45).

Torah

  • Gen 1:31: "God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."
  • Gen 6:3; 15:16: 120 years before the flood. "The iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full": over 400 years of waiting.
  • Ex 33:19: "I will make all my goodness pass before thee."
  • Num 14:18: "The LORD is longsuffering… and by no means clearing the guilty."

Prophets

  • Isa 30:18: "Therefore will the LORD wait, that he may be gracious unto you."
  • Jer 7:13, 25: "Rising up early and speaking… rising up early and sending" the prophets, for centuries.
  • Joel 2:13: "He is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil."
  • Nah 1:3: "The LORD is slow to anger… and will not at all acquit the wicked." Patience is not weakness.

Writings

  • Ps 34:8: "O taste and see that the LORD is good."
  • Ps 145:8–9: "Slow to anger… The LORD is good to all."
  • Neh 9:17, 30: "Many years didst thou forbear them."
  • Eccl 8:11: Because sentence is not executed speedily, men's hearts are set to do evil. People misread God's patience.

New Testament

  • Luke 13:6–9: The barren fig tree is given one more year.
  • Rom 2:4: "The goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance."
  • Rom 3:25: In His forbearance God "passed over" sins committed before the cross. The cross shows this was not injustice.
  • 1 Tim 1:16: Paul was shown mercy so that Christ might show "all longsuffering" as a pattern.
  • 2 Pet 3:9, 15: "Not willing that any should perish… account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation."
  • Gal 5:22: Longsuffering and goodness are fruit of the Spirit in us.

Thread: God's patience answers the question "Why doesn't God judge evil now?" He will, but every day of delay is mercy offered.

9. Wrath and Judgment / ఉగ్రత, తీర్పు

God's wrath is not a temper. It is His settled, holy opposition to everything that destroys what He loves. A God who loves the oppressed must oppose the oppressor; a God with no wrath would not be good.

Torah — wrath revealed

  • Gen 6:5–7; 19:24: The flood, and Sodom and Gomorrah.
  • Ex 12:12–13: Judgment on Egypt's gods. "When I see the blood, I will pass over you." Wrath and rescue come on the same night.
  • Ex 32:10–14: At the golden calf, Moses intercedes and wrath is turned away.
  • Num 16:46–48: Aaron stands "between the dead and the living" with incense, and the plague is stayed. This is a picture of Christ.
  • Lev 16:21–22: The scapegoat bears the people's iniquities away.

Prophets — the cup of wrath

  • Isa 51:17, 22: "The cup of his fury… I have taken out of thine hand."
  • Jer 25:15–17: The wine cup of fury given to the nations.
  • Isa 53:4–6, 10: "It pleased the LORD to bruise him… the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."
  • Ezek 33:11: "I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn."
  • Zeph 1:14–18; Mal 4:1–2: The day of the LORD burns like an oven, but for those who fear His name, the Sun of righteousness rises with healing.

Writings — wrath understood

  • Ps 2:12: "Kiss the Son, lest he be angry… Blessed are all they that put their trust in him."
  • Ps 7:11: "God judgeth the righteous, and God is angry with the wicked every day."
  • Ps 30:5: "His anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life."
  • Ps 90:11: "Who knoweth the power of thine anger?"

New Testament — wrath borne and escaped

  • Matt 26:39; 27:46: In Gethsemane Jesus prays "let this cup pass," then drinks it on the cross: "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"
  • John 3:36: "He that believeth not the Son… the wrath of God abideth on him."
  • Rom 1:18: "The wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness."
  • Rom 5:9: "Justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him."
  • Rom 8:1: "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus."
  • 1 Thess 1:10; 5:9: Jesus "delivered us from the wrath to come." "God hath not appointed us to wrath."
  • Rev 6:16–17; 20:11–15: "The wrath of the Lamb." The great white throne. Books are opened.

Thread: Wrath is never removed by ignoring sin. It is either borne by the sinner or borne by the Substitute. The cup was passed to Christ so it would not be passed to us.

Where They Meet: The Cross / సిలువ వద్ద కలయిక

At the cross no attribute of God was set aside to make room for another. Every one was fully expressed at the same moment.

"Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other." (Ps 85:10)

"To declare, I say, at this time his righteousness: that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus." (Rom 3:26)

Attribute Its demand or desire How the cross fulfils it
Justice Sin must be punished; no partiality Sin was punished in full, in the Son (Isa 53:5; 1 Pet 3:18)
Righteousness God must act in keeping with His own standard Christ's perfect obedience is credited to believers (2 Cor 5:21)
Holiness Nothing unclean can come near The blood cleanses and the veil is torn (Heb 10:19–22)

Patterns, Principles, Practices / మాదిరులు, సూత్రాలు, ఆచరణ

Patterns (what repeats across all four divisions)

  1. Paired attributes. Scripture almost never names one without another: justice and judgment, mercy and truth, grace and truth, righteousness and peace, goodness and severity (Rom 11:22).
  2. Judgment with a way of escape. The ark in the flood, the blood at Passover, Rahab's scarlet cord, Nineveh's repentance, and the cross.
  3. Substitution. Isaac's ram, the Passover lamb, the Day of Atonement, the Suffering Servant, and the Lamb of God.
  4. The vulnerable are protected. The widow, orphan, stranger and poor are named in the Torah, defended by the Prophets, sung for in the Writings, and served by Christ.
  5. Delay, not denial. God waits (Amorites, Israel, Nineveh, the present age), but judgment comes.
  6. The questioner is answered by presence. Abraham, Job, Asaph and Habakkuk raise fairness questions; each is answered by seeing God.

Principles (what is always true)

  • God's attributes never compete; they are unified in His one character.
  • God never forgives by pretending sin did not happen. He forgives by dealing with it justly in Christ.
  • Grace never cancels righteousness; it produces it (Titus 2:11–12).
  • Every apparent unfairness now will be put right before His throne (Gen 18:25; Rev 15:3).
  • How we treat others shows whether we have understood God's mercy (Matt 18:21–35).

Practices (how we live it)

  • Do justly: honest weights and wages; no favoritism by wealth, caste, language or status (Jas 2:1–9).
  • Love mercy: forgive as you have been forgiven (Eph 4:32); show compassion before judging.
  • Walk humbly: stand only in Christ's righteousness, not your own (Phil 3:9).
  • Leave vengeance to God: entrust wrongs to Him that judges righteously (Rom 12:19; 1 Pet 2:23).
  • Speak for the voiceless: today's widow, orphan, migrant and poor (Prov 31:8–9).
  • Be holy: separate from sin, not from sinners (1 Pet 1:15; Luke 15:1–2).
  • Be patient: reflect God's longsuffering toward difficult people (Col 3:12–13).
  • Preach both: proclaim the cross as God's justice and God's mercy at once.

Title: "Just, and the Justifier" / "నీతిమంతుడు మరియు నీతిమంతునిగా తీర్చువాడు"

Discussion Questions and Memory Verses / చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు, కంఠస్థ వచనాలు

Discussion questions

  1. Which attribute of God do you find easiest to believe? Which is hardest? Why?
  2. In Exodus 34:6–7, God says He forgives iniquity yet will not clear the guilty. How does Romans 3:26 resolve this?
  3. Where have you, like Asaph (Ps 73), envied people who seemed to get away with wrong? What changed his view?
  4. Is it ever right to say God is "unfair"? Compare Ezekiel 18:25 and Matthew 20:15.
  5. What is the difference between mercy, chesed and grace? Give one example of each from your own life.
  6. How does knowing God will judge justly free you to forgive (Rom 12:19)?
  7. Where in your church or community is partiality shown (by wealth, language, background)? What would Micah 6:8 look like there?
  8. How does God's holiness change the way you approach His mercy (Heb 4:16; 12:28–29)?
