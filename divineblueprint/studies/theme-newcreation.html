The New Creation

Overview

The Bible begins with "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth" (Genesis 1:1) and ends with "I saw a new heaven and a new earth" (Revelation 21:1). God does not abandon His first creation; He renews it. The new creation has already begun in the risen Christ, "the beginning of the creation of God" (Revelation 3:14). It is at work now in every believer: "if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature" (2 Corinthians 5:17). It will be completed when He says, "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5).

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. The first creation — very good, then cursed through sin
  2. New beginnings as previews — Noah after the flood; Israel through the sea
  3. New heavens and a new earth promised — a new heart, a new spirit, a new song
  4. The new creation begun — in Christ's resurrection and in the believer
  5. All things made new — creation released from bondage; the curse removed

Word study

Word Language / Strong's Meaning Key use

chadash Hebrew H2319 New, fresh "A new heart also will I give you" (Ezekiel 36:26)
chadash (verb) Hebrew H2318 To renew, repair "Thou renewest the face of the earth" (Psalm 104:30)
kainos Greek G2537 New in quality; fresh, unprecedented "A new creature" (2 Corinthians 5:17); "a new heaven and a new earth" (Revelation 21:1)
neos Greek G3501 New in time; young, recent "The new man" (Colossians 3:10)
ktisis Greek G2937 Creation, creature "The creature itself also shall be delivered" (Romans 8:21)
palingenesia Greek G3824 Rebirth, regeneration, renewal "In the regeneration" (Matthew 19:28); "the washing of regeneration" (Titus 3:5)
anakainoō Greek G341 To renew, make new again "The inward man is renewed day by day" (2 Corinthians 4:16)

Semantic note: Revelation uses kainos, not neos: the new creation is new in quality, not a brand-new replacement starting from nothing. Palingenesia ("again-genesis") is used both for personal rebirth (Titus 3:5) and cosmic renewal (Matthew 19:28). The same God who creates (bara') at the beginning creates again at the end (Isaiah 65:17).

Torah

In the beginning; very good — Genesis 1:1–3, 31

1 In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. 2 And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. 3 And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

31 And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

Meaning: The pattern for every new creation: Spirit, Word and light out of darkness. Paul applies it to conversion: "God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts" (2 Corinthians 4:6).

Cursed is the ground — Genesis 3:17–18

17 And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life; 18 Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field;

Meaning: Human sin brings creation into bondage. The thorns return in Christ's crown (Matthew 27:29); the curse is lifted in Revelation 22:3: "there shall be no more curse."

A new beginning after the flood — Genesis 8:21–22; 9:1

8:21 And the LORD smelled a sweet savour; and the LORD said in his heart, I will not again curse the ground any more for man's sake; for the imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth; neither will I again smite any more every thing living, as I have done. 22 While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.

9:1 And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.

Meaning: A world washed through judgment and restarted with Genesis 1:28's blessing. Peter sees the flood as a picture of baptism and of the coming renewal (1 Peter 3:20–21; 2 Peter 3:6–13).

The beginning of months — Exodus 12:2

2 This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you.

Meaning: Redemption resets the calendar. Israel's new life begins at the Passover, a pattern of being made new through the Lamb.

Prophets (Nevi'im)

Behold, I will do a new thing — Isaiah 43:18–19

18 Remember ye not the former things, neither consider the things of old. 19 Behold, I will do a new thing; now it shall spring forth; shall ye not know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert.

Meaning: God's coming act will outshine even the Exodus. Something new springs out of barrenness.

New heavens and a new earth — Isaiah 65:17–19, 25; 66:22

65:17 For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind. 18 But be ye glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create: for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy. 19 And I will rejoice in Jerusalem, and joy in my people: and the voice of weeping shall be no more heard in her, nor the voice of crying.

65:25 The wolf and the lamb shall feed together, and the lion shall eat straw like the bullock: and dust shall be the serpent's meat. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, saith the LORD.

66:22 For as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith the LORD, so shall your seed and your name remain.

Meaning: The phrase "new heavens and a new earth" first appears here, with bara', the creation verb. Weeping ends, violence ends, and the serpent is humbled to the dust (Genesis 3:14). Revelation 21:1–4 quotes this directly.

The earth full of the knowledge of the LORD — Isaiah 11:6, 9

6 The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid; and the calf and the young lion and the fatling together; and a little child shall lead them.

9 They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain: for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea.

Meaning: Under the Branch of Jesse, creation's hostility is healed and God's knowledge fills the earth like the sea.

A new heart and a new spirit — Ezekiel 36:26–27

26 A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. 27 And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.

Meaning: New creation begins inside. Jesus' "born of water and of the Spirit" (John 3:5) draws on this passage (36:25).

Every thing shall live whither the river cometh — Ezekiel 47:9, 12

9 And it shall come to pass, that every thing that liveth, which moveth, whithersoever the rivers shall come, shall live: and there shall be a very great multitude of fish, because these waters shall come thither: for they shall be healed; and every thing shall live whither the river cometh.

12 And by the river upon the bank thereof, on this side and on that side, shall grow all trees for meat, whose leaf shall not fade, neither shall the fruit thereof be consumed: it shall bring forth new fruit according to his months, because their waters they issued out of the sanctuary: and the fruit thereof shall be for meat, and the leaf thereof for medicine.

Meaning: Life flows from God's sanctuary and heals even the Dead Sea. Revelation 22:1–2 takes up this river and these trees, and Eden is restored.

Writings (Ketuvim)

No new thing under the sun — Ecclesiastes 1:9

9 The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun.

Meaning: From "under the sun," a fallen world only goes in circles. Real newness has to come from above the sun, from God. That makes the gospel's "all things are become new" (2 Corinthians 5:17) striking.

Create in me a clean heart — Psalm 51:10

10 Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.

Meaning: David uses bara', the creation verb. Only God can create a new heart. This is the inner side of Ezekiel 36:26.

Thou renewest the face of the earth — Psalm 104:30

30 Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth.

Meaning: The Spirit is the agent of creation and renewal, from Genesis 1:2 to Romans 8:23.

As a vesture shalt Thou change them — Psalm 102:25–27

25 Of old hast thou laid the foundation of the earth: and the heavens are the work of thy hands. 26 They shall perish, but thou shalt endure: yea, all of them shall wax old like a garment; as a vesture shalt thou change them, and they shall be changed: 27 But thou art the same, and thy years shall have no end.

Meaning: Hebrews 1:10–12 applies this to the Son. Creation will be changed like clothing. The Creator, unchanging, is the guarantee of the new.

A new song; all creation rejoices — Psalm 96:1, 11–13

1 O sing unto the LORD a new song: sing unto the LORD, all the earth.

11 Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad; let the sea roar, and the fulness thereof. 12 Let the field be joyful, and all that is therein: then shall all the trees of the wood rejoice 13 Before the LORD: for he cometh, for he cometh to judge the earth: he shall judge the world with righteousness, and the people with his truth.

Meaning: Creation itself welcomes its coming Judge, because His coming means setting things right. The "new song" returns in Revelation 5:9.

New Testament

The first day; He breathed on them — John 20:1, 19–22

1 The first day of the week cometh Mary Magdalene early, when it was yet dark, unto the sepulchre, and seeth the stone taken away from the sepulchre.

19 Then the same day at evening, being the first day of the week, when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews, came Jesus and stood in the midst, and saith unto them, Peace be unto you. 20 And when he had so said, he shewed unto them his hands and his side. Then were the disciples glad, when they saw the Lord. 21 Then said Jesus to them again, Peace be unto you: as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you. 22 And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost:

Meaning: Resurrection happens on "the first day," a new Genesis that begins "when it was yet dark" (Genesis 1:2–3). Jesus is met in a garden (20:15), and He "breathed" on His disciples as God breathed into Adam (Genesis 2:7). The new creation has begun.

A new creature — 2 Corinthians 5:17; Galatians 6:15

2 Corinthians 5:17 Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

Galatians 6:15 For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but a new creature.

Meaning: "New creature" can be read "new creation." Being in Christ means belonging already to the coming world. Isaiah 43:18–19 is fulfilled personally.

Created in Christ Jesus; the new man — Ephesians 2:10; 4:22–24

2:10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

4:22 That ye put off concerning the former conversation the old man, which is corrupt according to the deceitful lusts; 23 And be renewed in the spirit of your mind; 24 And that ye put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness.

Meaning: "Workmanship" (poiēma) is a made work. God's new creation includes good works He "before ordained." The new man is the image of God restored (theme 15).

Creation groans, waiting — Romans 8:19–23

19 For the earnest expectation of the creature waiteth for the manifestation of the sons of God. 20 For the creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope, 21 Because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God. 22 For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now. 23 And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body.

Meaning: The Genesis 3 curse was given "in hope." Creation's groans are birth pains, not death throes. Its freedom is tied to the glorification of God's children (theme 27).

In the regeneration — Matthew 19:28

28 And Jesus said unto them, Verily I say unto you, That ye which have followed me, in the regeneration when the Son of man shall sit in the throne of his glory, ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.

Meaning: Palingenesia: the world's "genesis again" when Christ reigns in glory.

New heavens wherein dwelleth righteousness — 2 Peter 3:10–13

10 But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up. 11 Seeing then that all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought ye to be in all holy conversation and godliness, 12 Looking for and hasting unto the coming of the day of God, wherein the heavens being on fire shall be dissolved, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat? 13 Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.

Meaning: Like the flood (3:6), a purifying judgment comes before renewal. "According to his promise" points back to Isaiah 65:17. Its practical result is holy living now.

Behold, I make all things new — Revelation 21:1–5

1 And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea. 2 And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. 3 And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. 4 And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. 5 And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new. And he said unto me, Write: for these words are true and faithful.

Meaning: The Blueprint fulfilled. The Bride (theme 21), the Dwelling (theme 19) and the covenant formula (theme 11) all come together. God says "all things new," not "all new things": renewal, not replacement.

No more curse — Revelation 22:1–3

1 And he shewed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb. 2 In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations. 3 And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:

Meaning: Ezekiel 47 and Eden together. The tree of life is open again, and the Genesis 3 curse is removed.

Interpretive balance: Christians debate whether the new creation is a renewal of this world, as Romans 8:21 and the "all things new" wording suggest, or a replacement after its destruction, as some read 2 Peter 3:10. Note that some ancient manuscripts of 2 Peter 3:10 read "shall be found" or "laid bare" rather than "burned up." Many hold that both pictures are true: a purifying fire that brings in a transformed creation, with continuity like that between Christ's crucified body and His risen body.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Spirit and Word bring life from darkness "The Spirit of God moved … Let there be light" (Genesis 1:2–3) "I will put my spirit within you" (Ezekiel 36:27) "Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created" (Psalm 104:30) "He breathed on them" (John 20:22); light "in our hearts" (2 Corinthians 4:6)
2. New creation through judgment The flood, then a blessed new world (Genesis 8:21–9:1) "Former things … not be remembered" (Isaiah 65:17) "They shall perish … they shall be changed" (Psalm 102:26) "Heavens … dissolved … new heavens" (2 Peter 3:12–13)
3. A new heart before a new world Circumcision of the heart (Deuteronomy 30:6) "A new heart also will I give you" (Ezekiel 36:26) "Create in me a clean heart" (Psalm 51:10) "A new creature" (2 Corinthians 5:17); "the new man" (Ephesians 4:24)
4. Redemption as a new beginning "The beginning of months" at Passover (Exodus 12:2) "I will do a new thing" (Isaiah 43:19) "Sing unto the LORD a new song" (Psalm 96:1) "The first day of the week" (John 20:1)
5. Creation's curse and release "Cursed is the ground" (Genesis 3:17) The wolf and the lamb (Isaiah 11:6; 65:25) "Let the field be joyful" (Psalm 96:12) "Delivered from the bondage of corruption" (Romans 8:21)
6. Life-giving waters and the tree The river of Eden (Genesis 2:10) "Every thing shall live whither the river cometh" (Ezekiel 47:9) "A river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God" (Psalm 46:4) "A pure river of water of life … the tree of life" (Revelation 22:1–2)
7. All things new "Very good" (Genesis 1:31) "New heavens and a new earth" (Isaiah 65:17; 66:22) Newness from above, not "under the sun" (Ecclesiastes 1:9) "Behold, I make all things new" (Revelation 21:5)

Principles

  1. God finishes what He starts. The Creator of the first creation brings it to renewal (Isaiah 65:17; Revelation 21:5).
  2. The new creation began at the resurrection. Christ is "the firstborn from the dead" and the beginning of the new world (Colossians 1:18; Revelation 3:14).
  3. Personal renewal comes first. God makes new people before He makes a new world (2 Corinthians 5:17; Ezekiel 36:26).
  4. The Spirit is the agent of new creation. From Genesis 1:2 to Romans 8:23 (Psalm 104:30; John 20:22).
  5. Creation itself is included in redemption. The physical world matters to God (Romans 8:19–21).
  6. Hope shapes holiness. Waiting for new heavens means living righteously now (2 Peter 3:11–13).
Principle Practice Anchor text
God finishes When something feels broken beyond repair, pray Revelation 21:5 over it. Revelation 21:5

New heart Pray Psalm 51:10 daily for a season. Psalm 51:10

For a teaching session: Read Genesis 1:1–3, John 20:1, 19–22 and Revelation 21:1–5 together. Trace the echoes: first day, darkness and light, garden, breath, a new world. Ask what it means to live as part of the new creation now.
