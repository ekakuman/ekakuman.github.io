The Divine Blueprint and Work

Overview

Work came before the fall. Before sin entered the world, "the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:15). God Himself is a worker: He made the world in six days and rested on the seventh. So work is part of being made in His image, not a punishment. Sin made work painful ("in the sweat of thy face," Genesis 3:19), but in Christ work is redeemed: "whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord" (Colossians 3:23). And it is heading somewhere: "your labour is not in vain in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 15:58).

This study walks work through the five groups of the blueprint:

  • A · Purpose: why God gave work, and what it shows of Him
  • B · Provision: what God supplies for it
  • C · People: who God makes us into through it
  • D · Goal: what it is heading toward
  • E · Consummation: how the end shapes work now

"Work" here means all of it: paid jobs, running a home, caring for family, studying, volunteering, business ownership, retirement service.

Key passage — Ephesians 6:5–9

5 Servants, be obedient to them that are your masters according to the flesh, with fear and trembling, in singleness of your heart, as unto Christ; 6 Not with eyeservice, as menpleasers; but as the servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart; 7 With good will doing service, as to the Lord, and not to men: 8 Knowing that whatsoever good thing any man doeth, the same shall he receive of the Lord, whether he be bond or free. 9 And, ye masters, do the same things unto them, forbearing threatening: knowing that your Master also is in heaven; neither is there respect of persons with him.

Meaning, verse by verse:

  • v. 5 "in singleness of your heart, as unto Christ": the believer's real employer is Christ. "Singleness" means sincerity, an undivided heart.
  • v. 6 "not with eyeservice, as menpleasers": no working only when watched. Work is "doing the will of God from the heart."
  • v. 7 "with good will … as to the Lord, and not to men": the same attitude in every task, because every task is done for Him.
  • v. 8 "the same shall he receive of the Lord": nothing faithful goes unseen. God rewards, whatever a person's status.
  • v. 9 "ye masters, do the same things": employers and managers carry the same obligation. No threatening or abuse, because they too answer to a "Master … in heaven" who shows no favouritism.

Context note: Paul wrote to households that included slaves, a system very different from modern employment and one that the gospel undermined (Galatians 3:28; Philemon 16; 1 Timothy 1:10 condemns "menstealers"). The passage does not endorse slavery. It teaches how believers in any position of work or authority honour Christ: sincerely, wholeheartedly, and answerable to Him.

Companion passage: Colossians 3:22–4:1 gives the same teaching and adds, "ye serve the Lord Christ" (3:24) and "give unto your servants that which is just and equal" (4:1).

The five lenses

A · Purpose — why God gave work

To dress it and to keep it — Genesis 2:15

15 And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.

Meaning: Work is God's gift from before the fall. "Dress" ('abad) is also the word for worship and service, and "keep" (shamar) means to guard and care for. Work is a form of service to God and care for His world.

My Father worketh hitherto — John 5:17

17 But Jesus answered them, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.

Meaning: God is a working God, and the Son shares His work. Made in His image (theme 15), we reflect Him when we create, order, build and care.

Filled with the spirit of God … in all manner of workmanship — Exodus 31:2–5

2 See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah: 3 And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship, 4 To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass, 5 And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship.

Meaning: The first person said to be "filled with the spirit of God" is a craftsman. Skill is a gift and a calling ("called by name"), and ordinary work can be Spirit-empowered (theme 18).

The unseen side. "Knowing that your Master also is in heaven; neither is there respect of persons with him" (Ephesians 6:9). Behind every earthly employer stands an unseen Master. "For ye serve the Lord Christ" (Colossians 3:24): ordinary work, done heartily, is service offered in the unseen realm and rewarded there.

B · Provision — what God supplies

It is He that giveth thee power to get wealth — Deuteronomy 8:17–18

17 And thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. 18 But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth, that he may establish his covenant which he sware unto thy fathers, as it is this day.

Meaning: Ability, health, opportunity and results all come from God. Success is meant to serve His covenant purposes, not our pride.

Except the LORD build the house — Psalm 127:1–2

1 Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain. 2 It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep.

Meaning: Work done without God is "vain," however hard it is. Anxious overwork is not faithfulness; trust allows rest.

All grace … to every good work — 2 Corinthians 9:8

8 And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work:

Meaning: Grace (theme 8) is not only for salvation. God supplies "all sufficiency" for "every good work."

C · People — who God makes us through it

Ye serve the Lord Christ — Colossians 3:23–24

23 And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men; 24 Knowing that of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ.

Meaning: "Heartily" means from the soul. Every job has the same ultimate Boss. The reward is "the inheritance" (theme 23), not just a wage.

Created … unto good works — Ephesians 2:10

10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

Meaning: We are God's work (poiēma) before we do any work. The good works are prepared "before," which ties daily work to the eternal plan (theme 1).

An excellent spirit was in him — Daniel 6:3–4

3 Then this Daniel was preferred above the presidents and princes, because an excellent spirit was in him; and the king thought to set him over the whole realm. 4 Then the presidents and princes sought to find occasion against Daniel concerning the kingdom; but they could find none occasion nor fault; forasmuch as he was faithful, neither was there any error or fault found in him.

Meaning: Daniel worked in a pagan government with excellence and integrity that even his enemies could not fault. This is faithfulness (theme 29) in a secular job.

Diligent in his business — Proverbs 22:29

29 Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before mean men.

Meaning: Skill and diligence open doors. Excellence honours God.

D · Goal — what work is heading toward

Six days … but the seventh day — Exodus 20:9–10

9 Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: 10 But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates:

Meaning: Work is aimed at rest (theme 22). A rhythm of work and rest says that God, not our effort, holds the world together. The rest extends to employees too.

Enjoy good in his labour — Ecclesiastes 2:24

24 There is nothing better for a man, than that he should eat and drink, and that he should make his soul enjoy good in his labour. This also I saw, that it was from the hand of God.

Meaning: Satisfaction in work is a gift "from the hand of God," a taste of shalom (theme 32).

That he may have to give — Ephesians 4:28

28 Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.

Meaning: Work's goal goes beyond self-support: it provides so we can give. Labour becomes love for neighbour.

E · Consummation — how the end shapes work now

Your labour is not in vain — 1 Corinthians 15:58

58 Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.

Meaning: This verse closes the great resurrection chapter. Because of the resurrection, faithful work is not wasted (theme 27).

The fire shall try every man's work — 1 Corinthians 3:13–14

13 Every man's work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man's work of what sort it is. 14 If any man's work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward.

Meaning: Work will be evaluated (theme 33). Quality and motive matter, not only results.

Mine elect shall long enjoy the work of their hands — Isaiah 65:21–23

21 And they shall build houses, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them. 22 They shall not build, and another inhabit; they shall not plant, and another eat: for as the days of a tree are the days of my people, and mine elect shall long enjoy the work of their hands. 23 They shall not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble; for they are the seed of the blessed of the LORD, and their offspring with them.

Meaning: In the new heavens and new earth (theme 26), work continues, freed from futility and injustice. There will be work in eternity, and it will be joyful.

Common struggles, and what the blueprint says

Struggle What the blueprint says Anchor text
Burnout and overwork Labour without the LORD is vain; rest is commanded, and sleep is His gift. Psalm 127:1–2; Exodus 20:9–10; Mark 6:31
Work as identity or idol We are God's workmanship before we are workers. Our worth is in Christ, not in titles or output. Ephesians 2:10; Deuteronomy 8:17–18
Meaninglessness ("it's just a job") All work done "as to the Lord" is service to Christ, and none of it is in vain. Colossians 3:23–24; 1 Corinthians 15:58
A difficult or unfair boss Serve sincerely, not for show, knowing God rewards. Joseph and Daniel served under hostile powers. Ephesians 6:5–8; 1 Peter 2:18–20; Genesis 39:2–3
Temptation to cut corners or lie God's people are known for truth; faithfulness in little matters. Luke 16:10; Proverbs 11:1; Daniel 6:4
Managing others Leaders answer to a Master in heaven: no threats, no favouritism, pay what is just. Ephesians 6:9; Colossians 4:1; James 5:4
Job loss or unemployment God is the provider, not the paycheck. He clothes the lilies and knows our need. Matthew 6:25–33; Philippians 4:19
Envy and comparison Each has a calling and gifts from God; seek the kingdom first. 1 Corinthians 12:4–7; Ecclesiastes 4:4
Separating "sacred" and "secular" work God fills a craftsman with His Spirit and calls him by name; all honest work can glorify Him. Exodus 31:2–5; 1 Corinthians 10:31

Practices

For yourself

Practice Anchor text
Before work, pray: "Lord, I'm working for You today." Colossians 3:23
Do one task this week with excellence that no one will notice. Ephesians 6:6
Keep a weekly day of rest, with work notifications off. Exodus 20:9–10
Set aside part of each paycheck to give before spending. Ephesians 4:28; Proverbs 3:9
At the end of the week, thank God for one thing your work produced. Ecclesiastes 2:24

At work

Practice Anchor text
Be scrupulously honest in small things: hours, expenses, credit for ideas. Luke 16:10
Pray quietly for colleagues by name, especially difficult ones. 1 Timothy 2:1–2
If you lead people, ask: am I "just and equal" with them in pay, workload and respect? Colossians 4:1
Seek the welfare of your workplace, the way Jeremiah told the exiles to seek the city's peace. Jeremiah 29:7

For the church

Practice Anchor text
Pray publicly for workers by profession (teachers, nurses, engineers, drivers, homemakers), not only for pastors and missionaries. Exodus 31:2–5
Teach that work is ministry, not a distraction from it. Colossians 3:24
Support members who are unemployed or overworked, practically and prayerfully. Galatians 6:2; Acts 20:35

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening Genesis 2:15 Ask: "Is work a blessing or a curse?" Work came before the fall.
8 min A · God's design John 5:17; Exodus 31:2–5 God works; we bear His image when we work. Skill is a Spirit-given calling.
7 min B · God's provision Deuteronomy 8:18; Psalm 127:1–2 God gives the ability and the results. Without Him, labour is vain.
10 min C · Working as to the Lord Ephesians 6:5–9; Colossians 3:23–24; Daniel 6:3–4 Sincere, wholehearted, faithful work for the real Boss; leaders answer to Him too.
5 min D and E · Where it is heading Exodus 20:9–10; 1 Corinthians 15:58; Isaiah 65:21–23 Rest now; labour not in vain; joyful work in the new creation.
5 min Response and prayer Colossians 3:23 Each person names one work practice for the week. Pray for people by profession.

Discussion questions

  1. Genesis 2:15 comes before the fall. How does that change how you think about your job?
  2. What does it mean to you that the first person "filled with the spirit of God" was a craftsman (Exodus 31:3)?
  3. Where are you tempted to work only "with eyeservice," when someone is watching (Ephesians 6:6)?
  4. Daniel worked with excellence in a hostile government (Daniel 6:4). What would that look like in your workplace?
  5. How does Psalm 127:2 speak to overwork and anxiety?
  6. If you manage or lead others, how does Ephesians 6:9 shape how you treat them?
  7. Isaiah 65:21–23 pictures work in the new creation. How does that give meaning to work that feels small today?
  8. Which one practice from this study will you start this week?

చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు

  1. ఆదికాండము 2:15 పాపం రాకముందే వస్తుంది. ఇది మీ ఉద్యోగం గురించి మీరు ఆలోచించే విధానాన్ని ఎలా మారుస్తుంది?
  2. "దేవుని ఆత్మచేత నింపబడిన" మొదటి వ్యక్తి ఒక శిల్పి (నిర్గమకాండము 31:3). ఇది మీకు ఏమి చెప్తుంది?
  3. ఎవరైనా చూస్తున్నప్పుడే పని చేయాలనే శోధన మీకు ఎక్కడ వస్తుంది (ఎఫెసీయులకు 6:6)?
  4. శత్రుత్వం గల ప్రభుత్వంలో దానియేలు నమ్మకంగా పనిచేశాడు (దానియేలు 6:4). మీ పనిస్థలంలో అది ఎలా కనిపిస్తుంది?
  5. అతిగా పనిచేయడం, ఆందోళన గురించి కీర్తనలు 127:2 ఏమి చెప్తుంది?
