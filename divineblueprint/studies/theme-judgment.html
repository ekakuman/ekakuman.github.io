The Final Judgment

Overview

The blueprint cannot be finished while evil goes unanswered. God "hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained" (Acts 17:31). The day is appointed (part of the plan) and the Judge is ordained (the risen Christ). Judgment is not a failure of the plan; it is how the plan sets everything right. For those in Christ, the judgment that fell on Him at the cross means "no condemnation" (Romans 8:1). For the world, it is the final vindication of God's justice (theme 28) and the clearing away of everything that ruins creation, so that the new creation (theme 26) can be a world "wherein dwelleth righteousness."

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. The Judge of all the earth — flood, Sodom and Egypt as earlier judgments in history
  2. The day of the LORD — the prophets announce a coming day of reckoning and rescue
  3. The books were opened — Daniel's throne scene; the Psalms long for God to judge
  4. All judgment committed unto the Son — the cross as judgment borne; the judgment seat of Christ
  5. The great white throne — final judgment, death destroyed, all things made right

Semantic note: Hebrew shaphat means more than punishing. It means putting things right: vindicating the oppressed as well as condemning the oppressor (Psalm 82:3; Isaiah 11:4). That is why the Psalms can rejoice that God "cometh to judge the earth" (Psalm 96:13). Greek krinō first meant "to separate." Judgment divides wheat from chaff and sheep from goats (Matthew 3:12; 25:32).

Torah

But Noah found grace — Genesis 6:5–8

5 And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. 6 And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart. 7 And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air; for it repenteth me that I have made them. 8 But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.

Meaning: The first world judgment comes from a grieved heart, not cold anger. Within it, grace provides an ark. Jesus and Peter treat the flood as a preview of the final judgment (Matthew 24:37–39; 2 Peter 3:5–7).

Lot sent out of the overthrow — Genesis 18:25; 19:29

18:25 That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked: and that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee: Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?

19:29 And it came to pass, when God destroyed the cities of the plain, that God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow, when he overthrew the cities in the which Lot dwelt.

Meaning: Judgment is discerning. The Judge separates the righteous from the wicked and rescues His own (2 Peter 2:6–9).

When I see the blood — Exodus 12:12–13

12 For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD. 13 And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.

Meaning: The pattern of the whole theme. Judgment falls, but those sheltered under the lamb's blood are passed over (1 Corinthians 5:7). Judgment also falls on the false gods.

To Me belongeth vengeance — Deuteronomy 32:35–36

35 To me belongeth vengeance, and recompence; their foot shall slide in due time: for the day of their calamity is at hand, and the things that shall come upon them make haste. 36 For the LORD shall judge his people, and repent himself for his servants, when he seeth that their power is gone, and there is none shut up, or left.

Meaning: Vengeance belongs to God alone, which frees His people from taking it (Romans 12:19). "Judge" here also means "vindicate": God acts for His helpless servants (Hebrews 10:30).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

The LORD shall judge the ends of the earth — 1 Samuel 2:10

10 The adversaries of the LORD shall be broken to pieces; out of heaven shall he thunder upon them: the LORD shall judge the ends of the earth; and he shall give strength unto his king, and exalt the horn of his anointed.

Meaning: Hannah's song, the first time Scripture uses "his anointed" (meshicho) for a king. Universal judgment and the Messiah appear together from the start.

With righteousness shall He judge — Isaiah 11:3–4

3 And shall make him of quick understanding in the fear of the LORD: and he shall not judge after the sight of his eyes, neither reprove after the hearing of his ears: 4 But with righteousness shall he judge the poor, and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth: and he shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked.

Meaning: The Messiah judges perfectly. He sees past appearances, vindicates the poor and defeats the wicked by His word (Revelation 19:15).

Whosoever shall call — Joel 2:31–32; 3:14

2:31 The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and the terrible day of the LORD come. 32 And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered: for in mount Zion and in Jerusalem shall be deliverance, as the LORD hath said, and in the remnant whom the LORD shall call.

3:14 Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision: for the day of the LORD is near in the valley of decision.

Meaning: The day of the LORD is terrible, yet a way of rescue is offered to "whosoever." Peter preaches it at Pentecost (Acts 2:20–21), and Paul applies it to all who call on Jesus (Romans 10:13).

Darkness, and not light — Amos 5:18

18 Woe unto you that desire the day of the LORD! to what end is it for you? the day of the LORD is darkness, and not light.

Meaning: A warning against complacency. Israel assumed judgment was only for others; God's people are not exempt (1 Peter 4:17).

I have no pleasure in the death — Ezekiel 18:32

32 For I have no pleasure in the death of him that dieth, saith the Lord GOD: wherefore turn yourselves, and live ye.

Meaning: The heart behind judgment: God would rather people turn and live (2 Peter 3:9).

Who may abide the day of His coming? — Malachi 3:2–3; 4:1–2

3:2 But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner's fire, and like fullers' soap: 3 And he shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver: and he shall purify the sons of Levi, and purge them as gold and silver, that they may offer unto the LORD an offering in righteousness.

4:1 For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the LORD of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch. 2 But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings; and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall.

Meaning: One fire, two effects. It refines those who fear God and consumes the proud. The same day that brings judgment brings healing and joy.

Writings (Ketuvim)

The ungodly shall not stand in the judgment — Psalm 1:5–6

5 Therefore the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous. 6 For the LORD knoweth the way of the righteous: but the way of the ungodly shall perish.

Meaning: The Psalter opens with two ways and one judgment that separates them.

He hath prepared His throne for judgment — Psalm 9:7–8

7 But the LORD shall endure for ever: he hath prepared his throne for judgment. 8 And he shall judge the world in righteousness, he shall minister judgment to the people in uprightness.

Meaning: Paul quotes verse 8 almost word for word in Acts 17:31. The throne of judgment is "prepared": judgment is part of the plan.

Who shall stand? — Psalm 130:3–4

3 If thou, LORD, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand? 4 But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared.

Meaning: If judgment were by our record, no one could stand. Forgiveness is the only way through, and it leads to reverence, not carelessness.

He cometh to judge the earth — Psalm 82:8; 98:8–9

82:8 Arise, O God, judge the earth: for thou shalt inherit all nations.

98:8 Let the floods clap their hands: let the hills be joyful together 9 Before the LORD; for he cometh to judge the earth: with righteousness shall he judge the world, and the people with equity.

Meaning: In Psalm 82, God judges unjust rulers and the psalmist cries for Him to act. In Psalm 98 creation celebrates His coming. Judgment is good news for a groaning world (Romans 8:19–21).

Every work into judgment — Ecclesiastes 12:13–14

13 Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man. 14 For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil.

Meaning: The Preacher's conclusion. Life "under the sun" seems meaningless, but judgment means every deed matters, even the secret ones.

The books were opened; judgment given to the saints — Daniel 7:9–10, 22

9 I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire. 10 A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him: thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened.

22 Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom.

Meaning: The heavenly court sits, the beasts (oppressive empires) are judged, and the saints are vindicated and receive the kingdom (theme 20). Revelation 20:4, 12 takes up this scene.

New Testament

Not to condemn, but that the world might be saved — John 3:17–18

17 For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved. 18 He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.

Meaning: Christ's first coming was for salvation. The verdict is already being decided by how people respond to Him.

All judgment committed unto the Son — John 5:22, 24, 27–29

22 For the Father judgeth no man, but hath committed all judgment unto the Son:

24 Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.

27 And hath given him authority to execute judgment also, because he is the Son of man. 28 Marvel not at this: for the hour is coming, in the which all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, 29 And shall come forth; they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation.

Meaning: The Judge is the Son of man of Daniel 7. The believer "shall not come into condemnation" because the verdict has already been passed. Resurrection leads to judgment for everyone (Daniel 12:2).

Now is the judgment of this world — John 12:31–32

31 Now is the judgment of this world: now shall the prince of this world be cast out. 32 And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.

Meaning: The cross is the turning point of judgment. Sin is judged in Christ, Satan is cast out, and people from every nation are drawn to Him.

He hath appointed a day — Acts 17:30–31

30 And the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent: 31 Because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained; whereof he hath given assurance unto all men, in that he hath raised him from the dead.

Meaning: The anchor text. The day is appointed, the Judge is ordained, and the resurrection guarantees it. The fitting response is repentance.

No condemnation — Romans 8:1; 2:5–6, 16

8:1 There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

2:5 But after thy hardness and impenitent heart treasurest up unto thyself wrath against the day of wrath and revelation of the righteous judgment of God; 6 Who will render to every man according to his deeds:

2:16 In the day when God shall judge the secrets of men by Jesus Christ according to my gospel.

Meaning: Two outcomes. Outside Christ, the righteous judgment of God according to deeds; in Christ, "no condemnation," because He bore the judgment (Isaiah 53:5).

The judgment seat of Christ — 2 Corinthians 5:10; 1 Corinthians 4:5

2 Corinthians 5:10 For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad.

1 Corinthians 4:5 Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts: and then shall every man have praise of God.

Meaning: Believers too will give account, not to be condemned but to have their lives evaluated and rewarded (1 Corinthians 3:12–15). "Then shall every man have praise of God."

The sheep and the goats — Matthew 25:31–32, 46

31 When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory: 32 And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as a shepherd divideth his sheep from the goats:

46 And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.

Meaning: Krinō as separation. The test is how people treated "the least of these my brethren" (25:40), love that shows living faith.

Once to die, and after this the judgment — Hebrews 9:27–28

27 And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment: 28 So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many; and unto them that look for him shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation.

Meaning: Human destiny (death, then judgment) is matched by Christ's (offered once, then appearing). For those who look for Him, His return means salvation.

The great white throne — Revelation 20:11–12, 14; 21:4

20:11 And I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was found no place for them. 12 And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works.

14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.

21:4 And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

Meaning: Daniel 7:10 fulfilled (theme 24). Death itself is judged and destroyed. Judgment clears the ground for a world with "no more death."

Interpretive balance: Christians differ on several details. Some hold a single general judgment; others see separate judgments (for example, the judgment seat of Christ for believers and the great white throne for the rest). The nature of final punishment is also debated. Most traditions hold to eternal conscious punishment (Matthew 25:46). Conditional immortality (annihilationism) reads "perish" and "destruction" as final ending (John 3:16; 2 Thessalonians 1:9). A minority hope for eventual universal restoration. All agree that judgment is certain, righteous and carried out by Christ, and that there is no condemnation for those in Him.

Principles

  1. Judgment is part of the blueprint. The day is appointed and the Judge ordained (Acts 17:31).
  2. God takes no pleasure in judgment. He calls people to turn and live (Ezekiel 18:32; 2 Peter 3:9).
  3. The cross is judgment borne. Christ took the condemnation so believers face none (John 12:31; Romans 8:1).
  4. Every life will be accounted for. Even believers give account at the judgment seat of Christ, for reward (2 Corinthians 5:10; Ecclesiastes 12:14).
  5. Judgment is good news for the oppressed. God's verdict vindicates the wronged (Psalm 82; Daniel 7:22).
  6. Judgment ends in renewal. Death itself is judged, and the new creation follows (Revelation 20:14–21:4).

For a teaching session: Read Exodus 12:12–13, John 5:24 and Romans 8:1. Ask: how does the Passover explain why believers do not fear the final judgment, and why does that make them more serious about life, not less?
