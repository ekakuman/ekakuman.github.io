Faith

Overview

God's provision is received by faith. Faith is how the people (group C) take hold of what the Purpose planned and the Provision supplied: "by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God" (Ephesians 2:8). Faith is not a work that earns. It is leaning one's whole weight on a faithful God (theme 29). The same Hebrew root gives both "faithfulness" ('emunah) and "believe" (he'emin): faith is the human "Amen" to God's reliability. From Abraham to the "great cloud of witnesses" (Hebrews 12:1), the people of the blueprint are people of faith, and their story ends in sight: "we shall see him as he is" (1 John 3:2).

The theme moves in five stages:

  1. Abraham believed — faith counted for righteousness; faith that obeys and offers
  2. Believe in the LORD, so shall ye be established — the prophets call for trust instead of alliances
  3. Trust in the LORD with all thine heart — the Writings show faith in daily life and under fire
  4. Faith in Christ — believing on the Son; justified by faith; faith working by love
  5. Faith becomes sight — the end of faith is the salvation of the soul

hypostasis Greek G5287 Substance; firm foundation; title-deed "The substance of things hoped for" (Hebrews 11:1)

Semantic note: He'emin literally means "to make oneself firm in" someone: to rest your weight on them. John's Gospel never uses the noun "faith"; it uses the verb pisteuō 98 times, often with eis ("believe into"). Faith is something done, a moving of oneself into Christ. Hypostasis in the papyri can mean a title-deed: faith is the deed of ownership to what is hoped for before it is seen.

Torah

Tell the stars … and he believed — Genesis 15:5–6

5 And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be. 6 And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.

Meaning: The Bible's first statement of faith. Abraham believes a promise that looked impossible: an old man with no son, shown a sky full of descendants. Faith rests on the word and character of the One who promises (Romans 4:18–21).

Faith tested on Moriah — Genesis 22:2, 8, 12

2 And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.

8 And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.

12 And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.

Meaning: Faith obeys even when the promise seems to be at risk. Hebrews 11:17–19 says Abraham reasoned "that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead." James 2:21–23 calls this faith "made perfect" by works.

Stand still, and see — Exodus 14:13–14, 31

13 And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will shew to you to day: for the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever. 14 The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.

31 And Israel saw that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians: and the people feared the LORD, and believed the LORD, and his servant Moses.

Meaning: Faith rests in God's action. Salvation is something to "see," not to produce.

How long ere they believe Me? — Numbers 14:11

11 And the LORD said unto Moses, How long will this people provoke me? and how long will it be ere they believe me, for all the signs which I have shewed among them?

Meaning: Unbelief after many signs. This is why a generation died in the wilderness and did not enter rest (theme 22; Hebrews 3:19).

Look and live — Numbers 21:8–9

8 And the LORD said unto Moses, Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live. 9 And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.

Meaning: Faith pictured as a look. No effort or medicine, only trusting God's appointed remedy. Jesus applies it to Himself (John 3:14–15).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

The LORD your God, He is God — Joshua 2:9, 11

9 And she said unto the men, I know that the LORD hath given you the land, and that your terror is fallen upon us, and that all the inhabitants of the land faint because of you.

11 And as soon as we had heard these things, our hearts did melt, neither did there remain any more courage in any man, because of you: for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath.

Meaning: A Canaanite woman's confession. Faith comes by hearing (Romans 10:17). Rahab is listed among the faithful (Hebrews 11:31) and in Christ's genealogy (Matthew 1:5).

If ye will not believe — Isaiah 7:9; 28:16

7:9 And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah's son. If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established.

28:16 Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste.

Meaning: A wordplay in Hebrew: im lo ta'aminu ki lo te'amenu, "if you will not be firm in faith, you will not be made firm." The sure foundation is the cornerstone, Christ (1 Peter 2:6).

Perfect peace, mind stayed on Thee — Isaiah 26:3–4

3 Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee. 4 Trust ye in the LORD for ever: for in the LORD JEHOVAH is everlasting strength:

Meaning: "Perfect peace" is shalom shalom (theme 32). Trust is a mind "stayed," leaning and resting on God.

A tree planted by the waters — Jeremiah 17:7–8

7 Blessed is the man that trusteth in the LORD, and whose hope the LORD is. 8 For he shall be as a tree planted by the waters, and that spreadeth out her roots by the river, and shall not see when heat cometh, but her leaf shall be green; and shall not be careful in the year of drought, neither shall cease from yielding fruit.

Meaning: Contrasted with trusting in man (17:5–6). Faith puts down roots in God and bears fruit even in drought.

Although the fig tree shall not blossom — Habakkuk 2:4; 3:17–18

2:4 Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith.

3:17 Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: 18 Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation.

Meaning: Habakkuk shows what "living by faith" looks like: joy in God when every visible support has failed.

Writings (Ketuvim)

Trust in the LORD with all thine heart — Proverbs 3:5–6

5 Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. 6 In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.

Meaning: Batach, trust as reliance. Faith is not only for salvation; it is the posture of daily decisions.

Commit thy way — Psalm 37:3–5

3 Trust in the LORD, and do good; so shalt thou dwell in the land, and verily thou shalt be fed. 4 Delight thyself also in the LORD; and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart. 5 Commit thy way unto the LORD; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass.

Meaning: "Commit" is literally "roll" your way onto the LORD. Trust, delight, commit: faith hands over the weight.

What time I am afraid — Psalm 56:3–4

3 What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee. 4 In God I will praise his word, in God I have put my trust; I will not fear what flesh can do unto me.

Meaning: Faith does not mean having no fear. It is a decision made in the middle of fear.

Though He slay me — Job 13:15

15 Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him: but I will maintain mine own ways before him.

Meaning: Faith that holds on to God when God Himself seems to be against you.

Believe in the LORD your God — 2 Chronicles 20:20

20 And they rose early in the morning, and went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa: and as they went forth, Jehoshaphat stood and said, Hear me, O Judah, and ye inhabitants of Jerusalem; Believe in the LORD your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper.

Meaning: Isaiah 7:9 applied in battle. Judah went out singing (20:21–22), and God gave the victory.

But if not — Daniel 3:17–18; 6:23

3:17 If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. 18 But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.

6:23 Then was the king exceeding glad for him, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God.

Meaning: Faith trusts God's power ("able") and stays loyal even without a guarantee ("but if not"). Hebrews 11:33–34 recalls those who "stopped the mouths of lions, quenched the violence of fire."

New Testament

Whosoever believeth in Him — John 3:14–16

14 And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: 15 That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life. 16 For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

Meaning: Numbers 21 fulfilled. The Love of God (theme 3) gives the Son, and faith is the look that receives Him.

Help Thou mine unbelief — Mark 9:23–24

23 Jesus said unto him, If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth. 24 And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.

Meaning: Weak faith brought honestly to Jesus is still faith. Its strength lies in its object, not its size (Luke 17:6).

Fully persuaded — Romans 4:20–21; 5:1–2

4:20 He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God; 21 And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.

5:1 Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ: 2 By whom also we have access by faith into this grace wherein we stand, and rejoice in hope of the glory of God.

Meaning: Faith gives glory to God by trusting His ability. Its fruit is justification, peace (theme 32), access and the hope of glory (theme 27).

Faith cometh by hearing — Romans 10:9–10, 17

9 That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. 10 For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.

17 So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.

Meaning: Faith is believed in the heart, confessed with the mouth and born from the Word.

Through faith … the gift of God — Ephesians 2:8–10

8 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: 9 Not of works, lest any man should boast. 10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

Meaning: The order of the blueprint: grace (theme 8) is the source, faith is the channel, and good works are the result, works that God "before ordained."

I live by the faith of the Son of God — Galatians 2:20; 5:6

2:20 I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.

5:6 For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love.

Meaning: Faith is union with Christ lived out daily, and it is active, working through love.

Faith without works is dead — James 2:17–18

17 Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone. 18 Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.

Meaning: Real faith shows itself, as Abraham's did on Moriah (2:21–23).

The substance of things hoped for — Hebrews 11:1, 6, 13

1 Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.

6 But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.

13 These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.

Meaning: Hebrews 11 is the gallery of the blueprint's people, from Abel to the prophets. Faith grasps the unseen future as if it were already present.

The author and finisher of our faith — Hebrews 12:1–2

1 Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us, 2 Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.

Meaning: Christ begins and completes faith. Faith keeps its eyes on Him, as Israel looked at the bronze serpent.

The end of your faith — 1 Peter 1:7–9; 2 Corinthians 5:7

1 Peter 1:7 That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ: 8 Whom having not seen, ye love; in whom, though now ye see him not, yet believing, ye rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory: 9 Receiving the end of your faith, even the salvation of your souls.

2 Corinthians 5:7 (For we walk by faith, not by sight:)

Meaning: Faith belongs to the present age. Tested now, it ends in glory when it turns into sight (1 John 3:2).

Interpretive balance: Paul ("justified by faith without the deeds of the law," Romans 3:28) and James ("by works a man is justified, and not by faith only," James 2:24) are reconciled in different ways. Most read Paul as describing how a person is put right with God and James as describing how genuine faith is shown to be real. Traditions also differ on Ephesians 2:8. Some see faith itself as God's gift; others see "that" as referring to the whole of salvation. All agree that salvation is by grace and that living faith works by love.

Patterns, principles and practices

Pattern Torah Prophets Writings New Testament
1. Faith rests on God's word of promise "Tell the stars … and he believed" (Genesis 15:5–6) "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace … because he trusteth" (Isaiah 26:3) "In God I will praise his word" (Psalm 56:4) "Faith cometh by hearing" (Romans 10:17)
2. Faith counted for righteousness Genesis 15:6 "The just shall live by his faith" (Habakkuk 2:4) "Blessed … unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity" (Psalm 32:2) "Justified by faith" (Romans 5:1)
3. Faith looks to God's remedy "When he looketh upon it, shall live" (Numbers 21:8) "A sure foundation: he that believeth" (Isaiah 28:16) "Mine eyes are ever toward the LORD" (Psalm 25:15) "Looking unto Jesus" (Hebrews 12:2); "so must the Son of man be lifted up" (John 3:14)
4. Faith tested and proved Abraham on Moriah (Genesis 22:12) "Although the fig tree shall not blossom" (Habakkuk 3:17–18) "But if not" (Daniel 3:18); "though he slay me" (Job 13:15) "The trial of your faith … more precious than of gold" (1 Peter 1:7)
5. Unbelief forfeits rest "How long … ere they believe me?" (Numbers 14:11) "If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established" (Isaiah 7:9) "They should not enter into my rest" (Psalm 95:11) "They could not enter in because of unbelief" (Hebrews 3:19)
6. Outsiders welcomed by faith The "mixed multitude" leaves with Israel (Exodus 12:38) Rahab: "the LORD your God, he is God" (Joshua 2:11) Ruth: "thy God my God" (Ruth 1:16) "The just shall live by faith" for Jew and Greek alike (Romans 1:16–17)
7. Faith that works Abraham obeys and goes (Genesis 12:4) "A tree planted by the waters … yielding fruit" (Jeremiah 17:8) "Trust in the LORD, and do good" (Psalm 37:3) "Faith which worketh by love" (Galatians 5:6); "faith without works is dead" (James 2:17)

Principles

  1. Faith is the way the people receive the provision. Grace is the source; faith is the channel (Ephesians 2:8).
  2. Faith is only as strong as its object. We lean on God because He is faithful (Romans 4:21; Hebrews 11:11).
  3. Faith is born from God's word. We hear, then believe (Romans 10:17).
  4. Faith is tested to be refined, not destroyed. Trials prove and purify it (1 Peter 1:7; James 1:3).
  5. Living faith works. It obeys, loves and bears fruit (Galatians 5:6; James 2:18).
  6. Faith looks forward. It embraces promises "afar off" and will one day give way to sight (Hebrews 11:13; 1 John 3:2).
Principle Practice Anchor text
Receive the provision Thank God that you are saved by grace through faith, not by performance. Ephesians 2:8–9
Strong object When doubt comes, focus on God's character rather than the strength of your feelings. Romans 4:20–21
Born of the Word Feed faith with daily Scripture reading, even a short passage. Romans 10:17
Tested faith In a current trial, write down "but if not" and still choose loyalty. Daniel 3:17–18
Faith in fear Memorise Psalm 56:3 and pray it the next time you're afraid. Psalm 56:3–4
Honest faith Pray "Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief" about one specific struggle. Mark 9:24
Working faith Pick one act of love that your faith requires this week, and do it. Galatians 5:6; James 2:17
Run with endurance Read one Hebrews 11 life each week and learn from it. Hebrews 12:1–2

For a teaching session: Read Numbers 21:8–9 and John 3:14–16 together. Ask: what did the bitten Israelite have to do? How does that picture saving faith, and how is it different from earning?
