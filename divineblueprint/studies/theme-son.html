The Son: Word and Wisdom

Overview

The Son is the Person at the center of the blueprint: "all things were created by him, and for him: and he is before all things, and by him all things consist" (Colossians 1:16–17). He is God's Word — God expressing Himself — and God's Wisdom — God's plan in personal form.

The Old Testament speaks of Him in shadows: the creating word ("And God said"), the Angel of the LORD who speaks as God, Wisdom rejoicing beside God before creation, the Son whom kings must honour, and the Son of man who receives an everlasting kingdom. The New Testament names Him: "In the beginning was the Word … and the Word was made flesh" (John 1:1, 14); "Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24).

Three prepositions summarise His place in the blueprint (Colossians 1:16):

  • By Him — the agent of creation and redemption
  • For Him — the goal; everything is His inheritance
  • In Him — all things hold together and are summed up

A note on Proverbs 8: Christian teachers read Wisdom there as a poetic personification fulfilled in Christ. Early church debates (against Arius) clarified that "possessed" and "brought forth" describe the Son's eternal relationship to the Father, not His creation. Passages below are given in full from the King James Version.

Two semantic links:

  1. Word and Wisdom are both creation words. God made the heavens "by the word" (Psalm 33:6) and "by wisdom" (Proverbs 3:19). John and Paul identify both with the Son (John 1:3; 1 Corinthians 1:24).
  2. "Firstborn" means rank, not origin. David, the eighth son, is called God's "firstborn, higher than the kings of the earth" (Psalm 89:27).

2. Torah

The Torah shows God acting through His spoken word and appearing through the Angel of the LORD, a messenger who speaks as God Himself. It ends with the promise of a Prophet like Moses.

And God said — Genesis 1:1–3

1 In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. 2 And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. 3 And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

Meaning: "And God said" appears ten times in Genesis 1. God creates through His word. John 1:1–3 opens with the same words — "In the beginning" — and names that Word as the Son.

The voice walking in the garden — Genesis 3:8–9

8 And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden. 9 And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?

Meaning: Literally "the voice … walking." Jewish Aramaic paraphrases render it "the memra (Word) of the LORD." The God who speaks also comes looking for lost people — the first sign of the Word seeking those who hide.

The Angel of the LORD on Moriah — Genesis 22:11–12, 15–16

11 And the angel of the LORD called unto him out of heaven, and said, Abraham, Abraham: and he said, Here am I. 12 And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.

15 And the angel of the LORD called unto Abraham out of heaven the second time, 16 And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son:

Meaning: The Angel says "from me" and "by myself have I sworn, saith the LORD." He is distinct from God yet speaks as God. Many Christian interpreters see in the Angel of the LORD appearances of the Son before His incarnation.

The burning bush — Exodus 3:2, 4–6

2 And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush: and he looked, and, behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.

4 And when the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush, and said, Moses, Moses. And he said, Here am I. 5 And he said, Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground. 6 Moreover he said, I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look upon God.

Meaning: "The angel of the LORD" (v. 2) becomes "the LORD" and "God" (v. 4) in the same scene. Here God gives the name "I AM" (3:14), which Jesus claims in John 8:58.

A Prophet like Moses — Deuteronomy 18:15, 18–19

15 The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;

18 I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him. 19 And it shall come to pass, that whosoever will not hearken unto my words which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him.

Meaning: A future Prophet will carry God's very words. Peter applies this to Jesus (Acts 3:22–23). Jesus says, "the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say" (John 12:49) — the Word speaking God's words.

3. Prophets (Nevi'im)

The Prophets describe a coming Son who is fully human — David's descendant, a Branch from Jesse — and yet more than human: everlasting, called by God's own names, and God's equal.

I will be his father — 2 Samuel 7:12–14, 16

12 And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom. 13 He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever. 14 I will be his father, and he shall be my son. If he commit iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men, and with the stripes of the children of men:

16 And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.

Meaning: The Davidic covenant. Solomon partly fulfils it, but "for ever" points beyond him. Hebrews 1:5 quotes "I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son" of Christ. He builds the true house for God's name — the church (Matthew 16:18).

The LORD our righteousness — Jeremiah 23:5–6

5 Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth. 6 In his days Judah shall be saved, and Israel shall dwell safely: and this is his name whereby he shall be called, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS.

Meaning: A human "Branch" (tsemach) from David is given the divine name: YHWH Tsidkenu. Paul echoes it: Christ "is made unto us … righteousness" (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Goings forth from everlasting — Micah 5:2, 4

2 But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.

4 And he shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD, in the majesty of the name of the LORD his God; and they shall abide: for now shall he be great unto the ends of the earth.

Meaning: Born in a small town, yet His origins are "from everlasting" (mimei olam). He shepherds ("feed") in God's strength and becomes great "unto the ends of the earth."

The man that is my fellow — Zechariah 13:7

7 Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith the LORD of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones.

Meaning: "Fellow" is amit, a companion or equal — a word used elsewhere for a neighbour of equal standing. God calls the struck Shepherd His equal. Jesus quotes this verse the night He was arrested (Matthew 26:31).

The first and the last — Isaiah 44:6; 48:12–13, 16

44:6 Thus saith the LORD the King of Israel, and his redeemer the LORD of hosts; I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God.

48:12 Hearken unto me, O Jacob and Israel, my called; I am he; I am the first, I also am the last. 48:13 Mine hand also hath laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand hath spanned the heavens: when I call unto them, they stand up together.

48:16 Come ye near unto me, hear ye this; I have not spoken in secret from the beginning; from the time that it was, there am I: and now the Lord GOD, and his Spirit, hath sent me.

Meaning: "First and last" is the LORD's own title, and Jesus takes it in Revelation 1:17 and 22:13. In 48:16 the speaker who laid earth's foundation says "the Lord GOD, and his Spirit, hath sent me" — the Father, Spirit and sent One in a single verse.

4. Writings (Ketuvim)

The Writings give the clearest Old Testament portraits: Wisdom with God before creation, a riddle about God's Son, the royal Son of Psalm 2, David's Lord at God's right hand, and the Son of man who receives the kingdom.

Wisdom before creation — Proverbs 8:22–31

22 The LORD possessed me in the beginning of his way, before his works of old. 23 I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning, or ever the earth was. 24 When there were no depths, I was brought forth; when there were no fountains abounding with water. 25 Before the mountains were settled, before the hills was I brought forth: 26 While as yet he had not made the earth, nor the fields, nor the highest part of the dust of the world. 27 When he prepared the heavens, I was there: when he set a compass upon the face of the depth: 28 When he established the clouds above: when he strengthened the fountains of the deep: 29 When he gave to the sea his decree, that the waters should not pass his commandment: when he appointed the foundations of the earth: 30 Then I was by him, as one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him; 31 Rejoicing in the habitable part of his earth; and my delights were with the sons of men.

Meaning: Wisdom is present before anything was made, beside God as craftsman (amon), His daily delight, and delighting in people. New Testament writers use this language of Christ: "before all things" (Colossians 1:17), "the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24), and "the Father loveth the Son" (John 5:20).

What is His Son's name? — Proverbs 30:4

4 Who hath ascended up into heaven, or descended? who hath gathered the wind in his fists? who hath bound the waters in a garment? who hath established all the ends of the earth? what is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell?

Meaning: Agur's riddle. Only God controls wind, waters and earth — and He has a Son. Jesus answers the first question: "no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man" (John 3:13).

Thou art my Son — Psalm 2:7–8, 12

7 I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee. 8 Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.

12 Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.

Meaning: The "decree" (choq) is God's settled counsel about His Son. The nations are His inheritance — Colossians 1:16's "for him." Acts 13:33 links "this day have I begotten thee" to the resurrection; Hebrews 1:5 to His superiority over angels. "Blessed are all they that put their trust in him" — trust is placed in the Son.

Sit thou at my right hand — Psalm 110:1, 4

1 The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.

4 The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.

Meaning: The most quoted Old Testament verse in the New Testament. David calls the coming King "my Lord" (Adoni). Jesus asks how David's son can be David's Lord (Matthew 22:41–45). He is King and Priest forever, by God's oath.

By the word of the LORD — Psalm 33:6, 9

6 By the word of the LORD were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth.

9 For he spake, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast.

Meaning: Word (davar) and breath (ruach, Spirit) together in creation — the same pairing as Genesis 1:2–3 and John 1:1–3.

One like the Son of man — Daniel 7:13–14

13 I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. 14 And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.

Meaning: Human ("like the Son of man") yet coming on clouds, a sign of deity in the Old Testament. He receives worship-service from all nations. "Son of man" was Jesus' favourite title for Himself, and He quoted this verse at His trial (Mark 14:62).

5. New Testament

The New Testament identifies the Word and Wisdom as Jesus: eternal with God, Creator of all, made flesh, God's full self-revelation, humbled to the cross and exalted above every name.

The Word made flesh — John 1:1–3, 14, 18

1 In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. 2 The same was in the beginning with God. 3 All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.

14 And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

18 No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.

Meaning: Verse 1 makes three claims: the Word existed eternally ("was"), was in relationship ("with God," pros ton theon, face to face), and was fully divine ("was God"). Verse 14 states the incarnation. "Declared" (v. 18) is exēgēsato — He is the exegesis of God, the full explanation of the Father.

Before Abraham was, I am — John 8:56–59

56 Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad. 57 Then said the Jews unto him, Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham? 58 Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am. 59 Then took they up stones to cast at him: but Jesus hid himself, and went through the midst of them, and passed by.

Meaning: Not "I was" but "I am" (egō eimi) — the name from the burning bush (Exodus 3:14). The crowd understood the claim to deity and reached for stones.

The image of the invisible God — Colossians 1:15–20

15 Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature: 16 For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: 17 And he is before all things, and by him all things consist. 18 And he is the head of the body, the church: who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all things he might have the preeminence. 19 For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell; 20 And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.

Meaning: The fullest blueprint text about the Son. Two halves: Lord of creation (vv. 15–17) and Lord of new creation (vv. 18–20). He is "firstborn" in both — over creation and from the dead. "Preeminence" (prōteuōn, holding first place) is the goal of the whole plan. "All fulness" (plērōma) dwells in Him.

The brightness of His glory — Hebrews 1:1–3, 8

1 God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, 2 Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds; 3 Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high;

8 But unto the Son he saith, Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: a sceptre of righteousness is the sceptre of thy kingdom.

Meaning: God's final word is a Son. Seven claims: heir, Creator, radiance, exact imprint (charaktēr), sustainer, purifier, enthroned. Verse 8 quotes Psalm 45:6, where the Father addresses the Son as "O God."

Christ the wisdom of God — 1 Corinthians 1:22–24, 30; Colossians 2:2–3

1 Corinthians 1:22 For the Jews require a sign, and the Greeks seek after wisdom: 23 But we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness; 24 But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.

30 But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:

Colossians 2:2 That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love, and unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding, to the acknowledgement of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ; 3 In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

Meaning: The Wisdom of Proverbs 8 is a crucified Person. To be "in Christ" is to have God's wisdom, righteousness (Jeremiah 23:6), holiness and redemption. Every treasure of wisdom is "hid" in Him, so seeking wisdom means seeking Him.

Emptied and exalted — Philippians 2:5–11

5 Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: 6 Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: 7 But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: 8 And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. 9 Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: 10 That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; 11 And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Meaning: The journey of the Son in seven steps down and up. "Form" (morphē) of God, then form of a servant. "Made himself of no reputation" is ekenōsen, emptied Himself — not of deity but by taking on humanity and humility. Verses 10–11 apply Isaiah 45:23 ("unto me every knee shall bow," said of the LORD) to Jesus. Paul presents it as a pattern for believers (v. 5).

The Word of God returns — Revelation 19:11–13, 16; 22:13

19:11 And I saw heaven opened, and behold a white horse; and he that sat upon him was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge and make war. 19:12 His eyes were as a flame of fire, and on his head were many crowns; and he had a name written, that no man knew, but he himself. 19:13 And he was clothed with a vesture dipped in blood: and his name is called The Word of God.

19:16 And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.

22:13 I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last.

Meaning: The Bible ends as it began, with the Word. The Word who created (John 1:3) returns to judge and reign. He claims the LORD's title from Isaiah 44:6: "the first and the last."

7. Principles and practices

  1. The Son is eternal and fully God. He was "with God" and "was God" before creation (John 1:1; 8:58). The blueprint is not centered on a creature but on God the Son.
  2. All things are by Him, for Him and in Him. He is the agent, goal and sustainer of everything (Colossians 1:16–17).
  3. He is God's complete self-revelation. To know what God is like, look at Jesus (John 1:18; Hebrews 1:3).
  4. God's wisdom is a Person, not only a principle. Wisdom is found by being "in Christ" (1 Corinthians 1:30; Colossians 2:3).
  5. He speaks God's final word. After many partial words, God has spoken "by his Son" (Hebrews 1:1–2).
  6. The way up is down. The Son's path — emptied, obedient, exalted — is the pattern for believers (Philippians 2:5–11).
  7. He must have first place in everything. The goal of the plan is His preeminence (Colossians 1:18).
Principle Practice Anchor text
1. Eternal and fully God Worship Jesus by name in prayer, not only the Father through Him. Read John 1:1–18 aloud weekly for a month. John 1:1; 20:28
2. By, for and in Him Before starting work or study, pray: "This was made by You and for You; hold it together today." Colossians 1:16–17
3. Self-revelation of God When a question about God troubles you, look for how Jesus acted in a similar situation in the Gospels. John 14:9; Hebrews 1:3
4. Wisdom is a Person Ask for wisdom (James 1:5) by seeking Christ in Scripture, not only seeking techniques. 1 Corinthians 1:30; Colossians 2:3
5. God's final word Test every teaching, dream or voice by what the Son has said and done. Hebrews 1:1–2; Deuteronomy 18:19
6. The way up is down Choose one hidden act of service each week, done without credit. Philippians 2:5–8
7. Preeminence Review your calendar and budget: where does Christ have first place, and where does He not? Colossians 1:18

For a teaching session: Read Proverbs 8:22–31 and Colossians 1:15–20 side by side. List what each says Wisdom or the Son did at creation. Then read Philippians 2:5–11 and ask how the Creator's humility should shape our relationships.
