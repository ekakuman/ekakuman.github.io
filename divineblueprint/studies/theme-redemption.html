Redemption and the Blood

Overview and word study

Redemption means buying back what was lost, at a price. In the blueprint, it was planned before creation — "redeemed … with the precious blood of Christ … foreordained before the foundation of the world" (1 Peter 1:18–20) — and it unfolds in three tenses: our souls redeemed (Ephesians 1:7), our lives belonging to the Redeemer (1 Corinthians 6:20), and our bodies awaiting redemption (Romans 8:23).

The Old Testament supplies the picture of the go'el, the kinsman-redeemer: a close relative who buys back land, frees a relative from slavery, and carries on the family name. To redeem humanity, the Son had to become our kinsman — "partakers of flesh and blood" (Hebrews 2:14).

The Greek market words show three steps: agorazō (bought), exagorazō (bought out, never to be sold again), lytroō (set free).

Torah

The Angel which redeemed me — Genesis 48:15–16

15 And he blessed Joseph, and said, God, before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac did walk, the God which fed me all my life long unto this day, 16 The Angel which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads; and let my name be named on them, and the name of my fathers Abraham and Isaac; and let them grow into a multitude in the midst of the earth.

Meaning: The first use of ga'al in the Bible. The dying Jacob identifies God, the Shepherd who "fed" him, and "the Angel which redeemed me" as one. Redemption is personal rescue "from all evil."

A stretched out arm — Exodus 6:6–7

6 Wherefore say unto the children of Israel, I am the LORD, and I will bring you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians, and I will rid you out of their bondage, and I will redeem you with a stretched out arm, and with great judgments: 7 And I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God: and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God, which bringeth you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians.

Meaning: Four "I will" promises: bring out, rid, redeem, take. Redemption has a destination: "I will take you to me for a people." It is freedom for relationship.

All the firstborn I redeem — Exodus 13:13–15

13 And every firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb; and if thou wilt not redeem it, then thou shalt break his neck: and all the firstborn of man among thy children shalt thou redeem. 14 And it shall be when thy son asketh thee in time to come, saying, What is this? that thou shalt say to him, By strength of hand the LORD brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage: 15 And it came to pass, when Pharaoh would hardly let us go, that the LORD slew all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both the firstborn of man, and the firstborn of beast: therefore I sacrifice to the LORD all that openeth the matrix, being males; but all the firstborn of my children I redeem.

Meaning: Every firstborn belonged to God because God spared them at Passover. They had to be redeemed (padah) by a substitute. The redeemed child is to ask "What is this?" — redemption is passed on through teaching.

The life is in the blood — Leviticus 17:11

11 For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.

Meaning: The key verse on blood. Blood is life (nephesh); God Himself "gave" it for atonement. Life is given for life. Hebrews 9:22 summarises: "without shedding of blood is no remission."

The kinsman-redeemer law — Leviticus 25:25, 47–49

25 If thy brother be waxen poor, and hath sold away some of his possession, and if any of his kin come to redeem it, then shall he redeem that which his brother sold.

47 And if a sojourner or stranger wax rich by thee, and thy brother that dwelleth by him wax poor, and sell himself unto the stranger or sojourner by thee, or to the stock of the stranger's family: 48 After that he is sold he may be redeemed again; one of his brethren may redeem him: 49 Either his uncle, or his uncle's son, may redeem him, or any that is nigh of kin unto him of his family may redeem him; or if he be able, he may redeem himself.

Meaning: Three conditions for a go'el: he must be a near relative, able to pay, and willing. Christ meets all three: He became our kinsman (Hebrews 2:14), paid with His own blood (1 Peter 1:19), and came willingly (John 10:18).

Prophets (Nevi'im)

I have redeemed thee — Isaiah 43:1–3

1 But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine. 2 When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee. 3 For I am the LORD thy God, the Holy One of Israel, thy Saviour: I gave Egypt for thy ransom, Ethiopia and Seba for thee.

Meaning: Redemption leads to belonging ("thou art mine") and presence in trouble ("I will be with thee"). "Ransom" is kopher.

Blotted out as a cloud — Isaiah 44:22–23

22 I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, thy transgressions, and, as a cloud, thy sins: return unto me; for I have redeemed thee. 23 Sing, O ye heavens; for the LORD hath done it: shout, ye lower parts of the earth: break forth into singing, ye mountains, O forest, and every tree therein: for the LORD hath redeemed Jacob, and glorified himself in Israel.

Meaning: Redemption comes before return: "return unto me; for I have redeemed thee." All creation is called to sing.

Redeemed without money — Isaiah 52:3

3 For thus saith the LORD, Ye have sold yourselves for nought; and ye shall be redeemed without money.

Meaning: Israel sold itself for nothing; it will be bought back without silver — pointing to a payment of another kind, the blood of Christ (1 Peter 1:18–19). This sits just before the Servant passage (52:13–53:12).

The Redeemer shall come to Zion — Isaiah 59:20

20 And the Redeemer shall come to Zion, and unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob, saith the LORD.

Meaning: The Redeemer is a coming Person. Paul quotes it in Romans 11:26.

Ransom from the grave — Hosea 13:14

14 I will ransom them from the power of the grave; I will redeem them from death: O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave, I will be thy destruction: repentance shall be hid from mine eyes.

Meaning: Both redemption words (padah, ga'al) aimed at death itself. Paul quotes it in his resurrection chapter: "O death, where is thy sting?" (1 Corinthians 15:55).

By the blood of thy covenant — Zechariah 9:9, 11

9 Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.

11 As for thee also, by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit wherein is no water.

Meaning: The lowly King's coming (fulfilled on Palm Sunday, Matthew 21:5) is linked to release of prisoners "by the blood of thy covenant."

Writings (Ketuvim)

The kinsman who could not, and the one who did — Ruth 4:4–6, 9–10

4 And I thought to advertise thee, saying, Buy it before the inhabitants, and before the elders of my people. If thou wilt redeem it, redeem it: but if thou wilt not redeem it, then tell me, that I may know: for there is none to redeem it beside thee; and I am after thee. And he said, I will redeem it. 5 Then said Boaz, What day thou buyest the field of the hand of Naomi, thou must buy it also of Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance. 6 And the kinsman said, I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance: redeem thou my right to thyself; for I cannot redeem it.

9 And Boaz said unto the elders, and unto all the people, Ye are witnesses this day, that I have bought all that was Elimelech's, and all that was Chilion's and Mahlon's, of the hand of Naomi. 10 Moreover Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance, that the name of the dead be not cut off from among his brethren, and from the gate of his place: ye are witnesses this day.

Meaning: The nearer kinsman "cannot redeem" without risking his own inheritance — a picture of the law, unable to save. Boaz redeems both land and bride, at cost, publicly. He is the clearest Old Testament portrait of Christ the go'el, who purchases His bride (Ephesians 5:25).

My Redeemer liveth — Job 19:25–27

25 For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth: 26 And though after my skin worms destroy this body, yet in my flesh shall I see God: 27 Whom I shall see for myself, and mine eyes shall behold, and not another; though my reins be consumed within me.

Meaning: In his darkest suffering, Job confesses a living go'el who will stand on the earth at the end. His hope includes bodily resurrection: "in my flesh shall I see God."

None can redeem his brother — Psalm 49:7–8, 15

7 None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him: 8 (For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceaseth for ever:)

15 But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me. Selah.

Meaning: No human wealth can pay for a soul. "But God" — only God can pay the price and receive us beyond death.

Plenteous redemption — Psalm 130:3–4, 7–8

3 If thou, LORD, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand? 4 But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared.

7 Let Israel hope in the LORD: for with the LORD there is mercy, and with him is plenteous redemption. 8 And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities.

Meaning: Redemption is from sin itself, not only from enemies. It is "plenteous" (harbeh), more than enough.

Let the redeemed say so — Psalm 107:1–2

1 O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever. 2 Let the redeemed of the LORD say so, whom he hath redeemed from the hand of the enemy;

Meaning: Redemption calls for testimony. Those bought back are to speak.

New Testament

A ransom for many — Mark 10:43–45

43 But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister: 44 And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all. 45 For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.

Meaning: Jesus' own definition of His mission. "Ransom" is lytron; "for" is anti, in place of. The Son of man of Daniel 7 is also the Servant of Isaiah 53.

My blood of the new testament — Matthew 26:26–28

26 And as they were eating, Jesus took bread, and blessed it, and brake it, and gave it to the disciples, and said, Take, eat; this is my body. 27 And he took the cup, and gave thanks, and gave it to them, saying, Drink ye all of it; 28 For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.

Meaning: At Passover, Jesus reinterprets the meal around Himself. His blood ratifies the new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31) and secures forgiveness.

Redeemed from the curse — Galatians 3:13; 4:4–5

3:13 Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:

4:4 But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, 4:5 To redeem them that were under the law, that we might receive the adoption of sons.

Meaning: "Redeemed" is exagorazō, bought out of the market. He took the curse by taking our place. He was "made of a woman" — our kinsman — and "made under the law" — under our debt. The goal is adoption (theme 14).

Redemption through His blood — Ephesians 1:7, 13–14

7 In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;

13 In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, 14 Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory.

Meaning: Redemption now (v. 7: forgiveness) and redemption later (v. 14: the full possession). The Spirit is the "earnest," the down payment guaranteeing the rest.

Bought with a price — 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

19 What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? 20 For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's.

Meaning: Redemption means a change of ownership. "Ye are not your own" — the redeemed body becomes God's temple.

Eternal redemption — Hebrews 9:11–14, 22

11 But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building; 12 Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us. 13 For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: 14 How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?

22 And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.

Meaning: Animal blood cleansed outwardly and repeatedly; Christ's blood cleanses the conscience once and for all. "Eternal" (aiōnian) redemption — it never needs repeating. Its goal is service: "to serve the living God."

The redemption of our body — Romans 8:22–23

22 For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now. 23 And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body.

Meaning: Redemption is not finished until the body is raised. Creation itself waits for it.

They overcame by the blood — Revelation 5:9; 12:11

5:9 And they sung a new song, saying, Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation;

12:11 And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

Meaning: The blood that redeems also gives victory over the accuser. Redemption ends in a new song from every nation.

Patterns, principles and practices

4. The redeemed belong to the Redeemer "All the firstborn … I redeem" (Exodus 13:15) "Thou art mine" (Isaiah 43:1) Ruth "purchased to be my wife" (Ruth 4:10) "Ye are not your own … bought with a price" (1 Corinthians 6:19–20)
5. Redemption from death "The Angel which redeemed me from all evil" (Genesis 48:16) "I will redeem them from death" (Hosea 13:14) "God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave" (Psalm 49:15) "The redemption of our body" (Romans 8:23)
6. Redemption calls forth song "The people which thou hast redeemed" — Song of Moses (Exodus 15:13) "Sing, O ye heavens … the LORD hath redeemed Jacob" (Isaiah 44:23) "Let the redeemed of the LORD say so" (Psalm 107:2) "They sung a new song … hast redeemed us" (Revelation 5:9)
7. Redemption in stages: now and not yet Exodus, wilderness, then Canaan "The ransomed of the LORD shall return … with songs" (Isaiah 35:10) "He shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities" (Psalm 130:8) "We have redemption" (Ephesians 1:7) … "until the redemption of the purchased possession" (Ephesians 1:14)

Principles

  1. Redemption was planned before the world. The price was foreknown (1 Peter 1:18–20).
  2. Only a kinsman can redeem. So the Son became human (Galatians 4:4; Hebrews 2:14).
  3. The price is life — blood. Nothing less than a life given can redeem (Leviticus 17:11; Mark 10:45).
  4. Redemption changes ownership. The redeemed are God's (1 Corinthians 6:20).
  5. Redemption is for relationship. "I will take you to me for a people" (Exodus 6:7).
  6. Redemption has three tenses. Forgiven now, owned now, bodies redeemed later (Ephesians 1:7, 14; Romans 8:23).
  7. The redeemed must testify. "Let the redeemed of the LORD say so" (Psalm 107:2).
