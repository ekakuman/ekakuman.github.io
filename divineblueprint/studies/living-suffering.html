The Divine Blueprint and Suffering and Loss

Overview

Suffering is where many believers feel the blueprint is hardest to see. Sickness, grief, betrayal, failure and long waiting can make God's plan seem far away. Scripture does not hide this. It gives whole books to it (Job, Lamentations, many Psalms) and puts the cross at the centre of the plan.

The blueprint does not explain every sorrow. It does tell us four things:

  • God's purpose is not cancelled by suffering. He works through it toward the end He has set (Romans 8:28–29).
  • God has given Himself in suffering. The Son suffered, and He suffers with His people (Isaiah 53:3–4; Hebrews 4:15).
  • God forms His people through suffering. It produces patience, hope and likeness to Christ (Romans 5:3–5; James 1:2–4).
  • God will end suffering. There will be no more death, sorrow, crying or pain (Revelation 21:4).

Key passage — Romans 8:18–28

18 For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us. 19 For the earnest expectation of the creature waiteth for the manifestation of the sons of God. 20 For the creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope, 21 Because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God. 22 For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now. 23 And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body. 24 For we are saved by hope: but hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man seeth, why doth he yet hope for? 25 But if we hope for that we see not, then do we with patience wait for it. 26 Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered. 27 And he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what is the mind of the Spirit, because he maketh intercession for the saints according to the will of God. 28 And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.

Meaning

Paul puts present suffering beside future glory and says the two cannot be compared (v. 18). The whole creation groans, and so do believers (vv. 22–23). Groaning is not a lack of faith; it is what waiting for redemption feels like. The Spirit groans with us and prays for us when we have no words (vv. 26–27). Verse 28 does not say all things are good. It says God works all things together for good, for those who are "called according to his purpose" — the same purpose the whole blueprint traces. Verse 29 names that good: to be "conformed to the image of his Son."

Pastoral notes

  • Do not rush to verse 28. Read verses 22–27 first. People in fresh grief need others who will groan and weep with them (Romans 12:15) before they hear explanations.
  • Suffering is not always punishment. Some suffering follows sin (Galatians 6:7), but Job's friends and Jesus' disciples were wrong to assume it always does (Job 42:7; John 9:2–3).
  • Lament is faithful. Bringing complaint, questions and tears to God is part of Scripture's prayer book (Psalm 13; 88).
  • Get practical help. Grief, depression or trauma may need a doctor or trained counsellor alongside the church. Seeking help is not unbelief.
  • Prosperity teaching. Teaching that faithful believers will not suffer is contradicted by Christ, the apostles and Hebrews 11:35–38. God does heal and deliver, but He does not promise a life free of pain before the end.

Suffering through the five lenses

A · The Purpose — God's plan is not cancelled

Genesis 50:20 — But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.

Romans 8:29 — For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren.

Meaning. Joseph's brothers meant real evil, and Joseph suffered real harm for years. Yet God was at work in the same events for a saving purpose. Romans 8:29 names the "good" of 8:28: likeness to Christ. God does not call evil good, but He is never shut out of a believer's story.

Theme links: The Purpose of God; The Counsel of His Will; Election.

The unseen side. Behind Job's losses lay a conversation in heaven he never heard (Job 1:6–12; 2:1–6). Believers rarely see why they suffer, but Scripture assures them that suffering is never outside God's sight or permission, and that "our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory" (2 Corinthians 4:17) — a weight measured in the unseen.

B · The Provision — God suffers with us and gives grace

Isaiah 53:3–5 — He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not. Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.

Hebrews 4:15–16 — For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

2 Corinthians 12:9 — And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.

Meaning. The provision of the blueprint is a suffering Saviour. Christ is "acquainted with grief" and is "touched with the feeling of our infirmities." Paul's thorn was not removed, but grace was given. Sometimes God's provision is deliverance from suffering; sometimes it is strength within it.

Theme links: The Lamb; Grace; Mercy; Priesthood.

C · The People — formed and comforted together

Romans 5:3–5 — And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; and patience, experience; and experience, hope: and hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.

2 Corinthians 1:3–4 — Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

Meaning. Suffering forms character: patience, proven faith, hope. And comfort received becomes comfort given. The suffering believer is not alone; God places them in a body where "whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it" (1 Corinthians 12:26).

Theme links: Sonship; Holiness; The Holy Spirit; Love.

D · The Goal — conformed to Christ, weighed against glory

2 Corinthians 4:16–18 — For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day. For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; while we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal.

1 Peter 4:12–13 — Beloved, think it not strange concerning the fiery trial which is to try you, as though some strange thing happened unto you: but rejoice, inasmuch as ye are partakers of Christ's sufferings; that, when his glory shall be revealed, ye may be glad also with exceeding joy.

Meaning. Paul calls his afflictions "light" and "for a moment" — not because they were small (see 2 Corinthians 11:23–28) but because of what they are weighed against. Suffering with Christ is the path to glory with Christ (Romans 8:17).

Theme links: The Image of God; Glorification; Inheritance.

E · The Consummation — sorrow ends

1 Thessalonians 4:13–14 — But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

Revelation 21:3–4 — And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.

Meaning. Christians grieve, but not "as others which have no hope." Death is real loss and will be undone by resurrection. The last picture of the blueprint is God wiping tears with His own hand.

Theme links: New Creation; The Dwelling of God; Rest; Final Judgment.

Practices

For yourself

Practice How Scripture
Lament honestly Pray a lament psalm in your own words: complaint, request, trust. Psalm 13; Psalm 42
Remember God's past faithfulness Write down times God carried you before. Lamentations 3:21–23; Psalm 77:10–12
Look at what is not seen Read one passage about the end each week of a hard season. 2 Corinthians 4:16–18; Revelation 21:1–5
Accept help Let others serve you; see a doctor or counsellor when needed. Galatians 6:2; 2 Corinthians 7:6

For others

Practice How Scripture
Be present Sit, listen and weep before you speak. Job 2:13; Romans 12:15
Practical care Meals, lifts, childcare, help with paperwork after a death. James 1:27; 1 John 3:17–18
Pass on comfort Share what God gave you in your own trouble, gently. 2 Corinthians 1:3–4
Keep remembering Contact grieving friends on anniversaries and months later. Proverbs 17:17

For the church

Practice How Scripture
Make room for lament Include lament psalms and prayers of grief in worship. Psalm 88; Lamentations 5
Care rota Organise visiting and meals for the sick, bereaved and housebound. Acts 6:1–3; James 5:14
Pray for the persecuted Remember suffering believers worldwide by name. Hebrews 13:3
Teach a theology of suffering Prepare people before trouble comes, not only during it. 1 Peter 4:12–13; Acts 14:22

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening Psalm 13 Ask: "What do people say to you when you suffer that helps — or hurts?" Scripture lets us lament.
8 min A · God's purpose Genesis 50:20; Romans 8:28–29 Evil is real, but God's purpose is not cancelled. The "good" is likeness to Christ.
8 min B · God's provision Isaiah 53:3–5; Hebrews 4:15–16; 2 Corinthians 12:9 A Saviour acquainted with grief; grace that is sufficient.
7 min C · Formed and comforted Romans 5:3–5; 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 Suffering produces hope; comfort received becomes comfort given.
7 min D and E · Glory and the end of sorrow 2 Corinthians 4:16–18; Revelation 21:3–4 Light affliction weighed against eternal glory; God wipes away every tear.
5 min Response and prayer Romans 12:15 Each person names one sufferer to visit or contact this week. Pray for those grieving.

Discussion questions

  1. Why do you think God included so many laments in Scripture (Psalm 13; 88)?
  2. Joseph said "God meant it unto good" (Genesis 50:20). How is that different from saying the evil was good?
  3. How does it help to know Christ was "acquainted with grief" (Isaiah 53:3)?
  4. Paul's thorn was not removed (2 Corinthians 12:7–9). How do we pray when God says no?
  5. What has suffering produced in you (Romans 5:3–5)?
  6. What helps a grieving person most? What hurts most (Job 2:13; 16:2)?
  7. How can Paul call his afflictions "light" (2 Corinthians 4:17; compare 11:23–28)?
  8. Who could you comfort this week with the comfort you have received (2 Corinthians 1:4)?

చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు

  1. దేవుడు లేఖనంలో ఇన్ని మొర్రలను ఎందుకు చేర్చాడని మీరు అనుకుంటున్నారు (కీర్తనలు 13; 88)?
  2. "దేవుడు దానిని మేలుకు ఉద్దేశించెను" (ఆదికాండము 50:20) అనడం, చెడు మంచిదని అనడానికి ఎలా భిన్నం?
  3. క్రీస్తు వ్యసనం ఎరిగినవాడని (యెషయా 53:3) తెలిసివుండడం ఎలా సహాయం చేస్తుంది?
  4. పౌలు ముల్లు తీసివేయబడలేదు (2 కొరింథీయులకు 12:7–9). దేవుడు "వద్దు" అన్నప్పుడు మనం ఎలా ప్రార్థిస్తాము?
  5. శ్రమ మీలో ఏమి కలిగించింది (రోమీయులకు 5:3–5)?
  6. దుఃఖిస్తున్న వ్యక్తికి ఏది ఎక్కువ సహాయం చేస్తుంది? ఏది ఎక్కువ గాయపరుస్తుంది (యోబు 2:13; 16:2)?
  7. పౌలు తన శ్రమలను "తేలికపాటివి" అని ఎలా అనగలడు (2 కొరింథీయులకు 4:17; 11:23–28 తో పోల్చండి)?
  8. మీరు పొందిన ఆదరణతో ఈ వారం ఎవరిని ఓదార్చగలరు (2 కొరింథీయులకు 1:4)?
