The Divine Blueprint and Friendships and Conflict

Overview

The Divine Blueprint is relational from the beginning. God is love (1 John 4:8), and the Father, Son and Spirit lived in loving fellowship before the world began (John 17:24). Sin broke our relationships along with our relationship with God: Adam blamed Eve, and Cain killed Abel. The gospel restores both. Christ calls His disciples "friends" (John 15:15), and the cross that reconciles us to God also tears down "the middle wall of partition" between people (Ephesians 2:14). So friendships, forgiveness and peacemaking are not side issues; they are the blueprint lived out between people.

This study walks friendships and conflict through the five groups of the blueprint:

  • A · Purpose: why God made us for love and friendship
  • B · Provision: the forgiveness God supplies
  • C · People: who God shapes us into through relationships and conflict
  • D · Goal: reconciliation and peace
  • E · Consummation: how the end shapes how we treat others now

Key passage — Ephesians 4:25–32

25 Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another. 26 Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: 27 Neither give place to the devil. 28 Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth. 29 Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers. 30 And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption. 31 Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: 32 And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you.

Meaning:

  • v. 25 Truth comes first, "for we are members one of another." Lies damage the body we belong to.
  • vv. 26–27 Anger itself is not always sin, but it must not be nursed overnight. Unresolved anger gives the devil a foothold.
  • v. 29 Words should build up and "minister grace unto the hearers." Speech can be a channel of God's grace.
  • v. 30 Damaged relationships "grieve" the Holy Spirit, who seals us for redemption.
  • v. 31 Six things to put away, from the inner root (bitterness) to its outward fruit (clamour, evil speaking, malice).
  • v. 32 Three things to put on: kindness, tenderness, forgiveness. The measure is "even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you." We forgive from what we have received.

The five lenses

A · Purpose — made for love and friendship

Love is of God — 1 John 4:7–8, 10–11

7 Beloved, let us love one another: for love is of God; and every one that loveth is born of God, and knoweth God. 8 He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love.

10 Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins. 11 Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another.

Meaning: Our love for others flows from God's love (theme 3). Love for people is proof of knowing God.

I have called you friends — John 15:13–15; Proverbs 17:17

John 15:13 Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends. 14 Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you. 15 Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you.

Proverbs 17:17 A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.

Meaning: Christ makes us His friends and shows what friendship costs: self-giving, openness and loyalty "at all times."

The unseen side. "Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: neither give place to the devil" (Ephesians 4:26–27). Paul forgives "lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices" (2 Corinthians 2:11). Unresolved conflict hands ground to an unseen enemy; forgiveness takes it back.

B · Provision — the forgiveness God supplies

God meant it unto good — Genesis 50:19–21

19 And Joseph said unto them, Fear not: for am I in the place of God? 20 But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive. 21 Now therefore fear ye not: I will nourish you, and your little ones. And he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them.

Meaning: Joseph forgives brothers who sold him. He leaves judgment to God ("am I in the place of God?") and trusts God's purpose (theme 1). He then shows kindness in action.

Seventy times seven — Matthew 18:21–22, 32–33

21 Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? 22 Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.

32 Then his lord, after that he had called him, said unto him, O thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all that debt, because thou desiredst me: 33 Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee?

Meaning: Forgiveness has no ceiling, because God's mercy (theme 9) to us has none. We forgive small debts because we have been forgiven an unpayable one.

C · People — who God shapes us into

Iron sharpeneth iron — Proverbs 27:6, 17

6 Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.

17 Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.

Meaning: True friends tell the truth even when it hurts, and they grow each other.

Go and tell him his fault — Matthew 18:15

15 Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother.

Meaning: Jesus' first step in conflict is private and personal, not gossip or public exposure. The goal is to gain your brother, not win the argument.

Swift to hear, slow to speak — James 1:19–20; Proverbs 15:1

James 1:19 Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: 20 For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.

Proverbs 15:1 A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.

Meaning: Christlike character in conflict: listening first, speaking gently and controlling anger (theme 17, Holiness).

D · Goal — reconciliation and peace

Esau ran to meet him — Genesis 33:4

4 And Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and fell on his neck, and kissed him: and they wept.

Meaning: Twenty years of estrangement end in an embrace. The same posture as the father in Luke 15:20: reconciliation is the goal.

First be reconciled — Matthew 5:9, 23–24

9 Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.

23 Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; 24 Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.

Meaning: Reconciliation with people is so important to God that it comes before worship. Peacemakers resemble their Father (theme 32, Peace).

As much as lieth in you — Romans 12:18

18 If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.

Meaning: We are responsible for our part. Peace may not always be possible, but the effort is always ours to make.

E · Consummation — how the end shapes relationships

Vengeance is Mine — Romans 12:19–21

19 Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. 20 Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head. 21 Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.

Meaning: Because God will judge justly (theme 33), we are free not to take revenge. We can even do good to enemies.

Charity never faileth — 1 Corinthians 13:8, 12–13

8 Charity never faileth: but whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away.

12 For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known. 13 And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.

Meaning: Love is the one thing that carries into eternity. Every act of love now is an investment in forever.

Common struggles, and what the blueprint says

Struggle What the blueprint says Anchor text
Unforgiveness and bitterness Bitterness is a root that "troubles" and "defiles many." We forgive as we have been forgiven. Hebrews 12:15; Ephesians 4:31–32; Matthew 18:32–33
Betrayal by a close friend David knew it ("it was thou, a man mine equal"), and so did Jesus. God sees and judges; we can bring the wound to Him. Psalm 55:12–14; 41:9; John 13:18
Gossip and slander A whisperer separates close friends; where there is no talebearer, the strife ceases. Proverbs 16:28; 26:20; Ephesians 4:29
Avoiding conflict at any cost False peace heals "slightly." Jesus says to go and talk privately. Jeremiah 6:14; Matthew 18:15
Harsh confrontation Restore "in the spirit of meekness," considering yourself. Galatians 6:1; Proverbs 15:1
Loneliness and few friends "A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly"; Christ is the friend closer than a brother. Proverbs 18:24; John 15:15
Bad company "Evil communications corrupt good manners"; walk with the wise. 1 Corinthians 15:33; Proverbs 13:20
Anger Be angry and sin not; settle it before sunset; be slow to wrath. Ephesians 4:26; James 1:19–20
Conflict online Answer only after hearing; let speech minister grace. Proverbs 18:13; Ephesians 4:29; Colossians 4:6

Pastoral note — forgiveness and reconciliation: Forgiveness is always commanded; reconciliation depends on both sides and takes time. Forgiving someone means releasing them from your judgment to God's, giving up revenge and wishing them good. It does not mean pretending wrong didn't happen, trusting an unrepentant person straight away, or staying in a harmful situation. Scripture warns against close friendship with violent or abusive people (Proverbs 22:24–25), and wise boundaries are consistent with forgiveness.

Practices

For yourself

Practice Anchor text
Name one person you need to forgive, and pray for their good by name. Matthew 5:44; Ephesians 4:32
Before speaking in a conflict, listen fully and ask a clarifying question. James 1:19; Proverbs 18:13
Refuse to pass on gossip; redirect or stay silent. Proverbs 26:20
Settle anger before bed, even if the issue takes longer to resolve. Ephesians 4:26
Invest in one friendship: call, visit or write this week. Proverbs 17:17; 18:24

In relationships

Practice Anchor text
If you've been wronged, go privately first, aiming to gain the person. Matthew 18:15
If you've wronged someone, go and make it right before worship. Matthew 5:23–24
Speak hard truth with love, and receive it with humility. Proverbs 27:6; Ephesians 4:15

For the church

Practice Anchor text
Teach and model the Matthew 18 steps for resolving conflict. Matthew 18:15–17
Train peacemakers and mediators in the congregation. Matthew 5:9; Philippians 4:2–3
Build small groups where real friendship can grow. Acts 2:46; Hebrews 10:24–25

For a teaching session (about 40 minutes)

Time Section Read Main point
5 min Opening John 15:15 Ask: "What makes a true friend?" Christ calls us friends.
8 min A · Made for love 1 John 4:7–11; Proverbs 17:17 Love is from God; we love because He first loved us.
7 min B · Forgiveness supplied Genesis 50:19–21; Matthew 18:21–33 Joseph's forgiveness; seventy times seven; forgiven people forgive.
10 min C · Formed through conflict Ephesians 4:25–32; Matthew 18:15; James 1:19–20 Truth, controlled anger, gracious words; go privately; listen first.
5 min D and E · Reconciliation and eternity Genesis 33:4; Matthew 5:23–24; Romans 12:19–21 Embrace the estranged; reconcile before worship; leave vengeance to God.
5 min Response and prayer Ephesians 4:32 Each person names one person to forgive or reconcile with. Pray quietly by name.

Discussion questions

  1. Jesus calls His disciples "friends" (John 15:15). What does that tell us about the kind of friend He is, and the kind we should be?
  2. Ephesians 4:26 says "be ye angry, and sin not." When is anger right, and when does it become sin?
  3. What helped Joseph forgive his brothers (Genesis 50:19–20)? What can we learn from him?
  4. Why do you think Jesus tells us to go privately first (Matthew 18:15)? What usually happens instead?
  5. What is the difference between forgiving someone and fully trusting them again?
  6. What does Romans 12:19–21 free us from, and free us for?
  7. How can our church become a place where conflicts are resolved well?
  8. Which one practice from this study will you start this week?

చర్చా ప్రశ్నలు

  1. యేసు తన శిష్యులను "స్నేహితులు" అని పిలిచాడు (యోహాను 15:15). ఆయన ఎలాంటి స్నేహితుడు, మనం ఎలాంటి స్నేహితులుగా ఉండాలి?
  2. "కోపపడినను పాపము చేయకుడి" (ఎఫెసీయులకు 4:26). కోపం ఎప్పుడు సరైనది, ఎప్పుడు పాపం అవుతుంది?
  3. యోసేపు తన సహోదరులను క్షమించడానికి ఏమి సహాయం చేసింది (ఆదికాండము 50:19–20)?
  4. మొదట ఏకాంతంగా వెళ్లి మాట్లాడమని యేసు ఎందుకు చెప్పాడు (మత్తయి 18:15)? సాధారణంగా దానికి బదులు ఏమి జరుగుతుంది?
  5. ఒకరిని క్షమించడానికి, వారిని మళ్ళీ పూర్తిగా నమ్మడానికి తేడా ఏమిటి?
  6. రోమీయులకు 12:19–21 మనల్ని దేని నుండి విడిపిస్తుంది, దేని కోసం స్వేచ్ఛనిస్తుంది?
  7. విభేదాలు బాగా పరిష్కరించబడే స్థలంగా మన సంఘం ఎలా మారగలదు?
  8. ఈ అధ్యయనం నుండి ఈ వారం మీరు ఏ ఒక్క అభ్యాసాన్ని మొదలుపెడతారు?
