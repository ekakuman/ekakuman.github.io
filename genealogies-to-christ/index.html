Abram's birth is placed when Terah was 130, reading Genesis 11:32 and 12:4 with Acts 7:4 (Abram left Haran after Terah died, aged 205, when Abram was 75). Genesis 11:26 gives Terah 70 when his first son was born; if Abram was that son, move Abram and his descendants 60 years earlier. The Greek Septuagint uses longer ages and gives different totals.

Identical from Abraham to David, then two different sons of David, meeting briefly at Shealtiel and Zerubbabel and again at Joseph. Matthew counts three sets of 14 (Matt 1:17); Luke runs back to Adam, “which was the son of God.”

The high priesthood passed father to son from Aaron (1 Chronicles 6:1–15). It ends at the exile with no heir who could be the Messiah, because Jesus came from Judah, “of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood” (Heb 7:14).

Again and again the promised line came down to one life. Read in order, these moments look like a sustained attack on the seed, ending at Bethlehem (Rev 12:4–5).

The shape of GenesisGenesis is built on ten “these are the generations of…” headings (Hebrew toledot): 2:4; 5:1; 6:9; 10:1; 11:10; 11:27; 25:12; 25:19; 36:1; 37:2. Matthew opens with the same phrase in Greek — “The book of the generation of Jesus Christ” (Matt 1:1), echoing “the book of the generations of Adam” (Gen 5:1). A new beginning, a new Adam.

Ancient genealogies usually listed only fathers. Matthew deliberately names five women, and each one's story would normally have been left out.
