The Camp of Angels

From Jacob's Mahanaim to the armies of heaven: how Scripture shows the host of God, from Genesis to Revelation.

One host, three acts

When Jacob met the angels on his way home, he said, “This is God's host,” and named the place Mahanaim, “two camps” . The rest of Scripture shows the same host: first seen in personal encounters, then at Sinai and in battle, then serving the Son, and finally filling the heavenly city.

What “host” meansWord study

Hebrew machaneh (camp) and tsaba (host, army) describe an organized company, not a crowd. That is why God is so often called “the LORD of hosts.” Luke uses the Greek stratia, an army, for the angels at Bethlehem.

Where the host came from, and the ones who fellBackground

Angels are created beings, made by and for Christ. Some “kept not their first estate” and followed the dragon; Scripture gives us that fact without the full story. The holy host and the fallen host are both real, and both are under God's authority.

Act I: the camp around Israel

In the Old Testament, angels come to individuals, stand over a nation, and fight for it.

The patriarchs: personal meetingsGenesis

The angel of the LORD finds Hagar in the desert, stops Abraham's hand on Moriah, and is sent ahead of Abraham's servant to find Rebekah. At Bethel the angels go up and down; at Mahanaim they meet Jacob on the road home, just before he faces Esau.

  • The pattern starts small: one person, one road, one crisis.
  • Jacob sees two camps, his own frightened one and God's around it.
Sinai and the Exodus: a nation guardedExodus

The angel of God moves behind Israel at the Red Sea, so that the cloud is darkness to Egypt and light to Israel. At Sinai the LORD comes with “ten thousands of saints,” and the psalmist counts the chariots of God in thousands.

The law “ordained by angels”Sinai in the NT

Exodus doesn't mention angels at the giving of the law, but Stephen, Paul and Hebrews all say the law came through them. Hebrews uses this to argue that the gospel, spoken by the Lord himself, is even weightier.

The Angel of the LORDChristophany?

This angel speaks as God, accepts worship (“loose thy shoe”), and is called by God's name. Many see him as the Son before his incarnation.

  • Scripture never says so outright. It is an inference from how he speaks and is treated, so hold it with care.
  • Joshua's “captain of the host” stands with a drawn sword, and he is neither for Israel nor for its enemies. He is for the LORD.
Judgment and mercy: David's census1 Chronicles

The angel with the drawn sword stands over Jerusalem until the LORD says “stay thine hand.” The place where he stopped became the threshing floor where the temple was later built.

Kings and prophets: the host at warKings

At Dothan, Elisha prays for his servant's eyes to be opened, and the mountain is full of horses and chariots of fire. “They that be with us are more than they that be with them.” Later one angel strikes the Assyrian army in a night.

Ezekiel and Daniel: the throne and the princesProphets

Ezekiel sees the cherubim around the glory. Daniel sees “thousand thousands” ministering before the Ancient of days, and is told of conflict with the “prince of Persia,” with Michael standing for Israel.

  • Daniel 10 shows that prayer was heard the first day, even though the answer was delayed by unseen conflict.

Act II: the host serves the Son

When Christ came, the host announced him, served him, stood ready, and was not called on.

Announcing the KingBirth

Gabriel comes to Zechariah and Mary; an angel reassures Joseph. Over Bethlehem, one angel brings the news, and then the army of heaven bursts into praise.

Ministering to the SonWilderness and garden

After the temptation, angels came and ministered to Jesus. In Gethsemane an angel strengthened him.

  • Luke 22:43-44 is missing from some early manuscripts. The KJV includes it.
Twelve legions, not calledThe arrest

A Roman legion was about 6,000 soldiers. Jesus says he could ask for more than twelve legions, and doesn't, “that the scriptures be fulfilled.” The host's greatest act at the cross is to stand down.

How Jesus foughtSpiritual warfare

Jesus didn't call on angels against Satan. He answered with Scripture (“it is written”), cast out demons by his word, and told the disciples to rejoice less over spirits being subject to them and more that their names are written in heaven.

  • The words “and fasting” in Mark 9:29 are absent from the oldest manuscripts; the KJV has them.
The empty tombResurrection

An angel rolls back the stone, not to let Jesus out but to let the witnesses in, and gives the first resurrection message: “He is not here: for he is risen.”

The church in ActsActs

Angels open prison doors, send Philip to the desert road, direct Cornelius to Peter, strike proud Herod, and stand by Paul in the storm. In each case the angel points people to the gospel or its messengers; it never replaces preaching.

  • The angel tells Cornelius to send for Peter. The angel could have preached the gospel, and doesn't: that task belongs to people.
John 5:4: the troubled poolText note

The angel stirring Bethesda's water is missing from the earliest manuscripts and may reflect a local belief. Either way, the man is healed by Jesus' word, not by the water.

Act III: an unseen host, and the eternal garrison

The letters teach the church to respect angels without fearing, seeking or worshipping them. Revelation shows the host made fully visible at last.

Christ is above the angelsHebrews 1

To which angel did God ever say “Thou art my Son”? Angels worship the Son; he made them, and he sits far above every principality. The whole first chapter of Hebrews is a warning not to put angels in Christ's place.

Don't worship or chase themWarnings

Paul warns against “worshipping of angels.” John twice falls at an angel's feet and is told, “See thou do it not.” Even an angel preaching another gospel is accursed, and Satan himself can look like an angel of light.

  • A message is tested by the gospel, not by how glorious the messenger looks.
Ministering spirits, often unnoticedToday

Angels are sent to serve those who will inherit salvation. Some have entertained them without knowing. Jesus speaks of the “little ones” whose angels always see the Father's face.

The angels are watching the churchSpectators

The apostles were a spectacle to angels. Through the church, God's manifold wisdom is made known to the principalities in heavenly places. The prophets' message of salvation is something “the angels desire to look into.”

Believers will judge angelsOur place

The world to come is not put under angels, but under the Son and those in him. Yet even Michael would not bring a railing accusation against the devil, but said, “The Lord rebuke thee.” Our authority is in his name, not our boldness.

The eternal garrisonRevelation

Believers have already come “to an innumerable company of angels.” John hears ten thousand times ten thousand around the throne, sees Michael's angels cast out the dragon, and watches heaven's armies follow the Faithful and True, though the battle is won by the sword from his mouth.

Six things angels do in Scripture

Kinds of heavenly beingsClassifications

Scripture names cherubim (guarding Eden and the throne), seraphim (crying “Holy” in Isaiah 6), Michael the archangel, Gabriel who stands before God, and ranks such as thrones, dominions, principalities and powers.

  • Detailed ranking systems (nine choirs and the like) come from later tradition, not from the Bible.
What Scripture does not teachGuard rails

Believers do not become angels when they die. Nowhere are we told to pray to angels, name them, command them or seek visits from them. They are fellow servants, sent by God, and our attention belongs on Christ.

For discussion

  1. Jacob saw God's camp just before facing Esau . What “Esau” are you walking toward, and what difference does Mahanaim make?
  2. Elisha prayed for his servant's eyes, not for more chariots . What would it look like to pray that way for someone today?
  3. Why do you think Jesus refused the twelve legions ? What does that teach about how God wins?
  4. In Acts, angels send people to preachers but don't preach themselves . What does this say about the church's task?
  5. Hebrews says we have come to an innumerable company of angels . How should that shape the way we gather to worship?
