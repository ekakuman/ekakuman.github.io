Study Journey: From Dust to the Heavenly Places

One story in twelve chapters, for working through in a study session. Each chapter gives the big idea, sets the earthly side against the heavenly side, lists the key passages, and links to the visuals further down the page. When you jump to a visual, a button at the bottom of the screen brings you back. Your notes are saved in this browser, and the page remembers which chapter you were on.

Combination Explorer

Every passage is tagged by which of the three Scripture itself names in that verse — body (body, flesh, dust, bones), soul (soul, life of the person), spirit (spirit, breath of life). Tap a region of the diagram to see the verses that join exactly those parts.

Reading this in Telugu (పరిశుద్ధ గ్రంథము)

The Telugu Bible does not keep one word for each part, so compare carefully when teaching from it.

  • Soul is usually ప్రాణము (Ps 42:1; Luke 1:46), but often ఆత్మ (Matt 10:28; James 1:21; Rev 6:9), the same word used for spirit.
  • Spirit is usually ఆత్మ, but in the Old Testament often మనస్సు (Ps 51:10, 17; Prov 16:32) or జీవాత్మ (Zech 12:1; Isa 57:16).
  • Body / flesh is శరీరము or దేహము. Dust is మన్ను (Gen 3:19).
  • Heb 4:12 joins soul and spirit into one word: ప్రాణాత్మలను.

The tags on each card follow the KJV wording. Telugu text: Bible Society of India Old Version (public domain).

Where the KJV writes “Spirit” with a capital S (e.g. Galatians 5:17, Romans 8:10–13), the Holy Spirit is meant, set against the flesh. These are included because Scripture sets them side by side, but they are noted on the card.

Clarence Larkin's “Threefold Nature of Man”

From Dispensational Truth (1918), headed 1 Thessalonians 5:23 and Hebrews 4:12. Larkin draws three circles. The body meets the world through five gates. The soul holds imagination, conscience, memory, reason and affections. The spirit has its own “sense faculties” (faith, hope, reverence, prayer, worship), with the Holy Spirit at the centre. One channel joins them all: up from the gates, through Memory, past The Will, into the spirit. Two stippled bands mark the “dividing asunder” of Hebrews 4:12, and he labels the circles with Paul's three men: Carnal, Natural, Spiritual. Tap any part of the chart.

The Man Christ Jesus — Spirit, Soul and Body

The Gospels speak of all three in the Lord's own humanity. This is the pattern of perfect manhood.

His Spirit

“He groaned in the spirit, and was troubled” (John 11:33). “He sighed deeply in his spirit” (Mark 8:12). “He was troubled in spirit” (John 13:21). “Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit” (Luke 23:46).

His Soul

“My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death” (Matt 26:38). “Now is my soul troubled” (John 12:27). “Thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin” (Isa 53:10). “His soul was not left in hell” (Acts 2:31).

His Body

“A body hast thou prepared me” (Heb 10:5). “This is my body” (Matt 26:26). “His own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree” (1 Pet 2:24). “Handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones” (Luke 24:39).

Two Adams at the Gates

1 John 2:16 names three doors of temptation. Eve met all three at the tree and fell. Christ met all three in the wilderness and stood. Tap a column to focus it, then reveal His answers.

Every answer comes from Deuteronomy (8:3; 6:13; 6:16), the book of Israel's wilderness years: where Israel failed, Christ stood. Linking each door to one of Larkin's gates is a teaching aid, not something the texts state.

Heaven and Earth Connected

Ephesians speaks five times of “heavenly places.” The believer's body is on earth, but his life is hid with Christ above, and real traffic runs between the two realms. Choose a thread to see what moves between them.

Mansoul: the City with Gates

Proverbs pictures a man as a city. Larkin's five gates become the gates of the town (the names come from Bunyan's Mansoul). Tap a gate to post a watchman, then compare a city “broken down, and without walls” with one rebuilt as Nehemiah rebuilt Jerusalem.

Waters Rising: Ezekiel 47

The river flows from the threshold of the house. Each thousand cubits it rises: to the ankles, the knees, the loins, then “waters to swim in.” Step through the measurements.

The deepening water is a teaching picture of the life of the Spirit filling the whole man (John 7:38–39). Ezekiel does not name body, soul and spirit.

Treasure in Earthen Vessels

Gideon's three hundred carried lamps hidden inside empty pitchers. Nothing was seen until the pitchers were broken. Paul uses the same picture: the light is God's, and the vessel is clay.

The Tent and the House

2 Corinthians 5:1–8 in three scenes: living in the earthly tent, the tent taken down, and being clothed upon with the house from heaven.

Two Fields

“Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.” Sow into either field, then let the seasons pass. The flesh field comes up fast and showy, then rots. The Spirit field is slow, then golden.

Other Perspectives

These are views from outside Scripture: how teachers and theologians have read the texts above. They are kept separate from the verse study so the Scriptures can speak first.

Trichotomy · 1918

Clarence Larkin

Dispensational Truth, “The Threefold Nature of Man.” Body (carnal, 1 Cor 3:1–3) with five gates: eye, nose, ear, mouth and feel. Soul (natural, 1 Cor 2:14) with imagination, conscience, memory, reason and affections. Spirit (spiritual) with faith, hope, reverence, prayer, worship and spiritual insight, and the Holy Spirit at the centre. The Will is the one door into the spirit, and Memory is the channel that leads to it. Two bands mark the “dividing asunder” of Heb 4:12. The gate names echo John Bunyan's The Holy War (1682), where the town of Mansoul has Eye-gate, Ear-gate, Mouth-gate, Nose-gate and Feel-gate.

Trichotomy · 1928

Watchman Nee

The Spiritual Man. The spirit has three functions: intuition, communion, conscience. The soul has three: mind, emotion, will. The body is the outward shell. The spirit should rule the soul, and the soul should direct the body. Nee draws the Tabernacle parallel explicitly. Note that he puts conscience in the spirit, where Larkin puts it in the soul.

Trichotomy · 1855

Franz Delitzsch

A System of Biblical Psychology. A scholarly trichotomist. He held that spirit and soul are truly distinct but not two separable substances: the soul comes from the spirit and stands between spirit and body.

Early church · c. 180

Irenaeus

In Against Heresies (Book V) he describes the complete man as flesh, soul and spirit, quoting 1 Thessalonians 5:23. Some early Alexandrian writers also spoke in three-part terms.

Dichotomy

Augustine · Calvin · Hodge · Berkhof · Grudem

Man is two parts: a material body and an immaterial soul/spirit. “Soul” and “spirit” name the same inner man from different angles. They point to Hebrew parallelism (Luke 1:46–47), to Jesus' “soul troubled” and “spirit troubled” used side by side (John 12:27; 13:21), and to death being described as giving up the soul (Gen 35:18) or the spirit (Luke 23:46).

Holistic / Hebraic

Unity of the Person

Some stress that Genesis 2:7 says man became a living soul, not that he has one. On this view the terms describe the whole person seen from different sides, not separate compartments. Critics answer that Scripture plainly speaks of conscious existence apart from the body (2 Cor 5:8; Rev 6:9).
