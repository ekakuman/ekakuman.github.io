Parenting in Genesis

God's purpose carried through imperfect families, and what the covenants teach parents today.

The family carries the promise

Genesis is a book of families. God's plan of redemption runs through fathers, mothers and children, and almost every one of them gets something wrong. The promise survives anyway, because it rests on God and not on the parents .

Why parenting is central in GenesisTheme

The covenant is made with Abraham “and thy seed after thee in their generations.” Passing on the promise is a family task: Abraham is chosen so that he will teach his children the way of the LORD.

  • The vehicle is the household.
  • The instruments are flawed people.
  • The faithfulness belongs to God.

Patterns that repeat

Read the patriarchs together and the same family problems keep coming back.

Favoritism, passed downGenerational

Isaac loved Esau; Rebekah loved Jacob. Jacob grew up in a divided house and then did the same thing: he loved Joseph more than all his sons and gave him a coat that said so. His sons saw it and hated their brother.

  • Children learn partiality by living with it.
  • Jacob was deceived by his sons with a goat and a garment , the same tools he used to deceive his own father .
Sibling jealousy that turned violentCrisis

Cain killed Abel. Simeon and Levi used deceit to massacre the men of Shechem, leaving Jacob afraid for the whole family. The brothers plotted to kill Joseph and sold him for twenty pieces of silver instead.

Passive fathersSilence

When Dinah was violated, Jacob “held his peace” until his sons came home. After their revenge, his only complaint was about the danger to himself. He spoke to Simeon and Levi about it only on his deathbed.

Human schemes against God's wordGenesis 25–27

God had said “the elder shall serve the younger.” Isaac set out to bless Esau anyway, privately. Rebekah and Jacob answered with a disguise and a lie. Everyone tried to manage the promise, and the family was broken for twenty years.

  • God's purpose stood, but the deception cost Rebekah her son: as far as Genesis records, she never saw Jacob again.
Election is not favoritismCareful!

“Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated” is about God choosing a line to carry his redemptive plan, decided before either was born. It is not a parent's moody preference, and God shows no partiality toward persons. Isaac's and Jacob's favoritism can't be justified by appealing to God's election.

Parenting through the covenants

As redemptive history unfolds, each covenant adds something to what God asks of parents.

Children of believers are “holy”New Covenant

Paul says the children of a believing parent are set apart, and Peter's Pentecost promise is “unto you, and to your children.” This is not automatic salvation; it is a place within reach of the gospel and the life of God's people.

Hebrews 12: discipline is a mark of sonshipDavidic echo

God disciplines every son he receives. Discipline that is real but loving gives children security, not fear: it tells them they belong.

From patterns to practice

Each covenant pattern gives a principle, and each principle can be lived out at home.

Children are God's, entrusted to usStewardship

Children are “an heritage of the LORD,” not possessions.

  • Make the Lord's character and word the center of the home.
  • Start early: train up a child in the way he should go.
Love without partialityGovernance

Genesis shows what favoritism does. Love each child openly and equally.

  • Move from control to counsel as children grow.
  • Don't provoke them, “lest they be discouraged.”
Leave and cleaveCreation

Scripture sets no age of independence. The decisive transition is marriage, when a son or daughter leaves father and mother to form a new household. Marriage is to be held in honor, and the household reserved for it.

Work, money and worshipDaily reliance

Where your treasure is, your heart is. Teach children to work diligently, provide for their own, avoid the servitude of debt, and never let the love of money set the course of a life.

Model the gospel; aim at the heartGrace

Parents can disciple faithfully, but only the Holy Spirit gives new birth. That frees parents from trying to manufacture outcomes.

  • Admit your own faults, and ask your children's forgiveness plainly.
  • Address what drives behavior, not only the behavior itself.
  • Forgive readily; don't keep a record.

For discussion

  1. Jacob repeated his parents' favoritism even though he had suffered from it . What patterns from your own upbringing need to stop with you?
  2. Rebekah had a true word from God and still chose deception to make it happen. When are parents tempted to “help God” in wrong ways?
  3. Jacob stayed silent about Dinah . Where is silence a failure in parenting?
  4. Deuteronomy expects children to ask questions . What in your home's life of faith would make a child ask “what does this mean?”
  5. Joseph's family was full of failure, and God still worked good through it . How does that encourage parents who feel they have failed?
