Fulfilled in Christ

Israel had three anointed offices: king, prophet and priest. Every one of them failed. By 586 BC the throne was empty, the temple burned and the prophets silenced. “Messiah” and “Christ” both mean the Anointed One: the one who would hold all three offices perfectly, and forever.

“God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things… when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high.”
Hebrews 1:1–3 — Prophet · King · Priest

In one chapter Jesus measured himself against all three offices, each one tied to a figure on this timeline.

The King

Rules for God over his people

The pattern on the timeline

  • Saul was rejected for disobedience; David sinned; Solomon's heart turned to other gods.
  • All 19 kings of Israel “did evil.” Even Judah's best fell short: Hezekiah's pride, Josiah's death at Megiddo.
  • The last king, Zedekiah, was blinded and led away in chains, and the crown was removed.

The promise

“Thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever.”2 Sam 7:16 — to David
“Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David.”Isa 9:7
“I will raise unto David a righteous Branch… THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS.”Jer 23:5–6
“But thou, Beth-lehem Ephratah… out of thee shall he come forth… that is to be ruler in Israel.”Mic 5:2

Fulfilled in Jesus

“The Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David… and of his kingdom there shall be no end.”Luke 1:32–33
“KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.”Rev 19:16

The Prophet

Speaks God's word to the people

The pattern on the timeline

  • Elijah fled in despair; Jonah ran the other way.
  • Micaiah was jailed, Jeremiah thrown into a cistern and his scroll burned.
  • Zechariah son of Jehoiada was stoned in the temple court, and Uriah was killed by Jehoiakim.
  • False prophets like Hananiah told kings what they wanted to hear.

The promise

“The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet… like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken.”Deut 18:15
“I… will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him.”Deut 18:18
“The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings.”Isa 61:1

Fulfilled in Jesus

On the mount, with Moses and Elijah beside him: “This is my beloved Son… hear ye him.”Matt 17:5 — echoing Deut 18:15
“O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets…”Matt 23:37
“The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.”John 1:14

The Priest

Represents the people before God

The pattern on the timeline

  • Eli's sons corrupted the priesthood; Abiathar was removed.
  • Jeroboam made priests “of the lowest of the people”; Urijah copied a pagan altar.
  • Pashhur beat Jeremiah; the last high priest, Seraiah, was executed and the temple burned.
  • Priests died and had to be replaced; sacrifices had to be repeated every day.

The promise

To Eli: “I will raise me up a faithful priest, that shall do according to that which is in mine heart.”1 Sam 2:35
“Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.”Ps 110:4
“He bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.”Isa 53:12

Fulfilled in Jesus

“He ever liveth to make intercession for them… this he did once, when he offered up himself.”Heb 7:25–27
“The veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom.”Matt 27:51
“Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up… he spake of the temple of his body.”John 2:19–21

Threads on the timeline that point to him

Moments in the history above that become signposts when read from the cross.

King and priest kept apart — until one comes who is both
ThenUzziah tried to burn incense as king and was struck with leprosy (2 Chr 26). No king could be a priest.
Pointing toDavid's Lord would be “a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek,” the king-priest of Salem (Ps 110:1–4). Hebrews 7 applies this to Jesus.
“Until he come whose right it is”
ThenTo Zedekiah: “Remove the diadem, and take off the crown… I will overturn, overturn, overturn it” (Ezek 21:26–27).
Pointing toThe throne sat empty for six centuries, held open “until he come whose right it is; and I will give it him.”
The high priest crowned as a sign
ThenSeraiah, the last high priest, was executed in 586. His grandson Joshua returned from exile as high priest.
Pointing toZechariah crowned Joshua as a sign of “the BRANCH… he shall be a priest upon his throne: and the counsel of peace shall be between them both” (Zech 6:11–13). Joshua's name is the Hebrew form of Jesus.
The royal seed nearly wiped out — and hidden
ThenAthaliah killed the royal family; infant Joash, the last son of David, was hidden in the temple by the priest Jehoiada.
Pointing toHerod slaughtered Bethlehem's children; the infant Jesus, Son of David, was hidden in Egypt (Matt 2:13–16). God guarded the line both times.
A curse on Coniah's line
Then“Write ye this man childless… no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David” (Jer 22:30).
Pointing toJesus' legal line through Joseph runs through Jeconiah (Matt 1:11–12), yet he was not Joseph's son by blood. Many interpreters see his descent from David through Mary in Luke 3, via David's son Nathan.
A king named centuries ahead
ThenThe man of God named Josiah about 300 years before he was born (1 Kgs 13:2), and it came true to the letter (2 Kgs 23).
Pointing toMicah named Bethlehem as the Ruler's birthplace about 700 years ahead (Mic 5:2; Matt 2:4–6). The God who names Josiah keeps every word.
Elijah must come first
ThenElijah confronted Ahab and was taken up in a whirlwind; Malachi promised, “I will send you Elijah the prophet” (Mal 4:5).
Pointing toJohn the Baptist came “in the spirit and power of Elias” (Luke 1:17); “this is Elias, which was for to come” (Matt 11:14), preparing the way for the King.
One anointing for all three
ThenSamuel anointed Saul and David; priests were anointed with oil (Exod 29:7); Elijah was told to anoint Elisha (1 Kgs 19:16).
Pointing toAt Nazareth: “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me” (Luke 4:18), the Christ, the Anointed One.
“Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, and hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father.”
Revelation 1:5–6

The story does not end with Christ alone holding the three offices. Through him his people share them: “a royal priesthood… that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light” (1 Pet 2:9).

Judah kept the Levitical priesthood at the Jerusalem temple; Zadok's family held the high priesthood from Solomon on. Israel had no lawful priesthood after Jeroboam replaced the Levites with priests of his own choosing.

About the dates. Reign dates follow Edwin Thiele's widely used chronology (The Mysterious Numbers of the Hebrew Kings); other systems differ by a few years. Striped portions show co-regencies or rival rule, which explain why reign lengths overlap. Prophets' dates are approximate; Joel and Obadiah are dated differently by scholars (early or post-exilic). Quotations are from the King James Version.
