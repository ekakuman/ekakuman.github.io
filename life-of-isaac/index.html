Three stages of Isaac's life

Isaac has fewer chapters than Abraham, Jacob or Joseph, yet his life carries the covenant from the first patriarch to the third. Main readings: , , , , .

Isaac as a childబాల్యంలో

Isaac's birth is a promise kept at an impossible age. He grows up in a home where Abraham was charged to command his children in the way of the LORD, endures Ishmael's mocking, and is finally offered on the altar.

  • Summary: God fulfills impossible promises and establishes the covenant through surrender.
  • Application: Trust God's timing and give everything to him; he provides for every sacrifice he asks.
Isaac in his youth and middle yearsయవ్వనంలో

He walks with his father to Moriah and lets himself be bound, with no struggle recorded. He accepts the marriage his father arranges. Through twenty childless years he prays. He works his own land and prospers so much that the Philistines envy him.

  • He reopens his father's wells and gives them “the names by which his father had called them.”
  • He builds an altar where God appears to him, as Abraham did.
  • He gives up disputed wells rather than fight, until God makes room (Rehoboth).
  • Caution: he also repeats his father's sin, calling his wife his sister .
Isaac in old ageవృద్ధాప్యంలో

Blind and planning to bless Esau against God's word , Isaac is deceived. But when he realizes what happened he trembles and says, “yea, and he shall be blessed.” He then sends Jacob away with the blessing of Abraham, and Hebrews counts that blessing as an act of faith.

  • Summary: past his frailty and mistakes, Isaac yields to God's sovereign choice.
  • Application: walk by spiritual sight; God's purpose prevails and leaves a legacy of faith.

The Genesis themes in Isaac's story

Genesis plants themes that grow through the whole Bible. Isaac's life touches many of them.

Covenant and blessingGen 12 → Gal 3

God repeats the oath to Isaac personally: “in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed.” It runs through Sinai and David to the New Covenant, where the blessing of Abraham comes on the Gentiles through Christ.

SacrificeGen 22 → Heb 9

From Abel's lamb and Noah's altar to Moriah, the tabernacle, and finally Christ, who appeared once to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.

Faith and righteousnessGen 15 → Rom 4

Abraham believed God and it was counted for righteousness. Hebrews 11 extends the line of faith to Isaac and Jacob.

MarriageGen 2 → Rev 19

Isaac and Rebekah: a bride sought from far away by a trusted servant and brought to a son who loves her. Many have seen in it a picture of Christ and the church.

PeaceGen 26 → Matt 5

Isaac's answer to conflict was to move on and dig again. Eventually even his opponents came to him and said, “We saw certainly that the LORD was with thee.”

God's sovereign choiceGen 25 → Rom 9

God's word about the two sons stood despite Isaac's plans and Rebekah's schemes. God's purpose doesn't depend on our cooperation, though our sin still has its consequences.

Five lessons for Christian life

For discussion

  1. Isaac didn't resist on Moriah . What makes that kind of trust possible, between a son and a father, and between us and God?
  2. Isaac waited twenty years for children and prayed . What are you waiting for, and how are you praying?
  3. He repeated his father's lie . Which of your parents' weaknesses do you need to watch for in yourself?
  4. At Rehoboth Isaac said, “now the LORD hath made room for us” . When has letting go of a fight led to God making room?
  5. Isaac tried to bless the wrong son, then submitted when he saw God's hand . How do you respond when God overrules your plans?
